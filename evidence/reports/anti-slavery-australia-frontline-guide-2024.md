A 54-page practice guide produced by **Anti-Slavery Australia at the University of Technology
Sydney** — the organisation behind the **My Blue Sky** national forced marriage service — for any
frontline worker or community member in health, youth work, domestic and family violence, migrant and
refugee support, education or faith leadership.

## The facts that make it evidence

- **49.6 million** people in modern slavery globally in 2021: **22 million in forced marriage**,
  **27.6 million in forced labour**.
- Forced marriage is "**widespread** – seen across cultures, religions, nationalities, and
  societies", and over the last decade has increasingly been treated as **a human rights abuse, a
  form of gender-based violence, child abuse and modern slavery**.
- It breaches two rights at once: the right to **freely and voluntarily choose one's marriage
  partner**, and the right to be **free of slavery**.
- Since **criminalisation in 2013**, forced marriage has become and remained **Australia's most
  identified and reported form of modern slavery**, with **more than 500 reports investigated by the
  AFP** — and it "is known to be significantly underreported".

The guide then sets out **practice principles** for identifying and responding across sectors, rather
than legal argument.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 — the form of modern slavery that is actually most reported in Australia.** Students write
  about trafficking and sexual servitude; the answer with the AFP numbers behind it is **forced
  marriage**, and this is the source for that.
- **Two rights in one breach (hr.1.1)** — choosing your own spouse, and freedom from slavery. A neat
  way to show that a single practice can engage several rights, and that both appear in the UDHR and
  the ICCPR before they appear in the *Criminal Code*.
- **hr.2.12 / hr.2.5 — what NGOs actually do.** My Blue Sky is a free national legal service run out
  of a **university law faculty**; the guide is its attempt to build identification capacity in
  people who are not lawyers. Use it as the concrete content of "the role of non-government
  organisations", instead of the phrase "raise awareness".
- **Criminalisation is not the whole response (hr.t.effectiveness)** — the offence has existed since
  2013, reporting has risen, and the guide exists because **the people most likely to notice are
  teachers, nurses and youth workers**, not police. An argument that legal responses depend on
  non-legal infrastructure.
- **Under-reporting as the measurable problem** — 500+ investigations and "significantly
  underreported" in the same sentence. Pair with the four-undetected-victims ratio in the *Sarah*
  record and the UN Special Rapporteur's findings.
- **Crossover to Family (fam.1.3 / fam.2.4)** — consent to marriage, and forced marriage as a form of
  family violence. Useful in both courses from the one document.

## Caution

A **practice guide**, not research: it is written to help workers act, and its statistics are quoted
from other sources (ILO/Walk Free global estimates for 2021, AFP reporting data). Published **July
2024** — the "more than 500" AFP figure is cumulative to then and will have grown; check the AFP's
current reporting before using a number. Anti-Slavery Australia is an **advocacy and legal service
provider**, so the framing is deliberately protective. The guide is **introductory and
Australia-focused** by its own description, and it does not analyse the offence provisions, so do not
cite it for the elements of the *Criminal Code* offence. Forced marriage involves **coercion**;
arranged marriage entered into freely is lawful, and conflating the two is both wrong and offensive
to the communities most often discussed.
