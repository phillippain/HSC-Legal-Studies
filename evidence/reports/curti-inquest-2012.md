**Roberto Laudisio Curti**, a 21-year-old Brazilian student, died in the Sydney CBD on 18 March 2012 after a pursuit by police over the theft of two packets of biscuits. He was unarmed and affected by LSD. Officers tasered him repeatedly (including in 'drive stun' mode while he was held on the ground), used capsicum spray and handcuffs.

## What the Coroner found (14 November 2012)

- The actions of some officers were **reckless, careless, dangerous and excessively forceful**, and showed little understanding of the safe use of Tasers or OC spray; training was inadequate.
- The Coroner recommended that **five officers** be considered for disciplinary action and referred police conduct to the Police Integrity Commission (now the LECC).
- The **NSW Ombudsman** reviewed Taser use (2012) and recommended clearer rules, including on repeated and drive-stun use.

## What happened next

Officers faced disciplinary proceedings; several decisions were later overturned on review. The State spent more than $1 million defending the officers. NSW Police Taser use nevertheless rose from 642 (2016–17) to 2,290 (2025–26) (BOCSAR).

## Use it for

- **Reasonable force (LEPRA s 231):** what excessive force looks like, and the limits of accountability when the remedy is internal discipline. Compare *R v White* [2025] NSWSC 243 (Clare Nowland), where an officer was prosecuted.
- **Accountability bodies:** the Coroner finds facts and recommends; it cannot punish.

## Caution

Written from media summaries of the findings; quote the Coroner's words from the findings themselves before using them in an exam answer. The cause of death was not formally attributed to the Taser alone.
