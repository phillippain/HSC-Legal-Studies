PropTech platforms now stand between applicants and landlords. They collect **identity documents**
(passports, licences), **employment and financial records** (payslips, bank statements), references
and tenant-database checks. Some application forms carry **around 50 data fields**, including
**"lifestyle-related insights"**. Lead researcher Dr Sophia Maalsen: applicants "may be unaware they
are being vetted by PropTech".

## The problems the report names

- **The black box.** Applicants are scored, and the weightings are not disclosed — to them, or to
  anyone outside the platform.
- **Data security and retention** — material of the most sensitive kind, held indefinitely by private
  intermediaries with no statutory retention limit.
- **Discriminatory outcomes** produced without any discriminatory rule. Overseas comparisons: US
  services scraping public records, a Canadian firm collecting social media data, UK services flagging
  applicants as **"new to country"** or using **"high risk language"**.

## What it recommends

Restrictions on the collection and use of personal information (as considered in NSW and **passed in
Victoria**), standardised rental application forms, data retention limits, periodic regulatory review,
and full delivery of National Cabinet's **"A Better Deal for Renters"** commitment on data practices.

## Use it for

- **The contemporary issue the syllabus keeps asking for**, and one almost no student has: shelter
  law meeting technology. It pairs directly with Preliminary `ind.3.1`–`ind.3.4` (the individual and
  technology, difficulties enforcing rights, future directions).
- **Discrimination without a discriminator** — anti-discrimination law attaches to grounds and to
  decisions a person makes. A weighting inside a scoring model is neither, which is the enforceability
  gap in one sentence.
- **Where the law has responded**: **s 23** of the amended *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW)
  bans charging applicants for background checks, and **s 35** requires a fee-free way to pay rent.
  Both target **cost-shifting** by these platforms — the money, not the data. That is a precise
  "responded partly" point.
