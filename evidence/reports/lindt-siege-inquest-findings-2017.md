Man Haron Monis took hostages in the Lindt Chocolat Café, Martin Place, at about 9.41 am on **15 December 2014**; the siege ended about 2.13 am on **16 December**. Hostages Tori Johnson and Katrina Dawson and Monis died. He was on bail when it began.

## Bail chronology

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 15 Nov 2013 | Charged as an accessory to the murder of his ex-wife |
| 12 Dec 2013 | Granted bail (Bail Act 1978). The Coroner found the ODPP solicitor's oral submissions were "inadequate" and that he "erroneously advised the court that Monis did not have to show 'exceptional circumstances'" (para 16) |
| 14 Apr 2014 | Arrested on three sexual assault charges; bail refused (SBS, 5 June 2015) |
| 20 May 2014 | Bail Act 2013 commences |
| 26 May 2014 | Granted bail on those charges, six days after the new Act began (SBS, 5 June 2015) |
| Oct 2014 | 37 further sex offence charges brought by court attendance notice, not arrest; on 10 October the prosecutor did not oppose bail (paras 22-25, 30) |

## What the Coroner found (Conclusions, paras 14-31)

- Police "made a mistake" in not arresting Monis in October 2014; that "increased Monis' chances of being granted bail" (para 23).
- The prosecutor "should have applied for Monis to be remanded in custody in relation to the sex offences" (para 26) and did not oppose bail without consulting a senior officer; he had been in the office under two months and had never appeared in a bail application (para 30).
- The senior police officer's decision not to ask the DPP to seek Supreme Court review of the murder-charge bail was reasonable (para 21).
- Recommended that the DPP develop a policy for overseeing decisions not to oppose bail (para 31).

## The argument that the law was not to blame

Nicholas Cowdery, former DPP (UNSW / SMH, 18 December 2014): "This atypical and aberrant case is certainly not a good basis for launching into yet more interference with the Bail Act." "Mental disturbance is not a reason to imprison a person."

## The reform response

The joint Commonwealth-NSW Martin Place siege review recommended that bail authorities consider links with terrorist organisations or violent extremism. The **Bail Amendment Act 2015** (No 44; second reading by the Attorney General, Gabrielle Upton, on 20 October 2015) requires bail authorities to consider associations with a terrorist organisation and statements or activities advocating support for terrorism or violent extremism, and applies an 'exceptional circumstances' test to some terrorism-related accused. The Australian Lawyers Alliance warned of "a very slippery slope towards authoritarianism" (2015).

## Use it for

- **Responsiveness:** a single extreme event leading to law reform. Ask whether the reform targeted the law or its enforcement.
- **Enforceability and accountability:** bail failed through application (prosecution and police decisions) as much as through the text of the Act.
