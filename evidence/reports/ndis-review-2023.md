The NDIS, created by the *National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013* (Cth), funds individual plans for about 650,000 people and had grown to more than $40 billion a year by 2023. The Albanese Government commissioned Bruce Bonyhady and Lisa Paul to review it.

## Findings and recommendations

- The scheme had become an "oasis in the desert": with mainstream services and the states having withdrawn, people with disability who did not meet the NDIS test had nowhere to go, and those who did were pushed to seek ever-larger plans.
- **Foundational supports** — a new tier of services outside the NDIS, funded jointly by the Commonwealth and the states — for people with lesser needs and for children with developmental delay.
- Needs-based planning by assessment rather than by negotiation; a navigator role to replace support coordinators; stronger regulation of providers and a crackdown on fraud.

## The 2024 Act

Defined "NDIS supports" (a list of what plans can and cannot fund), introduced the new planning framework, and let the Agency vary plans — with a stated aim of holding growth to 8% a year. The ART (which replaced the AAT in October 2024) hears appeals against plan decisions.

## Use it for

- **exp.5.4** — alternative approaches to justice for people with disability: an entitlement scheme with administrative review, rather than courts, as the main path to a remedy.
- **exp.6.2** — barriers: eligibility, the adversarial planning process, and the cost of an appeal; **chg.4.2** — reform that cuts both ways.

## Caution

Foundational supports were still being negotiated with the states in 2025–26; cite the current status. The ART caseload of NDIS matters is the measurable access-to-justice figure — look for it in the ART annual report.
