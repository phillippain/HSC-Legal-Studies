> "They just move me around like a doggy in the pound pretty much, moving cage to cage."

The first Special Inquiry ever held under Part 5 of the *Advocate for Children and Young People Act
2014*. An **alternative care arrangement** is emergency out-of-home care in a hotel, motel, caravan
park, serviced apartment or short-term rental, where the child is supervised by **sub-contracted
workers from non-accredited agencies or labour hire companies** on a fee-for-service basis. They are
meant to be a last resort and short term.

## The numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| In high-cost emergency arrangements, 30 June 2023 | **~500** children, of whom **118** in ACAs — 26 in hotels and motels, 37 in serviced apartments, 55 in short-term rentals including caravan parks |
| At 31 March 2024 | **433** in HCEAs, **76** in ACAs |
| Cost, 2022–23 | **~$100 million**, averaging **$965,000 per child per year** |
| How long | **Most stay more than three months**; in the most extreme cases **more than 600 days** |
| Why they end up there (OCG 2020–21) | **32%** high-needs behaviours · **28%** placement breakdown · **14%** no available carers |

The Advocate's conclusion was unqualified: the system "is not functioning appropriately", the
children are "at significant risk of experiencing negative short and long-term impacts", and the use
of ACA accommodation **"should not be continued"**.

## Four recommendations

1. End the use of ACAs, and support the children who have been in them — including **immediate
   access to therapeutic support** on transition.
2. Invest in wellbeing supports: early therapeutic help even within "therapeutic placements", better
   **remuneration, training and support for volunteer foster carers**, professional and therapeutic
   care models alongside volunteers, and mental health first aid and trauma training for every carer.
3. An **independent complaints and oversight mechanism** — a complaints function with the authority
   to require action, an oversight body **reporting to Parliament** with power to review the
   wellbeing of children in care, and an independent socio-legal advocacy service.
4. Record keeping and **data sharing** between DCJ, health and education, independent audit of case
   files, and public reporting of placement data.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection of children)** — the strongest single source in the library on what
  the state actually provides to the children it has removed. A figure like **$965,000 a year to
  house a child in a motel** does the work of a paragraph.
- **Effectiveness measured against purpose** — the Act's object is the child's safety, welfare and
  wellbeing. Use the duration data: a "last resort, short-term" measure running past 600 days is the
  gap between the law's design and its operation.
- **chg.2 / dis.3.5 / dis.3.6 (agencies of reform, statutory bodies)** — an independent statutory Advocate
  using a power never used before, who **cannot order anything** and whose central recommendation is
  for an oversight body that reports to Parliament rather than to the department. Pair with the
  Ombudsman's ROSH findings and the Audit Office reports it cites.
- **The method is the argument** — private hearings with **65 children and young people**, so the
  evidence is their own words. Good for whether participation makes a report more effective.
- **Read with `telegraph-2025-aca-ended`**, which reports the recommendation carried out: this is one
  of the few complete reform cycles in the library.

## Caution

A **report by an advocate**, not a court or an audit: its job is to put the children's case, and it
says plainly that it does not endorse any service or type of care and provides **no cost comparison**
between ACAs and other placements. Figures come from DCJ data supplied during the inquiry and from
budget estimates answers; some are point-in-time and some are averages, so check which you are
quoting. It builds on *Family is Culture* (2019), ACYP's 2021 report and two 2024 Audit Office
reports, and does not restate their recommendations. A content warning applies — the material
includes children's direct accounts of harm.
