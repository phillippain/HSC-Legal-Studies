## The benchmarks that make the numbers mean something

This is what turns a statistic into a judgement, and most answers skip it. **Demographia** treats
housing as affordable at around **three times** median household income. **AHURI** and the **OECD**
identify housing stress where a household spends more than **30%** of income on housing. A balanced
rental market has vacancy rates around **3%**. Against each benchmark, Sydney is roughly double the
threshold.

Underneath them sit the **UDHR** and **ICESCR art 11(1)**, which recognise adequate housing as part
of an adequate standard of living.

## The judgement the sheet reaches

Legal measures aimed at the housing market — the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW), planning
law, property regulation — have been **only partially effective**. They deliver procedural
protection to tenants and regulate development, but the sustained rise in prices, rents and loan
sizes shows the framework does not reach the causes of unaffordability: supply, tax settings and
finance.

## Caution

Median prices and rents are averages across very different submarkets, and price-to-income ratios
move with interest rates as well as with prices. Lower rates today sit against much larger
mortgages, so repayment burden has not fallen with the rate.
