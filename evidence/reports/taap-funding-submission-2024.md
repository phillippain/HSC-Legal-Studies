The **Tenants Advice and Advocacy Program** funds the statewide network of Tenants Advice and
Advocacy Services and the Tenants' Union. It is financed **entirely from the interest on rental bonds**
held under the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW) — a legal mechanism paying for the non-legal
response that makes the legal one usable.

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Case files opened, 2023–24 | **more than 39,000** |
| Assistance sessions | **more than 140,000** |
| Unique users of the Tenants' Union website | **746,000** (2.2 million sessions) |
| Unmet demand | **39% statewide** — about **24,000 people a year** turned away |
| Baseline funding | **about $16.8 million** a year |
| Share of all bonds held | **0.83%**, down from **1.08%** in 2008 |
| Assistance funded per bond lodged | **under 12.5 minutes**, down from **20 minutes** in 2008 |

The submission sought an increase of either $9.165 million (preferred) or $4.979 million.

## Use it for

- **The proposition the whole "legal and non-legal" question turns on**: non-legal responses are the
  *access mechanism* for legal ones. A right a person cannot identify, in a forum they cannot
  navigate, resolves nothing.
- **Interdependence rather than alternatives** — this is the cleanest single example in the course of
  a legal mechanism financing a non-legal one. Neither category is self-sufficient.
- **Effectiveness capped by funding, not design.** 39% unmet need and a per-bond decline from 20 to
  12.5 minutes is an evaluation with numbers in it, which is what the criteria ask for.
- Read it beside NCAT's **28,193 tenancy applications**: the tenants defending those applications are
  the people this funding decides whether to help.

## Caution

An advocacy document by an interested party, and the funding figures are its own case for more
money. The service-delivery numbers are reported by the sector rather than audited.
