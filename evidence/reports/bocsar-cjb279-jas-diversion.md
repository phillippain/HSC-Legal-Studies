JAS Diversion provides intensive case coordination towards a **s 14 MHCIFPA 2020** application. It
is delivered by the **Intellectual Disability Rights Service**, an NGO community legal centre, under
NSW Government funding, and launched July 2022 at six court locations.

Study period July 2022 – June 2025: 718 JAS Diversion against 2,852 JAS Advocacy; 480 v 2,056 at
12-month follow-up. Groups matched by entropy balancing.

## Key findings

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| s 14 case dismissal | **+86%** (+22.4 p.p.) |
| Guilty outcome | −17% (−9.3 p.p.) |
| Custodial sentence length | −44 days (−15%) |
| **Reoffending within 12 months** | **−16%** (−6.3 p.p.) |
| Breach of justice order offences | −38% (−10.1 p.p.) — the main driver |
| Violent / property / drug offending | no significant change |
| NDIS package value | +$42,183/yr (+22%) |

## The reform chain, complete

s 32 *Mental Health (Forensic Provisions) Act 1990* → NSWLRC Reports 135/138 → **MHCIFPA 2020** →
Disability Royal Commission → pilot → **this evaluation**. Very few reforms in the course can be
tracked end to end with published evidence at each step; this one can.

## Evaluation

Strong on protection of individual rights, meeting society's needs and responsiveness. **Weak on
accessibility** — 6 of more than 100 Local Court locations. Resource efficiency is contested: a
cost shift from justice to disability services, not a saving.

## Caution

The comparison group also had cognitive impairment and also received (lighter) support, so it is
not an untreated control — the authors say this likely **understates** the effect. Quote the
percentage-point and relative figures, not rates derived from them.
