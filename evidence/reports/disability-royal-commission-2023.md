Established in April 2019 after years of advocacy by disability organisations and a Senate inquiry, the Commission held 32 public hearings and received almost 8,000 submissions and 1,785 private sessions.

## Findings

- People with disability experience violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation at far higher rates than others, in every setting — homes, group homes, schools, workplaces, hospitals, prisons and the justice system — and the systems meant to protect them often did the harm.
- The *Disability Discrimination Act* and state laws are reactive and individual; there is no national human rights Act and no enforceable right to inclusion.
- Women with disability, First Nations people with disability and people with cognitive disability in the criminal justice system were singled out for the most serious failures.

## Recommendations (222)

- A new **Disability Rights Act** giving domestic effect to the CRPD; a National Disability Commission; reform of the *DDA*; a federal Minister for Disability Inclusion.
- Phasing out group homes, segregated employment paying sub-minimum wages, and — on the split that defined the report — special schools: three commissioners recommended ending segregated education by 2051, three did not.
- Better oversight of restrictive practices and of the NDIS; supported decision-making to replace substitute decision-making; stronger protections in the criminal justice system.
