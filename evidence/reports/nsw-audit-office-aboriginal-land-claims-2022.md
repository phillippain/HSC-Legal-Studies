A performance audit of the Department of Premier and Cabinet, the Department of Planning and Environment (the Crown Lands Minister's department, which assesses claims) and NSWALC.

## Findings

- **53,861** claims lodged since 1983 to 31 December 2021; **38,257** undetermined (about 70%), over about 1.12 million hectares of Crown land.
- About 60% of undetermined claims had waited more than five years and 20% more than ten; the average time to determine a claim was about 4.4 years, the longest 27.6 years.
- The backlog grew from about 9,000 in 2007 to 26,000 in 2014 and over 38,000 by 2021 while lodgements ran at about 2,500 a year against a processing target of 1,750.
- The department had not met its statutory duty to determine claims within a reasonable time; there was no resourced strategy, no interagency governance, no transparent prioritisation, and a claims register not upgraded since the 1990s.

## Recommendations

A resourced ten-year plan to lift processing above the lodgement rate; a five-year workforce strategy; an interagency governance group led by DPC; an upgraded register; a spatial information system; better information-sharing with Land Councils — with deadlines through 2022–23.

## Use it for

- **chg.5.1** — independent evidence on the operation of the Act: the statutory right is real and the state's administration of it has failed the "reasonable time" test it set itself.

## Caution

Figures are to December 2021; the 2026 NSWALC release gives the later picture (44,000+). The audit assessed administration, not the merits of any claim.
