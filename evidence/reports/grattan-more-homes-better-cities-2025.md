A 39-slide briefing pack (with its chart data in the companion spreadsheet) from the Grattan
Institute's Housing and Economic Security program. The argument is about **supply and planning
law**: Australia has built too few homes where people most want to live, and the reason is
restrictive zoning, slow and uncertain approval processes, and planning governance that "favours
existing residents and is biased against change".

## The evidence

- From the 1940s to the 1980s the dwelling stock grew much faster than the adult population; since
  2000 **dwellings per 1,000 adults have fallen** — Australia is one of only a handful of OECD countries
  where that is true.
- Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide are among the **least dense** cities of their size in
  developed countries. If Sydney's inner 15 km were as dense as Toronto's it "would already have an
  extra 250k homes".
- Between 2006 and 2021 Sydney's inner and middle suburbs (within 15 km) added the **fewest** new
  homes of any mainland capital; growth happened on the fringe.

## What Grattan wants

| Who | Reform |
|---|---|
| **States** | Upzone capital cities: a *Low-Rise Housing Standard* (three-storey townhouses permitted on all residential land) and a *Mid-Rise Housing Standard* (6+ storeys around transit hubs); use complying-development and deemed-to-comply pathways; subject planning controls to cost-benefit analysis |
| **Commonwealth** | The New Home Bonus "isn't working" — most states will not qualify; pay states for specific planning reforms through a revived National Competition Policy; have the Productivity Commission audit planning systems |
| **NSW v Victoria** | Both have upzoned around transit hubs (NSW's Transport Oriented Development and Low and Mid-Rise Housing policies), but Victoria's Townhouse Code goes further on modest density; "recent planning reforms in NSW and Victoria don't go far enough" |

## Caution

An **advocacy briefing**, not a neutral review: the density comparisons are population-weighted and
filtered to richer countries, and the supply-only diagnosis is contested by those who stress demand,
tax settings and social housing. Slide format — quote the figures with their notes. The chart-data
spreadsheet sits beside the pack in the library for anyone who wants to replot them. For Sydney
specifically, pair with Grattan's February 2026 report.
