The Attorney General referred bail law to the Commission on **8 June 2011**. The Commission received 40 submissions, held 19 consultation meetings and reported in **April 2012**, concluding that a new, simplified Bail Act was required (para 0.1).

## What it found about the Bail Act 1978

- **Complexity.** Amendments had made the Act "difficult to comprehend and operate, even for those with legal expertise" (para 0.9) and NSW had "one of the most convoluted and restrictive bail statutes in Australia" (para 0.10).
- **Offence-based presumptions.** Amendments had removed the presumption in favour of bail for some offences, introduced a presumption against it for others and required "exceptional circumstances" for others again (para 0.10).
- **Remand.** The tightening cut failures to appear but at a significant cost in remand numbers; there was no evidence it reduced crime or offending on bail (para 0.11). Remand prisoner numbers had **more than trebled in 15 years** and the rate per 100,000 had more than doubled (para 0.12). Young people on remand rose from about 225 (2000) to over 400 (2010) (para 0.13).
- **The 'one shot' rule (s 22A).** Introduced to stop meritless repeat applications, extended in 2007 (which substantially increased remand, especially for young people) and moderated in 2009 (para 0.71).

## What it recommended

- A new Act in plain English.
- A **'justification' model** (a person is released unless detention is justified by five mandatory considerations) rather than the Victorian 'unacceptable risk' model (para 0.32; Rec 10.1, 10.2).
- A **uniform presumption in favour of release**, except on appeal (para 0.27; Rec 8.1).
- Two repeat applications for adults before the bar applies (Rec 19.1).

## What Parliament did

The Attorney General, Greg Smith, introduced the Bail Bill on **1 May 2013**: the 1978 Act had been amended by more than 80 other Acts; presumptions applied by offence and "may not reflect the actual seriousness of the alleged offending"; the "key feature" was "a simple unacceptable-risk test". The Bail Act 2013 commenced on **20 May 2014**. Parliament took the call for a simpler Act but not the 'justification' model or the presumption in favour of release.
