From 2015 to 2019 Centrelink raised debts by "income averaging" — dividing a year's ATO income evenly across fortnights — and put the onus on welfare recipients to disprove them. About 470,000 debts were unlawful. A Federal Court class action settled in 2020 for $1.8 billion (repayments, interest and $112 million compensation), and the Commonwealth conceded the method was unlawful.

## The commission

- Established August 2022 under the *Royal Commissions Act 1902* (Cth); public hearings compelled ministers and secretaries to give evidence.
- Found the scheme was unlawful from the start, that legal advice saying so was ignored or buried, and that the Commonwealth Ombudsman's 2017 investigation was misled by the department.
- 57 recommendations: legal advice to be shared, automated decision-making to be regulated, a statutory duty of care, reform of the Ombudsman's powers and of the (then) AAT, and a sealed chapter referring individuals to the AFP, the National Anti-Corruption Commission and professional bodies.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.6 / dis.3.8** — what a royal commission can do that review and courts could not: the class action got money back, the commission got findings about who knew.
- **dis.3.2 / inv.1.8** — a dispute with the state caused by the state's own misuse of law; procedural fairness (inv.1.6) reversed onto the citizen.

## Caution

The NACC decided in 2024 not to investigate the six referred officials, then reopened the question after an inspector found that decision affected by apprehended bias — the accountability story is not finished. Quote the report, not the news coverage, for findings.
