The Turnbull Government resisted a royal commission for eighteen months and then called one on 14 December 2017 after the major banks themselves asked for it. The Commission took 10,000 public submissions and held 68 days of hearings in 2018 in which senior executives were cross-examined on live television.

## Findings

- "Fees for no service": AMP and the big four banks charged ongoing advice fees to customers who received no advice — some of them dead. Remediation ultimately exceeded $4 billion.
- Irresponsible lending, pressure selling of insurance to vulnerable customers, and remuneration that rewarded sales over service: "the pursuit of short-term profit at the expense of basic standards of honesty".
- Regulators had preferred negotiation to litigation; Hayne told ASIC to ask "why not litigate?".

## Recommendations (76)

- End grandfathered commissions and move financial advice to fee-for-service; extend the executive accountability regime to all APRA-regulated entities (now the FAR Act 2023); a compensation scheme of last resort (commenced 2024); a single default superannuation account; stronger enforcement by ASIC.
- 24 referrals of possible breaches to ASIC and APRA; the Commissioner declined to recommend structural separation of the banks.

## Caution

Hayne's hearings produced reputational and political consequences; prosecutions from the referrals were few, and ASIC lost several of the cases it brought. Keep "named the cause as greed" as the Commissioner's finding, not as a legal conclusion.
