On **14 June 2019** the residents of **Mascot Towers**, 1–5 Bourke Street, Mascot, were ordered to
evacuate after cracking appeared throughout the building. The residential floors stayed empty; in
late December 2020 the ground-floor shops and restaurants became inaccessible too. Owners and
business operators were locked out of their own property for **over three years**, and the report
records the "significant hardship" that followed.

The Department commissioned an investigation under **s 430 of the *Local Government Act 1993***
into the former **City of Botany Bay Council**, after public reporting that the council's
development file could not be located and had "gone missing" — which turned out to be delay in
finding it rather than loss.

## The finding

On the council's file, the report concluded there was adequate documentation showing the council:

- **satisfied its statutory obligations as the consent authority** in processing, assessing and
  determining the development application;
- **satisfied its statutory obligations as the Principal Certifying Authority** appointed to certify
  the development and issue the construction and occupation certificates required by the consent;
  and
- **satisfactorily exercised its statutory functions** as a local council under the LG Act.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (securing other types of shelter)** — this is the record that makes the strata-defects
  argument properly. A building fails so badly that it is condemned, an independent investigation is
  commissioned, and the answer is that **every legal requirement was met**. If the regulator complied
  and the outcome was still catastrophic, the inadequacy sits in the standards and the structure of
  certification, not in enforcement.
- **Private certification** — the same council acted as **both consent authority and certifier**.
  That combination of roles, and the private certification regime generally, is the target of the
  reforms that followed. Use the finding to show why reform targeted the system rather than
  individuals.
- **chg.1 (conditions giving rise to reform)** — Mascot Towers and Opal Tower are the two events
  behind the **Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020** and the **Residential Apartment Buildings
  (Compliance and Enforcement Powers) Act 2020**, and behind the duty of care in **DBP Act s 37**
  that the High Court applied in ***Pafburn***. This document is the evidence of what they were
  reacting to.
- **Remedies and their limits (shel.2.5, dis.3.4)** — the owners' problem was not that no law applied
  but that compliance with it gave them nothing. Pair with *Brookfield Multiplex*, where the High
  Court denied owners a duty of care in negligence, and with *Pafburn*, where the statutory duty
  filled part of that gap.

## Caution

A **law firm's report to a department on one narrow question** — whether the council met its
statutory obligations — not an inquiry into why the building cracked. It does **not** determine the
cause of the defects, assign responsibility to the builder, developer or certifier, or recommend
reform, and it expressly records its own limitations and the documents it reviewed. The findings are
**opinions based on the council's file**, framed "in our opinion" and "based on information
available in the public domain", and parts rest on what the authors understood rather than
established. Do not use it to say nobody was at fault for Mascot Towers; use it for what it says,
which is that **the council was not**.
