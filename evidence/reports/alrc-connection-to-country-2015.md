The Attorney-General referred the connection requirements of the *Native Title Act 1993* (s 223) and the authorisation and joinder provisions to the ALRC in 2013, after two decades in which proving native title had become slower and more expensive than *Mabo* had implied — *Yorta Yorta* (2002) had required claimants to show their traditional laws and customs had been observed "substantially uninterrupted" since sovereignty.

## Recommendations (30)

- Amend s 223 so that claimants need not prove a traditional society has continued "substantially uninterrupted", that laws and customs may adapt and evolve, and that native title may include commercial rights.
- Allow a claim to be made on behalf of a group where there has been "succession" to country.
- Streamline authorisation of claimants and the joinder of other parties.

## What happened

The connection recommendations were not adopted. The *Native Title Legislation Amendment Act 2021* (Cth) took up the procedural recommendations — authorisation, the role of registered native title bodies corporate, historical extinguishment on parks — and left s 223 as the courts had read it.
