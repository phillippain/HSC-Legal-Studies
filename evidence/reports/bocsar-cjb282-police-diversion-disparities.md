BOCSAR analysed **56,742** police events involving **23,605** children. **68%** of Aboriginal children's events went to court
against **38%** of non-Aboriginal children's; among events eligible for YOA diversion the figures were **54%** and **24%**.

## How police dealt with eligible matters

| | Aboriginal children | Non-Aboriginal children |
|---|---|---|
| Sent to court | 54% | 24% |
| Warned | 10% | 22% |
| Cautioned | 30% | 48% |
| On bail when proceeded against | About 30% | About 13% |
| First formal contact sent to court | 10% | 5% |

Of the **30-point** gap, about 22 points link to offence type (5), bail status (5) and prior contact (the rest); about
**8 points are unexplained** (18–22 explained under other weightings). The gap grows from 15 points at ages 10–11 to 36 at 17,
because each formal contact makes later diversion less likely.

## The diversion ladder

Section 7 says the least restrictive suitable option should be used and Aboriginal over-representation addressed through
diversion. Excluded offences (s 8) go to court; warnings suit non-violent summary offences; cautions needed an admission
(from 1 July 2026 a non-denial statement suffices, with a three-cautions-in-three-years limit); conferences for more serious
eligible offending.

- **History**: BOCSAR found 64.8% of eligible Indigenous young people diverted against 85.1% of others (2013); the youth
  diversion rate fell from 51.3% to 46.8% (2024); the Productivity Commission says NSW is worsening on Closing the Gap Target 11.

## In the media (AAP, 10 November 2026)

AAP's report ("Racism blamed for Indigenous youth police treatment") adds Aboriginal Legal Service and ministerial views; no NSW
Police response was included. A [second class summary, built on the AAP report](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1B3EVBfgdwTPmw1RBuGuCfWOuI458iE8U/view) notes
the YOA's objects (s 3(d)) and that about **72%** of the gap is linked to recorded factors — leaving the unexplained 8 points as a
question of equality before the law.

## Use it for

- **crime.5.5 / crime.2.1** — police discretion under the YOA and whether it is applied fairly.
- **Effectiveness** — diversion is the law's main tool for keeping children out of court; unequal use undermines it.

## Caution

A **class summary of a statistical bulletin**: the findings are associations, not proof of bias or its cause.
