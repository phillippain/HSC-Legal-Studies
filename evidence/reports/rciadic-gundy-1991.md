On 27 April 1989 members of the Special Weapons and Operations Section raided a house in Marrickville before dawn, looking for John Porter, who was wanted for shooting police officers. Porter was not there. **David Gundy**, an Aboriginal man asleep in his bedroom with his young son, was shot dead by police.

## What the Commission found

- The raid was part of a series of searches based on thin information; the warrants were obtained and executed in ways the Commission strongly criticised, including the failure to properly identify police and announce the purpose of the entry.
- Police planning placed the aim of catching Porter above the safety of occupants; the Commission found Gundy's death resulted from a wrongful and unjustified use of force in the execution of a search.
- The Commission's broader findings led to the recommendations behind the **Custody Notification Service** and changes to police practice.

## Use it for

- **Use of search warrants (LEPRA Part 5):** a warrant is only a safeguard if it is obtained on proper grounds and executed lawfully; the occupier's notice and the duty to identify exist because of cases like this.
- **Accountability:** a Royal Commission found facts no criminal prosecution did; Gundy's family later received compensation.
