The **National Student Ombudsman** (NSO) was created by Part IIF of the *Ombudsman Act 1976*
(passed 28 Nov 2024, operating from 1 Feb 2025) as the escalated-complaints pathway for higher
education students. After the **26 March 2025** National Day of Action for Palestine protest at
UTS — where a guest speaker singled out a Jewish counter-protester — **five** students complained;
**two** never received an individual response. UTS commissioned an external review, widened it to an
approved AUJS event, published an Action Plan on 7 August 2025 that wrongly found AUJS lacked
approval, and gave AUJS no right of reply. Two students escalated; the NSO investigated from
9 October 2025 and reported on **20 October 2026**.

## Findings and recommendations

| Finding (s 21AV: "wrong" ×5, "unjust" ×1) | Recommendation → UTS response |
|---|---|
| Breached its own Complaints Policy (45 working days); two complainants got nothing | Apologise — **accepted**, done |
| Called an unauthorised protest a "peaceful gathering" while publicly naming the compliant AUJS | Review protest rules — **accepted**, Campus Policy v3.4 (May 2026) |
| Failed to identify potential bias by ActivateUTS, which shaped the review's wrong AUJS findings | Public apology; guidance on overseeing external reviews — **accepted**, guidance still in progress |
| Poor communication; last-minute conditions on an AUJS event | Review event guidelines — **accepted** |
| No procedural fairness before naming AUJS in the Action Plan (**unjust**) | Apology — **accepted**; **reissue the Action Plan — declined** (plan already removed) |

Recommendations are **not binding**: the NSO relies on publication and can ask the Minister to
table a report. Education Minister Clare said UTS "let Jewish students down".

## Use it for

- **hr.2.4 / ind.2.7 (statutory authorities, ombudsmen)** — a new oversight body in action: how a
  complaint escalates, what a finding of "wrong or unjust" means, and the enforcement limit.
- **ls.1.6 (procedural fairness)** — the right to be heard before an adverse finding is published,
  applied to a student organisation.
- **hr.3.1 / hr.t.values** — freedom from racial discrimination against protest and expression:
  the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Human Rights (Feb 2025, 10 recommendations), the Special
  Envoy's July 2025 plan with possible funding cuts, and the AHRC's *Racism@Uni* study (Feb 2026:
  over 90% of religious Jewish respondents reported racism; 60–80% of those experiencing racism
  dissatisfied with complaint processes) on one side; the NTEU and Amnesty warning that antisemitism
  definitions chill academic freedom and ICCPR art 19 rights on the other.
- **lip.2.3 (effectiveness)** — six of seven accepted, apologies issued, policy changed — but the
  incorrect claim about AUJS remains in circulation because reissue was refused.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **news report and the university's own response**, not a full reading
of the report. The ABC misnames AUJS (it is the *Australasian* Union of Jewish Students) and says six
recommendations are implemented when UTS reports one still in progress. The NSO investigated
**complaint handling and review oversight**, not whether the protest breached anti-discrimination
law; it made no finding of discrimination. Protest organisers' and ActivateUTS's views are not in
the article. Check the full report before quoting findings.
