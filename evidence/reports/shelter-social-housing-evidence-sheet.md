## The judgement the sheet reaches

**Legal measures** — allocation and tenancy regulation under the *Housing Act 2001* (NSW) and
tenancy protections under the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW) — deliver procedural fairness
and accountability in **how** social housing is allocated, and have almost no capacity to address
**how much** of it exists. Moderately effective at the thing they govern; irrelevant to the
shortage.

**Non-legal measures** — the Tenants' Union of NSW advising and campaigning, Mission Australia and
the St Vincent de Paul Society providing emergency accommodation, and media reporting driving policy
attention — support individuals and raise the issue, and have not changed the scale of demand.

## Use it for

The cleanest example in the option of a distinction worth making in any effectiveness answer:
**procedural effectiveness and substantive effectiveness are different things**. The law allocates a
scarce resource fairly and does nothing about the scarcity. Saying so, with the waiting list figure
attached, is a band 6 move.

Pair with the ANAO finding that 1,432 of 40,000 Housing Australia Future Fund homes had been
delivered by May 2026 — the supply response, audited.

## Caution

"Social housing" covers public, community and Aboriginal housing, and the split between them has
shifted towards community providers, so a falling public housing figure is not the whole story.
Waiting list figures count **approved applicants**, not everyone in need.
