Young people given a youth justice conference in 2007 were matched (propensity scores) with
conference-eligible young people dealt with in the Children's Court. There were **no significant
differences** in the proportion who reoffended, the seriousness of reoffending, the time to
reoffending or the number of new offences. The authors concluded conferences were 'no more
effective than the NSW Children's Court in reducing juvenile re-offending'.

## Use it for

- **Alternatives to court** (`crime.5.5`): conferences are cheaper and victims report high
  satisfaction, so 'no worse than court' can still favour them.

## Caution

The data are from 2007 and predate the 2026 Young Offenders Act reforms.
