Walk Free's fifth Index, and the source of nearly every modern slavery number quoted in Australian
reporting. It ranks **160 countries** on prevalence, vulnerability and government response.

## The figures worth knowing

- **50 million** people living in modern slavery on any given day in 2021 — on the underlying 49.6
  million estimate, **27.6 million** in forced labour and **22 million** in forced marriage. More than half of forced labour (**54%**) is in
  upper-middle or high-income countries.
- Highest **prevalence**: **North Korea, Eritrea, Mauritania**. Mauritania **32.0 per 1,000**.
- Strongest **government responses**: **United Kingdom, Australia, Netherlands**. Australia's
  response score **67**.
- **Australia: 1.6 per 1,000 people — 41,000 people** in a population of 25.5 million, ranking 26th
  in its region.
- **G20 countries import US$468 billion** worth of products at risk of being made with forced
  labour — including **US$19.7 billion of palm oil** and **US$14.8 billion of solar panels**.
- Australia's *Modern Slavery Act 2018* is cited as a positive development: companies with
  consolidated revenue over **AU$100 million** (about US$67 million) must report on their actions.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 — the scale and the ranking in one source.** Any answer on modern slavery needs a number;
  this is where the 50 million and the 41,000 come from, so **cite the Index rather than a newspaper
  quoting it**.
- **The contradiction at the centre of the issue** — Australia has one of the **strongest government
  responses in the world** and still has 41,000 people in modern slavery. That tension is the whole
  evaluation: a strong legislative response is not the same as an effective one.
- **The supply chain argument (hr.2.9 / crime.6)** — US$468 billion of at-risk imports into G20
  countries, with palm oil and solar panels named. It converts "modern slavery is a global problem"
  into a measurable flow of goods into wealthy economies, and it is the argument for the reporting
  obligation in the *Modern Slavery Act*.
- **54% of forced labour in richer countries** — the single most useful line for dismantling the
  assumption that this is a developing-world problem.
- **Prevalence against response (hr.2.3 / hr.2.5)** — the Index measures both, so you can argue the
  relationship between what a state does and what happens in it, and note that the worst-prevalence
  states are also the least accountable.
- **Non-government organisations as a source of evidence (hr.2.12)** — the data governments rely on
  here is produced by an **NGO**, not an official statistical agency. Worth a sentence in itself about
  how the issue is monitored.

## Caution

These are **modelled estimates**, not counts — built from nationally representative surveys, ILO and
IOM data, and extrapolation, with wide and uneven margins of error; Walk Free itself cautions that
estimates for regions with limited survey coverage are highly variable. The headline figures describe
**2021**, published **May 2023**, so a 2026 answer should say so; the 2025 edition and the ILO's own
global estimates will have moved. Walk Free is an **advocacy organisation** founded by the Minderoo
Foundation: the Index is well regarded, but it is not neutral and it is designed to produce pressure.
"Modern slavery" here is an umbrella for forced labour, forced marriage, debt bondage, servitude,
trafficking and the worst forms of child labour — not a single legal category, and the definitions
differ from the offences in the *Criminal Code*. The **government response score of 67** is Walk
Free's own composite measure; do not treat it as an objective grade.
