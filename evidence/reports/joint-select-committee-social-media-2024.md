Established in May 2024 as Meta withdrew from paying for news and public concern about young people's mental health grew, the Committee took evidence from platforms, researchers, eSafety, parents and young people through 2024.

## What it recommended

- A statutory **digital duty of care** on platforms, with eSafety enforcing it — the model later taken up in the 2026 bill.
- Greater transparency over algorithms and recommender systems; stronger data and privacy protection for children; funding for digital literacy.
- On age: it noted the evidence was divided, recommended that any minimum age be set with young people's input and with age assurance that protects privacy, and **did not recommend** the 16-year ban. The Committee reported on 18 November 2024; the *Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Bill* was introduced on 21 November and passed on 28 November after a one-day Senate inquiry.

## Use it for

- **chg.6.3** — drivers: a parliamentary inquiry as a formal driver, alongside the informal ones (the *36 Months* campaign, News Corp's *Let Them Be Kids*, the October 2024 Social Media Summit) that outran it.
- **chg.6.4** — process: the gap between the inquiry's advice and the executive's decision shows the executive method (political will) at work.

## Caution

The report has 12 recommendations across a wide field; cite the duty-of-care and age findings specifically. The speed of the November 2024 bill is itself a point about process — mention the Senate committee's one-day hearing as fact, not as judgement.
