Homelessness NSW is the not-for-profit **peak body** for 200+ specialist homelessness services and
allied organisations. This report, prepared with **Equity Economics** (a survey of services and
economic modelling of a 50% funding uplift), is the evidence base for its *A Place to Call Home*
campaign.

## The crisis, in its figures

- Modelling puts the number of people experiencing homelessness in NSW at **up to 80,000**, with
  almost **700,000** more at risk.
- **Over 300,000** households in rental stress; one in five rental households and one in seven
  mortgaged households are low-income and in financial stress.
- In 2022 almost **12%** of NSW households needed social or affordable housing; social housing is
  **4.6%** of stock.
- **67,721** households on the social housing waiting list in October 2025, up over 8% in a year;
  **12,031** on the priority list; general-list waits exceed **10 years** in many parts of NSW.
- Unassisted requests for help have **doubled** since 2018–19; in 2023–24 only **36%** of clients
  needing short-term or emergency housing were provided with it.

## The two asks

1. **Increase homelessness services funding by 50%** — to shift services "from crisis to
   prevention", fund early intervention, crisis accommodation, transitional housing and wraparound
   support.
2. **Grow social housing by 10,000 homes a year until 10% of all housing is social housing.**

## Caution

**Advocacy with a funding ask**: the 80,000 and 700,000 are the authors' modelling, not official
counts (the 2021 Census counted 35,011 homeless in NSW); the survey is of the sector's own members.
Government funding figures are presented from the sector's side. Check DCJ's published waiting-list
data for the current waitlist number before quoting it. The Drive link to this document resolves after
the next nightly map.
