The 2024 Act required platforms to take "reasonable steps" without saying how. The Government commissioned an independent trial — run by the UK-based Age Check Certification Scheme with Australian partners — of the technologies platforms might use, and released the final report in August 2025, four months before the obligation began.

## Findings

- More than **60 technologies from 48 vendors** were tested across three approaches: age *verification* (documents, bank or government credentials), age *estimation* (facial analysis, behavioural signals) and age *inference* (existing account data).
- Age assurance "can be done in Australia" and is "practical and achievable"; no single solution suits every service or every user, so a layered approach is appropriate.
- Methods exist to counter spoofing, forged documents and VPN use, though none is perfect; estimation is least accurate around the 16-year boundary, which is where the law draws its line.
- Vendors showed strong awareness of privacy, and the trial found no need to retain personal data to check age.

## Use it for

- **chg.6.4** — the executive process of turning a principle into enforceable practice; eSafety's regulatory guidance drew on the trial.
- **chg.6.5** — outcomes: benefits (a workable standard; privacy-preserving methods) and limitations (error rates at 16; the ten-month enforcement figures showing 42% of 10–15-year-olds still holding accounts).

## Caution

A commissioned report that supported the government's position; critics (digital-rights groups, some researchers) dispute the accuracy claims. Use its facts about the technologies, and attribute the "achievable" judgement to the report.
