Bourke, in far western NSW, had some of the highest rates of youth offending and domestic violence
in the state. From **2013** Aboriginal community leaders, with **Just Reinvest NSW**, built
**Maranguka** ('caring for others' in Ngemba), a **justice reinvestment** model: use local data to
move money from prisons into early support.

## KPMG's 2018 impact assessment (2017 compared with 2016)

| Measure | Change |
|---|---|
| Police-recorded domestic violence | **−23%** |
| Charges, top five juvenile offence categories | **−38%** |
| Days in custody | **−42%** |
| Bail breaches | −14% |
| Year 12 retention | **+31%** |
| Economic impact, 2017 | **$3.1 million** |

## Use it for

- **Social crime prevention** (`crime.1.9`): the strongest Australian evidence that addressing causes
  reduces crime.
- **Self-determination**: designed and led by the Aboriginal community.
- **Evaluation**: a single-town, before-and-after comparison, so causation is not proven; set it
  against BOCSAR's randomised trial of Youth on Track, which found no significant fall in reoffending.

## Caution

The figures come from KPMG's 2018 assessment; look for later evaluations before quoting it as current.
