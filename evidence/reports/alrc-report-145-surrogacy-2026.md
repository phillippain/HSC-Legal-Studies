The ALRC's final report makes **47** recommendations. Domestic gestational surrogacy births rose from an estimated **27** to **84** a year, and overseas surrogacy births to Australians from **246** to **375** (2015–2024); about **80%** of Australians' surrogacy arrangements take place overseas.

## Current law v the ALRC model

| Current law | ALRC proposal (Report 145) |
|---|---|
| Eight separate regimes; states regulate only about 20% of arrangements | One Commonwealth law or uniform laws; a National Regulator (Recs 3, 4, 6) |
| Profit banned; surrogates often out of pocket | Profit ban kept; full reimbursement plus optional capped loss payments (Recs 1, 23, 24) |
| Mostly checked after birth; only Vic and WA require pre-pregnancy approval | A licensed Surrogacy Support Organisation approves before any pregnancy attempt (Recs 8, 9) |
| NSW: court parentage order 30 days to 6 months after birth | Intended parents are legal parents from birth for approved arrangements (Rec 28) |
| Overseas offences in ACT, NSW, Qld, WA, never used | Repeal; register and take advice; civil penalties (Recs 36–44) |
| No national register of gestational origins | A National Surrogacy Register and birth certificate addendum (Recs 30–32) |

None of this is law: implementation needs Commonwealth legislation and state referral or mirror laws (Rec 4).
