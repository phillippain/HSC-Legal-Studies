**Youth on Track (YoT)** is a NSW **early intervention** scheme that identifies and responds to
young people **aged 10–17** at risk of long-term involvement in the criminal justice system — before
the Children's Court, not after it.

## How it works

**Youth Justice NSW**, within the Department of Communities and Justice, **funds non-government
organisations** to engage eligible young people **and their families** in case management and to
deliver "evidence-informed interventions that respond to assessed levels of risk and criminogenic
needs".

It began on **1 July 2013** in three pilot sites — **Blacktown, Hunter and Mid North Coast**;
extended in **2016** to **Coffs Clarence, New England and Central West**; and added a **seventh
site**, the **Riverina Police District**, in **2019**.

## What the 2020 snapshot measured

Risk is assessed with the **Youth Level of Service Case Management Inventory – Australian Adaptation
(YLS/CMI-AA)**, a validated tool measuring a young person's risk of reoffending.

- **66%** of those who completed YoT in 2020 **started** at medium-high or high risk.
- By completion, **75%** had moved **down** to low or medium risk. (Entry: 3% low, 31% medium, 54%
  medium-high, 12% high. Exit: 9% low, 16% medium, 53% medium-high, 22% high — as the snapshot's own
  chart labels report.)
- Against internal targets for the proportion of participants reducing risk factors over time:
  **65% after three months** (target 50%), **74% after six months** (target 70%), **100% at program
  completion** (target 100%).

The snapshot itself notes that **BOCSAR was evaluating YoT through a randomised controlled trial**,
"considered the gold standard of evaluations, and the most robust evaluation method to assess the
impact of YoT on reoffending", with the report due late 2021.

## Use it for

- **crime.5.5 (alternatives to court) — an intervention that happens before the law does.** The
  *Young Offenders Act 1997* diversions (warnings, cautions, youth justice conferences) all follow an
  offence. YoT targets young people **at risk**, which is a different and more ambitious thing. Name
  the scheme, the age range, the start date and the seven sites.
- **Read it with the RCT, and that pairing is the whole point.** This snapshot reports that **75% of
  completers lowered their assessed risk** and that it **met every internal target**.
  `bocsar-cjb249-youth-on-track` reports the randomised trial finding **no significant fall in
  reoffending**, though participants were more likely to be employed. Both are true. The difference
  is that one measures a **risk-assessment score** and the other measures **whether they offended**.
  That is the most valuable methodological lesson in the Crime option: **a program can hit its
  performance indicators and not change the outcome it exists to change.**
- **Self-reported performance against self-set targets (dis.3.5)** — the targets (50%, 70%, 100%) are
  the program unit's own, and the report is by the unit that runs the scheme. Note that, and then note
  that the same report **points to the independent RCT** — which is to its credit.
- **Outsourced delivery (crime.5.5 / exp.2.5)** — the state funds **NGOs** to do the work, the same
  structure as out-of-home care in `smh-2025-jack-died-in-state-care`. Worth a sentence on
  accountability for publicly funded, privately delivered intervention.
- **Early intervention versus the age of criminal responsibility (crime.5.1)** — the scheme starts at
  **10**, the age of criminal responsibility. Use it in any answer about raising that age: the
  argument for raising it is that children of 10 need **services**, not charges, and YoT is what a
  service-based response looks like.
- **Geography (inv.1.4)** — seven sites in eleven years, mostly regional. A young person's access to
  early intervention depends on their **postcode**, which is a concrete equality point.

## Caution

Published by the **Youth on Track Unit itself** — it is **program reporting, not evaluation**, and
it says so by pointing to the BOCSAR RCT. **Do not use it as evidence that YoT reduces offending**:
it measures **change in an assessed risk score** among those who **completed** the program, which
excludes those who dropped out, and the independent RCT found **no significant reduction in
reoffending**. Cite `bocsar-cjb249-youth-on-track` for effectiveness and this for **what the program
is and how it reports**.

The figures are for the **2020 calendar year** — a COVID year, which affected both offending and
service delivery, and the report does not control for that. The entry and exit percentages were read
from the snapshot's **chart labels**, so check them against the document before quoting, and note
that the exit distribution as labelled does not obviously reconcile with the stated "75% lowered to
low or medium" — treat the headline statements as the report's claims and verify before use. The
scheme has been running since 2013 and may have expanded, changed or ended since: check the current
Youth Justice NSW position. The **YLS/CMI-AA** is described as "validated" by the body using it.
