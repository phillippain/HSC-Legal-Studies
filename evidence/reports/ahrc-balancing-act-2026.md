Released **19 October 2026**, the same day Four Corners aired allegations from **104 women** of
pregnancy and carer discrimination inside the Australian Public Service. The report is built on
**five roundtables and 13 meetings with 84 stakeholders** (March–April 2026) plus a literature
review — **qualitative, not a prevalence survey**. Its headline figure, **49% of mothers** reporting
discrimination, is from the AHRC's **2014** national review, which also found **91%** never lodged a
formal complaint. AHRC pregnancy complaints rose from **54 (2021) to 72 (2025)**.

## Current law v what the AHRC proposes

| | Now | Reform area |
|---|---|---|
| Flexible work | **Right to request** (*Fair Work Act* ss 65–65C): employer must discuss, answer in 21 days, refuse only on reasonable business grounds; FWC can arbitrate since June 2023 | A **presumptive right** — provided unless refused |
| Service | 12 months before unpaid parental leave or a flexible request | Remove the 12-month requirement |
| Family responsibilities | *Sex Discrimination Act 1984* s 7A covers **direct** discrimination only | Prohibit **indirect** discrimination too |
| Redundancy | Return-to-role guarantee "works in theory" | Limit redundancies near parental leave; employer to prove it is genuine |
| Paid parental leave | 26 weeks at minimum wage plus super (from 1 July 2026) | Up to 52 weeks at wage replacement; more reserved weeks per parent; extend to foster, kinship and grandparent carers; drop work and residency tests |
| Access to justice | AHRC conciliation delays; power imbalance at FWC | Fund AHRC, legal aid and CLCs; change FWC process |

Two pathways exist: a **flexible work dispute** (FWC conciliation, then arbitration under s 65C) or a
**discrimination complaint** (AHRC conciliation under SDA ss 7, 7A, 14, then 60 days to the Federal
Court or FCFCOA). Since 2 October 2024 federal discrimination applicants are generally shielded from
adverse costs — but not from their own fees.

## The Four Corners case

A Home Affairs employee's request for a compressed four-day week after parental leave was refused on
"reasonable business grounds" (the role, the department says, changed after the Bondi attack). FWC
mediation failed; she did not go to arbitration or court because of cost and took a voluntary
redundancy. Minister Rishworth: there *is* a right to request; the public service should be a
model employer. The government has not committed to any recommendation.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.2 / fam.1.5 (changing parental responsibility; parents' rights)** — the clearest current
  evidence that caring responsibilities are where family law meets workplace law, and that the
  protections are procedural (a right to ask) rather than substantive.
- **hr.2.4 / dis.3.5 / dis.3.6 (statutory authorities)** — the AHRC doing its three jobs at once: complaint
  handling, inquiry and advocacy — and the limits of a body that cannot change the law it reports on.
- **hr.2.7 / inv.6.3 / inv.7.2 (incorporation)** — CEDAW art 11(2) and **ILO Convention 156** (ratified 1990)
  behind the SDA; a good example of a treaty obligation carried into statute but not into practice.
- **exp.6 (effectiveness)** — the enforcement gap: 91% did not complain; the one who did stopped at
  cost.

## Caution

A **class-made summary**. The report is **advocacy from consultations**, not new data, and its 49%
figure is twelve years old. The Four Corners accounts are **untested allegations**; only one has a
departmental response, and the ABC story does not mention the AHRC report at all. The 18 reform areas
are **recommendations, not law or policy** — no government response was found. Fair Work Act and
Paid Parental Leave rules changed in 2023 and 2026, so check current provisions before quoting them.
