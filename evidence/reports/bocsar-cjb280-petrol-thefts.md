Independently peer reviewed. Three empirical approaches: **two-way fixed effects** and
**instrumental variables** to test causation, and an **error correction model** for the medium-term
relationship with living costs, wages and population.

## Findings

- **A 1% increase in petrol prices causes thefts to rise by about 3%** (instrumental variables).
- **No immediate effect** of short-run daily price differences (two-way fixed effects) — daily
  variation is small, and a theft takes some planning.
- Over the medium term, thefts are positively associated with petrol prices but have a **stronger**
  relationship with the level of **living costs and wages**.

The puzzle that prompted it: between 2022 and 2024 petrol theft incidents in NSW rose considerably
while prices stayed relatively stable, and in 2025 prices stayed stable while thefts fell slightly.

## Use it for

- **Factors affecting criminal behaviour** with something almost no student has: an
  Australian study that establishes **causation**, not correlation, and says how. Most answers assert
  that disadvantage causes crime; this measures one channel and finds that a broader one — the cost
  of living — matters more.
- **Situational and social crime prevention**: if the driver is economic pressure rather than
  opportunity, then pre-payment pumps and number-plate cameras address the symptom and wage and
  cost-of-living policy addresses the cause. That is a crime prevention argument with evidence under
  it.
- **The purposes of punishment**: deterrence assumes a calculating offender. A 3% elasticity to price
  suggests need is doing more work than calculation.
- Pairs with the recorded-crime trends, where **retail theft is the only property offence rising**.

## Caution

Petrol theft is a "fail to pay" incident and is **detected at the point of sale**, so reporting is
near complete — unusually good data, but not generalisable to offences that go unreported. A causal
estimate from one offence type does not license a general claim that economic pressure causes crime.
