## The remand story

| | June 2017 | June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Adults on remand | 4,309 | **6,423** — up 49% |
| Adults sentenced | 8,783 | 7,537 — down 14% |
| Remand share, adults | about a third | **46%** |
| Remand share, young people | | **85.1%** — a record high |
| Aboriginal adults in custody | 3,235 | **4,726** — up 46%, while the non-Aboriginal population fell 6% |
| Aboriginal adults on remand | 1,060 | 2,270 — **up 114%** |

Of 24,209 receptions into custody in 2025–26, **90.4% were on remand**. Median stay 46 days. Of the
22,455 adults who left remand, **11,788 — more than half — were released** rather than sent on to a
sentence.

Aboriginal adults are about **3.4%** of the NSW adult population and **33.9%** of the custody
population.

## Use it for

- **Bail or remand**, and the presumption of innocence. Five in six children in detention have not
  been sentenced. That single figure does more work in an answer than any argument about it.
- **Balancing victims, offenders and society**: more than half of those held on remand were
  released without a full-time sentence, so the system detained them and then decided it did not
  need to.
- The offence mix has shifted to violence — sexual offences +61%, DV assault +54%, weapons +85% —
  while robbery fell 52% and burglary 38%. Pair with the recorded-crime trends, which split the
  same way.
- **The prison population is ageing** — 18–24 down 28.5%, 65+ up 83.9% — largely from longer
  sentences for historical sexual offences.

## Caution

A **stock** figure is a headcount on one night; **flow** counts receptions and discharges. Remand
figures describe *alleged* offending, since the most serious offence is a charge, not a conviction.
