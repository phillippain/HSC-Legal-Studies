The **Australian Housing and Urban Research Institute** is the national, government-funded housing
research network; its Final Reports are peer-reviewed and written for policy makers. No. 347 is the
capstone of a three-year Inquiry into *an effective homelessness service system*, drawing on four
projects, systems thinking and Indigenous research methods, and comparisons with Finland, Canada,
the United States and the four UK nations.

## Diagnosis

- Australia's homelessness service system is **"largely crisis-driven"**: over 1,500 Specialist
  Homelessness Services funded from Commonwealth, state, charitable and local sources — "a highly
  fragmented system".
- **Australia does not have a national homelessness strategy**, only a funding agreement with the
  states and territories.
- The system "has known strengths but its effectiveness is constrained for different population
  groups" — young people, families, older people and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people
  each need different responses.
- "Overseas experience shows that homelessness can be prevented and virtually ended."

## The redesign

1. **Prevention and early intervention** rather than crisis response — universal welfare services
   (schools, hospitals, Centrelink, corrections) screen for risk of homelessness.
2. A **"duty to assist"** protocol: every person at risk is quickly given appropriate help, with
   brokerage funding to keep existing housing or find new housing (modelled on Wales's *Housing
   (Wales) Act 2014* and Scotland's homelessness law).
3. **Housing First** for people already homeless — long-term housing first, support attached.
4. **Long-term plans for an adequate supply of social and affordable housing.**
5. **Place-based alliances** of homelessness services, mainstream agencies and governments, and
   an **integrated national homelessness initiative** with new funding arrangements.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.3 (homelessness)** — the authoritative statement of *what an effective response would
  look like*, against which NSW's policies and the sector's 2025 asks can be measured.
- **shel.2.8 / shel.2.9** — the relationship between government agencies and NGO services, and why
  fragmentation undermines both.
- **right-to-housing-comparative / hr.2.12** — the "duty to assist" as a statutory, enforceable
  obligation in Wales and Scotland that Australia lacks: the international comparison the syllabus
  rewards.
- **Evaluation** — the Homelessness NSW report (2025) shows the sector still "from crisis to
  prevention" five years on: evidence the recommendations were not implemented.

## Caution

**2020**: pre-dates the 2021 Census, the HAFF and the 2024 National Housing and Homelessness
Agreement, so use it for the *framework*, not current numbers. It is a 100-plus page research
report; the executive summary (pp 1–11) is enough for Legal Studies. AHURI recommends; it does not
legislate — check what NSW's *Homelessness Strategy 2025–2035* has adopted before claiming anything
was or was not done.
