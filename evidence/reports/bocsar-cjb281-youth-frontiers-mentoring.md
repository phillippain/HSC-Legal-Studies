From January 2023 to June 2025, **450** young people aged 10–17 took part across four NSW sites; **51%** were Aboriginal and **70%**
were young women or girls. The median mentee had **5** sessions (mean 9.7), and **under 20%** reached the required 52 hours. The
evaluation was co-designed with Aboriginal researchers.

## Prevention or punishment?

| | Youth Frontiers mentoring | s 22C bail test |
|---|---|---|
| Who | 10–17-year-olds in contact with justice or child protection | 14–17-year-olds charged with repeat car theft or serious break and enter |
| How | Trusted mentor, goals, life skills, culture and community connection | Bail refused unless the court has high confidence of no serious offence |
| Aboriginal young people | 230 of 450 mentees; two Aboriginal-led sites | 79% of s 22C cases and 84% of refusals |
| Reach | Four sites; under 20% reached 52 hours | 454 first bail appearances (April 2024 to December 2025); 70% refused |
| Evidence of effect | Self-reported less justice contact at two sites; no comparison group | A government review says regional youth crime stabilised; data not seen |
| Fit with principle | YOA s 7; CROC art 40(3)(b) | Critics say it conflicts with CROC art 37(b) |

Referrals come from police, Youth Justice, the Youth Koori Court, PCYC, Elders and schools; mentoring is meant to run 12 months.
Mentor recruitment did not keep pace with referrals, causing waitlists.

## Use it for

- **crime.5.5 / crime.1.9** — social crime prevention and alternatives to court for young people.
- **Effectiveness** — a program that sits beside, not inside, formal YOA diversion, while NSW tightens youth bail.

## Caution

A **process evaluation**: it shows how the program runs, not whether it reduces offending; it relies on people still engaged, and
client survey response rates were too low to measure outcomes.
