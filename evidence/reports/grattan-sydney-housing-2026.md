| Finding | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Sydney house prices up about **12×** since the early 1980s | Long-term affordability decline, not a recent shock |
| Most residential land in Sydney is zoned primarily for **detached housing** | Townhouses and apartments are prohibited, not merely unbuilt |
| Only a small share of housing within walking distance of a train station is medium or high density | Land near infrastructure used inefficiently |
| Complex approval processes limit construction | A regulatory barrier to supply, with holding costs passed into prices |
| Population growth and demand for well-located housing have outpaced supply | The immediate driver of prices and rents |

The *Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979* (NSW) governs land use planning and development
approvals. It balances development against environmental protection and community consultation —
and, on this research, restrictive zoning and approval processes limit construction and push costs up.

## Use it for

- **The cause, not just the symptom.** Most Shelter answers describe unaffordability; this names a
  legal mechanism that produces it. Zoning is law, made under statute, and it is the lever a
  government can actually pull.
- **A non-government organisation as an agency of law reform** (Preliminary ls.4.2) — a think tank
  producing the evidence base for a legislative change.
- The counterweight to the tenancy-regulation argument: if supply is the binding constraint, then
  reforms that protect tenants improve security without improving affordability. Holding both of
  those at once is a band 6 judgement.

## Caution

Grattan is an independent think tank with a consistent pro-supply position, and the causal claim
that zoning drives prices is contested by researchers who emphasise tax settings and finance. Say
so, and the judgement gets stronger rather than weaker.
