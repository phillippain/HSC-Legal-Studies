The library copy is the Commission's **Fact Sheet 1**, a one-page summary of the inquiry.

## What it found

- The inquiry began in **October 2018** and comprised **seven investigations**, plus a review of NSW Police Force policies, training and complaint handling.
- **Four investigations found the strip searches unlawful** (Operations Brugge, Gennaker, Sandbridge and Mainz). Operation Sandbridge made findings of serious misconduct against individual officers.
- Every investigation noted **systemic problems with the training** officers received about when and how to strip search.
- Expert opinion described the psychological impact on young people as ranging from humiliation and distress to trauma.

## What it recommended

**25 recommendations.** Three asked Parliament to clarify LEPRA itself:

- an exhaustive definition of a strip search
- whether police can require a person to squat, bend over or move their genitals
- how the "seriousness and urgency" test for strip searches in the field should be read

The others cover instructions to officers, record keeping, training and quality assurance, plus a joint audit by the Commission and the Police Force once the new policies had settled.

## Use it for

- **Police powers and compliance**: LEPRA ss 31–34 set clear limits, yet police routinely failed to follow them. Compliance by the police, not only by the public, is part of the theme.
- **Effectiveness of oversight**: the Commission investigated and recommended, and the Police Force changed its policies. But the Commission cannot prosecute, and the remedy for individuals came later through the courts (*Meredith v State of NSW*, 2025).
- **The rights of children when questioned or arrested**: s 33 requires a parent or support person for 10–17-year-olds, and s 34 bars strip searching anyone under 10.

This is a summary fact sheet, not the 2020 final report. Cite the final report for detail, and give its date (15 December 2020). The fact sheet's own figures are about the inquiry, not about how many strip searches police carry out; use BOCSAR's policing activity data for counts.
