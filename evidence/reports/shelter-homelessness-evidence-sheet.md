| Indicator | 2016 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| People experiencing homelessness, NSW | 37,715 | **35,011** |
| Rate per 10,000 | 50 | 43 |
| Rough sleeping | 1,532 | **963** |
| Supported accommodation | 6,813 | 5,043 |
| Temporarily with other households | 5,012 | 4,100 |
| Boarding houses | 9,909 | 8,842 |
| **Severely overcrowded dwellings** | 12,525 | **14,640** |

Every category fell **except severe overcrowding**, which rose — and it is now the largest single
category. NSW Street Count, February 2025: **2,192** rough sleepers, above the 2016 figure of about
2,000.

Specialist Homelessness Services clients in NSW rose from about 63,000 in 2016–17 to **67,900** in
2023–24, and national **unmet need for emergency accommodation** rose from about 27% to **31%**.

## Use it for

- **The definition is the argument.** The ABS counts rough sleeping, supported accommodation,
  temporary arrangements, boarding houses and severe overcrowding. Change the definition and the
  trend changes direction. An answer that says homelessness fell between 2016 and 2021 is quoting
  the headline and missing the composition.
- **Hidden homelessness**: 14,640 people in severely overcrowded dwellings have no tenure, no
  *Residential Tenancies Act* protection and no place in the visible statistics.
- **Unmet need at 31%** is the sharpest measure of whether the non-legal response is adequate:
  nearly one in three people who ask for emergency accommodation do not get it.

## Caution

The Census counts on one night and is known to undercount rough sleeping. The street count and the
Census use different methods and are not directly comparable. Service figures count **clients**, not
people without housing.
