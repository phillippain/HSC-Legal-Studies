The **Household, Income and Labour Dynamics in Australia** survey has followed the same households
every year since 2001 — about 17,000 people in 9,000 households, funded by the Department of Social
Services and run by the Melbourne Institute. Because it follows the same people over time it shows
**trends**, not snapshots, which is what makes it evidence about whether legal responses have
improved access to shelter. The class brief reorganises the 2026 report (data to 2024) for the
Shelter option.

## The figures worth memorising

| Finding | 2024 | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Australians in **housing stress** | **11.2%** — a record in 24 years | low of 7.4% in 2021 |
| Lower-income **Sydney** private renters in stress | **31%** | highest of any mainland capital |
| Social housing tenants rating housing "less than adequate" | **18.7%** | 13.6% in 2002 |
| Median weekly rent, mainland capitals | **$536** | $352 in 2001 (+52% real); regional $228 → $360 |
| Median weekly mortgage costs, capitals | **$705** | $548 in 2021 — +$157 (29%) in three years |
| Single-parent families in housing stress | **26.7%** | the most of any family type |
| 18–29-year-olds living with parents | **50%** | 39% in 2001 |
| Private renters vs mortgage holders in stress | **23.1% vs 9.6%** | renters more than twice as likely |

Real median household disposable income **fell 4.3%** from 2021 to 2023 and in 2024 was still 3.3%
below its 2021 level.

## Three terms to use precisely

- **Housing stress** — the *30/40 rule*: more than 30% of disposable income on rent or mortgage
  **and** in the bottom 40% of incomes. Not simply paying over 30%.
- **Housing adequacy** — the household's own rating: space, bedrooms, light, temperature, damp,
  access to transport and services.
- **Housing suitability (CNOS)** — the ABS bedroom standard. In 2024 only **4.9%** were
  overcrowded, while **75.6%** had a spare bedroom.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1** — the headline evidence for affordability, with a trend line behind it rather than a
  single year.
- **shel.3.4** — adequacy in social housing getting worse, not better, over two decades.
- **shel.2.2** — why leasing matters: renters carry the stress, without equity or security.
- **Evaluation** — the record 11.2% is the number to set against Commonwealth Rent Assistance,
  social housing programs and tenancy law reform when arguing effectiveness.

## Caution

Survey data, self-reported, and to 2024 — it cannot capture 2025–26 changes. Chart values in the
brief were read off the report's figures, so quote them as approximate; the exact tables are in
chapters 11 (affordability and adequacy), 8 (housing stress), 3 (single parents) and 9 (food
insecurity). "Housing stress" is a defined measure: use it precisely or not at all.
