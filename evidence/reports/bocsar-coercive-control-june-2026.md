**s 54D** *Crimes Act 1900* (NSW), in force 1 July 2024, criminalises a **course of conduct** of
abusive behaviour against a current or former intimate partner, done with intent to coerce or
control, where a reasonable person would think it likely to cause fear of violence or serious impact
on daily life. Maximum penalty **7 years**. This is BOCSAR's monitoring of its first two years.

## The funnel — the number to quote

| Stage | Two years to 30 June 2026 |
|---|---|
| Incidents recorded by police | **682** (average 4.4 controlling behaviours each) |
| No charge of any kind | **55%** (382 events) |
| Other charges only — stalking, DV assault | **39%** (269 events) |
| Charged with coercive control | **6%** (41 events; 42 people, all male) |
| Median time from event to charge | **187 days** |
| Finalised coercive control charges | 10 — **4 proven, 6 withdrawn** |
| Still pending | 32 |
| Sentences | 2 imprisonment, 1 intensive correction order, 1 community correction order |

Recorded incidents are rising: 72 in the July–September 2024 quarter to 103 in April–June 2026
(+43%), and charges from 3 in 2024 to 20 in the first half of 2026.

## What the behaviour looks like

The top five recorded behaviours are all non-physical: harassment, monitoring or tracking (59%),
threats or intimidation (55%), **financial abuse (54%)**, shaming or degrading (47%), social
isolation or cultural abuse (46%). Only **33%** involved physical violence. Coercive control was the
only offence recorded in 40% of events.

- **Victims**: 95% female; 93% of matters are a female victim with a male alleged offender. 47% had
  a prior domestic violence victimisation (average 2.5 records); 24% already had an ADVO in force.
- **Where**: regional NSW **15.2 per 100,000** against Greater Sydney's **4.3** — a regional rate
  3.5 times Sydney's. Central West 3.4× the NSW rate; the Eastern Suburbs 0.0×.
- **Flags**: only 4% of events were flagged mental-health related and 7% alcohol related.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.4 and crime effectiveness** — the single best evidence that creating an offence is not the
  same as enforcing one. Pair it with *Fairleigh*, where the charge was dropped in a plea deal.
- **crime.2.2 / police discretion** — in 269 events police laid other charges instead, offences that
  are easier to prove; the 187-day median shows why building a pattern case is slow.
- **crime.1.8 / equity** — the regional–metropolitan gap, and the absence of alcohol and mental
  health flags, point to power and control rather than intoxication or illness.
- **Law reform** — a worked example of law responding to changing community values, and of the
  implementation lag that follows.

## Caution

A rise in *recorded* crime can mean better awareness and reporting, not more abuse — BOCSAR says so,
and an answer that ignores it misreads the data. The charge and court figures are small numbers
(10 finalised charges), so treat percentages of them carefully, and note that court outcomes lag
recording by design.
