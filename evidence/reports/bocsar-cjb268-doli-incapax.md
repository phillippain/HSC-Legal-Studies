For 10–13-year-olds in the NSW Children's Court, 2015–16 to 2022–23:

| Measure | 2015–16 | 2022–23 |
|---|---|---|
| Proven outcome | 76% | **16%** |
| All charges withdrawn | 12% | **53%** |
| Guilty plea | 54% | 14% |

The number of children charged was mostly stable. Victoria (75% to 7%) and South Australia (41% to
8%), which also relied on the common law, showed the same decline; Queensland, WA and Tasmania,
which had codified the test, did not.

The SMH (11 July 2025, 'NSW youth crime: how doli incapax means children are beating serious
criminal charges') used these figures and the case of 'Harry', a child with more than 100 police
interactions, to argue for change.

## Use it for

- **Age of criminal responsibility** (`crime.5.1`): NSW had a *de facto* minimum age near 14 while
  the legal minimum stayed 10.
- **Law reform**: step 2 of the chain RP (2016) → BOCSAR (2025) → Bellew and Loy review → s 5 (2026).

## Caution

The bulletin shows association with the timing of RP, not proof that RP alone caused the change.
Advocates argue the fall shows the presumption working as intended.
