Two complaints under the **ICCPR**. In the first, **24 unaccompanied minors** intercepted at sea were held
on Christmas Island (2013–14) and then transferred to the Regional Processing Centre on **Nauru** in 2014.
Most were recognised as refugees in 2014 but stayed in detention. The second concerned an Iranian woman
recognised as a refugee in 2017 and not released.

## Found

- Australia had **significant control and influence** over the Nauru facility, so the people held there were
  within Australia's jurisdiction under the ICCPR.
- Their detention was **arbitrary**, contrary to **article 9**.
- A state cannot avoid its human rights obligations by outsourcing asylum processing to another state.

Australia argued the alleged violations did not occur within its jurisdiction. Teacher: check the
Government's formal response before using this point.

## Use it for

- **exp.5.1 / exp.5.2** — international protection, and a UN body as an authority that advocates.
- **exp.6.2** — a clear barrier: the finding has no force in Australian law.
- **hr.2.1 / hr.2.2** — state sovereignty and the role of the UN.

## Caution

These are **views**, not a court judgment. The Committee cannot order Australia to do anything. The source
linked is a news report of the decision; the Committee's own text is on the UN treaty body database.
