Senator **Lidia Thorpe** introduced two private senator's bills on **18 August 2026** amending the
*Crimes Act 1914* (Cth): proposed **s 87A** bans any restraint during labour, just before or after birth;
proposed **s 90C** bans strip or partial strip searches of anyone **under 18** — both overriding state
law, relying on the **external affairs power** (s 51(xxix)). The Senate debated them on 15 October 2026
without a vote. On 26 October two UN **Special Rapporteurs** criticised the lack of a decision and called
for national standards.

## Current position v the bills

| | Now | The bills |
|---|---|---|
| Restraint in childbirth | State policies (Queensland: "last resort") | Total ban (s 87A); Bangkok Rule 24 is also absolute |
| Strip searching children | Permitted under state law; NSW Police searched **1,546** children 2016–23, **44.6%** First Nations (6.2% of NSW children) | Total ban (s 90C) |
| Who decides | Each state and territory | Commonwealth law prevails |
| Committee view | Shackling bill should not pass — a state matter; collect national data | — |
| Status | Debated 15 Oct, no vote; still before the Senate | — |

Media reports that the Senate "voted down" the shackling bill conflict with the Senate record, which
shows no decision. Days before the release, a woman was allegedly restrained after a caesarean in
Townsville (QCS is reviewing).

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of an advocacy release** that names no bills and gives no figures; the
figures come from Redfern Legal Centre data and committee reports. The crossbench-support claim and the
experts' letter to the government are unverified. Check Senate Hansard for 15 October.
