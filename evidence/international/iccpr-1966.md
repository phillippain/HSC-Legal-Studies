Australia ratified the ICCPR in **1980**. Because Australia is a **dualist** state, ratification does
not make a treaty part of domestic law: it must be incorporated by statute. Large parts of the ICCPR
never have been.

Art 18 (freedom of thought, conscience and religion) is the provision in tension with CROC art 19 in
*Attar & Chawd*.

## Use it for

- **How international law is incorporated into Australian law** (Year 11 inv.6.3 / inv.7.2 as well as
  hr.2.7): ratification, then incorporation — or not.
- **A Charter of Rights**: the argument that Australia has accepted obligations it has given its own
  citizens no way to enforce. The counter-argument is parliamentary sovereignty.
- **Compliance and non-compliance** at the level of a state, not an individual.
