## UNTOC (the Palermo Convention)

**Article 3(2)** defines an offence as **transnational** if it is:

1. committed in more than one state;
2. committed in one state but **planned, directed or controlled** in another;
3. committed in one state by an **organised criminal group** active in more than one; or
4. committed in one state with **substantial effects** in another.

Three protocols: **trafficking in persons**, **smuggling of migrants**, and **firearms**. States agree to
criminalise participation in organised crime, laundering and corruption, and to cooperate on extradition
and mutual assistance. Australia has ratified the Convention and its protocols.

## The UN Convention against Cybercrime

Adopted by the General Assembly in **December 2024**, the first global cybercrime treaty. Australia
**signed in Hanoi in October 2025**. It is **not yet in force** (it needs 40 ratifications). Human rights
groups criticised its broad surveillance powers.

## Use it for

- **Define transnational crime** using the treaty's four tests.
- **International measures**: treaties set common standards, but bind only states that join and
  enforce them.
