*Falepili* is a Tuvaluan word for the values of good neighbourliness, care and mutual respect. The treaty was signed on the sidelines of the Pacific Islands Forum — the regional intergovernmental organisation through which Australia and the Pacific states cooperate — and ratified by both parliaments in 2024.

## What it provides

- **Climate and statehood**: Australia recognises that Tuvalu's statehood and sovereignty will continue, and its maritime zones will be preserved, notwithstanding sea-level rise — the first such recognition in a treaty.
- **Mobility**: a special visa pathway for up to **280 Tuvaluans a year** to live, work and study in Australia with access to services; the first ballot in 2025 drew applications from a large share of Tuvalu's 11,000 people.
- **Security**: Australia commits to respond to a natural disaster, health pandemic or military aggression on request; Tuvalu agrees to "mutually agree" with Australia any security or defence arrangement with a third country — the clause read as a veto on Chinese security ties.

## Use it for

- **inv.6.5** — an intergovernmental organisation (the Pacific Islands Forum) as the setting in which cooperation is negotiated, and a bilateral treaty as its product.
- **inv.6.2** — state sovereignty: a small state trading part of its freedom of action for survival guarantees; Australia using treaty law to extend its influence.

## Caution

A bilateral treaty, not a Forum instrument — the Forum provided the occasion, not the text. The statehood recognition binds only Australia; whether a state without territory remains a state under general international law is unsettled.
