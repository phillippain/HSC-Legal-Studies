Adopted in Rome on **17 July 1998** (not 1968, as one textbook prints it); the Court began on
**1 July 2002**, the day Australia's ratification took effect. In 2026 it has **125 states parties**;
the US, Russia, China, India and Israel are not among them.

## The four crimes

| Article | Crime | The element that matters |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Genocide | Intent to destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group, in whole or in part |
| 7 | Crimes against humanity | A widespread or systematic attack on a civilian population; no war needed |
| 8 | War crimes | Serious breaches of the laws of armed conflict (Geneva Conventions 1949) |
| 8 bis | Aggression | A leader's planning or launching of a manifest violation of the UN Charter (Kampala 2010; active 2018) |

The ICC cannot try aggression by nationals of non-member states, which is why the **Council of Europe
adopted a Special Tribunal for the Crime of Aggression against Ukraine on 15 May 2026**; Australia has
said it intends to join.

## How it works

- **Complementarity (art 17)** — the Court acts only when states are unwilling or unable.
- **Triggers (art 13)** — a state party referral, a UN Security Council referral, or the Prosecutor's own motion.
- **Jurisdiction (art 12)** — crimes on a member's territory or by a member's national.
- **Cooperation (arts 86–89)** — no police force; it depends on states to arrest and surrender.
- **Presence (art 63)** — no trials in absentia.
- **Withdrawal (art 127)** — effective one year after notice.

## Australia

Australia is **dualist**, so the Statute needed legislation. The *International Criminal Court Act 2002*
provides for arrest and surrender to the Court; the *International Criminal Court (Consequential
Amendments) Act 2002* placed the crimes in **Div 268 of the Criminal Code**, so Australia can prosecute
them itself. Prosecution needs the **Attorney-General's consent (s 268.121)**.

## Use it for

- **Define and categorise** (`crime.6.1`): the four crimes, in the Statute's own terms.
- **Domestic and international measures together** (`crime.6.2`): Div 268 is the domestic face of an
  international treaty, and complementarity is the reason Australia prosecutes its own soldiers.
- **Limitations**: every strength of the Statute depends on state consent, which is the evaluation in
  one sentence.
