**Art 11(1)** recognises "the right of everyone to an adequate standard of living … including
adequate food, clothing and housing, and to the continuous improvement of living conditions".

Obligations under the ICESCR are of **progressive realisation** to the maximum of available
resources, which is why it is so much weaker in practice than the ICCPR — and why it is such good
evidence for a question about the difference between having a right and being able to enforce one.

**CESCR General Comment No 4** sets out what "adequate" housing means: legal security of tenure;
availability of services; affordability; habitability; accessibility; location; and cultural
adequacy. That list is a ready-made set of criteria for evaluating Australian shelter law.

## Use it for

- **Right to shelter** and **obligation to provide shelter** — Australia has ratified the covenant
  but has not enacted a domestic right to housing.
- General Comment No 4 as an **externally supplied evaluation framework**, which is more persuasive
  than criteria a student invents.
