A **declaration** of the UN General Assembly, so not itself binding on states. Its content has since
passed into treaty law (the ICCPR and ICESCR, both 1966) and much of it is now argued to be
**customary international law**.

Art 25(1) includes housing within an adequate standard of living — the starting point for the right
to shelter.

## Use it for

- **Formal statements of human rights**, and the difference between a declaration, a covenant and
  customary law. A student who can explain why a non-binding instrument still matters is arguing at
  band 5–6.
- **State sovereignty**: the UDHR asserts universality against the principle that a state's treatment
  of its own citizens is its own business. That tension is the Human Rights core.
