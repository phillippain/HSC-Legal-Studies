The WHO is the UN's specialised agency for health — an intergovernmental organisation of 194 member states governed by the World Health Assembly. After COVID-19 exposed the failures of the existing *International Health Regulations* (late reporting, vaccine hoarding), members agreed in December 2021 to negotiate a binding instrument.

## The Agreement

- Commitments to prevention and surveillance ("One Health"), to resilient health systems, to a global supply chain and logistics network, and to technology transfer "as mutually agreed".
- A **Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing system** — countries share pathogen samples and sequences; manufacturers set aside a share of vaccines and treatments (the target discussed is 20%) for WHO distribution — whose detailed annex was left for a further negotiation, so the Agreement cannot open for signature until that is done.
- Explicit preservation of state sovereignty: nothing in the Agreement gives the WHO authority to direct national law, lockdowns or vaccination.
- Adopted on 20 May 2025 by 124 votes to none, with 11 abstentions; the United States, which had announced its withdrawal from the WHO in January 2025, took no part.

## Use it for

- **inv.6.5 / inv.6.4** — an IGO encouraging cooperation: what it can do (set a framework members agree to) and what it cannot (bind a state that stays out or compel compliance).
- **inv.6.1** — the formation of international law through instruments, including the gap between adoption, signature, ratification and entry into force.

## Caution

Not yet in force; Australia supported adoption but has not ratified anything. Claims that the Agreement would override national sovereignty were a feature of the campaign against it and are contradicted by its text.
