The Act creates the **Legal Aid Commission of New South Wales** (trading as Legal Aid NSW) as an
independent statutory body, funded jointly by the NSW and Commonwealth governments under the
**National Access to Justice Partnership** (2025–30), with a board that includes Law Society, Bar
and community representatives.

## How legal aid is rationed

- **Means test** — income and assets thresholds set by Commission policy; most applicants are on
  Centrelink payments.
- **Merit test** — the matter must have reasonable prospects and be a reasonable use of public
  funds.
- **Policy** — the Commission decides *which kinds of matters* it funds. Civil law aid (including
  housing) is the smallest of its three programs after crime and family, and much of it is **advice
  and minor assistance**, not representation at NCAT.
- **Duty and outreach** — the **Civil Law Service for Aboriginal Communities**, the **Homeless
  Persons' Legal Service** partnership, and a dedicated **Housing and Homelessness** team, with duty
  lawyers at some NCAT lists.

The community-legal side of tenancy help — the **Tenants' Advice and Advocacy Program** (TAAP) — is
funded not under this Act but from rental bond interest through NSW Fair Trading.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.7 (legal aid)** — the only statute on the dot point: what Legal Aid is, who decides who
  gets it, and the money behind it.
- **shel.2.5 / shel.2.6** — NCAT is designed for self-representation (leave is needed for lawyers),
  so legal aid in tenancy means advice beforehand more than advocacy on the day.
- **Evaluation** — Legal Aid's own report of a **19% rise** in housing demand and tightened
  eligibility (LSJ, 2025), and TAAP's 39% unmet need: accessibility measured in people turned away.
- **ls.1.5 / *Dietrich*** — there is no right to legal aid in civil matters; *Dietrich* applies only
  to serious criminal trials.

## Caution

Eligibility thresholds and funded matter types are **policy**, revised regularly — quote the
current Legal Aid NSW policy online, not the Act, for numbers. Legal Aid NSW's annual report is the
source for demand figures. The Commonwealth funding agreement changed in 2025; check its name and
amounts before citing.
