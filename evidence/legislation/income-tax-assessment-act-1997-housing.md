Tax law is Shelter law by effect rather than design. Nothing in the *Income Tax Assessment Act* is
about housing, but three of its provisions shape who buys, who rents and what a home costs.

| Setting | Provision | How it works | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Negative gearing** | s 8-1 (losses incurred in gaining assessable income are deductible) | An investor whose rent is less than interest and costs deducts the loss from *all* income, including wages — so the tax system subsidises loss-making property investment | ~$2–3 billion a year (Treasury) |
| **CGT discount** | Division 115 | Since 1999, individuals pay tax on only **50%** of a capital gain on an asset held over 12 months; before 1999 the gain was indexed for inflation instead | ~$20 billion a year across all assets |
| **Main-residence exemption** | Subdivision 118-B | The family home is **exempt** from CGT altogether, and from the Age Pension assets test | ~$50 billion a year — the largest tax expenditure in the Budget |

Together they make housing the most tax-favoured asset in Australia, which is why investors
compete with first-home buyers and why the ABC's "home owners' welfare state" record calls the
system a driver of inequality.

## The 2026 Budget changes (from 1 July 2027)

- Losses on **established** homes bought after budget night can be offset only against property
  income (new builds keep full deductibility); existing holdings grandfathered.
- The 50% discount replaced by **inflation indexation** with a minimum 30% rate on gains made after
  July 2027.
- The main-residence exemption is **untouched**.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — the demand-side cause and the 2026 legal response in one place;
  Treasury's 75,000 / −35,000 modelling is the evaluation evidence.
- **shel.1.3** — Siminski and Wilkins' point: the state's largest housing subsidy goes to those
  who already own, and the obligation to provide shelter is met for owners through the tax system
  and for renters through a waiting list.
- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.3** — the longest-running reform argument in Australian housing (Hawke
  1985–87, Labor 2016 and 2019, 2026): conditions for reform, and the Senate.
- **lip.2.1** — investors, owners, renters, the Treasury: competing interests in a single Act.

## Caution

The 2026 measures must be **enacted by amending legislation** — check aph.gov.au that the bill has
passed and in what form before saying they are law. Tax-expenditure figures are Treasury's annual
*Tax Expenditures and Insights Statement*; quote the latest. Section numbers are for the current
Act and may change with the amendments.
