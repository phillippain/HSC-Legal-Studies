On **20 October 2026** the Attorney General, Police Minister and Industrial Relations Minister
announced legislation for **Workplace Protection Orders (WPOs)**: court orders against a person who
stalks, intimidates or commits a personal violence offence at a workplace, where there are
reasonable grounds to fear it will continue. Retail first; other public-facing workplaces by
regulation; commencement within 12 months of passage. The model came from a March 2026 consultation
and a September announcement, and follows SA (bill Oct 2025), the Commonwealth (commenced 5 May
2026) and Victoria (assent 1 Sept 2026).

## APVO today v the proposed WPO

| | APVO — *Crimes (DPV) Act 2007* | Proposed WPO |
|---|---|---|
| Who applies | The person needing protection, a guardian, or police (s 48) | The **business** (PCBU under the WHS Act), or a business group, **union** or health and safety representative with authorisation |
| Grounds | Reasonable fear of violence, intimidation or stalking (s 19) | Stalking, intimidation or personal violence at work; reasonable fear it will continue |
| Children | Only police may apply where every subject is a child (s 48(3)) | Applies from **age 14**; no safeguards described |
| Conditions | Prohibitions necessary for safety (ss 35–36) | Bans on entering the workplace, contacting staff, possessing weapons |
| Breach | Offence: up to **2 years and/or $5,500** (s 14) | **Same maximum** |
| Scope | Any person, any place | Retail workplaces first |

The release gives **no bill name, court, standard of proof, interim-order rule or maximum duration**
(Victoria and SA cap orders at 12 months). The government's own figures: **673** people charged with
**884** offences under the 2023 retail-worker assault laws (*Crimes Act* s 60G, maximums 4, 6 and 11
years), and **2,073** charged under Operation Percentile since October 2025 — neither of which
measures WPOs.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.9 (crime prevention)** — a *situational* measure (exclusion) wrapped in a *social*
  justification (the SDA's claim that 10% of offenders cause 60% of problems — private Auror data,
  unverified).
- **crime.4.6 / crime.t.balance** — a **civil** order enforced by a **criminal** penalty: community
  protection for workers against the rights of a person who has not been convicted of anything.
- **crime.5.4** — orders reaching **14-year-olds** with no stated capacity safeguard, where Victoria
  requires courts to consider a child's ability to comply.
- **ls.4.1–4.3 (law reform)** — the conditions (retail violence, union and retailer lobbying), the
  agencies (SDA, Australian Retail Council, Woolworths and Coles), the mechanism (Parliament copying
  four other jurisdictions), and the gap in the evidence base.
- **Critics' line** — Victorian community legal centres warned the orders would fall hardest on
  homeless people, people in mental-health crisis and Aboriginal people, and that APVOs already exist
  for workers. No NSW civil-liberties, Law Society or ALS response had been found.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **government announcement, not the law**. The bill was not located, so
the court, standard of proof, safeguards and duration are **unverified**; the release quotes only
supporters, misnames the 2023 Act (*Assaults on Retail Workers*), and its charge figures are its own.
Check the bill, explanatory note and second reading speech, and whether it passes or is amended,
before relying on any detail.
