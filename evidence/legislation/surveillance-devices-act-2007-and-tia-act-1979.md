Two Acts draw the line between lawful and unlawful surveillance — one for devices in NSW, one for
the telecommunications network, which is Commonwealth. Both work the same way: a general
**prohibition** on individuals, with **warrants** for law enforcement.

## Surveillance Devices Act 2007 (NSW)

| Device | Offence (Part 2) | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| **Listening device** (s 7) | Recording a private conversation you are not party to, or are party to without the others' consent | Consent of all principal parties; a party recording to protect their lawful interests; police under warrant |
| **Optical surveillance** (s 8) | Filming a private activity in premises or a vehicle where entry or interference with the property was needed | Consent; warrant; a person's own premises |
| **Tracking device** (s 9) | Attaching a GPS tracker to a person or vehicle without consent — the *Guardian* 2026 GPS-tracking record | Consent; warrant; vehicle owner's own car |
| **Data surveillance** (s 10) | Installing software to monitor a computer without consent (law enforcement only) | Warrant |
| **Communicating** (s 11) | Publishing a record obtained by a device, even if lawfully made | Court proceedings; lawful-interest protection |

Penalties: 5 years, 100 penalty units. **Part 4** sets the warrant regime: an eligible judge or
Magistrate may issue a surveillance device warrant to police, ICAC, LECC or the Crime Commission
for a "relevant offence" (3+ years), with reporting to the Attorney-General and the Ombudsman
inspecting records. The 2026 covert-stalking reforms added a new offence of covert tracking in
domestic relationships (see that record).

**Workplace Surveillance Act 2005** — employers must give 14 days' notice of camera, computer or
tracking surveillance; covert surveillance only by Magistrate's authority.

## Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979 (Cth)

- **Interception** of a communication passing over the network (phone call, SMS, email in transit)
  is an offence except under a warrant from an eligible judge or ART member, available for "serious
  offences" (7+ years) to the AFP, state police, ASIO and the anti-corruption bodies.
- **Stored communications** (messages already delivered) need a stored-communications warrant.
- **Metadata retention** (2015): carriers must keep subscriber, time, location and device data —
  not content — for **two years**; access by authorisation without a warrant (journalists' data
  needs a warrant). The 2020 PJCIS review recommended tightening; partially implemented.
- **ANOM** (*CD v Commonwealth*, 2025) — the High Court on whether the AFP's encrypted-phone
  operation was interception at all.

## Use it for

- **ind.3.2** — the legal boundary of private surveillance, and the GPS-tracking and camera-glasses
  records as the breaches.
- **crime.2.2 (investigation)** — the warrant regime as the legal basis for covert policing, and

- **ind.1.2 / ind.t.balance** — privacy v security, with the Ombudsman and PJCIS as the oversight.
- **crime.3.5 (evidence)** — unlawfully obtained recordings and the discretion to exclude

## Caution

Section numbers for the NSW offences are stable; the warrant provisions were amended in 2026 — check
before quoting. Metadata-access numbers are in the Home Affairs annual TIA report. The Commonwealth
Act is under long-promised rewrite (the "electronic surveillance" reform) — note it as proposed,
not done.
