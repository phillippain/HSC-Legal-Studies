Before 2005 each NSW court had its own rules; the *Civil Procedure Act* and the **Uniform Civil
Procedure Rules** gave the Local, District and Supreme Courts one procedure. Its design principle is
in **s 56**: the courts must facilitate "the just, quick and cheap resolution of the real issues in
the proceedings", and the parties must help them do it.

## The civil process it governs

| Stage | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Jurisdiction** | Local Court up to $100,000 (Small Claims Division under $20,000: no lawyers' costs, informal); District Court to $1.25 million (unlimited for personal injury from motor accidents and work); Supreme Court unlimited |
| **Starting** | Statement of claim or summons; defence within 28 days; default judgment if none |
| **Case management** (ss 56–62) | Courts set timetables and may dismiss, strike out or order costs against a party who delays; the "overriding purpose" overrides tactical litigation |
| **Pre-trial** | Discovery of documents, interrogatories, expert evidence (single joint experts encouraged), **offers of compromise** with costs consequences |
| **ADR (Part 4)** | **s 26** — the court may refer proceedings to **mediation** "with or without the consent of the parties"; **s 38** — referral to arbitration for money claims; mediation is privileged (s 30) |
| **Trial** | Adversarial, before a judge (civil juries are rare in NSW — defamation only, and abolished there in 2021) |
| **Costs** | Follow the event: the loser usually pays the winner's costs on the "ordinary" basis — the main barrier to access |
| **Enforcement (Part 6)** | Writ for levy of property, garnishee orders on wages and bank accounts, charging orders, examination of the judgment debtor, bankruptcy as the last step |

## Use it for

- **dis.2.5 (tribunals and courts)** — what a court does with a dispute between individuals, from
  filing to enforcement, and why NCAT exists beside it (no costs rule, no lawyers by default).
- **dis.2.5 (ADR)** — court-annexed mediation under s 26 is now the main public mediation route
  since Community Justice Centres closed in 2025; it is *compulsory* in a way CJC mediation never
  was.
- **dis.1.5 / dis.2.5 (civil procedure and personnel)** — the stages and the roles: solicitor, barrister,
  registrar, judge, sheriff.
- **inv.1.4 / inv.7.4** — the costs rule as the reason "access to justice" is a civil-law problem:
  a valid claim may be too expensive to run.

## Caution

Monetary limits were last raised in 2022 (District Court) and should be checked. The Act does not
apply to NCAT (which has its own Act) or to the Land and Environment Court's own rules. Enforcement
is only as good as the debtor's assets — a judgment is not money.
