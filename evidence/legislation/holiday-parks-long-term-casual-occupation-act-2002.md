NSW divides park living into two laws. People who **live** in a caravan park or manufactured home
estate are covered by the *Residential (Land Lease) Communities Act 2013*. People who **keep** a van,
annexe or cabin on a site but use it for holidays — "long-term casual occupants" — are covered by
this Act, provided their agreement is for more than 50 days a year, and they are not permitted to
occupy for more than **180 days** in any twelve months. Tens of thousands of families on the NSW
coast hold sites this way, often for decades.

## What the Act does

- **Occupation agreement** — must be written, in the standard form, for a fixed term (up to five
  years) or ongoing; the park must give a disclosure statement.
- **Fees** — increases only once a year with 60 days' written notice; no "key money".
- **Termination** — by the occupant on notice; by the park only on prescribed grounds (non-payment,
  breach, closure or change of use of the park) with set notice periods, and with NCAT able to order
  compensation where the park's change of use ends the agreement.
- **Property on the site** — rules about removal and sale of a van or structure left behind.
- **Disputes** — NCAT's Consumer and Commercial Division.

The Act was passed after a wave of park closures and redevelopments on the North Coast in the late
1990s, when casual occupants found they had no rights at all.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (caravan parks)** — the second of the two park statutes; students who cite only
  the land lease Act miss that the law distinguishes *living* from *staying*.
- **shel.1.4** — a right to occupy that is not a residence: the law defines shelter by how it is
  used, not by the structure.
- **shel.3.3** — the 180-day limit matters when people in housing stress try to live in a holiday
  van; exceeding it is a breach, not a tenancy.
- **Evaluation** — a small, specific Act that closed a specific gap; effectiveness is in the
  absence of the 1990s complaints, not in case law.

## Caution

A niche statute with little commentary; the facts above are from the Act's structure and Fair
Trading's guidance, so check day limits and notice periods against the current text before quoting
them. Not to be confused with holiday *letting* (short-term rental accommodation), which is a
planning and *Fair Trading Act* matter.
