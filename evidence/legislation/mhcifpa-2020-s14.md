A Local Court magistrate may **dismiss a charge** against a defendant with a mental health or
cognitive impairment where it is "more appropriate to deal with the person under this section than
according to law". The dismissal is conditional and the court can recall the person within 12
months. It replaced s 32 of the *Mental Health (Forensic Provisions) Act 1990*.

About **1 in 17** adult defendants in court, and more than **1 in 10** adults in custody, have a
cognitive impairment.

## Why it is the best diversion example in the course

The legal right already existed and was **underused**. What changed the outcomes was a non-legal
service — the Justice Advocacy Service, run by the Intellectual Disability Rights Service, an NGO —
coordinating the application. See the BOCSAR CJB 279 evaluation: s 14 dismissals rose **86%**
(+22.4 percentage points) and reoffending fell **16%**.

Use it whenever a question asks whether **legal or non-legal measures** are more effective: here
the answer is that the legal measure only worked once a non-legal one reached it.
