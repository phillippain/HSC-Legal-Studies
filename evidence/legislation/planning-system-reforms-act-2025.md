The **Environmental Planning and Assessment (Planning System Reforms) Bill 2025** was introduced in
the NSW Parliament on **17 September 2025** and **passed both houses by mid-November 2025**. It amends
the *Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979* and the EP&A Regulation 2021, with consequential
amendments to the *Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016* and the *Protection of the Environment
Operations Act 1997*. The government described it as the biggest overhaul of the NSW planning system
in decades.

## What it changes

- **The objects of the Act are rewritten.** The primary object becomes promoting land uses that
  improve the **social and economic welfare** of communities, with a secondary object directed to
  **housing supply, including affordable housing**. For a 1979 Act whose objects were built around
  environmental protection and orderly development, this is the most significant change in the Bill.
- **Development Coordination Authority (DCA)** — a new Division 2.3B creating a "single front door".
  The DCA (the **Planning Secretary**) centralises referrals for integrated development and decides
  whether general terms of approval are issued, in place of several agencies each considering
  different aspects of one application, and may use other public authorities' staff and facilities.
- **Housing Delivery Authority** — created by ministerial order in December 2024 and now written into
  the Act under a new Division 2.3A. It assesses major housing proposals and recommends whether they
  should be declared **state significant development**.
- **Complying development** — variations to complying development standards are permitted, and an
  application to vary a standard is **deemed approved if not determined within ten days**.
- **Targeted assessment development** — a new Division 4.3A letting State environmental planning
  policies classify development as "targeted assessment development", bypassing full assessment and
  **prevailing over Part 4** of the Act. It is designed to sit between full assessment and complying
  development where strategic planning and community consultation have already happened.
- **Duplication removed** — layers of approval including **regional planning panels** and outdated
  assessment pathways go, and **more than 100 consultation plans** are replaced with a **single
  statewide Community Participation Plan**.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 — the law that decides what gets built.** Affordability answers that stay on taxes and
  subsidies miss the obvious lever: the planning system decides whether a dwelling can exist at all.
  This is the most current and most substantial NSW reform of that system.
- **Law reform, with everything a question needs (chg.1.3 / shel.t.reform)** — a dated announcement
  (17 September 2025), an identifiable pressure (housing supply and assessment delay), a named
  mechanism (the EP&A Act), and passage within two months. Use it as the worked example of how
  pressure for change becomes legislation.
- **Changing the objects of an Act is the sharpest point here (inv.1.1)** — the same statute, the same
  decision-makers, and a different purpose clause, which changes how every discretion under it is
  exercised. A rare and very teachable illustration that objects provisions do real work.
- **Speed against participation (shel.t.values)** — a **ten-day deemed approval**, a bypass of full
  assessment, panels abolished and 100-plus consultation plans collapsed into one. Argue it both ways:
  faster housing delivery, or less scrutiny and fewer chances for a community to be heard. This is
  the central evaluation and it has a real answer on each side.
- **Centralisation (dis.3.1 / dis.3.4)** — approvals move toward the **Planning Secretary** and a
  state authority, and away from councils and regional panels. Good for administrative law, for
  accountability, and for who can challenge a decision once it is made.
- **Housing versus environment (shel.2.6)** — consequential amendments to the *Biodiversity
  Conservation Act* and the *POEO Act*, and an objects clause reordered toward welfare and supply.
  A genuine competing-interests paragraph.
- **Pair it with the delay evidence** (`smh-2025-missing-middle-delay`, `grattan-more-homes-better-
  cities-2025`) for the problem, and with the investor-exit record for what happens on the demand
  side at the same time.

## Caution

The library document is a **teaching evidence summary** written soon after the **17 September 2025
announcement**, so it describes the Bill as announced rather than as enacted, and its framing ("the
biggest overhaul of NSW planning laws in 50 years") is the government's. The detail in this record was
checked against the Department of Planning's own statement that the Bill **passed Parliament** and
against practitioner commentary on the Bill as introduced; **the Act as passed may differ from the
Bill**, so check the consolidated *Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979* on the NSW
legislation site before quoting any provision, division number or timeframe. **Commencement is
staged and was not stated** in the sources used — do not assert that any particular change is in
force. There is **no evaluation yet**: the reforms were months old at the time of writing, so nothing
is known about whether approval times or housing completions actually improved, and any claim that
they have (or have not) worked needs current data. Environmental and local government groups have
opposed parts of the package; this record sets out the mechanisms, not the debate, so find a critic
before writing an evaluation.
