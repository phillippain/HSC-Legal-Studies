The **Australian Human Rights Commission** (the Human Rights and Equal Opportunity Commission until
2008) is Australia's national human rights institution, an independent statutory body with a
President and seven Commissioners (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Social Justice, Age,
Children's, Disability, Human Rights, Race, Sex). The Act rests on the external affairs power and
the ICCPR, CROC, the Declaration on the Rights of Disabled Persons and other scheduled instruments.

## What the Commission can do

| Function | Provision | Enforceability |
|---|---|---|
| **Conciliate unlawful discrimination complaints** under the RDA, SDA, DDA and ADA | Part IIB (ss 46P–46PH) | Compulsory first step; if conciliation fails or is terminated, the complainant may apply to the Federal Circuit and Family Court or Federal Court within 60 days — the only route to enforceable orders |
| **Inquire into acts of the Commonwealth** that may breach human rights under the scheduled instruments (ICCPR, CROC, etc.) | s 11(1)(f), s 20 | Report to the Attorney-General, tabled in Parliament; **no binding orders** — e.g. findings on immigration detention |
| **National inquiries and reports** | s 11(1)(e), (j) | *Bringing Them Home* (1997), *Same-Sex: Same Entitlements* (2007), *Balancing Act* (2026, on pregnancy and parenting discrimination); recommendations only |
| **Education and promotion** | s 11(1)(g)–(h) | Schools resources, Human Rights Day, guidelines |
| **Intervene in court cases** with leave | s 11(1)(o) | Amicus role on human rights questions |
| **Examine legislation** for consistency with human rights | s 11(1)(e) | Advisory; separate from the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Human Rights (*Human Rights (Parliamentary Scrutiny) Act 2011*) |

## Use it for

- **hr.2.9 (statute law)** — the central Commonwealth human rights statute, and the reason students
  can say Australia protects rights "by statute" rather than a charter.
- **hr.2.4 / hr.2.11 (independent statutory authorities)** — what an NHRI is and the gap between
  *investigate and report* and *enforce*; the YiSSA and *Balancing Act* records are current examples.
- **dis.3.5 / dis.3.6** — alongside the Ombudsman and ICAC, a body individuals can complain to about the
  state without going to court.
- **hr.2.14** — the Commission's own 2023 proposal for a federal Human Rights Act; the Act's limits
  are the argument for one.
- **inv.7.4 (difficulties enforcing rights)** — conciliation is confidential and voluntary; court
  is costly and risks an adverse costs order.

## Caution

The Commission **cannot award compensation or make orders**; it settles, reports and recommends.
Complaint and conciliation statistics are in its annual report. Commissioner titles and the
Commission's budget have changed with governments — check the current line-up. The Act does not
create the unlawful-discrimination grounds; those are in the four discrimination Acts.
