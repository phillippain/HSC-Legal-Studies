A royal commission is an inquiry appointed by the Governor or Governor-General, usually headed by a
judge, into a matter the government names in its **letters patent**. It has no power to decide
anything; its power is to **find out** and to **recommend**. The two Acts supply the teeth.

## What the Acts provide

| Power | NSW 1923 Act / Cth 1902 Act |
|---|---|
| **Summons** | Compel any person to attend, give evidence on oath and produce documents; failure is an offence; arrest warrants for non-attendance |
| **Privilege** | Privilege against self-incrimination and legal professional privilege can be overridden (with use-immunity in later prosecutions); Cabinet documents remain contested |
| **Hearings** | Public unless the commission orders otherwise; private sessions for vulnerable witnesses (added for the child abuse commission, 2013) |
| **Protection** | Witnesses protected from victimisation; commissioners have judicial immunity |
| **Search** | Warrants under the Cth Act; NSW commissions use police |
| **Contempt** | Offences for refusing to answer, giving false evidence or obstructing |

NSW also has the lighter **Special Commission of Inquiry** (*1983 Act*), used for the 2019–20
inquiry into the Ruby Princess and the 2022–23 Lindt café-related and LGBTIQ hate-crime inquiries.

## Recent commissions and what followed

| Commission | Years | Legal response |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse (Cth) | 2013–17 | National Redress Scheme (2018); NSW *Crimes Act* s 316A (concealment), reportable-conduct scheme, abolition of limitation periods for civil claims |
| Aged Care Quality and Safety (Cth) | 2018–21 | *Aged Care Act 2024* |
| Special Commission into the Drug "Ice" (NSW) | 2018–20 | 109 recommendations; the Government's 2022 response and the 2024 Drug Summit |
| Violence, Abuse, Neglect and Exploitation of People with Disability (Cth) | 2019–23 | 222 recommendations; group homes and segregated education still being argued |
| Robodebt Scheme (Cth) | 2022–23 | Referrals to the NACC and APSC; $1.8 billion settlement had preceded it |
| Defence and Veteran Suicide (Cth) | 2021–24 | 122 recommendations; a new statutory oversight body |

## Use it for

- **dis.3.5 / dis.3.6** — the third "statutory body" with the Ombudsman and ICAC: temporary, powerful, and
  toothless after it reports.
- **chg.1 / chg.2 (law reform)** — a royal commission as the agency that turns a scandal into a
  reform program; the Ice inquiry shows a government declining most of it.
- **dis.3.2 / hr.2.12** — victims, advocates and NGOs using a commission to be heard when courts
  and complaints have failed.
- **Evaluation** — count recommendations accepted, implemented and funded; most commissions are
  judged on the gap between the three.

## Caution

A royal commission is appointed **by the executive** — its terms of reference, budget and
commissioner are the government's choice, which is the standard criticism. "Recommendations
accepted" is not "implemented". Dates and recommendation counts are from the commissions' final
reports; check the government response documents for what was actually done.
