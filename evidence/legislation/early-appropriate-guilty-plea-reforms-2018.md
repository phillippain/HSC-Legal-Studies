Most indictable matters in NSW end in a guilty plea, but before 2018 a large share were entered
**late** — on the day of trial, after the victim and witnesses had prepared and a jury had been
summoned. The NSW Law Reform Commission's *Encouraging Appropriate Early Guilty Pleas* (Report 141,
2014) proposed a structured early-plea scheme; the 2017 Act enacted it.

## The scheme (Criminal Procedure Act Ch 3 Pt 2)

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Brief of evidence** | Police serve a mandatory brief on the DPP and the defence |
| **Charge certificate** (s 66) | The **DPP** (not police) reviews the evidence and certifies which charges will proceed — the point at which overcharging is meant to be corrected |
| **Case conference** (s 70) | Prosecutor and defence lawyer must confer (the accused must be legally represented, or the step is waived); a **case conference certificate** records any offers and the plea; this is where **charge negotiation** formally happens |
| **Committal for trial or sentence** | The Magistrate commits on the papers; the old committal hearing with cross-examination of witnesses is abolished except by leave for substantial reasons |
| **Discounts** (*Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Act* ss 25D–25E) | A fixed **25%** discount for a plea entered in the Local Court before committal; **10%** for a plea at least 14 days before the first trial day; **5%** thereafter — replacing the open-ended *Thomson and Houlton* discretion for indictable matters |

Local Court summary matters keep the discretionary discount (up to 25%, *Thomson and Houlton*,
2000).

## What it was for, and whether it worked

- **Aims**: earlier pleas, fewer late collapses, less trauma for victims and witnesses, savings for
  Legal Aid and the courts.
- **BOCSAR (2021–23)** found the proportion of pleas entered in the Local Court rose and late pleas
  fell, but District Court trial delays were not cut as hoped, partly because fewer pleas were
  entered overall in some offence types (sexual assault in particular).
- **Criticisms**: the scheme pressures the accused to plead before seeing all the evidence; the
  25/10/5 steps reward timing over remorse; the loss of committal cross-examination removed a
  check on weak cases.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.4 (pleas and charge negotiation)** — the only NSW statute that structures charge
  negotiation; the case conference is the exam answer to "how does charge negotiation work".
- **crime.3.2 (committal)** — what replaced the committal hearing.
- **crime.4.1 / crime.4.2** — sentencing discounts as a statutory factor; the ODPP Prosecution
  Guidelines on when a negotiated plea is acceptable.
- **exp.6** — a reform with a BOCSAR evaluation: resource efficiency v the rights of the accused
  and the interests of victims.

## Caution

Discount percentages are fixed for indictable matters only. BOCSAR's findings are mixed — cite the
specific report rather than saying the scheme "worked" or "failed". The ODPP's own statistics on
charge certification are in its annual report.
