Marriage is a Commonwealth matter under **s 51(xxi)** of the Constitution, so one Act applies
across Australia. Before 1961 each state had its own marriage law; the *Marriage Act* unified them
and, since 2017, has defined marriage without reference to sex.

## The requirements

| Requirement | Provision | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | s 5(1) | "the union of **2 people** to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life" (from 9 December 2017; "a man and a woman" from 2004 to 2017) |
| **Marriageable age** | ss 11–12 | 18; a court may authorise a 16- or 17-year-old to marry a person over 18 in "exceptional and unusual" circumstances — never two minors |
| **Not already married** | s 23B(1)(a) | A bigamous marriage is void; bigamy is an offence (s 94) |
| **Prohibited relationships** | s 23B(2) | Ancestor–descendant and siblings (including half and adopted) |
| **Real consent** | s 23B(1)(d) | Void if consent was obtained by **duress or fraud**, by mistake as to identity or the nature of the ceremony, or if a party was mentally incapable |
| **Notice and celebrant** | ss 41–48 | Notice of intended marriage at least **one month** (and no more than 18 months) before; solemnised by an **authorised celebrant** (minister of a recognised denomination, state registry, or Commonwealth-registered celebrant) before two adult witnesses; the words "I call upon the persons here present…" |
| **Overseas marriages** | Part VA | Recognised if valid where made and not contrary to Part VA (age, consent, prohibited relationship) |

A marriage that fails a requirement is **void** — never valid — and either party may seek a **decree
of nullity** under the *Family Law Act* s 51; it is not a divorce.

## Related criminal law

**Forced marriage** is a Commonwealth offence (*Criminal Code* ss 270.7A–270.7B, since 2013) —
coercing a person into marriage, including a person under 16 regardless of consent. The *Good
Weekend* forced-marriage record shows how it operates.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.3** — the statutory list itself; students are expected to name the requirements and the
  consequence of breach (void, nullity).
- **fam.3.1** — the 2017 amendment, after the postal survey (61.6% yes), as the legal recognition of
  same-sex relationships; *Commonwealth v ACT* (2013) is the High Court case that made federal
  legislation the only route.
- **fam.1.2 / ls.2.6** — marriage as a head of Commonwealth power, and why the states could not act.
- **fam.t.values** — a definition changed by Parliament to track social attitudes, 43 years apart
  (1961, 2004, 2017).

## Caution

Section numbers for the consent and prohibited-relationship rules are in s 23B for marriages after
1986; older marriages fall under s 23. The 2017 Act also added religious-freedom exemptions for
ministers and religious celebrants (ss 47, 47A) — relevant to debates about discrimination but not to
validity. Forced-marriage statistics come from the AFP, not this Act.
