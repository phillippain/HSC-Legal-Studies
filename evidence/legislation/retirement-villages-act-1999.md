A **retirement village** is a complex for people over 55 (or retired) who pay an ingoing
contribution for the right to occupy a unit under a **village contract** — which may be a lease,
licence, strata lot or company share — and then recurrent charges, and on leaving pay a **departure
fee** (often 25–35% of the price) while waiting for an exit entitlement. The Act, administered by
NSW Fair Trading, regulates the whole life of that contract.

## Key provisions

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Disclosure** | A general inquiry document and a **disclosure statement** at least 14 days before the contract; a standard-form contract since 2013; a **settling-in period** of 90 days during which a resident can leave with limited fees |
| **Fees and charges** | Recurrent charges can rise only by a fixed formula or by resident consent/NCAT order; **budgets** must be approved by residents annually; residents' committees have statutory standing |
| **Exit entitlements** (2020 reforms) | Operators must pay the former resident's exit entitlement within **6 months** (Sydney, Newcastle, Wollongong) or **12 months** (elsewhere) of the unit being vacated, whether or not it has been resold; **aged-care rule** lets a resident moving to aged care draw on the entitlement to pay the accommodation deposit |
| **Asset management** | 10-year plans for capital works; operators responsible for capital items, residents for maintenance |
| **Disputes** | Internal process, Fair Trading mediation, then **NCAT**; the **Retirement Village Ambassador** (since 2018) is a non-statutory advocate |
| **Termination** | Limited grounds; NCAT order required to terminate a residence right |

The reforms of 2017–21 followed the **Greiner inquiry** (2017), after *Four Corners* and Fairfax
exposed exit-fee practices in one major operator's villages.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 / shel.3.2 (older people)** — a tenure that exists only for the old, and whose
  reform history shows how vulnerable residents and complex contracts combine.
- **shel.1.4** — "ownership" in a village may be a lease or licence: what the resident has bought is
  a right, not a title.
- **shel.2.10 / chg.2** — media investigation → inquiry → amendment Act in four years: a clean
  reform chain.
- **Evaluation** — the 6/12-month buy-back is enforceable and specific; compare the Commonwealth's
  rights-based but harder-to-enforce *Aged Care Act 2024*.

## Caution

Retirement villages are **not aged care**: no care is provided under this Act, and residents who
need care move to a facility under the Commonwealth Act. Fee percentages vary by contract; the 25–35%
figure is typical, not statutory. Check the current Regulation for the buy-back periods and the
settling-in rules.
