Family law disputes in Australia are heard by a federal court under the *Family Law Act 1975*
(except in Western Australia). From 1976 that was the **Family Court of Australia**; from 2000 most
simpler matters went to the **Federal Magistrates Court** (later the Federal Circuit Court), so two
courts with different rules handled the same kind of case. After the 2019 *Australian Law Reform
Commission* report and a 2018 PwC review, the Morrison Government merged them.

## The structure since 1 September 2021

| | Division 1 | Division 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Former court** | Family Court of Australia | Federal Circuit Court |
| **Work** | Complex family law matters and **appeals** from Division 2 | The single **entry point** for all family law filings; also migration and general federal law |
| **Judges** | About 35 | About 75 (family law) |
| **Appeals** | To a Full Court of Division 1 (three or, for less complex appeals, one judge); then High Court by special leave | |

- **One set of rules** (the *Federal Circuit and Family Court (Family Law) Rules 2021*) and a
  **national triage** system: every case is assessed on filing and streamed; a **Lighthouse**
  risk-screening model for family violence; **Evatt List** for the highest-risk parenting cases.
- **Target**: 90% of matters resolved within 12 months; median time to trial fell from about
  24 months to under 12 in the court's first years.
- **Registrars** do much of the early work (interim orders, dispute resolution conferences), freeing
  judges.

## Dispute resolution before and inside the court

- ***Family Law Act* s 60I** — before filing a parenting application a party must attend
  **family dispute resolution** and obtain a s 60I certificate, unless exempt (family violence,
  urgency, child abuse). Provided by Family Relationship Centres and accredited FDR practitioners.
- **Conciliation conferences** and **mediation** for property; **arbitration** available for
  property (s 13E); the 2023 amendments added **Independent Children's Lawyer** duties to meet the
  child.
- **State courts** — the Local Court can make interim orders and hears ADVOs; the Children's Court
  hears care matters: the state/federal split in fam.1.2.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.5** — the courts and the dispute-resolution ladder (FDR → registrar conference → trial),
  with the merger as the reform and the 12-month target as the measure.
- **fam.1.2** — federal jurisdiction over marriage, divorce and children; state jurisdiction over
  domestic violence, care and protection and adoption; the 1986–90 referrals of power over
  ex-nuptial children and the 2009 referral over de facto property.
- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.3** — a reform driven by delay and cost, opposed by the Law Council and family
  violence groups who feared the loss of a specialist court: both sides in one case study.
- **Evaluation** — the court's annual reports give filing, clearance and time-to-trial figures.

## Caution

Judge numbers and timeframes change; use the current annual report. Western Australia keeps its own
Family Court. The merger passed the Senate by one vote in February 2021 against strong professional
opposition — present it as contested, not as consensus.
