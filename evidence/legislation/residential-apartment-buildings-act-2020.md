Passed in the wake of **Opal Tower** and **Mascot Towers**, the Act gives **Building Commission NSW**
powers directed at the building itself rather than at a defendant:

- **Prohibition orders** — preventing an occupation certificate from issuing, and preventing
  registration of a strata plan, which stops the apartments being sold;
- **Building work rectification orders** — requiring a developer to fix **serious defects**, or to
  stop work;
- **Investigation and audit powers** over developments before completion.

## Use it for

- **Resolution by prevention** — the clearest example in the option. Everything else in shelter law
  gives a person a claim after the harm; this stops the harm reaching a purchaser at all.
- **Enforceability**, contrasted with litigation: *Brookfield* took years and produced no duty;
  *Pafburn* took years and produced one. An order from a regulator takes weeks, costs the owner
  nothing, and needs no plaintiff.
- **Who the law protects**: the power exists to protect **future purchasers** — people who are not
  yet parties to anything. Ordinary private law cannot easily do that, which is the argument for
  regulation over rights of action in this field.
