## The Act as it stands

Entities with annual consolidated revenue over **$100 million** must publish an annual **modern
slavery statement** against seven mandatory criteria. There is **no substantive penalty** for a
deficient statement. The Act works by **transparency and "naming and shaming"**, and the Australian
Anti-Slavery Commissioner has raised **widespread non-compliance**.

## The 2026 proposal

| | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Duty | Transparency — publish a statement | **Positive duty to prevent** modern slavery in supply chains |
| Liability | None | **Corporate criminal liability**, plus civil penalties for reporting breaches |
| Enforcement | Naming and shaming; a register | Prosecution; possibly deferred prosecution agreements |
| Defence | N/A | Complete defence where the company took **"reasonable steps"** |
| Victims | Not provided for | Remedies to be considered |

The model already exists in Australian law: the offence of **failing to prevent foreign bribery** by
an associate imposes **absolute liability**, with an "adequate procedures" defence. A company can be
convicted **without knowing** about the conduct, and **even if the associate was never convicted**.
Maximum penalties there give the scale — the greatest of 100,000 penalty units (about **$36.4 million**
at 1 July 2026), three times the benefit, or **10% of annual turnover**.

## Use it for

- **Where criminal law meets human rights**, and the doctrinal point that makes it examinable:
  liability attaches to the **corporate structure**, the prosecution need not prove **mens rea**, and
  the **onus shifts to the accused** to establish the defence. That is a departure from the ordinary
  criminal standard, argued as necessary because the ordinary standard cannot reach a supply chain.
- **Enforceability** as the whole issue: eight years of statements, no penalties, documented
  non-compliance. It is the strongest available example of a law that recognises a right and does not
  enforce it.
- **Law reform in progress** — announced July 2026, **no legislation drafted**, penalties and the
  scope of the defence undecided. Say so; a proposal described as law is an error markers notice.
