Australia has no constitutional or common-law right to privacy (*ABC v Lenah Game Meats*, 2001,
left the door open; no court walked through it). Privacy is a **statutory** right, and the *Privacy
Act* is the statute — written for government databases in 1988, extended to business in 2000 and
now the main law for the data economy.

## The Act

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who it binds** | Commonwealth agencies; private organisations with turnover over **$3 million**; all health providers; credit reporting bodies; and, regardless of size, businesses that trade in personal information. **Exempt**: small businesses, employee records, political parties, the media (journalism), state governments |
| **Australian Privacy Principles** (Sch 1) | 13 APPs: open and transparent management (1), anonymity (2), collection only when reasonably necessary (3), notification (5), use and disclosure for the purpose collected (6), direct marketing (7), cross-border disclosure (8), **security** and destruction (11), **access** (12) and correction (13); "sensitive information" (health, biometrics, sexuality, ethnicity) needs consent |
| **Notifiable Data Breaches** (Part IIIC, 2018) | Eligible breaches must be reported to the OAIC and affected individuals — Optus and Medibank (2022) were the breaches that drove reform |
| **Regulator** | The **Office of the Australian Information Commissioner**: complaints, conciliation, investigations, determinations (compensation), enforceable undertakings; civil penalties up to the greater of **$50 million**, three times the benefit, or 30% of turnover (since December 2022) |

## The 2024 reforms (first tranche)

The *Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024* implemented part of the 2022 Attorney-General's
review:

- A **statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy** (Sch 2, from **10 June 2025**) — intrusion
  on seclusion or misuse of private information, where the plaintiff had a reasonable expectation of
  privacy, the conduct was intentional or reckless and serious, and the public interest in privacy
  outweighs countervailing interests (free speech, journalism exempt). Damages up to $478,550, with
  no need to prove loss.
- A **doxxing offence** (*Criminal Code* s 474.17C) — publishing personal data menacingly.
- A **children's online privacy code** to be made by the OAIC; transparency about automated
  decisions; tiered civil penalties; the Commissioner's powers expanded.
- Still to come (second tranche): a "fair and reasonable" test for handling data, removal of the
  small-business and employee-record exemptions, and a right to erasure.

## NSW: PPIP Act 1998

NSW public sector agencies (and, under the *Health Records and Information Privacy Act 2002*,
health bodies) follow 12 Information Protection Principles; complaints go to the agency's internal
review, then NCAT (ADR Act route), overseen by the **Information and Privacy Commission NSW**. A
mandatory data-breach scheme began in November 2023.

## Use it for

- **chg.1.3 / chg.1.3 / chg.6.1** — the central technology statute: what it covers, who it leaves out, and
  the Optus/Medibank/Kmart records as the breaches and surveillance that test it.
- **chg.6 (law reform)** — a live reform in two tranches; the tort is the first new individual
  privacy right in Australian law.
- **chg.6.5** — enforcement by complaint and regulator, no private right of action under the APPs
  themselves — hence the tort.
- **hr.2.9 / hr.2.10** — ICCPR art 17 given effect by statute because the common law would not.

## Caution

Penalty amounts and the tort's damages cap are indexed; check the OAIC site. The second tranche has
not passed (as at this record) — don't describe the "fair and reasonable" test or the erasure right
as law. The media exemption means most privacy disputes with journalists go through defamation or
the Press Council, not this Act.
