LEPRA consolidated police powers that had been spread across many Acts and the common law.

| Section | Power | Threshold or safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| s 21 | Stop, search and detain a person | Reasonable suspicion (*R v Rondo*) |
| ss 31–34 | Strip search | Serious and urgent outside a station; never under 10; parent or support person for 10–17 |
| Part 4A | Knife scanning in a declared area | No suspicion about the individual (made permanent October 2026) |
| Part 5 | Search warrants | Authorised officer; occupier's notice |
| ss 76AA–76AQ | Digital evidence access orders | Court order; widened in 2026 |
| s 99 | Arrest without warrant | Reasonable suspicion **and** arrest reasonably necessary (*DPP v Carr*) |
| Part 9 (ss 114–131) | Detention after arrest | Up to 6 hours, extendable once by 6 by detention warrant; custody manager; rights |
| ss 145–150 | Drug detection dogs | Authorised places; no individual suspicion |
| ss 197–199 | Move-on directions | Reasonable grounds about the person's conduct |
| s 201 | Safeguards for any power | Name, place of duty, reason |
| s 231 | Use of force | Only reasonable force |

## Use it for

- **crime.2.1** — powers and their limits in one place; most need reasonable suspicion about the person.
- The exceptions (knife scanning, drug dogs) attach the threshold to a **place**, which is why they are contested.
- **shel.3.3 (homelessness)** — the **move-on direction** (ss 197–199) is the power used daily against rough sleepers; the *NSW Protocol for Homeless People in Public Places* says homelessness alone is not a ground. See the Pari record and the *Sydney Public Reserves (Public Safety) Act 2017*.

## Caution

Check section numbers against the Act in force, especially Part 4A after the October 2026 amendments. Library: *NSW Police Powers – Two-Page Summary*.
