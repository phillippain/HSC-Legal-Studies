**Land lease communities** (formerly "residential parks") are the tenure in which a person owns a
manufactured home but rents the site beneath it — predominantly over-55 and retiree communities on
the NSW coast. They are governed by the *Residential (Land Lease) Communities Act 2013* (NSW),
not the tenancy Act. The Act's **five-year statutory review** produced 48 recommendations; the
2024 amending Act implements **21** of them, commencing **25 September 2024**, with utility-billing
provisions from **11 December 2024**. The Tenants' Union article summarises the changes for
residents.

## What changed on 25 September 2024

| Area | Reform |
|---|---|
| **Site fees** | Land value no longer relevant when NCAT decides if an increase is excessive; "by notice" increases must explain their reasons; fixed-method increases limited to one element (12-month transition) |
| **Electricity** | Embedded-network charges capped at the **IPART median retail offer** for the district; the "Reckless" per-kWh method ends; separate supply and usage charges return; low-amp discounts (60% under 30 amps, 30% for 30–60 amps) now extend to tenants and third-party-supplied residents; operators must disclose supply costs yearly and review contracts every two years |
| **Agreements** | Voluntary sharing agreements cannot include entry or exit fees; residents must be offered a rent-only alternative with cost information |
| **Termination** | Notice period for an unlawful site rises from 90 to **120 days**, with **compensation** if the site became unlawful through the operator's act; operators can no longer end agreements for three years' non-use |
| **Living in the home** | Minor alterations without operator consent; limits on operator entry; dilapidation notices only where the home owner caused the dilapidation; 30 days' notice of any development application affecting the community; tested emergency evacuation procedures |

From 11 December 2024: residents pay for separately metered utilities only on **itemised bills**
meeting National Energy Rules standards. The remaining **27 recommendations** were left for 2025.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4** — the only current, specific evidence on the land-lease tenure; most students know
  only caravan parks. The Brooms Head NCAT record shows the same Act applied to an over-55s rule.
- **shel.2.9 / ls.4.1 (agencies of reform)** — "the result of many years of advocacy and law reform
  engagement by residents and supporters": a statutory review, an NGO campaign, then an Act — the
  textbook reform pathway, delivered in instalments.
- **shel.2.5** — NCAT's role in site-fee disputes and the 2018–20 electricity cases that drove the
  pricing reform.
- **shel.3.2 (older people)** — a tenure whose residents are mostly older and asset-rich but
  income-poor; the fee and utility caps as protection for a vulnerable group.

## Caution

The library document is the **Tenants' Union's explainer**, not the Act; cite the Act by name and
check the current consolidated *Residential (Land Lease) Communities Act 2013* on legislation.nsw.gov.au
for section numbers. The "40,000-plus" residents figure is the sector's usual estimate, not from
the article. The second-phase reforms (the remaining 27 recommendations) should be checked for
progress since 2025.
