The **Bondi Beach terrorist attack** on 14 December 2025 killed 15 people. Ten days later NSW passed the
**Terrorism and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2025**, and in August 2026 NSW became the first state to
join a **National Gun Buyback**, citing recommendations 13 and 14 of a Royal Commission interim report.
Bookings opened on 26 October 2026; surrenders run from **2 November 2026** at six police stations,
widening in stages to February 2027, for about 12 months.

## Before and after

| | Before | After the 2025 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Number of guns | No fixed cap | **4** (recreational hunting, vermin) or **10** (most other genuine reasons); collectors and dealers unlimited |
| Licence term | Up to 5 years | **2 years** (licences from 1 July 2026) |
| Review of decisions | External appeal to **NCAT** | Internal review only (except Firearm Prohibition Orders) |
| Permit to acquire | — | Safe-storage inspection and terrorism-related checks first |
| Categories | Straight-pull, pump, lever-release lower | Moved to Category C; belt-fed prohibited |
| Club membership | Not required for all A/B holders | Required once proclaimed |

## The buyback

Notice letter → book through the **Gun Safe** portal (no walk-ins) → surrender at a station → fixed
compensation (**$450–$1,000** per firearm; Phase 2 valuations above $3,000, capped at $10,000) →
destruction, funded by the Commonwealth. Up to **274,000** firearms (about 24% of NSW's registered
guns) could be surrendered.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of a government media release** with a political purpose; it omits prices
and partisan claims are disputed. The 18% of licence holders and $42.8 million Registry figures are
unverified. Several provisions await proclamation and the cap-exception regulation is unfinished.
