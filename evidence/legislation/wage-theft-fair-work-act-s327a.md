From **1 January 2025** an employer commits an offence if it **intentionally** engages in conduct that
results in an employee being underpaid wages or entitlements under the *Fair Work Act*, an award or an
enterprise agreement.

| | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Individual | **10 years' imprisonment**, or a fine of $1.56 million or three times the underpayment |
| Company | **$7.8 million** or three times the underpayment, whichever is greater |

- **Inadvertent underpayment is not a crime**: the prosecution must prove intention.
- The **Fair Work Ombudsman** investigates and refers matters to the **CDPP** or the AFP.
- Small businesses that follow the **Voluntary Small Business Wage Compliance Code** have a defence;
  larger employers that self-report may be offered a cooperation agreement instead of prosecution.

## Use it for

- **White-collar crime** (`crime.1.5`): a recent example of economic offending by businesses.
- **Mens rea**: the line between a crime and a payroll error is intention.
- **Self-interest** as a factor (`crime.1.8`), and **law reform** after years of underpayment
  scandals.
