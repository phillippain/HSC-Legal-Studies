The *Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024* (Cth) set the principle — platforms must take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s holding accounts — and left the detail to legislative rules. The Minister for Communications made the Rules in July 2025, announcing on 30 July 2025 that YouTube would not be exempt after all.

## What the Rules do

- Exclude classes of service from the definition of "age-restricted social media platform": messaging, online games, services whose sole or primary purpose is education or health, and professional networking.
- On that basis the eSafety Commissioner identified ten platforms as covered when the obligation started on **10 December 2025**.
- The **Amendment Rules 2026** (registered 25 March 2026, commenced 26 March 2026) narrowed the definition: a service is now caught only if it has a recommender feature, a logged-in feature, or both.
- Enforcement sits with eSafety: information notices, enforceable undertakings, infringement notices, injunctions and civil penalties up to **$49.5 million**; by May 2026 23 notices had gone to ten platforms and five were under investigation.

## Use it for

- **chg.6.4** — the executive and legislative *processes* of the reform: Act → ministerial rules → amendment within four months → regulator's guidance.
- **inv.3.4** — delegated legislation in practice: parliament set the age, the Minister decided which companies, and the change in 2026 needed no vote.

## Caution

The named platforms change; cite eSafety's current list rather than a news report. Rules are disallowable, so Parliament kept a veto it did not use.
