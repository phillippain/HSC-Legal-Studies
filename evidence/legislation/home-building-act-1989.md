The *Home Building Act* is the consumer-protection statute for residential construction. It sits
underneath the two 2020 Acts passed after Opal and Mascot Towers (the *Design and Building
Practitioners Act* and the *Residential Apartment Buildings Act*), and is the law an ordinary owner
of a house or unit relies on when the work is defective.

## Key provisions

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Licensing (Part 3)** | Residential building work over $5,000 may be done only by a licensed contractor; Fair Trading licenses, disciplines and publishes a register |
| **Contracts (Part 2)** | Work over $5,000 needs a written contract with prescribed terms; over $20,000, the consumer-guide and a cap on deposits (10%); cooling-off for larger contracts |
| **Statutory warranties (Part 2C, s 18B)** | Implied into every contract: work done with due care and skill, in accordance with plans, with suitable materials, in compliance with law, within the time agreed, and resulting in a dwelling fit for occupation. **s 18E**: **six years** for major defects (structural, fire safety, waterproofing), **two years** for other defects, from completion — and they run with the land to later purchasers (s 18D) |
| **Home Building Compensation (Part 6)** | Insurance (through icare's HBCF) compulsory for work over $20,000, covering the owner if the builder dies, disappears or becomes insolvent — up to $340,000; "last resort" cover only |
| **Disputes** | Fair Trading inspectors can issue rectification orders; NCAT's Home Building list hears claims up to $500,000; above that, the District or Supreme Court |

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 (purchase)** — what a buyer of a new home actually gets in law beyond the title: the
  warranties, and why the six-year clock matters when buying an apartment three years old.
- **shel.2.5 / shel.2.6** — the Fair Trading → NCAT → court ladder, with a $500,000 tribunal
  limit.
- **shel.2.4 (strata)** — owners corporations sue under s 18B for common-property defects; most
  big NSW building disputes are this Act plus the 2020 Acts.
- **Evaluation** — "last resort" insurance that does not cover a builder who simply refuses; the
  2020 Acts exist because the warranties were found to be unenforceable in practice against
  phoenixed companies.

## Caution

Thresholds ($5,000, $20,000, $500,000, $340,000) and warranty periods have been amended several
times; check the Act in force. The 2020 Acts, not this one, create the **statutory duty of care**
(DBP Act s 37) that lets owners sue designers and builders in negligence. The Act is being reviewed
as part of the "Building Bill" consolidation — check whether that has passed.
