Buying a home in NSW is a two-stage process: a **contract** (governed by this Act) and then
**registration** of the transfer (governed by the *Real Property Act 1900*). The *Conveyancing Act*
is the century-old statute that sets the terms on which the contract can be made.

## Key provisions

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| **s 54A** | A contract for the sale of land is unenforceable unless it is in **writing** and signed — no handshake sales. |
| **s 52A** and the **Sale of Land Regulation** | **Vendor disclosure**: the vendor must attach prescribed documents to the contract before it is signed — title search, deposited plan, s 10.7 planning certificate, sewer diagram, and (for strata) the strata documents — and gives implied warranties that there are no undisclosed adverse affectations. A purchaser may rescind for breach. |
| **ss 66S–66W** | **Cooling-off**: a purchaser of residential property has **five business days** after exchange to rescind, forfeiting **0.25%** of the price (s 66U). No cooling-off at auction, or where the purchaser's solicitor or conveyancer signs a **s 66W certificate** waiving it — which is standard practice. |
| **s 66X, Part 4 Div 8** | Deposit rules; a 10% deposit is the norm, usually held by the agent as stakeholder. |
| **Electronic conveyancing** | Since 2019 NSW transfers must be lodged electronically (PEXA) under the *Electronic Conveyancing National Law*. |

Related consumer rules sit elsewhere: the *Property and Stock Agents Act 2002* (agents and
underquoting), the *Home Building Act 1989* (statutory warranties on new homes) and the *National
Credit Code* (the mortgage).

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 (purchase)** — the actual legal steps: exchange, cooling-off, settlement, registration.
  Students usually know only the Torrens half.
- **shel.1.4** — a home is acquired by contract and recorded by registration: ownership as a
  bundle of legal rights.
- **shel.2.5 / shel.2.6** — disputes over deposits, rescission and disclosure go to the Supreme
  Court (Equity) or, for small sums, the District/Local Court — not NCAT.
- **Evaluation** — vendor disclosure and cooling-off protect buyers *before* the mortgage is
  signed; compare the thinner protections for renters at the start of a lease.

## Caution

Section numbers are stable but the **Regulation** (the list of prescribed documents and warranties)
is remade every five years — the current one is 2022. Conveyancing is usually done by a solicitor or
licensed conveyancer (*Conveyancers Licensing Act 2003*); the Act does not require it, but the
cooling-off waiver effectively does.
