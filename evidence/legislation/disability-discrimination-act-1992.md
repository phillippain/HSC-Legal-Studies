The DDA makes it unlawful to discriminate on the ground of disability — broadly defined to include physical, intellectual, psychiatric, sensory and learning disability, and disease — in employment, education, goods and services, accommodation, access to premises and Commonwealth programs.

## Mechanics

- **Direct discrimination** (less favourable treatment, including failure to make reasonable adjustments, s 5) and **indirect discrimination** (a requirement a person cannot meet because of disability, s 6).
- **Unjustifiable hardship** (s 11) is the main defence; the burden of proving it lies on the respondent.
- **Disability Standards** (education 2005, accessible public transport 2002, access to premises 2010) turn the general duty into specific rules; compliance with a Standard is a complete defence.
- **Enforcement**: complaint to the Australian Human Rights Commission → compulsory conciliation → if unresolved, the Federal Court or Federal Circuit and Family Court. The Commission cannot order anything; the court can award damages.

## Use it for

- **exp.5.1 / exp.5.2** — protection under domestic law and the authority (the AHRC and its Disability Discrimination Commissioner) through which it is accessed; the Act gives effect in part to the CRPD (exp.5.1 international).
- **exp.6.2** — barriers: the individual must complain, prove the case and pay the costs risk in court; most complaints end in conciliation or withdrawal.

## Caution

State law (the *Anti-Discrimination Act 1977* (NSW), heard in NCAT) runs in parallel; a complainant chooses one. Pair the Act with a case or an AHRC conciliation outcome — the statute alone proves nothing about effectiveness.
