The *Housing Act 2001* is the statutory basis for public housing in NSW. It replaced the *Housing Act
1985* and continues the **NSW Land and Housing Corporation** as the body that owns the state's
public housing stock — around 100,000 dwellings — and lets it to tenants under the *Residential
Tenancies Act 2010* (which has a dedicated Part for social housing tenancies).

## What it does

- **Objects (Part 2)** — to ensure that public housing is developed as a viable and diversified
  form of housing choice, to maximise opportunities for people on low incomes to obtain appropriate
  and affordable housing, and to promote the delivery of housing for people in need. These are
  *objects of the Act*, not rights of applicants.
- **The Corporation (Part 3)** — a statutory corporation with power to acquire, build, manage and
  sell housing and to enter arrangements with community housing providers. Since 2024 it is
  administered as part of **Homes NSW** within the Department of Communities and Justice, which also
  runs the Aboriginal Housing Office and homelessness services.
- **Policy, not statute** — eligibility (income limits, residency, assets), priority housing
  categories, rent (set at 25–30% of household income under the rent-setting policy), the waiting
  list and the "Housing Pathways" register are all **administrative policy** made under the Act, which
  is why they can change without Parliament.
- **Review** — tenants and applicants can seek internal review and then go to the **Housing Appeals
  Committee**, an independent body established by policy, not the Act; tenancy disputes go to NCAT.

## Use it for

- **shel.1.3 (obligation to provide shelter)** — the key point: the Act sets objects for a
  landlord but gives no person a *right* to housing. Compare the ICESCR art 11 and the "duty to
  assist" in Wales (AHURI 347).
- **shel.3.4 (social housing)** — 67,721 households were waiting in October 2025 (Homelessness NSW):
  demand is governed by policy, supply by budget, and neither by the statute.
- **shel.2.8** — Homes NSW as the government organisation; the Corporation as the legal person that
  evicts, sells and builds.
- **shel.3.2** — residency rules exclude many temporary visa holders and some New Zealanders (the
  Pari case).

## Caution

The Act is **short and structural**; everything students usually want to quote (waiting times,
eligibility, rent rules) lives in policy documents on the Homes NSW website and should be cited as
such. Stock figures change — take the current number from the DCJ annual report. The Aboriginal
Housing Office has its own Act.
