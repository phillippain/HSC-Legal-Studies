Sections 82–84 of the *Crimes Act 1900* (NSW) made "unlawfully" procuring a miscarriage a crime punishable by up to ten years. From *R v Wald* (1971) courts read "unlawfully" to allow abortion where a doctor honestly believed it necessary for the woman's physical or mental health — a common-law exception that most people and some doctors did not know existed, and which left the procedure technically criminal for 48 years.

## The reform

- A private member's bill (Alex Greenwich MP, with 15 co-sponsors across parties) in August 2019; a conscience vote in both houses; the Legislative Council sat late into the night on amendments; assent 2 October 2019.
- Abortion lawful on request up to 22 weeks; after that with two doctors' agreement; conscientious objection with a duty to refer; a new offence of an *unqualified person* performing one.
- Reasons: changing community values (polling showed large majorities in favour), relevance (every other state had already reformed), and an outdated law (chg.1.5) whose operation depended on a 1971 District Court ruling.

## Use it for

- **chg.1.5** — amending an outdated law whose text and practice had diverged for decades.
- **chg.1.1 / chg.2.2** — values and informal drivers: the rallies for and against, the Pro-Choice Alliance, church opposition, and the Premier allowing a free vote.

## Caution

A legislative repeal, not a court decision; the 1971 *Wald* ruling was never overruled, it was made irrelevant. Keep the Queensland (2018) and South Australian (2021) reforms as comparisons, not as NSW evidence.
