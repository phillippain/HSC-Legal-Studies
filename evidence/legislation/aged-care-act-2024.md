Residential aged care is shelter for about 190,000 Australians at any time, and the law governing
it is Commonwealth. The *Aged Care Act 1997* was a funding statute written from the provider's
side; the **Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety** (final report March 2021, 148
recommendations) found "a shocking tale of neglect" and recommended a new Act built on the rights of
the older person. The *Aged Care Act 2024* passed in November 2024 with bipartisan support; its
commencement was deferred from 1 July 2025 to **1 November 2025**.

## What changes

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Statement of Rights** | Older people's rights to independence, dignity, choice, safety and culturally safe care; providers must take all reasonable steps to act compatibly, and complaints can be made about breaches |
| **Statement of Principles** | Guides every decision made under the Act |
| **Single assessment and entry** | One pathway (My Aged Care) into home, community and residential care, replacing separate programs |
| **Support at Home** | Replaces Home Care Packages and the Commonwealth Home Support Programme from 1 November 2025, with eight funding classifications |
| **Supported decision-making** | Registered supporters replace informal "representatives" — a shift from substitute to supported decision-making, consistent with the CRPD |
| **Regulation** | Providers must be registered in categories; the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission gets stronger monitoring, enforcement and (from 2026) a new Complaints Commissioner; a duty on providers and responsible persons, with civil penalties, to ensure care does not harm |
| **Means testing** | New residents pay more towards daily living and accommodation; a lifetime cap on contributions; the "no worse off" principle for people already in care |

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (aged care)** — the statute itself, and the clearest Shelter example of a
  **rights-based** law: the Statement of Rights is enforceable through complaints, not the courts.
- **shel.3.2 (older people)** — the Royal Commission's evidence of neglect is the discrimination
  dot point's hardest material; the Act is the legal response.
- **chg.1** — reform driven by a Royal Commission and media (*Four Corners*, 2018), and delayed
  by the scale of change: responsiveness measured in years.
- **shel.1.3** — the obligation to provide shelter for people who cannot provide it themselves,
  funded by the Commonwealth and delivered by (mostly) private and not-for-profit providers.

## Caution

The Act is **new**; its effect is untested and the rules under it (the Aged Care Rules) are long and
still being amended. Use the Royal Commission for the problem and the Act for the response, not for
outcomes. Retirement villages are a separate, state-regulated tenure with no care component.
