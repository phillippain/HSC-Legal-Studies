- On the State's application, the **Supreme Court** may make an **extended supervision order** (ESO)
  or a **continuing detention order** (CDO) against a high-risk sex offender or high-risk violent
  offender.
- The court must be satisfied to a **high degree of probability** that the offender poses an
  **unacceptable risk** of committing another serious offence if not supervised (or detained).
- Orders last up to **5 years** and can be renewed. The *Terrorism (High Risk Offenders) Act 2017*
  does the same for terrorism-related offenders.

## Use it for

- **Preventative and continued detention** (`crime.4.8`) — the balance between community protection
  and liberty for a person who has served their sentence.
- **Separation of powers** — *Kable* (1996) struck down a law aimed at one named man; general schemes
  with proper court process were upheld in *Fardon* (2004).

## Caution

Critics, including human rights bodies, say detention for predicted crimes breaches ICCPR art 9; the
courts have upheld the schemes because they apply to a class and use judicial process.
