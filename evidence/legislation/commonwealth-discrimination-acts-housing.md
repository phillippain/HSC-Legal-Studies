The Commonwealth's discrimination laws rest on the external affairs power: each Act gives domestic
effect to a treaty (CERD, CEDAW, the CRPD and ILO conventions). All four contain a section on
**accommodation**, and all four are enforced the same way.

| Act | Section | Ground | Treaty |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Racial Discrimination Act 1975* | s 12 — land, housing and other accommodation | Race, colour, descent, national or ethnic origin | CERD |
| *Sex Discrimination Act 1984* | s 23 | Sex, pregnancy, marital status, family responsibilities, sexual orientation, gender identity | CEDAW |
| *Disability Discrimination Act 1992* | s 25 (and s 54A assistance animals) | Disability, including a requirement to make reasonable adjustments | CRPD |
| *Age Discrimination Act 2004* | s 29 | Age | — |

Each makes it unlawful to **refuse** an application, to offer accommodation on **less favourable
terms**, to deny access to benefits, or to **evict** or otherwise disadvantage the occupier because
of the attribute. Exemptions: accommodation in which the provider or a near relative also lives (small
premises), accommodation for a particular age group (retirement villages, student housing), and
charitable or religious bodies in some cases.

## Enforcement — the *Australian Human Rights Commission Act 1986*

1. Written complaint to the **AHRC** (within 24 months).
2. **Conciliation** by the Commission — confidential; outcomes are negotiated.
3. If terminated, the complainant may apply within 60 days to the **Federal Circuit and Family
   Court** or **Federal Court**, which can award damages and other orders. Costs risk discourages
   most applicants.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.2** — the Commonwealth layer over the NSW Act: a tenant can choose either regime (not
  both for the same conduct).
- **hr.2.7 / inv.6.1** — the clearest Shelter example of international law becoming domestic law.
- **shel.2.4 / shel.3.2 (older people, disability)** — assistance animals (DDA s 54A) and
  age-restricted communities (ADA exemption) turn up in NCAT and the Brooms Head dispute.
- **Evaluation** — the AHRC can conciliate and report, but not order; the "Balancing Act" and YiSSA
  records show what that looks like in practice.

## Caution

Like the NSW Act, **none covers source of income or homelessness**. Section numbers are for the
accommodation provisions only; each Act has its own definitions and exemptions. The AHRC also hears
complaints under the AHRC Act about breaches of the ICCPR and other instruments against Commonwealth
bodies — those are not unlawful-discrimination complaints and lead to a report, not a court.
