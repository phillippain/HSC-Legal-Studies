The ombudsman is a Swedish idea (1809) that reached NSW in 1974 and the Commonwealth in 1976: an
officer of Parliament, independent of government, who investigates complaints about
administration. It is the **cheapest and most used** way an individual takes on the state.

## NSW Ombudsman Act 1974

- **Jurisdiction (s 12 and Schedule 1)** — conduct of "public authorities": government departments,
  local councils, statutory bodies, public universities, Corrective Services, Youth Justice and
  (since 2017, instead of police) some private providers of community services. **Excluded**: police
  (LECC), courts and judges, Parliament, ministers acting as such, and ICAC.
- **"Wrong" conduct (s 26)** — contrary to law; unreasonable, unjust, oppressive or improperly
  discriminatory; based on a mistake of law or fact; made for an improper purpose or with irrelevant
  considerations; or where reasons should have been given and were not.
- **Powers** — the powers of a Royal Commission (compel evidence, enter premises); investigations are
  private; the Ombudsman may make **recommendations** to the authority and the Minister and
  **report to Parliament** if they are not acted on — but cannot change a decision, award
  compensation or discipline anyone.
- **Other functions** — oversight of **child-protection** reportable conduct (now with the Children's
  Guardian), **community services** (Community Services division), **public interest disclosures**
  (whistleblowers), and the **Official Community Visitors** to detention and care facilities.
- **The 2026 wrongful-detention report** (record on this site) shows the Ombudsman's reach into
  Corrective Services: people held past their release dates, found by investigation and fixed by
  recommendation.

## Commonwealth Ombudsman Act 1976

The same model for Commonwealth agencies: Centrelink, the ATO, Home Affairs (immigration
detention — the Ombudsman reviews every detention over two years under the *Migration Act* s 486N),
the NDIA, and the **AFP** (as Law Enforcement Ombudsman). It also runs the Defence Force,
Postal Industry and Overseas Students ombudsman functions.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.5 / dis.3.6 (Ombudsman and statutory bodies)** — the statute, the jurisdiction and the limits:
  "investigate and recommend" against ICAC's "find" and a court's "order".
- **dis.3.4** — the Ombudsman as the bridge between **internal review** (the agency
  reconsiders) and **external review** (ART, NCAT, judicial review); a complaint can be made at any
  stage and costs nothing.
- **inv.7.4** — why a report is not a remedy; compare the AHRC.
- **crime.4.5 (post-sentencing)** — prisoner complaints and the Official Visitors scheme.

## Caution

Complaint numbers are in the NSW Ombudsman's annual report (tens of thousands a year, mostly
resolved informally). Police complaints left the Ombudsman in 2017 — older notes that say the
Ombudsman handles police are out of date. Section numbers above are stable but the Schedule of
excluded bodies changes.
