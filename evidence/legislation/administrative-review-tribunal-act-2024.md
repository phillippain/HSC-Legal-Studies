A person who disagrees with a Commonwealth decision has two different routes, and the syllabus
asks students to tell them apart.

## Merits review — "was it the right decision?"

The **Administrative Review Tribunal** stands in the shoes of the original decision-maker, looks at
the facts afresh and can **substitute** its own decision. It replaced the Administrative Appeals
Tribunal (1976–2024), which the Government abolished after a Senate inquiry found that a large
share of members appointed between 2013 and 2022 had political connections.

| Feature | ART Act 2024 |
|---|---|
| **Jurisdiction** | Over 400 Commonwealth Acts: social security (Centrelink), **child support**, **migration and refugee** decisions, NDIS, veterans' entitlements, taxation, FOI, workers' compensation (Comcare) |
| **Jurisdictional areas** | Eight lists, each with a Deputy President; a dedicated **guidance and appeals panel** for significant or inconsistent decisions |
| **Appointments** | Members appointed through a merit-based, publicly advertised process (s 205 onwards) — the reform's centrepiece |
| **Process** | Informal, inquisitorial, usually no legal representation funded; application fees waived for social security and most migration protection matters; **internal review** by the agency is normally required first |
| **Appeal** | To the Federal Court on a **question of law** only (s 172) |
| **Oversight** | A statutory **Administrative Review Council** restored to monitor the system |

The site's child-support (*WNZP*, *XTXD*) and refugee ("ARTA") records are ART decisions.

## Judicial review — "was it lawfully made?"

A court does not remake the decision; it asks whether the decision-maker had the power, followed
procedural fairness, considered the right things and acted reasonably — and if not, sets it aside
and sends it back.

- **ADJR Act 1977** — a simplified statutory route in the Federal Court with a list of grounds
  (s 5: breach of natural justice, error of law, improper purpose, irrelevant considerations,
  unreasonableness) and a **right to written reasons** (s 13). Migration decisions are largely
  excluded from it.
- **Constitution s 75(v) and *Judiciary Act* s 39B** — the entrenched right to seek writs
  (mandamus, prohibition, injunction) against Commonwealth officers in the High Court or Federal
  Court; this is why migration judicial review cannot be legislated away (*Plaintiff S157*, 2003;
  the *Da Silva* and *Plaintiff M98* records).
- **Procedural fairness** — *Kioa v West* (1985): a person affected by a decision must be told the
  case against them and given a hearing.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.4 (external review)** — the merits/judicial distinction with the right body for each,
  and the 2024 reform as the current story.
- **dis.3.4 (internal review)** — the mandatory first step before the ART in most schemes.
- **inv.5.3 / inv.5.4** — a tribunal is not a court (Chapter III); why the ART cannot decide
  questions of law finally, and why s 75(v) matters.
- **chg.6.5** — accessibility: free and informal, but a 2024 backlog of tens of thousands of
  migration cases inherited from the AAT.

## Caution

The ART is new; its case-load and timeliness figures are in its first annual reports. Section
numbers in the ART Act are indicative. NSW has its own system (the ADR Act 1997 and NCAT) — do not
mix the two. "ARTA" is the ART's neutral citation prefix.
