NSW's own discrimination statute, older than every Commonwealth equivalent. It works attribute by
attribute: each Part names a protected ground and then lists the areas of life in which
discrimination on that ground is unlawful — work, education, goods and services, registered clubs
and **accommodation**.

## Protected grounds

| Part | Ground |
|---|---|
| Part 2 | Race (and racial vilification) |
| Part 3 | Sex, including pregnancy and breastfeeding; sexual harassment |
| Part 3A | Transgender status (and vilification) |
| Part 4 | Marital or domestic status |
| Part 4A | Disability (and HIV/AIDS vilification) |
| Part 4B | Responsibilities as a carer |
| Part 4C | Homosexuality (and vilification) |
| Part 4G | Age |

## What "accommodation" covers

For each ground it is unlawful to **refuse** an application for accommodation, to offer it on
**worse terms**, to deny or limit **access to benefits** connected with it, or to **evict** or
subject the occupier to any other detriment — because of the attribute. The classic cases are a
landlord or agent who won't rent to a single mother, a family on Centrelink (not a ground in NSW —
see Caution), a person with an assistance animal, or an Aboriginal applicant. Exemptions exist for
accommodation shared with the owner or their family in small premises, and for accommodation
provided for a particular age group or by religious bodies.

## Enforcement

1. Complaint to **Anti-Discrimination NSW** (the President) within 12 months.
2. **Conciliation** — most complaints end here, often with an apology, compensation or policy change.
3. Referral to **NCAT** (Administrative and Equal Opportunity Division) if unresolved, which can
   award damages up to $100,000 and order an apology or a change of practice.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.2 (discrimination)** — the statute that applies to NSW landlords and agents, and the
  mechanism (complaint, not prosecution) that decides how effective it is.
- **shel.2.2 / shel.2.3** — a discriminatory eviction can be challenged under this Act as well as the
  *Residential Tenancies Act*.
- **Evaluation** — complaint-driven, individual, confidential conciliation, with no NSW power to
  investigate systemic rental discrimination; compare the AHRC's and the Rental Commissioner's roles.

## Caution

**Source of income, receipt of social security and homelessness are not grounds under the NSW Act**
(the ACT, Victoria and Queensland cover some of these) — the most common rental discrimination has
no NSW remedy. The 2024 NSW Law Reform Commission review of the Act recommended modernising it;
check what has been implemented. Section numbers vary between Parts; cite the Part and the ground.
