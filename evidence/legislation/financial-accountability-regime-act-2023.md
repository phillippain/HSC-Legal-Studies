Commissioner Hayne's 2019 report recommended extending the Banking Executive Accountability Regime to the whole financial sector (recommendations 3.9, 4.12, 6.6–6.8) and establishing a compensation scheme of last resort (recommendation 7.1). Both took until 2023 to pass.

## The Financial Accountability Regime

- Banks, insurers and superannuation trustees must register "accountable persons", map their responsibilities and defer part of their variable pay for at least four years so it can be clawed back after misconduct.
- Obligations to act with honesty, integrity, due skill and care; APRA and ASIC administer the regime jointly; civil penalties on the entity, and disqualification of individuals.

## The Compensation Scheme of Last Resort

- Pays up to **$150,000** to a consumer who holds an unpaid determination from the Australian Financial Complaints Authority against a firm that has become insolvent — personal advice, credit and securities dealing are covered; managed investment schemes are not.
- Funded by an industry levy; the first year was paid by the Commonwealth and the ten largest firms.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.7** — the *outcome* of royal commission recommendations: slow, partial (the CSLR excludes the collapsed Dixon Advisory-style managed investments that generated much of the demand) but real.
- **chg.4.1–4.2** — benefits and limitations of reform through a royal commission.

## Caution

Hayne made 76 recommendations; the government said in 2019 it would act on all of them, and some (mortgage broker commissions, for instance) were later abandoned. Cite the Treasury implementation roadmap, not a blanket "all implemented".
