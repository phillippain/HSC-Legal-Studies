## How a decision is made

1. **Show cause** offence? The accused must show why detention is not justified (s 16A).
2. **Unacceptable risk** test (s 17): failing to appear, committing a serious offence, endangering safety, interfering with witnesses (factors in s 18).
3. Can **conditions** manage the risk? Including accommodation requirements, residential rehabilitation (s 28).

## Reform timeline

| Year | Reform | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | Bail Act 2013 replaces the 1978 Act (commenced May 2014) | NSW Law Reform Commission review |
| 2014 | Bail Amendment Act: show cause | Media outrage over bail granted to Mahmoud Hawi and Lloyd Fesus; Hatzistergos review |
| Apr 2024 | Bail and Crimes Amendment Act: **s 22C** — 14–17-year-olds charged with serious break-ins or car theft while on bail for similar offences must be refused bail unless there is a 'high degree of confidence' | Regional youth crime |
| Jun 2024 | Bail and Other Legislation Amendment (Domestic Violence) Act: show cause for serious DV offences, 'red flag' behaviour, stays of release, electronic monitoring | Murder of Molly Ticehurst by Daniel Billings, on bail (April 2024) |
| Jun 2026 | Organised crime links considered at bail | Gang violence |
| Sep 2026 | s 22C extended to April 2028 | — |

s 22C review: **454** young people faced the test, **70%** refused bail; Aboriginal young people **84%** of refusals.

## Caution

Hawi and Fesus are named from teaching notes; read the 2014 Hatzistergos review before relying on the details of either case.

## s 22C extended (September 2026)

The statutory review (tabled 15 September 2026) found s 22C helped stabilise youth crime in regional NSW and recommended extending it. The Justice Legislation Amendment (Miscellaneous) Bill (No 2) 2026, introduced 15 September and passed **24 September 2026**, extended s 22C to **4 April 2028** (it was due to lapse on 1 October 2026). The government paired it with an $8.4m Tamworth diversion pilot for 8–13-year-olds (AAP, 15 Sept 2026). Critics, including the ALS, point to remand rising and Aboriginal young people making up most refusals.
