Australia's online-harms law has grown in layers: criminal offences for individuals (state and
Commonwealth), a civil **takedown** regulator for content (eSafety, since 2015), and now a
**platform-design** obligation (the minimum-age law).

## Online Safety Act 2021

| Scheme | What the eSafety Commissioner can do |
|---|---|
| **Cyberbullying (children)** | Order a service to remove material targeting an Australian child within **24 hours**; end-user notices to the person who posted it |
| **Adult cyber-abuse** (new in 2021) | Removal notices for material intended to cause serious harm to an adult — a higher threshold |
| **Image-based abuse** | Removal of intimate images shared without consent; civil penalties for the poster (up to 500 penalty units) |
| **Illegal and restricted content** | Class 1 (child sexual abuse, terrorism) removal anywhere in the world; Class 2 restrictions |
| **Abhorrent violent material** | Blocking requests to ISPs (added after Christchurch, 2019) |
| **Basic Online Safety Expectations** | Ministerial determination; platforms must report on compliance (transparency notices to Twitter/X, Google, Apple, Meta in 2022–24) |
| **Industry codes and standards** | Mandatory codes for eight industry sectors; the Commissioner can impose a standard if a code is inadequate |
| **Penalties** | Civil penalties up to about $800,000 for platforms per breach (higher under the 2024 Act); the *X Corp* litigation over the Wakeley stabbing video (2024) tested the limits of global takedowns |

## Social Media Minimum Age Act 2024

- Platforms (an "age-restricted social media platform": Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, X,
  YouTube and others as determined) must take **reasonable steps** to prevent Australians under
  **16** from holding accounts, from **10 December 2025**.
- Penalties up to **$49.5 million** for systemic breaches; no penalty on children or parents.
- Age assurance by the platforms, guided by an eSafety-commissioned trial; no requirement to hand
  over ID. Exemptions for messaging, gaming and education services.
- Passed in November 2024 with bipartisan support after a News Corp campaign and the Hinch
  family's advocacy; opposed by the platforms, some mental-health bodies and the Human Rights
  Commissioner (CROC art 13 and 17 concerns). Reviewed after two years.

## Criminal offences for individuals

- **NSW Crimes Act Div 15C** (2017): recording or distributing an intimate image without consent
  (ss 91P–91Q, 3 years), and threatening to (s 91R); courts can order destruction.
- **Commonwealth Criminal Code**: using a carriage service to menace, harass or offend (s 474.17,
  5 years) and the aggravated private-sexual-material offence (s 474.17A); **deepfake** sexual
  material (2024 amendment, s 474.17A as amended); the doxxing offence (2024).

## Caution

The minimum-age law commenced in December 2025; its enforcement record is short — report what
eSafety has published, not predictions. Platform lists and penalty amounts are set by legislative
instrument and change. The *X Corp* case ended with eSafety discontinuing — avoid overstating the
Commissioner's global reach.
