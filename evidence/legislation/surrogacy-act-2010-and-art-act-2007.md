Three NSW Acts answer the questions birth technology raises: *is it allowed?* (Surrogacy Act, ART
Act), *who are the parents?* (Status of Children Act) and *what can the child know?* (ART Act).

## Surrogacy Act 2010

| Rule | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Altruistic surrogacy lawful** | Reasonable expenses may be reimbursed; the surrogate must be 25+, the intended parents must have a medical or social need, and all parties need independent legal advice and counselling before a written pre-conception agreement |
| **Commercial surrogacy an offence** | s 8 — up to 2 years' imprisonment and 1,000 penalty units; s 11 applies the offence **extraterritorially** to NSW residents who arrange commercial surrogacy overseas (since 2011) |
| **Parentage order** | Part 3 — the **Supreme Court** may transfer parentage from the surrogate (who is the legal mother at birth) to the intended parents, applied for between 30 days and 6 months after birth, if in the child's best interests and the preconditions are met; without an order, the surrogate and her partner remain the legal parents |
| **Advertising** | Advertising for or as a surrogate is an offence; agreements are unenforceable except for expenses |

## Assisted Reproductive Technology Act 2007

- Clinics must be **registered** with NSW Health and comply with NHMRC guidelines.
- **Donor identification** — a central register; children conceived from gametes donated after
  1 January 2010 can obtain the donor's identity at **18**; donors cannot be used for more than five
  families.
- Gametes cannot be used without written consent; posthumous use only with prior consent; the
  2025 Monash IVF embryo mix-up record shows the regulatory gap being argued.

## Status of Children Act 1996 (Part 3)

The **presumptions of parentage** for ART: the birth mother is the mother; her consenting husband
or de facto partner (including a female partner, since 2008) is the other parent; a **sperm or egg
donor is not a parent**. These presumptions are what a surrogacy parentage order displaces. The
Commonwealth *Family Law Act* s 60H mirrors them for parenting orders.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3** — the complete NSW position: lawful altruistic, criminal commercial, parentage by
  court order, donor identification.
- **fam.1.5 / fam.3.2** — legal parentage as a status conferred by statute, not biology (*Farnell
  v Chanbua*, the Baby Gammy case, is the cautionary example of commercial surrogacy abroad).
- **chg.1 / chg.2** — the 2025 NSW parliamentary inquiry into surrogacy and the proposals to
  lift the extraterritorial ban or allow regulated compensation; the 2026 "baby broker" record is
  the enforcement story.
- **exp.6** — an unenforced extraterritorial offence (no NSW prosecution to date) as a test of
  enforceability.

## Caution

The extraterritorial offence has never been prosecuted and is under review — check the outcome of
the 2025 inquiry before describing the law as settled. Donor-register rules differ for pre-2010
donations. Medicare funding for IVF (the *Herald Sun* record) is Commonwealth policy, not these
Acts.
