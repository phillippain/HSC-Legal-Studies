The Commonwealth has no housing department of its own and owns no public housing; its lever is
money. The **Housing Australia Future Fund (HAFF)** is the Albanese Government's flagship
mechanism: rather than appropriating money for housing each year, Parliament created a
**$10 billion fund** whose investment returns are spent on housing.

## The scheme

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The Fund** | $10 billion credited in 2023, invested by the **Future Fund Board of Guardians** |
| **Disbursements** | Up to the fund's annual earnings, with a floor of **$500 million a year** from 2024–25 (the Greens' condition), paid to **Housing Australia** and to other Commonwealth programs |
| **Housing Australia** | The statutory body under the *Housing Australia Act 2018* (renamed from NHFIC in 2023) that runs the Affordable Housing Bond Aggregator (cheap loans to community housing providers), the National Housing Infrastructure Facility, the Home Guarantee Scheme and now HAFF funding rounds |
| **Target** | 30,000 social and affordable homes in five years — 20,000 social, 10,000 affordable for key workers — plus acute-need funding ($200m remote Indigenous housing, $100m crisis and transitional housing for women and children, $30m veterans) |
| **Passage** | Blocked in the Senate from February to September 2023; passed 14 September 2023 after the Government added $1 billion to the National Housing Infrastructure Facility and $2 billion in direct grants to the states (the "Social Housing Accelerator") |

## Use it for

- **shel.3.4 (social housing)** — the Commonwealth's main contribution, and the three records that
  judge it: the Telegraph's "costly flop" (800 homes contracted, none built, February 2025), the
  ANAO audit and *Four Corners* (2026).
- **ls.2.4 / ls.4.3** — a bill held up by a hostile Senate, changed by negotiation with a minor
  party: the mechanics of Commonwealth law-making on display.
- **ls.2.9 (division of powers)** — the Commonwealth cannot build or zone; it funds states and
  community providers. The HAFF is federalism in housing form.
- **Evaluation** — resource efficiency (a fund that earns money versus a grant that is spent),
  responsiveness (two years from Act to first contracts), effectiveness (homes delivered v
  30,000).

## Caution

Disbursement figures and the number of homes contracted or completed change each round — use the
ANAO record and Housing Australia's latest annual report, not this card, for numbers. The Fund's
earnings depend on markets; in a bad year the $500 million floor is paid from the Budget. The Act
itself is machinery; the policy choices (what counts as "affordable", who qualifies) sit in the
investment mandate and program guidelines.
