- Jurors are selected at random from the electoral roll; some are ineligible or excused; each side has 3 peremptory challenges.
- **2004** (after *R v Skaf*): juror inquiries became an offence — s 68C, up to 2 years.
- **2006** Jury Amendment (Verdicts) Act (after hung juries such as *R v Burrell*): **majority verdicts** of 11–1 or 10–1 after at least **8 hours** of deliberation, if unanimity is unlikely (s 55F).
- **2007**: up to 15 jurors in long trials, balloted to 12.
- **2024** Jury Amendment Act (passed May 2024; in force 2025): excusal, additional and replacement jurors, employment protections, email notices. The proposal to cut the 8-hour minimum to **4 hours** was removed in the Legislative Council (Greens release, 8 May 2024) after the Law Society warned of 'rushed verdicts'.
