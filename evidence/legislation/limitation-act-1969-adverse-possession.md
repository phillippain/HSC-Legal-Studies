"Squatter's rights" is not a right to squat. It is the combined effect of two statutes:

1. **Limitation Act 1969 s 27(2)** — an action to recover land must be brought within **12 years**
   from when the cause of action accrued (when adverse possession began). For **Crown land** the
   period is 30 years (s 27(1)). When the period expires, **s 65** extinguishes the owner's title.
2. **Real Property Act 1900 Part 6A** — for Torrens-title land (nearly all NSW land), a person who
   has been in possession for the limitation period may lodge a **possessory application** with the
   Registrar-General (s 45D) to be recorded as proprietor. The Registrar-General must be satisfied the
   title of the registered owner is extinguished; the owner is notified and may object, and disputes
   go to the Supreme Court.

## What "adverse possession" requires (from the cases)

| Element | Meaning | Where it failed |
|---|---|---|
| **Factual possession** | Physical control of the land as an owner would — living there, fencing, maintaining | *Willis v Abraham*: storing boxes is not possession as a residence |
| **Intention to possess** | To exclude the world, including the owner | — |
| **Open** ("not by stealth") | Visible to anyone who looked, so the owner had the chance to act | *Willis*: broken windows left so the house looked empty |
| **Without consent** | Permission, a lease or a licence defeats the claim | *Willis*: she said the owner "gave us the keys" |
| **Continuous for 12 years** | Successive possessors can be added together | *Rogers*: fifty years at Gymea Bay |

## Use it for

- **shel.1.4 (legal nature of shelter)** — the sharpest illustration that ownership is a legal
  relationship the law can take away: Torrens title is "indefeasible" except where statute says
  otherwise, and this is one of the exceptions.
- **shel.2.4 (squats)** — the only legal route from squatting to ownership, and why it is nearly
  never available to homeless people (12 years, openly, in one place).
- **shel.2.1** — a buyer's lawyer searches for occupation on site for this reason.
- **Evaluation** — a law that rewards use over paper title: *justice* for the Gymea Bay family,
  harsh for the absent Rozelle owner's heirs? The cases give both sides.

## Caution

The Limitation Act does not use the words "adverse possession"; the doctrine is common law given
effect by the limitation period and s 65. Section numbers in Part 6A of the *Real Property Act*
should be checked against the Act in force. Nothing here applies to **public land** (Crown land,
council parks) in the 12-year form, and **trespass** remains an offence under the *Inclosed Lands
Protection Act 1901* throughout the period.
