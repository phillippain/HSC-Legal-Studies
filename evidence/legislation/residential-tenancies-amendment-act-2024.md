Passed in **October 2024**, in force **19 May 2025**, amending the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010*
(NSW). It reaches **2.2–2.3 million renters** and about **600,000 landlords**.

## What it changes

| Change | Substance |
|---|---|
| **No-grounds evictions abolished** | A landlord must give a **valid reason** to end a tenancy — breach, sale, the landlord moving in, redevelopment |
| **Longer notice periods** | On certain leases, so a tenant has time to find somewhere |
| **Easier exit after eviction** | A tenant evicted may end a subsequent lease early if they find a property |
| **Pets** | Application on a standard form; refusal only on specified grounds; **consent automatic if the landlord does not answer within 21 days** |
| **Fees banned** | No charging prospective tenants for background checks or lease preparation |
| **Fee-free rent payment** | At least one method with no fee — bank transfer or Centrepay |
| **Rental Taskforce** | Inside NSW Fair Trading, to enforce compliance |

Premier Chris Minns: "By banning no-grounds evictions, this will give renters much-needed housing
security. This will let them make a house a home."

## Use it for

- **Leasing** — the single best example of the *rights and obligations* balance moving, and recently
  enough to be the strongest reform evidence in the option. Pair it with the **s 8(c)** exclusion in
  the parent Act: the reform enlarges a protection that boarders and lodgers still do not get.
- **Responsiveness** measured honestly: promised by successive Labor leaders, on the government's
  agenda since **July 2023**, passed **October 2024**, in force **May 2025**. Nearly two years from
  commitment to commencement, and that is the fast version.
- **Enforceability** — the reform's own answer to "a right you cannot enforce is not a right" is the
  Rental Taskforce. Whether an $8.4 million taskforce is a match for 600,000 landlords is the question
  to put.
