Roughly one in ten Australians lives in strata. The scheme's own **by-laws** bind them, the **owners
corporation** enforces them, and NCAT supervises.

| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| **s 139(1)** | A by-law must not be **harsh, unconscionable or oppressive** — the provision *Cooper* was decided under |
| **s 137B** | Inserted after *Cooper*, commenced **25 August 2021**: a by-law cannot **unreasonably prohibit** the keeping of an animal, and consent cannot be unreasonably withheld |
| **s 147** | Monetary penalties for breach of a by-law |
| **s 86** | Recovery of unpaid contributions |
| **s 232** | NCAT's general order-making power for strata disputes |

**Mediation through NSW Fair Trading is a precondition** to most applications for NCAT orders — but
not to all. The exempt applications include **s 27**, **s 86** (unpaid contributions), **s 124**
(access for necessary work), **s 147** (by-law penalties), **s 231** and **s 237**. The pattern is
worth naming: **the exemptions are largely the applications an owners corporation brings, not ones a
lot owner brings.**

## Use it for

- **Court initiates, parliament generalises.** *Cooper* bound the parties and everyone who could
  afford an appeal; **s 137B** bound every scheme in the State. That pairing answers `ls.4.3`
  (mechanisms of reform) as well as it answers a shelter question.
- **ADR that is mandatory for one side more than the other** — a concrete, checkable example for the
  alternative dispute resolution dot point, and better than the usual generalities about mediation.
- **Private law-making**: a by-law is law made by a body corporate and enforced against residents.
  s 139(1) is the limit on it, which makes strata a neat illustration of "just laws" in miniature.
