On 24 May 2020 Rio Tinto blasted two rock shelters in Juukan Gorge in the Pilbara with evidence of 46,000 years of continuous occupation, on the country of the Puutu Kunti Kurrama and Pinikura peoples. The destruction was lawful: a consent under s 18 of the *Aboriginal Heritage Act 1972* (WA), granted in 2013, could not be revisited and the Traditional Owners had no right of appeal. A Commonwealth parliamentary inquiry (*A Way Forward*, 2021) called the 1972 Act unfit, and Rio Tinto's chief executive and chairman resigned.

## The reform and its reversal

- The *Aboriginal Cultural Heritage Act 2021* replaced s 18 consents with a tiered permit system, local Aboriginal cultural heritage services and a right for Traditional Owners to be consulted on any activity likely to harm heritage. It commenced on 1 July 2023.
- Within weeks, farmers and pastoralists objected to the permit and survey requirements on small landholdings; the Cook Government announced the repeal on 8 August 2023 and the *Amendment and Repeal Act 2023* restored the 1972 Act (with a new right for native title parties to seek review of s 18 decisions) from 15 November 2023.

## Use it for

- **inv.2.3** — what the law was protecting: sites that embody the interconnection of Country, Peoples and Cultural Practice, and whose loss cannot be compensated.
- **inv.1.8 / inv.1.2** — a lawful act that most people saw as an injustice (misuse of a lawful power; a law that was not effective), then a reform that was not accepted by the community it regulated (effective law must be known and accepted).
- **chg.4.2** — the limits of reform: a law can be repealed as fast as it is made.

## Caution

The Commonwealth *Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Heritage Protection Act 1984* still applies as a last resort and was not changed. NSW has no standalone Aboriginal heritage Act either — heritage sits in the *National Parks and Wildlife Act 1974* — which is the relevant comparison for a NSW class.
