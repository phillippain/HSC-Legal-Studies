Following the **Royal Commission into Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse**, NSW relaxed the
restrictions on **tendency and coincidence evidence** — evidence of an accused's prior convictions or
demonstrated sexual interest in children — in child sexual abuse prosecutions.

## The argument on each side

> "While maintaining an accused person's right to a fair trial, these reforms will help to ensure that
> offenders don't evade justice through the exclusion of relevant evidence to one of the most
> despicable and damaging crimes." — Attorney-General Mark Speakman

Against it, lawyers warned that the change could **put fair hearings in jeopardy**, shifting the
balance against defendants and relying on juries to use propensity evidence fairly
("Change to child sex abuse law could tip balance against accused: lawyers", *SMH*, 2020).

Read alongside **s 272.9(5) of the *Criminal Code Act 1995* (Cth)**, which places a legal burden on the
accused to prove lack of intent in certain child sexual abuse offences — a reversal of onus in the
same field.

## Use it for

- **Responsiveness** — a royal commission finding converted into statute, which is the cleanest example
  of the reform cycle in the crime topic outside sentencing.
- **The balance of rights**, with the unusual feature that the reform is defended *as* rights-preserving
  and attacked as rights-eroding, on the same text. Quote both.
- Pairs with the **provocation reform** (2014) and the **victim consultation reform** (2010): three
  instances of NSW narrowing a protection for the accused after public pressure.
