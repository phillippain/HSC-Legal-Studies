Two bills passed the NSW Parliament on **20 October 2026** (Council amendments agreed by the
Assembly); the ministerial release followed on 21 October. Both await assent and commence by
proclamation; neither applies to past conduct.

## 1. Break and enter after *BA v The King*

In July 2019 BA kicked in the door of the Queanbeyan apartment he still leased after separating,
and assaulted his former partner. The District Court directed a not-guilty verdict on the s 112
count: a tenant cannot break into his own premises. The CCA allowed the Crown's appeal; the
**High Court (10 May 2023, 4–3)** restored the acquittal — break and enter needs a trespass, and his
tenancy gave lawful authority to enter. Kiefel CJ, Gageler and Jagot JJ dissented, saying the result
did not reflect contemporary values about safety at home. (He had pleaded guilty to assault,
intimidation and property damage.) A private member's bill in 2023 lapsed; the government bill was
introduced 25 March 2026.

| | Before | After (new **s 115AA**) |
|---|---|---|
| Lease or ownership | A leaseholder with exclusive possession cannot break and enter | No defence where a **non-occupant** commits or intends a personal violence offence against a **current or former intimate partner** in a dwelling |
| Who is an occupant | A right to occupy survived moving out | Not an occupant while an **AVO, court order, bail or parole condition** bars living there; other occupancy factors go to the jury |
| Who is protected | — | Intimate partners only; other family members excluded |
| Review | — | Minister must review after two years |

## 2. Covert stalking and tracking devices

The NSW Crime Commission's **Project Hakea** (June 2024) found that of 3,000+ tracking-device
buyers, **25%** had a DFV history and 37% were known to police.

- **s 13(3)** (*Crimes (DPV) Act 2007*): stalking that a reasonable person would expect to cause
  fear **if the victim knew of it** — intent to cause fear no longer defeated by secrecy.
- **s 8(1)(d)**: directing a third party who then stalks counts as stalking.
- **Surveillance Devices Act s 13A**: advertising a device to promote unlawful use — up to 5 years
  or $11,000; $55,000 for a business. The release says the offence applies even without intent; the
  bill *as introduced* required intent, so check the bill as passed.
- DV offenders barred from security licences.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.4 (domestic violence)** — the paired reform: one closes a gap the courts opened, the
  other follows an agency report on technology-facilitated abuse.
- **chg.3 / inv.5.4 / inv.3.1 / inv.3.2** — Parliament overriding a High Court interpretation of the common
  law: the cleanest current example of statute responding to precedent, with the dissent as the
  values argument.
- **chg.1 / chg.2** — conditions (a publicised acquittal), agencies (Crime Commission, the Women's
  Safety Commissioner), mechanisms (a lapsed private member's bill, then a government bill).
- **crime.1.2 / crime.t.balance** — the element the reform rewrites (trespass) and the critics'
  concern: defence lawyers say separations are rarely clean and an objective covert-stalking test
  may catch private investigators or conduct with no genuine threat; Redfern Legal Centre prefers
  education to new offences.
- **Operation Amarok** — 8,000+ arrests and 17,000 charges (government figure, no period given).

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **government release that names neither bill** and omits critics. The
release overstates two points: the old s 13 never required proof the victim *felt* fear (the
barrier was proving intent), and the advertising offence as introduced required intent. Council
amendments were not checked, no Law Society, Bar, ALS or DV peak-body position was found, and
neither bill is in force. Check the bills as passed and Hansard before quoting sections or penalties.
