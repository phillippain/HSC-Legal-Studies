The **Independent Commission Against Corruption** was the Greiner Government's first legislation
after the corruption scandals of the 1980s. It investigates **corrupt conduct** in the NSW public
sector — not crime in general, and not police (LECC) — and has since become the template for every
other state and, in 2023, the Commonwealth.

## The Act

| Element | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Corrupt conduct** | ss 7–9 — conduct of a public official that involves dishonest or partial exercise of functions, breach of public trust or misuse of information; or conduct of anyone that adversely affects an official's honest exercise of functions — and (s 9) that could be a criminal offence, a disciplinary offence, grounds for dismissal, or (for MPs) a substantial breach of a code of conduct |
| **Jurisdiction** | Ministers, MPs, judges, public servants, local councillors and council staff, public universities, state-owned corporations; and private persons who deal with them |
| **Powers** | Compel witnesses (no privilege against self-incrimination, but evidence given under compulsion cannot be used in a prosecution — s 26); search warrants; telephone interception; **public inquiries** (s 31) where in the public interest |
| **Outcomes** | Findings of corrupt conduct (s 13); recommendations that the DPP consider prosecution (s 74A) — ICAC **cannot charge or punish**; recommendations for systemic change |
| **Oversight** | The Inspector of the ICAC and a parliamentary joint committee; the Commission is now led by a Chief Commissioner and two Commissioners (2017 restructure after Operation Hale) |

**Notable**: Premier Nick Greiner resigned in 1992 after an ICAC finding later overturned by the
Court of Appeal (*Greiner v ICAC*); Barry O'Farrell resigned in 2014 over evidence to Operation
Spicer; Gladys Berejiklian resigned in 2021 on the announcement of Operation Keppel, which found
serious corrupt conduct in 2023 (her challenge failed in the Court of Appeal in 2024). The 2015
High Court decision in *ICAC v Cunneen* narrowed s 8, and Parliament amended it within months.

## National Anti-Corruption Commission Act 2022 (Cth)

The NACC began on 1 July 2023 after a decade of debate, with jurisdiction over Commonwealth
ministers, MPs, staff, public servants, the AFP and contractors. Its hearings are private unless
"exceptional circumstances" justify a public one — the main difference from ICAC, and the main
criticism of it.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.5 / dis.3.6** — the strongest "statutory body" an individual can complain to, and the one that most
  clearly shows the investigate/find/refer limit.
- **inv.1.7 (rule of law)** — ministers and Premiers investigated by a body they created; and the
  counter-example, Parliament rewriting s 8 after *Cunneen*.
- **dis.3.3 / dis.3.2** — public hearings as a non-legal pressure (media, resignations) running
  ahead of any legal outcome.
- **Evaluation** — prosecutions following ICAC findings are slow and often fail because compelled
  evidence is inadmissible: effectiveness depends on what you count.

## Caution

ICAC findings are not convictions; be careful with the word "guilty". Operation names and outcomes
should be checked on the ICAC website. The NACC's first public hearing and its findings are recent
and contested — treat its record as unsettled.
