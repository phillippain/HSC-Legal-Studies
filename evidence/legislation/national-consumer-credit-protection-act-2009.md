Until 2010 consumer credit was state law (the Uniform Consumer Credit Code). After the global
financial crisis the states referred the power and the Commonwealth enacted the **NCCP Act**,
administered by **ASIC**, with the **National Credit Code** as its Schedule 1. Every home loan to an
individual for a residence is a "credit contract" under the Code, and the mortgage securing it is
regulated too.

## Key provisions

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Licensing (Ch 2)** | Lenders and brokers need an Australian credit licence and must be members of the **Australian Financial Complaints Authority** (AFCA) |
| **Responsible lending (Ch 3)** | Before lending, a licensee must make reasonable inquiries into the borrower's requirements and financial situation and verify them, and must not enter a contract that is **unsuitable** — the borrower could not repay without substantial hardship |
| **Hardship (Code s 72)** | A borrower who cannot meet repayments because of illness, unemployment or other reasonable cause may give a **hardship notice**; the lender must respond within 21 days and consider varying the contract (deferral, longer term). Disputes go to AFCA |
| **Default and enforcement (Code ss 88–89)** | Before repossessing or suing, the lender must serve a **default notice** giving at least **30 days** to remedy; the borrower may apply to postpone enforcement |
| **Unjust contracts (Code s 76)** | A court may reopen a contract that was unjust when made |
| **Mortgage enforcement** | Possession is then obtained under the *Real Property Act* and Supreme Court rules; the lender must act in good faith and obtain market value on sale (*Conveyancing Act* s 111A) |

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 (purchase, mortgages)** — the half of the purchase that is a loan; most syllabus notes
  stop at the contract and title.
- **shel.3.1 / shel.3.3** — HILDA's mortgage-stress figures (median capital-city mortgage costs
  $705 a week in 2024) are the demand for the hardship provisions; repossession is a route into
  homelessness the Code is meant to slow.
- **inv.5.1 / inv.5.2 (division of powers)** — a **referral of power** by the states under s 51(xxxvii) of the
  Constitution: how the Commonwealth came to regulate mortgages at all.
- **Evaluation** — responsible lending was weakened in 2020–21 proposals (ultimately not passed)
  and the Banking Royal Commission (2019) found lenders had breached it; AFCA data on hardship
  complaints is the measure.

## Caution

Section numbers refer to the **Code** (Schedule 1), not the Act, except Chapter 3. Business and
investment loans are outside the Code. ASIC and AFCA publish hardship and complaint statistics —
use those for numbers. For NSW enforcement procedure, the *Real Property Act* and the Supreme Court
Possession List are the sources.
