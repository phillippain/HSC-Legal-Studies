Until 1988 a separated parent seeking money for a child had to sue the other parent in court for
**maintenance**, and most orders went unpaid. The Hawke Government's **Child Support Scheme** moved
the whole thing out of the courts: the *Registration and Collection Act 1988* created the Child
Support Agency (now part of Services Australia) to collect, and the *Assessment Act 1989* created
the **formula** that decides how much.

## How the formula works (Part 5)

1. Each parent's **child support income** = adjusted taxable income minus a self-support amount
   (about one-third of average weekly earnings).
2. The incomes are combined; the **costs of the children** are read from a table based on the
   combined income and the children's ages.
3. Each parent's **income percentage** is compared with their **care percentage** (nights per year:
   under 14% care means no cost credit; 14–34% "regular care"; 35–65% "shared care"). The parent
   whose income share exceeds their cost share pays the difference.
4. A **minimum** rate applies even to parents on benefits; a **fixed rate** applies where income is
   understated.

## Changing or challenging an assessment

- **Change of assessment (Part 6A)** — either parent may ask the Registrar to depart from the
  formula on one of ten grounds (special needs, high care costs, the parent's real earning capacity
  being higher than their taxable income).
- **Objection**, then review by the **Administrative Review Tribunal** (since October 2024;
  formerly the AAT) — the *WNZP*, *XTXD* and *Copeland* records are ART decisions on care
  percentage and income.
- **Court** — only on questions of law, or where parents want a binding **child support agreement**
  or an order under the *Family Law Act* for a child over 18.
- **Collection** — private collect, or Child Support collect with deduction from wages and tax
  refunds, departure prohibition orders and (rarely) prosecution.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.5 (obligations of parents)** — the enforceable financial obligation to a child, which
  survives separation and does not depend on seeing the child.
- **fam.2.2 (consequences of separation — children)** — the money half of parenting after
  separation; the three ART records show the formula being argued over.
- **ind.2.6** — administrative review (ART) as the pathway, not the courts: accessibility and the
  cost of appeals.
- **Evaluation** — Services Australia reports around $1.7 billion a year transferred but more than
  $1.5 billion in accumulated debt: enforceable in design, under-enforced in practice.

## Caution

Dollar figures (self-support amount, costs table) are indexed each year — quote the formula, not the
numbers, or take them from the Services Australia website for the current year. The 1988 Act and
1989 Act are usually cited together; the scheme is administered under both. Debt figures come from
the Services Australia annual report.
