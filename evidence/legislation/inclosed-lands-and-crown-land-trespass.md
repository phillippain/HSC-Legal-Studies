Squatting is not an offence in itself in NSW, but getting into the building almost always is. Three
statutes do the work.

## Inclosed Lands Protection Act 1901

- **s 4** — a person who, without lawful excuse, enters **inclosed lands** (any land, public or
  private, fenced, walled or otherwise enclosed — a house, a yard, a building site, a school) without
  the consent of the owner or occupier, or who **remains after being asked to leave**, commits an
  offence: up to **10 penalty units** (currently $1,100), or 50 for "prescribed premises" such as
  schools, hospitals and child-care centres.
- **s 4AA** — the aggravated offence of entering and **interfering with the conduct of business**
  (used against protesters and, since 2016, animal-rights activists on farms).
- A squatter who breaks in may also be charged with **break and enter** (*Crimes Act 1900* s 112)
  or **malicious damage**; an owner may use reasonable force to remove a trespasser, or obtain an
  order for possession from the Supreme Court.

## Crown Land Management Act 2016 and the Local Government Act 1993

- The CLM Act makes it an offence to **camp, erect a structure or remain overnight** on Crown land
  (including Crown reserves and most beaches) contrary to a reserve's rules or a direction from an
  authorised officer, and lets officers remove structures and property.
- Councils manage most parks under the *Local Government Act*; **s 632** makes it an offence to
  breach a notice erected by council ("no camping", "no overnight stay") — the usual legal basis for
  moving rough sleepers from council land, alongside LEPRA move-on directions.
- The *Sydney Public Reserves (Public Safety) Act 2017* adds a specific removal power for CBD
  reserves.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (squats)** — why squatting is precarious: trespass is an offence from day one, and
  adverse possession needs 12 years of it (the *Rogers* family's half-century at Gymea Bay was never
  challenged as trespass).
- **shel.3.3 (homelessness)** — the three-layer enforcement toolkit (trespass, camping offences,
  move-on) that the *Protocol for Homeless People in Public Places* is meant to soften.
- **shel.1.4** — the legal nature of shelter as **exclusion**: ownership is largely the right to
  keep others out, and these Acts are how the state enforces that right.
- **exp.5** — property rights v the need for shelter, in the plainest form.

## Caution

Penalty-unit amounts change (a NSW penalty unit is $110). Section numbers in the CLM Act are
indicative — cite the Act and the conduct. Police rarely charge rough sleepers under these Acts;
the practical instrument is the move-on direction, so use this record for the *legal basis*, not
for enforcement statistics.
