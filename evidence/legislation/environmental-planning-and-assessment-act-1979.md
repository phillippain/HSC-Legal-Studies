The EP&A Act is NSW's planning statute. It matters to Shelter because every home needs
**development consent** (or must fit a code), and consent depends on what the land is **zoned** for.

## How it works

- **Environmental planning instruments** — councils make **Local Environmental Plans** (zoning,
  height, floor-space ratio); the Minister makes **State Environmental Planning Policies** that
  override them. The *SEPP (Housing) 2021* covers affordable housing, boarding houses, build-to-rent,
  seniors housing and secondary dwellings.
- **Development assessment (Part 4)** — a consent authority (usually the council) must weigh the
  matters in **s 4.15**: the planning instruments, the likely impacts, the suitability of the site,
  submissions, and the public interest. **Complying development** (Div 4.5) is fast-tracked
  certification against a code (the Codes SEPP), with no merit assessment.
- **State significant development** — large projects assessed by the Department or Independent
  Planning Commission. From 2025 the **Housing Delivery Authority** pathway sends major housing
  proposals (over $60 million in Sydney) straight to state assessment.
- **Appeals** — applicants (and, for designated development, objectors) can appeal to the **Land and
  Environment Court** (Class 1); third parties can seek judicial review (Class 4). The Mosman
  challenge to the low- and mid-rise reforms (SMH, August 2025) is a Class 4 case.

## The 2024–25 housing reforms

| Reform | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Transport Oriented Development** SEPP (2024) | Six-storey residential flat buildings permitted within 400 m of 37 stations; eight "accelerated precincts" |
| **Low and Mid-Rise Housing** policy (Dec 2024, Feb 2025) | Dual occupancies and terraces permitted on all low-density residential land in Sydney, Newcastle, Wollongong, the Central Coast and Illawarra; 3–6 storeys within 800 m of town centres and stations |
| **Affordable housing bonus** (SEPP Housing) | 20–30% extra height and floor space for projects with 15% affordable housing for 15 years |

Grattan's verdict (November 2025): these "don't go far enough"; a three-storey townhouse standard on
all residential land would.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — the *supply-side* legal response: upzoning as law reform; set
  beside tax changes (demand side) and the HAFF (direct provision).
- **shel.2.8** — councils, the Department of Planning, the Housing Delivery Authority and the Land
  and Environment Court as the agencies that decide whether homes get built.
- **shel.2.4** — boarding houses and seniors housing are permitted through the Housing SEPP.
- **exp.5 (balancing interests)** — existing residents, developers, councils and the state: the
  Mosman case and the TOD precinct disputes are the live examples.

## Caution

The Act was **renumbered in 2018** (s 79C became s 4.15); older textbooks use the old numbers. The
housing reforms are made by SEPP and ministerial order, so they change often — check the Planning
Portal for the current version. Grattan and the Productivity Commission are advocates of one view;
councils and heritage bodies argue the opposite.
