Most crimes need *mens rea* — a guilty mind. **Strict liability** offences do not: the prosecution
proves the *actus reus* and the defendant is guilty unless they prove an **honest and reasonable
mistake of fact** (*Proudman v Dayman*, 1941; *He Kaw Teh*, 1985 on when Parliament intends
strict liability). **Absolute liability** removes even that defence. Road law is where students meet
both.

## The offences

| Offence | Provision | Liability | What the prosecution proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Speeding** | Road Rules 2014 r 20 | Strict (effectively absolute for camera offences) | The speed, by camera or radar; "I didn't see the sign" rarely succeeds |
| **Drink-driving (PCA)** | *Road Transport Act* s 110 | Strict | Driving with the prescribed concentration of alcohol (0.05; 0.02 special; 0.00 learner/P) — not intoxication, the number; mistake about the reading is no defence, mistake about having drunk alcohol can be |
| **Drug presence** | s 111 | Strict | A detectable illicit drug in saliva or blood, regardless of impairment |
| **Mobile phone use** | Road Rules r 300; camera enforcement under the *Road Transport Act* Sch 3 | Strict | Holding or using a phone while driving — the 2019 detection cameras record on this site |
| **Unregistered / uninsured vehicle** | ss 68, 2A | Strict | Driving it; "I thought it was registered" is the honest-and-reasonable-mistake argument |
| **Negligent / dangerous driving** | s 117 | **Fault required** | Negligence or danger — the contrast case |
| **Dangerous driving occasioning death** | *Crimes Act* s 52A | Fault required | Up to 10 years (14 aggravated) |

## Why Parliament uses strict liability here

- **Regulatory offences** of low moral stigma and high volume: around 4 million penalty notices a
  year in NSW; proving intent in each would be impossible.
- **Deterrence by certainty**: the point of a 0.05 limit is that it is a number.
- **Enforcement by machine**: speed, red-light, phone and seatbelt cameras (and from 2024 average
  speed cameras for all vehicles) — the *Road Transport Act* makes the registered owner liable
  unless they nominate the driver.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.3 (strict liability)** — the definition, the defence and the absolute-liability
  variant, with offences students have actually encountered.
- **crime.1.2 (elements)** — the exception that explains the rule: what *mens rea* is for, seen by
  its absence.
- **crime.2.1 / crime.2.6** — breath testing (random breath testing since 1982, *Road Transport
  Act* Sch 3) as a police power without suspicion; penalty notices as summary justice without a
  court.
- **crime.4.2 (sentencing)** — immediate licence suspension, mandatory interlocks and
  demerit points as penalties outside the court.
- **lip.2.3** — BOCSAR and Transport for NSW road-toll data as the effectiveness measure (RBT cut
  alcohol-related fatalities by about a third in its first decade).

## Caution

Section numbers in the *Road Transport Act* and Road Rules are correct at the time of writing but
the Act is amended most years (the 2026 Greta bus-crash reforms touched heavy-vehicle and seatbelt
rules). Whether a given offence is strict or absolute is a matter of statutory interpretation —
cite the case (*He Kaw Teh*) for the test rather than asserting it.
