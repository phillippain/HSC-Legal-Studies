Child protection is state law, and this Act is NSW's. It gives the Department of Communities and
Justice (DCJ) the power to investigate and remove children, the **Children's Court** the power to
make care orders, and sets the principles that are supposed to govern both.

## Principles (Chapter 2)

- **s 9(1)** — the **safety, welfare and wellbeing** of the child is paramount (the child
  protection version of "best interests").
- **Least intrusive intervention**; the child's views to be taken into account; continuity of
  relationships; timely decisions.
- **ss 11–13** — **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander principles**: self-determination,
  participation, and the **placement principle** (with extended family, then community, then other
  Aboriginal people, then non-Aboriginal carers who will maintain culture).

## The process

| Stage | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory reporting** | s 27 — teachers, doctors, nurses, police, childcare and welfare workers must report a reasonable suspicion that a child is at **risk of significant harm** (ROSH) to the Child Protection Helpline; s 23 defines ROSH (basic needs, abuse, domestic violence, neglect) |
| **Assessment and support** | DCJ assesses; most reports lead to no action or a referral; **parent responsibility contracts** and **temporary care arrangements** are the voluntary options |
| **Emergency removal** | ss 43–44 — DCJ or police may remove a child at immediate risk without a warrant; a care application must be filed within three working days |
| **Care orders** | Chapter 5 — the Children's Court must find the child is "in need of care and protection" (s 71) and may order supervision, parental responsibility to the Minister or another person, or contact; the Court is closed, less formal and children may be separately represented |
| **Permanency planning** | s 83 (2018 amendments) — a permanency plan within **6 months** for a child under 2, **12 months** otherwise, in order of preference: restoration, guardianship, adoption, parental responsibility to the Minister |
| **Out-of-home care** | Chapter 8 — DCJ and accredited NGOs (Barnardos, Life Without Barriers, Aboriginal agencies) provide foster, kinship and residential care; about 14,000 children in NSW, 40%+ Aboriginal |
| **Alternative dispute resolution** | s 37 — "dispute resolution conferences" before contested hearings |

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4** — the statute itself, plus the two sides of its evaluation: the 2018 Family is
  Culture review (Aboriginal over-representation; the NIT 2024 record) and the SMH 2023 and 2026
  records on departmental failures and foster-care costs.
- **fam.1.5 / fam.3.2** — parental responsibility is conditional: the state can remove it by
  court order.
- **fam.2.4** — exposure to domestic violence is a ROSH ground (s 23); the Act and the ADVO system
  intersect.
- **inv.6.3 / inv.7.2** — CROC arts 3, 9, 12 and 20 are visible in ss 9–13.
- **exp.4** — children in care, and Aboriginal children in particular, as a group suffering
  disadvantage.

## Caution

Care numbers and the Aboriginal proportion come from the DCJ and AIHW annual reports — quote the
current year. The Act is long and frequently amended (2014, 2018, 2022); section numbers for
principles are stable, process sections less so. The Children's Court also hears criminal matters
under a different Act (the *Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987*).
