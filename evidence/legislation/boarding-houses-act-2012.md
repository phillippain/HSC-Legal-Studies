Boarders and lodgers — people who rent a room, usually with shared facilities and without exclusive
possession — are **excluded from the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010*** (s 8). Until 2012 they had
only the common law of licences, which meant they could be put out with "reasonable notice" and had
no tribunal to go to. The *Boarding Houses Act 2012* was the response, after years of advocacy by
the Tenants' Union, Shelter NSW and disability groups following deaths in unregistered homes.

## What the Act does

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Part 2 — Registration** | Every boarding house with 5+ paying residents must be registered with the local council; councils must inspect within 12 months and may enter and inspect. Registration is public. |
| **Part 3 — Occupancy principles** (Schedule 1) | Residents of **general boarding houses** are entitled to: a written occupancy agreement; premises that are reasonably clean, secure and in good repair; quiet enjoyment; notice before entry; a receipt for payments; a maximum of two weeks' money in advance; reasonable notice before an increase in fees or eviction; a dispute-resolution process. |
| **Part 3 Div 3 — NCAT** | A resident or proprietor may apply to NCAT for orders about breaches of the principles, including compensation. |
| **Part 4 — Assisted boarding houses** | Houses accommodating people with "additional needs" (disability, mental illness, age) must be **authorised** by the Department of Communities and Justice, meet staffing and care standards, and are subject to compliance notices and closure. |

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (boarding houses)** — the statute itself, and the gap it was built over: boarders are
  outside tenancy law by definition.
- **shel.3.2 / shel.3.3** — boarding houses shelter people who cannot get or keep a tenancy — older
  single men, people with mental illness, newly arrived migrants, students; the assisted boarding
  house provisions exist because of their vulnerability.
- **shel.2.3** — "reasonable notice" is still the only eviction protection; Selwyn Street,
  Paddington (28 boarders v four luxury apartments, 2025) shows what that means when a developer
  buys the building.
- **Evaluation** — rights on paper, enforcement by councils with few inspectors; the statutory
  review (2019–21) found compliance patchy and the stock shrinking as houses are sold.

## Caution

The occupancy principles are **far thinner than tenancy rights**: no bond scheme, no minimum
notice period in days, no restriction on grounds for eviction. "Boarding house" in planning law
(Housing SEPP, "co-living") is a different definition from this Act's. Section numbers should be
checked; cite the Part and the principle.
