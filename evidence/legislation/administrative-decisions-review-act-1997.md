The NSW counterpart to the Commonwealth's ART and ADJR Acts. Originally the *Administrative
Decisions Tribunal Act 1997*, it was renamed when the ADT was folded into **NCAT** in 2014; the
tribunal provisions moved to the *NCAT Act*, and this Act now supplies the **review rights and
procedure** for "administratively reviewable decisions".

## Merits review in NCAT

| Step | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Which decisions** | Only where an Act says a decision is reviewable by NCAT — about 60 Acts: firearms and security licences, working-with-children checks, FOI (GIPA) decisions, victims' support, guardianship-related and some Homes NSW decisions; **not** general policy or most planning decisions |
| **Reasons** | s 49 — a person may request a written statement of reasons within 28 days; the agency must give findings of fact, evidence and reasoning |
| **Internal review** | s 53 — a person must usually apply for **internal review** by the agency (a different, more senior officer) within 28 days before going to NCAT; the agency has 21 days |
| **NCAT review** | s 63 — NCAT decides the "correct and preferable" decision on the material before it, and may affirm, vary, set aside and substitute, or remit; the **Administrative and Equal Opportunity Division** |
| **Appeal** | To an NCAT Appeal Panel, then to the Supreme Court on a question of law |

Homes NSW tenancy and eligibility decisions mostly go to the **Housing Appeals Committee** (policy,
not this Act) and then NCAT under the *Residential Tenancies Act*; the shelter dispute-resolution map
on the site shows the routes.

## Judicial review in the Supreme Court

- **Supreme Court Act 1970 s 69** — the Court's supervisory jurisdiction to grant relief "in the
  nature of" prohibition, mandamus and certiorari against any NSW public body, including for error
  of law on the face of the record. NSW has **no ADJR-style statute**; judicial review is common
  law, in the Court's Administrative Law List.
- **Grounds** — jurisdictional error, denial of procedural fairness (*Kioa v West*), unreasonableness,
  failure to consider or considering irrelevant matters; the **Land and Environment Court** (Class 4)
  for planning decisions (*Goldman v Inner West Council*); the Court of Appeal for ICAC findings.
- **Privative clauses** cannot exclude review for jurisdictional error (*Kirk v Industrial Court*,
  2010) — the state equivalent of s 75(v).

## Use it for

- **ind.2.5 (internal review)** — the only NSW statute that makes internal review a defined step
  with timeframes.
- **ind.2.6 (external review)** — merits (NCAT) v judicial (Supreme Court), with the *HGY* and
  *Goldman* records as current NSW examples.
- **ls.2.3 / ls.2.7** — NCAT's limits as a non-court (it cannot decide matters between residents
  of different states: *Burns v Corbett*); the Supreme Court's inherent supervisory role.
- **ind.3.3** — the gap: most NSW decisions are reviewable only if an Act says so.

## Caution

Timeframes (28/21 days) are from the Act but vary under specific enabling Acts. GIPA review is the
most common use and has its own Act. NCAT's Administrative and Equal Opportunity Division annual
figures are in the NCAT annual report (on this site for 2024–25). The Act's 2014 renaming confuses
older textbooks that cite the "ADT".
