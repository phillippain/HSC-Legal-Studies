Strata title — ownership of a **lot** (the apartment) plus a share of the **common property** — was
invented in NSW in 1961. Two Acts of 2015 replaced the 1970s legislation from 30 November 2016: the
*Strata Schemes Management Act* (how a scheme is run: owners corporation, by-laws, levies, disputes)
and this one, the *Strata Schemes Development Act* (how a scheme is **created, varied and
terminated**).

## What it covers

- **Creation** — registration of a strata plan under the *Real Property Act*; lots and common
  property; staged and part-strata developments; the role of the Registrar-General.
- **Variation** — subdivision and consolidation of lots, conversion of common property.
- **Termination** — by unanimous agreement or Supreme Court order; and, since 2016, by **strata
  renewal**.

## Part 10 — collective sale and redevelopment

Before 2016 a scheme could be wound up only with the consent of **every** owner, so a single
holdout could block the sale of an ageing building. Part 10 allows:

1. A **strata renewal proposal** considered by a committee of owners, then a **plan** (sale or
   redevelopment) put to a general meeting.
2. Support from owners of at least **75% of lots** (by number, excluding utility lots).
3. An application to the **Land and Environment Court**, which must be satisfied the plan is **just
   and equitable** — including that each dissenting owner receives at least **compensation value**
   under the *Land Acquisition (Just Terms Compensation) Act 1991* plus reasonable costs.
4. The Court's order binds the dissenting owners; the scheme is terminated and the land sold or
   redeveloped.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 (strata)** — the half of strata law students usually miss; the Management Act is about
  living there, this Act is about whether the building survives.
- **shel.1.4 (legal nature of shelter)** — the sharpest NSW example of ownership being
  *conditional*: a 25% minority can lose their homes to a majority vote plus a court order.
- **lip.2.1 / shel.3.1** — balancing older owners who want to stay against renewal of unsafe or
  uneconomic buildings, and the supply argument that renewal adds homes.
- **shel.2.6** — the Land and Environment Court, not NCAT, has jurisdiction over renewal.

## Caution

Few renewal orders have actually been made — the process is slow and expensive — so cite it as a
**power**, not a trend. The threshold and compensation rules should be checked against the current
Act and the 2023–25 strata reform tranches (the Miscellaneous Bill 2025 record covers the latest).
