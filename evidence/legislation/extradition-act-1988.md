## The process

1. A request from an **extradition country**.
2. A **magistrate** decides whether the person is eligible.
3. The **Attorney-General** decides whether to surrender.
4. **Judicial review** of each step is possible.

## Safeguards

- **Dual criminality**: the conduct must be an offence in both countries.
- No surrender for a **political offence**, or where the person would be persecuted.
- No surrender to face the **death penalty** without an undertaking that it will not be imposed.

## Two cases

- **Malka Leifer**: extradited from **Israel** in 2021 after a six-year fight, convicted of child sexual
  abuse in Melbourne in 2023. Cooperation worked, slowly.
- **Daniel Duggan**: a former US Marine pilot arrested in NSW in **2022** at the US's request, accused of
  breaching US arms export laws by training Chinese pilots (2010–12). The Attorney-General approved
  extradition in **December 2024**; on **16 April 2026** the Federal Court (Stellios J) dismissed his
  challenge, which argued the conduct was not an offence in Australia at the time.

The *Mutual Assistance* Act lets Australia request and provide **evidence**, not people.

## Use it for

- **Balance of rights**: the accused is protected, which is why it is slow.
- Contrast with **deportation** used as a shortcut (Zakaria).
