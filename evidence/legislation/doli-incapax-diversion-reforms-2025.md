## The review

The Hon Geoffrey Bellew SC and Jeffrey Loy APM were appointed in May 2025 to review doli incapax for
children under 14. Their report was released in October 2025 (LSJ, 27 Oct 2025): codify the
principles consistently with *RP v The Queen*, give police more training, remove barriers to
diversion (a child 'not denying' rather than admitting the offence), and create a new intervention
pathway for children under 14.

## Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987, from 1 March 2026

- **s 5**: under 10, conclusively incapable; 10–13, presumed incapable unless the prosecution proves
  beyond reasonable doubt the child knew the conduct was seriously wrong. The court must consider the
  conduct, the circumstances, the child's intellectual and moral development and education, and the
  environment in which the child was raised. Knowledge must not be inferred merely from the conduct,
  but rebuttal is open on the facts of the offending alone.
- **s 5(8)**: cautions, conferences and admissions made to access diversion cannot be used to rebut
  the presumption.

## Young Offenders Act 1997, from 1 July 2026

- **s 10**: a **non-denial statement** replaces the admission needed for a caution or conference; it
  must be made with a responsible person, approved adult or lawyer present.
- **s 67A**: officials may offer the chance to make one; it is inadmissible in later proceedings.
- **s 20(7)**: no caution for a child cautioned three or more times in the previous three years.
- Conferences: cultural needs considered (s 34(3)(f)); 42 days, not 28 (s 43); safety and welfare
  checks (ss 43A, 44); no outcome if the child denies the offence (s 48(4)).
- Warnings now available for graffiti and some larceny offences.

## What came next (September 2026)

An **$8.4 million voluntary diversion pilot in Tamworth** for children aged 8–13 with complex needs,
run with Aboriginal community-controlled organisations from early 2027 (responding to the review's
recommendation 6), and consultation on a mandatory diversion pathway (model by the end of 2026).

## Use it for

- **Age** (`crime.5.1`) and **alternatives to court** (`crime.5.5`): two rules redesigned to work
  together, so that diversion no longer forces a child to confess to something the law presumes they
  cannot do.

## Caution

Year 11 notes written before July 2026 give 1 March 2026 for every change; the Young Offenders Act
changes began on 1 July 2026. Case law on the new s 5 is still developing.
