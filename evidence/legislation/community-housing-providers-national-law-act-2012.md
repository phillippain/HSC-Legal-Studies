"Social housing" is two things: **public housing** owned by the state (Homes NSW), and **community
housing** owned or managed by registered not-for-profit providers — Bridge Housing, Mission
Australia Housing, Link Wentworth, Evolve, and Aboriginal providers among about 130 in NSW. Since
2010 NSW policy has shifted stock and new supply toward community housing, which can borrow, claim
Commonwealth Rent Assistance for tenants, and partner with developers. This Act is the law that
regulates them.

## The National Law

- NSW is the **host jurisdiction**: the National Law is Schedule 1 to this Act and is adopted by
  the other participating states and territories (Victoria and WA run their own systems).
- **Registration** in three **tiers** by size and risk — Tier 1 (large developers and managers),
  Tier 2 (medium), Tier 3 (small, management only).
- **Performance outcomes** — tenant and housing services, housing assets, community engagement,
  governance, probity, management and financial viability; providers report annually and are
  assessed.
- **The Registrar** — in NSW the Registrar of Community Housing (within the Department of
  Communities and Justice) can issue notices of non-compliance, appoint an administrator and
  **cancel registration**; on cancellation, housing funded with public money is transferred to
  another provider rather than lost.
- **Tenancies** — community housing tenancies are "social housing tenancy agreements" under the
  *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* Part 7, with the same eviction grounds and NCAT process as public
  housing, plus rent set at a proportion of income.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.4 (social housing)** — the half of the system that is growing; the HAFF funds only
  registered providers, so this registration is the gateway to Commonwealth money.
- **shel.2.9 (NGOs)** — housing NGOs as *regulated providers*, not just advocates: the Act is the
  accountability side of outsourcing.
- **shel.2.8 / ls.2.9** — a national scheme hosted by NSW: cooperative federalism without a
  Commonwealth Act.
- **Evaluation** — the "transfer" of 14,000 public tenancies to providers in 2018–19 was justified
  by better management and Rent Assistance; tenant advocates point to the loss of a direct
  government landlord.

## Caution

Provider numbers and the public/community split change — take current figures from the Registrar's
annual report and the DCJ data. The National Law regulates providers, not tenants' rights, which
remain in the tenancy Act. Aboriginal community housing providers can register under this law or
under the AHO's own standards.
