Money laundering turns crime into usable wealth, so the AML/CTF regime targets the money rather than
the offender. **AUSTRAC** regulates reporting businesses and turns their reports into financial
intelligence for police.

## Enforcement before 2026

| Entity | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Westpac | **$1.3 billion** (2020), a record |
| Commonwealth Bank | $700 million (2018) |
| Crown Resorts | **$450 million** (2023) |

## Tranche 2

Australia was one of a handful of countries that did not regulate **'gatekeeper' professions**. The
2024 amendments brought about **90,000 businesses** in from **1 July 2026**: lawyers, accountants, real
estate agents, trust and company service providers, and dealers in precious metals and stones. They
must verify customers, run an AML/CTF program, and report **suspicious matters** and cash transactions of
**$10,000 or more**.

## Use it for

- **Responsiveness**: a law reform driven by international standards (FATF).
- **Prevention** rather than punishment: making crime less profitable.
- **Cost**: compliance falls on small businesses, a resource-efficiency argument.
