The **Working With Children Check**. Its object is to protect children by **not permitting certain
persons** to work with children and by **requiring everyone else who does** to hold a clearance
(s 3). Section 4 states the paramount consideration: the safety, welfare and well-being of children,
and in particular protecting them from child abuse.

## How it works

- **Child-related work** (s 6) means work involving **direct contact** with children that is a usual
  part of the work and more than incidental to it. Two classes of clearance: **volunteer** (unpaid
  work only) and **non-volunteer** (s 12).
- A **risk assessment** is triggered by the matters in Schedule 1 — relevant charges or convictions,
  findings of misconduct involving children, reportable conduct notifications, a negative notice
  elsewhere (s 14).
- The Children's Guardian **must** grant a clearance to a person assessed **unless** satisfied they
  pose a risk to the safety of children (s 18(2)). "Risk to the safety of children" means a **real
  and appreciable** risk (s 5B).
- A **disqualified person** — convicted as an adult of a Schedule 2 offence — cannot hold one
  (s 18(1)(a)); and a "conviction" includes a **finding of guilt with no conviction recorded**
  (s 5(1)).
- Refused applicants generally cannot reapply for **five years** (s 13A).

## What the 2025 amendments changed

The *Child Protection (Working with Children) and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2025*, assented
**23 September 2025**:

- **Abolished external review.** NCAT no longer reviews a refusal of a WWCC or NDIS worker check.
  Applicants now seek **internal review by the Children's Guardian** instead.
- **Abolished s 28 enabling orders.** A disqualified person can no longer ask the Tribunal to declare
  that they are not disqualified; they apply to the Children's Guardian for a **disqualification
  reassessment**.
- **Mutual recognition of negative notices** (from 1 December 2025): barred in another Australian
  jurisdiction means no NSW clearance.
- Clearance required **before starting work**; peak and professional bodies may require one as a
  condition of membership; a prohibition notice under the education and care services national law
  now triggers a risk assessment; the Children's Guardian may seek **expert advice** on reviews and
  reassessments.
- The Minister must **review the amendments** about 18 months after commencement, reporting within
  30 months.

Transitional provisions preserved the old pathway for applications made before **5 August 2025** —
which is the only reason *HCV* and *GTA* were decided at all.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the half of the dot point students miss. Care and protection is
  not only the *Care Act*, removal and the Children's Court; it is also a **preventive screening
  regime** covering every teacher, coach, carer and volunteer in the state.
- **Law reform, with a date (fam.t.reform / chg.6)** — a 2025 statute that **removed a right of
  external review**. That is a rare and powerful example: most reform narratives are about rights
  expanding. Argue it either way: faster and more consistent child protection, or the loss of an
  independent check on an administrative decision.
- **Rights in tension (fam.t.values)** — protection of children against the individual's right to
  work and to procedural fairness. The Act answers that tension explicitly in s 4, and *CXZ* says the
  legislature "preferred the risk of injustice to an applicant to risk to the safety of children".
- **Enforceability** — a clearance is a precondition to the work itself, backed by s 8. Compare it
  with schemes that rely on reporting after the fact.
- **Accessibility (inv.1.4)** — internal review by the same agency that refused you is cheaper and
  faster than a tribunal, and less independent. Exactly the kind of trade-off an evaluation question
  wants.
- **Federal/state patchwork (inv.6)** — mutual recognition of negative notices is a
  Standing Council of Attorneys-General commitment being implemented state by state.

## Caution

The Act has been **amended repeatedly** — check the current consolidation on the NSW legislation site
before quoting a section number, and note that the sections discussed in *HCV* and *GTA* are the
**pre-23 September 2025** versions. The internal review and reassessment pathway was still bedding
in through 2026, and the **training requirement has commenced but no training is yet prescribed**, so
do not say that training is required. The statutory review was not complete at the time of writing,
so there is no official evaluation of the changes yet. This is a **NSW** scheme; other states have
their own, which is the problem mutual recognition addresses.
