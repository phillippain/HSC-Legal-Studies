- **July 2012:** Thomas Kelly, 18, was punched once in Kings Cross and died. **Kieran Loveridge** was
  sentenced in 2013 to **7 years 2 months** in total for manslaughter and other assaults that night.
- **2014:** on a Crown appeal the Court of Criminal Appeal raised the total to **13 years 8 months**
  (*R v Loveridge* [2014] NSWCCA 120). It also said it is not meaningful to treat one-punch
  manslaughters as 'a single class of offences'. Loveridge was **paroled in April 2024**.
- **s 25A** (Crimes and Other Legislation Amendment (Assault and Intoxication) Act 2014): assault
  causing death, maximum **20 years**; if the offender (18 or over) was intoxicated, **25 years with a
  mandatory minimum of 8 years**. Passed in the same package as Sydney's lockout laws, after the
  deaths of Thomas Kelly and Daniel Christie.
- **s 19B** (2011): **mandatory life imprisonment** for murdering a police officer acting in the
  execution of duty.

## Use it for

- **Statutory guidelines and judicial discretion** (`crime.4.1`) — the clearest NSW example of
  Parliament removing discretion; compare *Muldrock* and the SNPP scheme, which keep it.
- **Crown appeals** (`crime.4.5`) — *Loveridge* shows an appeal correcting a sentence the community
  saw as manifestly inadequate.
- **Purposes** (`crime.4.2`) — mandatory minimums rely on general deterrence and denunciation, though
  impulsive, alcohol-fuelled assaults are poorly deterred (The Conversation, 2014: 'Mandatory
  sentences can't deliver justice or stop one-punch killings').
- **Charge choice** (`crime.3.4`) — the prosecutor decides between s 25A and manslaughter, so
  discretion moves rather than disappears.

## Caution

Few people have been charged under s 25A; most one-punch deaths are still prosecuted as
manslaughter. Do not say the mandatory minimum applies to manslaughter, or to offenders under 18.
