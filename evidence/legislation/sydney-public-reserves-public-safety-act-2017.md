In mid-2017 a camp of rough sleepers grew in **Martin Place**, metres from the NSW Parliament and
the Reserve Bank. The City of Sydney said it had no power to remove the camp and would not; the
Premier (Gladys Berejiklian) said the state would. The Act was introduced and passed within days,
with Labor's support, and the camp dispersed before it commenced.

## What it does

- Applies to **Crown public reserves in the Sydney CBD** and other public reserves the regulations
  prescribe.
- Empowers an **authorised officer** (Crown Lands) or a **police officer** to direct a person to
  **leave** and to **remove property** where the person is unlawfully occupying the reserve, or
  their occupation or conduct is affecting public safety, security or amenity, or obstructing the
  reserve's use.
- Property removed may be impounded; failure to comply with a direction is an **offence**.
- The Act contains no obligation to offer accommodation before giving a direction; the Government
  relied on the *NSW Protocol for Homeless People in Public Places* and outreach by FACS (now Homes
  NSW) to do that.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.3 (homelessness)** — the clearest NSW example of law made *about* homeless people
  rather than *for* them: speed, scope and silence on housing all tell.
- **shel.2.8 / lepra-2002** — alongside LEPRA move-on directions (Part 14) and the *Inclosed Lands
  Protection Act*, one of three enforcement powers used against rough sleepers; the Pari case shows
  the daily version.
- **chg.3 / exp.5** — Parliament responding to media pressure in a week; the City of Sydney,
  homelessness NGOs and the Government in open conflict over who should act.
- **Evaluation** — enforceable, immediate, and ineffective by its own measure: rough sleeping in the
  CBD rose again within two years (City of Sydney street counts).

## Caution

The Act is short and rarely used by name since 2017; its significance is as **precedent** for the
public-order approach. Check the current list of reserves in the Regulation. The City of Sydney's
street count numbers are the right source for before-and-after figures; this record does not supply
them.
