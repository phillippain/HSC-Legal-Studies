Saxon Mullins' complaint against Luke Lazarus went through two trials and two appeals (2015–2017) and ended with no conviction: the Court of Criminal Appeal accepted a finding that Lazarus had an honest belief in consent even though Mullins had not consented. Her 2018 *Four Corners* interview led the Attorney-General to refer consent law to the NSW Law Reform Commission, whose Report 148 (2020) recommended an affirmative model.

## The reform

- **Consent** is free and voluntary agreement that can be withdrawn; a person who does not say or do anything to communicate consent does not consent (s 61HI).
- **Knowledge**: an accused's belief in consent is not reasonable if they did not, within a reasonable time, *say or do anything* to find out whether the other person consented (s 61HK) — the "affirmative consent" rule.
- Jury directions on misconceptions (delay in complaint, clothing, prior relationship) were added, and the reforms are under statutory review.

## Use it for

- **chg.1.5 / chg.1.1** — amending a law that had failed to reflect community values about consent; the full reform chain from case (driver) → media → law reform commission (formal agency) → legislation (process).
- **dis.1.2 / dis.1.4** — the elements of a crime and the burden of proof: the reform changed what the prosecution must prove about the accused's state of mind.

## Caution

The Act changed the law prospectively; it did not and could not alter the *Lazarus* result. Evidence on whether conviction rates have changed is still emerging — treat claims either way as contested.
