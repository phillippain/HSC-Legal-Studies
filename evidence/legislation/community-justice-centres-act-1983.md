**Community Justice Centres (CJC)** began as a 1980 pilot and were put on a statutory footing in
1983 — one of the first mediation statutes anywhere. For four decades they provided **free
mediation** by trained community mediators, in person, by phone or online, anywhere in NSW, within
what is now the Department of Communities and Justice.

## What the Act provided

- **Services** — mediation of any dispute the Director accepted: most commonly **neighbour**
  disputes (fences, trees, noise), then family, workplace and housing disputes between
  **co-tenants, flatmates, strata neighbours and landlords and tenants**.
- **Voluntary** — no one could be compelled to attend, and an agreement was not itself enforceable
  unless made a contract or adopted by NCAT or a court.
- **Confidential and privileged** — what was said in mediation could not be used in later
  proceedings; mediators could not be called as witnesses.
- **Referrals** — courts and NCAT could refer matters to a CJC; many Local Court neighbour matters
  were mediated first.

## The closure

In January 2025 the NSW Government announced CJCs would close; the service ended on **30 June
2025**. In 2022–23 it had resolved **79%** of the neighbourhood disputes it mediated. Local
Government NSW opposed the closure, warning that residents would be pushed into formal proceedings
they could not afford and that court workloads would rise. Mediation now sits with NCAT's own
conciliation, NSW Fair Trading's tenancy mediation, court-annexed mediation under the *Civil
Procedure Act 2005*, and fee-charging private mediators.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.5 / ind.2.2 (ADR)** — the clearest current example that ADR in NSW is *contracting*:
  a free service with a four-in-five settlement rate closed while NCAT's Consumer and Commercial
  Division takes 50,000-plus applications a year.
- **ls.1.6 / lip.2.2** — what mediation offers (cheap, fast, preserves relationships) and what it
  cannot (binding orders, precedent).
- **Evaluation — resource efficiency v accessibility** — the saving in one budget becomes cost in
  another, borne by the people least able to pay for a private mediator or a lawyer.
- **ls.4.1** — reform in reverse: a service removed without a law reform process.

## Caution

The record of the closure on this site is a Local Government NSW media release; take the
Government's stated reasons and any replacement arrangements from its own announcements. Whether
the Act has been formally repealed or merely left without a service should be checked on
legislation.nsw.gov.au before describing it either way. Settlement figures are for 2022–23.
