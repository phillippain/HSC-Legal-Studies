**Community Justice Centres (CJC)** began as a 1980 pilot and were put on a statutory footing in
1983 — one of the first mediation statutes anywhere. They now sit within the Department of
Communities and Justice and provide **free mediation** by trained, accredited community mediators,
in person, by phone or online, anywhere in NSW.

## What the Act provides

- **Services** — mediation and conflict management for any dispute the Director accepts; the most
  common are **neighbour** disputes (fences, trees, noise), **family** and **workplace** matters, and
  housing disputes between **co-tenants, flatmates, strata neighbours and landlords and tenants**.
- **Voluntary** — no one can be compelled to attend, and any agreement reached is not itself
  enforceable unless the parties make it a contract or NCAT/a court adopts it.
- **Confidential and privileged (ss 28–29)** — what is said in mediation cannot be used in later
  proceedings, and mediators cannot be called as witnesses.
- **Referrals** — courts and NCAT may refer matters to a CJC; many Local Court neighbour matters and
  some NCAT tenancy matters are mediated first.
- **Immunity and standards** — mediators are protected from liability; the Director sets
  accreditation.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.5 (dispute resolution)** — the non-legal (or "less legal") end of the ladder: Fair Trading
  complaint → CJC mediation → NCAT → court. Students can describe each rung.
- **shel.2.2 / shel.2.4** — co-tenant and strata disputes are where mediation is used most; the
  group-renting record shows the kind of flatmate conflict that ends up here.
- **ls.1.6 / lip.2.2** — mediation as alternative dispute resolution: cheap, fast, preserves
  relationships, but produces no precedent and depends on both sides turning up.
- **Evaluation** — CJC reports settlement rates around 80% of mediations held, but no data on the
  disputes that never come.

## Caution

Outcomes are **not binding** and there is no appeal — mediation suits disputes between people who
must keep living near each other, not a tenant facing eviction next week. Settlement figures
should be taken from the DCJ annual report. Tenancy mediation is also offered by NSW Fair Trading
under the *Residential Tenancies Act*; the two services overlap.
