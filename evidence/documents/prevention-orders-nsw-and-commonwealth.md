Preventive orders are the part of the criminal justice system that acts **before** an offence, or
**after** a sentence has been served. This document maps them, which no other record in the library
does.

## NSW

- **ADVOs and APVOs** — *Crimes (Domestic and Personal Violence) Act 2007*. Restrictions to protect a
  person from domestic or personal violence; **breach is a criminal offence**.
- **Serious Crime Prevention Orders** — *Crimes (Serious Crime Prevention Orders) Act 2016*. Made by
  the **Supreme Court** against people or organisations involved in serious crime.
- **Extended Supervision Orders** — *Crimes (High Risk Offenders) Act 2006*. Monitoring and control of
  serious violent or sexual offenders **after release from prison**.
- **Public Safety Orders** — *Law Enforcement (Powers and Responsibilities) Act 2002*. Police may
  prohibit a person or group from attending a public place or event.
- **Child Protection Prohibition Orders** — *Child Protection (Offenders Prohibition Orders) Act
  2004*. Restrict a convicted child sex offender's access to places or activities involving children.
- **Firearms Prohibition Orders** — made by the **Commissioner of Police**, prohibiting possession or
  use of firearms, parts or ammunition, and allowing police to **search premises, vehicles or the
  person without a warrant**.
- Interim control orders directed at criminal organisations, and digital evidence access orders.

## Commonwealth

- **Control orders** — *Criminal Code Act 1995*, **Division 104**. Obligations, prohibitions and
  restrictions on a person to prevent a terrorist act, including on movement, communication and
  internet use.
- **Preventative detention orders** — *Criminal Code Act 1995*, **Division 105**. Short-term
  detention to prevent an imminent terrorist act or preserve evidence.
- Control orders directed at serious and organised crime; **proceeds of crime orders**
  (*Proceeds of Crime Act 2002*); and family violence orders under the *Family Law Act 1975*.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.8 (post-sentencing considerations) — the map is the value.** Extended supervision orders
  and continuing detention under the *Crimes (High Risk Offenders) Act 2006* are the standard answer;
  this puts them in a **family** of regimes so you can argue about the category rather than one Act.
- **The rule of law problem, stated once and applied to all of them (crime.t.balance)** — every order
  here restricts liberty **on the civil standard**, for conduct that **has not occurred**, often after
  the sentence for the original offence has been fully served. Set that against the purposes of
  punishment and the presumption of innocence, and you have the central evaluation for this dot
  point. Note *Kable v DPP* and *Fardon* are the constitutional backdrop.
- **Who decides (crime.t.discretion / dis.3.1)** — a **court** for ESOs and SCPOs, **police** for
  public safety orders, and the **Commissioner of Police alone** for a firearms prohibition order,
  which then authorises **warrantless search**. Ranking these by the safeguards attached is a precise
  way to argue about procedural fairness.
- **Breach converts prevention into punishment (crime.1.2)** — an ADVO is civil, and **breaching it
  is a crime**. That structure recurs across the list, and it is how a preventive order acquires
  teeth.
- **Terrorism as the outer limit (crime.6.2)** — Division 105 **preventative detention** allows
  detention without charge. Pair it with `r-v-lodhi-2006` and the 2025 hate crime reforms' mandatory
  minimums for the pattern of exceptional powers justified by exceptional risk.
- **Crossovers** — ADVOs link to `fam.2.4`, child protection prohibition orders to `fam.3.4`, and
  firearms prohibition orders to the firearms injunction in `alberdingk-alberdingk-2026`. One concept,
  three topics.

## Caution

**An unattributed teaching survey, and it contains errors** — treat every line as a lead to check,
not a citation. Two to fix before use. **Sex offender registration is not a Commonwealth *Crimes Act
1914* scheme**: in NSW it is the *Child Protection (Offenders Registration) Act 2000*, with the
Australian National Child Offender Register coordinating between jurisdictions. And the NSW
**criminal organisations control** regime has a complicated history — the 2009 Act's scheme was held
invalid in ***Wainohu v New South Wales*** (2011), so cite the current Act and check what survives.
"Digital evidence access orders" are an **investigative** power, not a preventive order, and belong
with search and surveillance.

The document gives **no section numbers, no penalties and no standards of proof**, which is most of
what you need — go to the Acts. It reads as AI-assisted, the same pattern as the rental subsidies and
build-to-rent explainers, and its closing "effectiveness" paragraph is **assertion, not analysis**:
there is no data here on whether any of these orders reduce offending, and a real evaluation needs
BOCSAR or a review. Its final list ("mental health treatment orders", "community treatment orders",
"harmful proceedings orders", "care and seclusion orders") is a **note to self**, not content. Several
of these regimes have been amended repeatedly; **verify currency** before relying on any of it.
