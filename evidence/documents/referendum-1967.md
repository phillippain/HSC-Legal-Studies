The Constitution of 1901 excluded "aboriginal natives" from the Commonwealth's race power (s 51(xxvi)) and from being counted "in reckoning the numbers of the people" (s 127). A petition launched in 1957 by the Aboriginal-Australian Fellowship (Faith Bandler and Jessie Street among its leaders) gathered just over 100,000 signatures; the Federal Council for the Advancement of Aborigines and Torres Strait Islanders (FCAATSI, founded 1958) carried the campaign and led the 1967 Yes vote, and the 1965 Freedom Ride built public support.

## The vote

- Carried in every state; **90.77%** Yes nationally, 5,183,113 to 527,007 — the highest Yes vote ever recorded.
- The second question put the same day (breaking the nexus between the sizes of the two houses) failed, with 40.25%.

## What it did and did not do

- It **did** give the Commonwealth power to legislate for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people (the power later used for the *Native Title Act 1993* and the *Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Heritage Protection Act 1984*), and removed the census exclusion.
- It **did not** give the right to vote (granted federally in 1962), confer citizenship (which Aboriginal people already held) or require the power to be used beneficially — *Kartinyeri v Commonwealth* (1998) left that open.

## Caution

Do not describe 1967 as "giving Aboriginal people the vote" or "citizenship"; both are common errors that markers notice.
