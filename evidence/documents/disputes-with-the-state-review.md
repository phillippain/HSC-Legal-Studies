A reference note. The worked instances are the migration and refugee records in this finder.

## Three layers, in order

**1. Internal review.** The agency looks at its own decision, usually by a different and more senior
officer. Free or nearly free, quick, and by far the most used. Its weakness is obvious and worth
stating plainly: the decision-maker and the reviewer work for the same body.

**2. Merits review — external.** A tribunal stands in the shoes of the original decision-maker and
asks **what the correct or preferable decision is**, on the evidence before it, including new
evidence. It can **affirm, vary, set aside and substitute** its own decision, or remit. The
**Administrative Review Tribunal** replaced the AAT in **October 2024** for Commonwealth decisions;
NCAT's Administrative and Equal Opportunity Division does the same work for NSW.

**3. Judicial review — the courts.** A court asks only whether the decision was made **lawfully**:
jurisdictional error, a breach of **procedural fairness** (*Kioa v West*), taking into account an
irrelevant consideration, acting for an improper purpose, unreasonableness. It **cannot** substitute
a better decision. Typically it quashes the decision and **sends it back to be remade** — by the same
department, lawfully this time, and possibly to the same result.

## Use it for

- **The distinction that carries the dot point**: merits review asks *was it right*, judicial review
  asks *was it lawful*. Everything else — who can apply, what remedies exist, how long it takes —
  follows from that.
- **The separation of powers is the reason for the limit** (`ls.2.7`). A court substituting its own
  view of a visa or a licence would be exercising executive power. So the narrowness of judicial
  review is a feature, and its cost to the individual is real.
- **Access in practice** — internal review is free, merits review costs a modest fee, judicial review
  costs a Federal Court proceeding. The further up the ladder, the stronger the remedy and the fewer
  the people who reach it.
