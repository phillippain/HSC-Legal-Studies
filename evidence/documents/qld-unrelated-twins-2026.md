**DZ**, 27, already a mother of five, agreed in September 2024 to carry a child for **BNJ and DRJ**,
a couple in their twenties; BNJ was born without a uterus. In April 2025 a single embryo was
transferred — and at about the same time DZ **also conceived naturally** with her husband. The
ultrasound showed two foetuses. In November 2025 a girl and a boy were delivered in the same
caesarean, and DNA testing confirmed the girl was BNJ and DRJ's and the boy was DZ and FZ's. Each
couple has raised their own child; nobody disputed who the parents were.

## The legal problem

In Australian law the **woman who gives birth is presumed to be the mother**, whatever the genetics,
so in an altruistic surrogacy the intended parents must apply for a **parentage order**. Without one
BNJ and DRJ would not be the legal parents of their own biological daughter.

But **s 24 of the *Surrogacy Act 2010* (Qld)** exists to stop children of the same surrogate
pregnancy being split between families: a court making a parentage order for one child must make one
for each living **"birth sibling"**. Read literally, that could have forced both babies onto the same
parents — handing BNJ and DRJ a child who was not theirs, or denying them the one who was.

## Held

**Judge Jodie Wooldridge KC** found the children were **not birth siblings** within the meaning of
the Act. Because they were conceived separately they were not born as a result of the **same
pregnancy**, which is the statutory test; DZ had in effect carried two concurrent pregnancies.
Section 24 was therefore never engaged, and a parentage order could be made for the girl alone.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3 (surrogacy and birth technologies)** — a vivid, memorable illustration that **parentage
  does not follow biology**: two sets of biological parents, and the only way the genetic parents
  became legal parents was a court order.
- **ls.2.4 / ls.1.4 (statute law, just laws)** — a protective provision that would have produced an
  absurd result if read literally. Good for statutory interpretation and for the proposition that a
  law written for the ordinary case can fail the unusual one.
- **fam.3.3 and law reform** — drafters writing "birth siblings" in 2010 did not imagine
  superfetation. Use it for whether the law keeps pace with reproductive technology.
- **fam.t.cooperation** — worth noting what made it easy: the arrangement was **altruistic**, nobody
  contested parentage, and the families remain in contact. Contrast with the international commercial
  cases where the same question is fought.

## Caution

A **class-made case study**, and it says so: the parties are anonymised, and "the reasoning
summarised here comes from media reporting of the judgment rather than the full published reasons".
The outcome was **uncontested**, so the point was not argued adversarially and its precedential
weight is limited; a **Children's Court** decision binds nothing. The ABC reported the case on
**27 August 2026** — that report is the check on the facts, and the judgment itself should be found
before quoting Judge Wooldridge. This is **Queensland** law: s 24 has no exact NSW equivalent, so do
not transplant it into an answer about the *Surrogacy Act 2010* (NSW) without checking.
