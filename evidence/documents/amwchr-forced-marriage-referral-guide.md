Two pages, produced by the **Australian Muslim Women's Centre for Human Rights** and funded by the
**Department of Home Affairs**, for people who work with young people. It is thin as a document and
useful precisely because of what it contains: **the referral network, written out**.

## Where a young person at risk is actually sent

- **AMWCHR** — 03 9481 3000
- **Red Cross Support for Trafficked People Program** — 03 9345 1800
- **Salvation Army Trafficking & Slavery Safe House** — 1300 473 560
- **Centre for Multicultural Youth** — 03 9340 3700
- **My Blue Sky** (Anti-Slavery Australia) — 02 9514 8115
- **Australian Federal Police** — 131 237
- **Kids Helpline** — 1800 55 1800

Five of the seven are **non-government organisations**. Only one is a law enforcement number, and
there is no court in the list.

## What it says about deterrents and consent

Why a young person may not act: **no appropriate accommodation** if they leave home; **mental health
crisis**; **guilt and betrayal**; **fear of disconnection from friends, family and community**; and
**potential criminal consequences for their family**.

Where a marriage would be **legal and consensual** but the worker is concerned about the young
person's age, the guide says to have a conversation to assess whether they feel pressured, explore
their understanding of **consent**, and give them information on their rights and on family violence
services — and then: "Ultimately, if the marriage is legal, then it is up to the young person to make
their own decision." It also flags that **mandatory reporting** obligations may apply where an
underage person discloses a forced marriage, and that in some cases "education, mediation, and
external support can bring parents and family members back towards a safe home environment".

## Use it for

- **hr.2.12 — the non-legal response, listed.** Instead of asserting that NGOs matter, name five of
  them and point out that the legal response (the AFP) is one line on a page of seven. Specificity
  like this is what separates a top-band answer.
- **Why criminalisation can deter the victim (hr.t.effectiveness)** — the deterrent that stands out is
  "**potential criminal consequences for their family**". The offence is intended to protect the young
  person, and the fear of prosecuting her parents is a reason she stays silent. One of the sharpest
  available arguments that a criminal prohibition can work against the person it protects.
- **exp.3.2 / exp.6.2 (barriers to achieving justice)** — housing, mental health, guilt, loss of
  community. Concrete barriers, not abstractions, and they apply to young people as a group.
- **Autonomy and the limits of intervention (hr.t.values / inv.1.5)** — the guide tells workers that a
  lawful, consensual marriage is the young person's own decision. Good for the tension between
  protecting someone and respecting their choices, which is the hardest part of this issue.
- **Mediation and family restoration** — the suggestion that support can return a family to safety is
  a **non-adversarial** response worth contrasting with prosecution.
- **Government funding an NGO (hr.2.9)** — Home Affairs funds the sheet, a community organisation
  writes it. A small but accurate picture of how the Australian response is delivered.

## Caution

A **two-page practitioner fact sheet**, not research or law: it contains **no data, no case and no
analysis of the offence**, so use it for the response network and the barriers, never as authority on
the law. The phone numbers are **Victorian** for four of the services and were current at
publication; **do not read them out or reproduce them as current advice** — direct students to My
Blue Sky or Kids Helpline instead. It is undated on its face; the version here sits alongside the
July 2024 My Blue Sky guide. The AMWCHR works primarily with Muslim women: forced marriage occurs
"across cultures, religions, nationalities and societies", and using a resource from one community
organisation to imply the practice belongs to one community would be both inaccurate and harmful.
Mandatory reporting duties differ by state and profession — the guide says to check your own, and so
should anyone citing it.
