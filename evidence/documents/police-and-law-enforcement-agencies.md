A reference note.

## State

**NSW Police Force** — general policing under the *Police Act 1990* (NSW), exercising the powers in
**LEPRA 2002**. Policing is a **residual power**: the Constitution gives the Commonwealth no general
police power, so the states do the ordinary work of law enforcement.

## Commonwealth

| Agency | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Australian Federal Police** | Commonwealth offences, crime that crosses state or national borders, counter-terrorism, policing in the ACT and territories |
| **Australian Criminal Intelligence Commission** | Intelligence on serious and organised crime; coercive examination powers |
| **AUSTRAC** | Financial intelligence — money laundering and terrorism financing reporting |
| **Australian Border Force** | Customs, immigration detention and enforcement at the border |

## Who watches them

- **Law Enforcement Conduct Commission** (*LECC Act 2016* (NSW)) — complaints and own-motion
  investigations into NSW police misconduct. It can **investigate and report**, not prosecute.
- **s 242 LEPRA** — independent statutory monitoring of the Act's operation.
- **Commonwealth Ombudsman** and the **ACLEI/NACC** arrangements for federal agencies.
- The **courts**, indirectly, through the **s 138 *Evidence Act*** discretion to exclude improperly
  obtained evidence, and through civil actions for trespass, assault and false imprisonment.

## Use it for

- **Federal and state police** (`ind.2.1`) — the division of responsibility is a **division of powers**
  question (`ls.2.6`), and saying so lifts the answer out of description.
- **Accountability without prosecution.** The LECC investigates and reports; the decision to prosecute
  belongs elsewhere. That gap is the standard criticism, and it connects to the STMP and strip-search
  findings in the young offenders record.
- **Discretion** as the invisible power — at every stage police choose whether to act at all, and none
  of these bodies reviews a decision *not* to investigate.
