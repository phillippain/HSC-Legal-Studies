The full version of the two-page explainer, with a page for each idea:

- **What it is, and why the law changed:** the pattern, eight forms of control, myths and facts, and a fictional case study (Jess and Dan). A timeline from the 2020 Joint Select Committee to the 2026 statutory review (Act passed Nov 2022; s 54D from 1 July 2024).
- **Section 54D as a flowchart:** six steps the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt, the **s 54E** reasonableness defence, and a table linking s 54D to Crime concepts (actus reus, mens rea, summary or indictable, defence).
- **The numbers:** 682 incidents, 42 charges, 4 proven; the most common behaviours; regional v Greater Sydney; how to read recorded-crime data (recorded is not actual; more recorded can mean more awareness).
- **From report to court:** why so few charges, and the first jail term for coercive control as reported by the SMH (16 September 2026).
- **Legal and non-legal measures:** the offence, ADVOs, bail, sentencing and victims, oversight and review, and federal family law (*Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) s 4AB), each with a strength and a limit; a statute cheat-sheet; a report-to-court page; and evaluation across victims, families and the accused.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.2 and crime.1.5** — applying actus reus, mens rea and offence classification to one offence.
- **fam.2.4** — evaluating how effectively the law protects victims of domestic violence, including the interaction of state criminal law and federal family law.
- Law reform or effectiveness answers that need the reform chain and a strength and a limit for each measure.

## Caution

A **class-made resource**, not a primary source: cite BOCSAR, the Act and the SMH report directly. The first-jail-term case comes from a news report, not a judgment, and later allegations in it are unproved, so pair one case with the statistics. Two years of data is a short period and most charges were still before the courts at 30 June 2026.
