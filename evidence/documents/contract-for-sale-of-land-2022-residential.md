The **standard form contract** published jointly by the **Law Society of NSW** and the **Real Estate
Institute of NSW** — the instrument that nearly every residential sale in the state uses. This copy
is a real, completed one for a house on the Central Coast, so it shows the form as it is actually
filled in rather than as a blank.

## What is on the front page

Everything the parties have to settle before anything else happens:

- **vendor**, **vendor's agent**, **vendor's solicitor**, **purchaser**, **purchaser's solicitor** —
  five participants named before a word of the terms;
- **date for completion** — here, "**42nd day after the contract date** (clause 15)";
- the **land**, identified three ways: street address, **Lot 14 in Deposited Plan 286016**, and
  **Folio Identifier 14/286016**. That is the Torrens system on the page: the folio identifier, not
  the address, is what the register recognises;
- **VACANT POSSESSION** or **subject to existing tenancies** — in block capitals, because clause
  20.15 provides that "where this contract provides for choices, a choice in BLOCK CAPITALS applies
  unless a different choice is marked";
- **improvements** (house, garage, carport, home unit, carspace, storage space) and a tick-box
  **inclusions** list — air conditioning, blinds, dishwasher, fixed floor coverings, light fittings,
  solar panels, stove, **EV charger**, pool equipment, TV antenna — plus **exclusions**;
- **price**, **deposit** ("**10% of the price, unless otherwise stated**"), **balance**, and an
  optional **GST amount**;
- where there is more than one purchaser, a choice between **JOINT TENANTS** and **tenants in
  common**, with a space for unequal shares;
- a **signing page**, including provision for a company signing under **s 127(1) of the
  *Corporations Act 2001***.

Four pages the agent may fill in, then the standard terms, then the attached documents.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 — show the thing itself.** A single slide of this front page teaches more about
  purchasing than a paragraph of description: contract law, Torrens title, agency, solicitors,
  deposits and co-ownership are all visible at once.
- **Joint tenants versus tenants in common** is a **tick box on page one** (inv.4.3 / fam.2.3). Right
  of survivorship or a divisible share, decided in a moment at exchange, with consequences on death
  or separation. The clearest possible illustration that private law choices made in a form have
  long-run legal effects.
- **Freedom of contract against a standard form (dis.2.3)** — the parties are free to agree, yet
  almost every sale in NSW uses one document drafted by two professional bodies, with only four pages
  the parties really complete. Good for whether standard forms protect consumers or just entrench the
  drafter's terms.
- **The 10% deposit** — the default, and the figure every "what happens if the buyer walks away"
  question turns on. Read it beside the **0.25%** cooling-off forfeiture in the purchasing guide
  record: two very different consequences for changing your mind, five business days apart.
- **"Folio Identifier 14/286016" (shel.1.2)** — Torrens title by registration. Pair it with the
  *Real Property Act 1900* and `rogers-v-registrar-general-2024` on what the register guarantees.
- **The inclusions list as dispute prevention (shel.2.5)** — most settlement disputes are about what
  was supposed to stay. A tick box is a cheap, effective mechanism, and the fact that **EV charger**
  now appears on it is a neat example of a standard form keeping pace with the world.

## Caution

**This is a real contract containing real people's personal information** — the vendors' names and
residential address, the property address, and the agent's and solicitor's names, email addresses and
phone numbers. **Redact it before showing it to a class or copying any page**, and do not reproduce
the names or addresses in any resource, assessment or published material. Teach from the **blank
form** or from the McKell Building record (a government vendor, no private individuals) where you
can. It is a **2022 edition** form: editions are updated and the current edition may differ, so check
the Law Society's current version before stating what the form says. Nothing in it is law — the
contract is a **private document** that operates within the *Conveyancing Act 1919*, the *Real
Property Act 1900* and the *Conveyancing (Sale of Land) Regulation*; the statutory requirements come
from those, not from the form. The completed fields are **this transaction's** terms, not standard
ones: the 42-day completion period, the inclusions and the vacant possession choice were all
negotiated. The file is 380 pages because the **attached certificates and searches** come with it;
that bulk is itself the point about vendor disclosure.
