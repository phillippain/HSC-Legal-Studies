Ten months after the final report, the Commonwealth published its response alongside a joint response from all governments on the recommendations they share.

## The numbers

| Commonwealth recommendations (172 of 222) | |
|---|---|
| Accepted in full | 13 |
| Accepted in principle | 117 |
| Subject to further consideration | 36 |
| Noted | 6 |

## What that meant

- Accepted in full: measures already under way or low-cost — improved data, a disability employment strategy, changes to the Disability Support Pension process.
- In principle: most of the rights and service reforms, with implementation "subject to further work".
- Further consideration: the Disability Rights Act, the National Disability Commission, and the phasing out of segregated education, employment and group homes — the recommendations that would have changed the legal architecture.

Disability organisations (People with Disability Australia, Inclusion Australia, the Disability Advocacy Network) described the response as a missed opportunity; governments pointed to the $4 billion already being spent on NDIS reform and foundational supports.

## Use it for

- **exp.6.1 / exp.6.2** — indicators of success and barriers to justice, measured by what the state did with its own commission's advice.

## Caution

The figures are the Commonwealth's categories; states responded separately on their own recommendations. Check the Department of Social Services progress reports for anything implemented since.
