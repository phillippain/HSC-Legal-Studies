AIATSIS is the national Indigenous research institution; its explainers are written and reviewed with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander authors and are the safest classroom source for content that carries ICIP obligations.

## What the explainers establish

- **Diversity**: the AIATSIS map shows more than 250 language groups and many more Nations; Lore is particular to Country, so there is no single "Aboriginal law".
- **Kinship** is a system of relationships — moiety, totem, skin name — that fixes who a person may marry, who they are responsible for, who teaches them and what they may speak about. Obligations run to people and to Country alike, and Elders hold and transmit them.
- **Country** is not property but a living relationship: a person belongs to Country, and caring for it (burning, water, ceremony, protecting sites) is an obligation of Lore, not a choice.
- **Interconnectedness**: ceremony, song, kinship and land management are aspects of one system; damage to one (a destroyed site, a dried river) is damage to the others.

## Caution

A teaching source, not evidence of a legal outcome; pair it with a case or statute (*Walker*, the Yarra Act, Circle Sentencing). Some knowledge is restricted and not for publication — use what AIATSIS has chosen to publish and nothing further.
