In January 1999 a 91-year-old woman, Rita Knight, was raped and assaulted in her home in Wee Waa, in north-west NSW. In **April 2000** police asked every man in the town aged 18 to 45 (about 600) to give a voluntary mouth swab for DNA comparison: Australia's first mass DNA screening.

## What happened

- Most men volunteered; those who declined were reported to feel under suspicion in a small town.
- **Stephen Boney**, 44, gave a sample and confessed about a week later, before his sample had been analysed. He pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 18 years.
- The **Crimes (Forensic Procedures) Act 2000 (NSW)** commenced soon after, regulating consent, orders by senior police and magistrates, and the destruction of samples from volunteers.

## Issues raised

- **Privacy and the presumption of innocence:** treating a whole population as suspects, with social pressure making 'voluntary' consent questionable.
- **Effectiveness:** the screening produced a confession, arguably through pressure rather than science, at considerable cost.
- **Retention:** what happens to the DNA of the hundreds of men who were not suspects.

## Use it for

- **Use of technology / DNA evidence:** the power of DNA and the rights it engages. Pair with *Farah Jama* (contamination) and *R v White* (2005) (covert collection).
