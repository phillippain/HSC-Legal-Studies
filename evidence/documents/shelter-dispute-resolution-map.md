## The ladder

**Self-help and negotiation → regulators → ADR → tribunal → courts.** The extent of each response's
role is largely a function of where on that ladder a dispute stops.

## 1. Self-help — the largest and least visible role

The *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW) supplies the bargaining positions: the **breach notice**
and remedy period, the tenant's right to arrange **urgent repairs** and be reimbursed (s 64),
**termination notices** under ss 84–87N with their prescribed evidence, and the **rent-increase
notice** (s 41). Providers have their own private ordering — licensed managing agents, landlord
insurance, arrears policies, strata by-law notices under the SSMA 2015.

With **2.3 million renters** in NSW and roughly 330,000 household moves a year, the **28,193** tenancy
applications to NCAT are a very small fraction of the relationships in which something went wrong.
Informal resolution is efficient but unsupervised, and it favours the party with better information
and less at stake.

## 2. Government organisations — resolution by regulation, not adjudication

| Body | What it resolves | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| **NSW Fair Trading** | Complaints, investigation, informal conciliation, penalty notices; runs Rental Bonds Online, which resolves the most common dispute administratively | Broad but **non-binding** — it cannot make an order |
| **Rental Taskforce / Rental Commissioner** | Proactive compliance: automated monitoring, inspections, prosecutions | Small volume, systemic effect |
| **Rental Bond Board** | Custodian of bonds (s 159 RTA) and, through the interest, funder of the entire tenant advice sector | Structurally central |
| **Housing Appeals Committee** | Independent review of Homes NSW decisions — eligibility, transfers, priority | The **only** review right social housing applicants have |
| **Building Commission NSW** | Prohibition orders and rectification orders for serious defects | **Pre-emptive** — resolves defects before purchasers become owners with a claim |
| **Anti-Discrimination NSW / AHRC** | Accommodation discrimination complaints, conciliation, referral onward | Conciliation-dependent |
| **ANAO / NSW Law Reform Commission** | Systemic resolution — auditing delivery, reviewing the law | Resolves nothing for an individual; the ANAO's finding that **1,432 of 40,000** HAFF homes had been delivered forced a response no litigant could compel |

## 3. Media — resolution by exposure

The media resolves nothing directly; it converts an issue with no forum into one a regulator or
parliament will act on. Coverage of the **Martin Place encampment** produced the *Sydney Public
Reserves (Public Safety) Act 2017* (NSW) within weeks — **resolving the issue against the people
affected**. Coverage of **Opal Tower and Mascot Towers** produced the *Design and Building
Practitioners Act 2020* (NSW) and the Building Commission. Attention is episodic and follows what is
visible and dramatic, not what is large and slow: an encampment, not a waiting list.

## The point to argue

1. **The role of each response is set by the type of issue, not by choice.** Where a right and a
   respondent exist, legal responses dominate. Where the problem is access, awareness or capability,
   non-legal responses dominate. Where the problem is **scarcity**, neither resolves it — there is no
   right to enforce and no one to enforce it against.
2. **They are interdependent, not alternative.** TAAP is funded from bond interest under the RTA.
3. **The same forum plays a different role for each party** — for a provider NCAT is a repeat-play
   debt and possession forum; for a renter it is a one-off, usually unrepresented, defence of home.
4. **Prevention and systemic resolution count.** A regulator that stops a breach resolves more than a
   tribunal that adjudicates one.
