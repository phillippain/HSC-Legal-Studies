Despite the library filename, this is not a residential Torrens example. It is the **contract for the
sale of the McKell Building, 2–24 Rawson Place, Haymarket** — **Lot 6 in Deposited Plan 820360, Folio
Identifier 6/820360** — on the **2005 edition** of the same Law Society / REINSW form.

The parties make it worth having:

- **Vendor: Government Property NSW**, Level 9, 4–6 Bligh Street, Sydney.
- **Vendor's agent: Macquarie Capital (Australia) Limited**, 1 Martin Place.
- **Vendor's solicitor: Herbert Smith Freehills**, MLC Centre.
- **Improvements: "Commercial Building"** — the "house / garage / carport / home unit" boxes all
  unticked. **Annexures A to I**, and a completion date defined by **special condition 1.1** rather
  than by the standard clause 15.

## The List of Documents — vendor disclosure, printed

The contract's second page is the schedule of attachable documents, which is the most useful page in
either contract record because it is the **s 52A disclosure regime made concrete**:

**General** — property certificate for the land; plan of the land; unregistered plan; plan of land to
be subdivided; **section 149(2) planning certificate** under the *Environmental Planning and
Assessment Act 1979*, and the s 149(5) information; **sewerage connections diagram** and **sewer
mains diagram**; any document creating an **easement, profit à prendre, restriction on use or
positive covenant**; **section 88G certificate** (positive covenant); **survey report**; **section
317A certificate** of compliance; **building certificate**; and the **insurance certificate and
brochure under the *Home Building Act 1989***.

**Strata or community title** (clause 23) — property certificate for common property, the plan
creating it, **by-laws not set out in legislation**, strata development contract or statement, strata
management statement, leasehold strata, and the equivalent sets for **neighbourhood**, **precinct**
and **community** property.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 — one form, every kind of land.** A first home buyer in Magenta and the State of NSW
  selling a city office tower use the **same contract**. That is a strong, concrete point about the
  generality of property law: the rules do not change with the size of the transaction.
- **The List of Documents is the best available illustration of vendor disclosure** — rather than
  saying "the vendor must disclose certain matters", name them: the planning certificate, the sewer
  diagram, the easements, the survey, the Home Building Act insurance. Pair it with the Law Society
  selling checklist record, which explains why each one is there.
- **shel.2.8 / shel.3.4 — the state as a property owner, and a seller.** Government Property NSW
  divesting a CBD landmark through an investment bank raises exactly the question a Shelter answer
  needs: when public land is sold, who benefits, and what happens to the public purpose it served.
  Set it beside the social housing records and the planning reform record.
- **Easements, covenants and restrictions on use (shel.2.4)** — items 10, 11 and 13 on the list. The
  reason a purchaser's solicitor searches, and the reason "you own it" never means "you may do
  anything with it".
- **Safe to teach from.** Unlike the residential contract in the library, there are **no private
  individuals** here — the parties are a state agency, a bank and a law firm. Use this one on a
  slide.
- **Strata, community, neighbourhood and precinct title** all appear on the one page, which is a
  compact way into `shel.2.4` and the different forms of ownership.

## Caution

The file is a **scanned image PDF with no text layer** (Fuji Xerox, created June 2013) and the detail
in this record was read by **OCR**, so verify any figure, reference or name against the document
before quoting it. It is the **2005 edition** of the contract and **two decades out of date**:
section numbering has moved — most importantly a **"section 149 certificate" is now a section 10.7
planning certificate** after the EP&A Act was renumbered in 2018 — and the *Conveyancing (Sale of
Land) Regulation* has been remade since, so check the **current** prescribed documents rather than
using this list as the present law. This is a **commercial** sale with special conditions and
annexures, so do not treat its terms as typical of a residential purchase. The record describes the
**contract**; it says nothing about the **price**, the outcome, or what happened to the building
afterwards, and nothing here should be presented as a judgement about the merits of the sale. Whether
this contract was ever completed on these terms is not established by the document.
