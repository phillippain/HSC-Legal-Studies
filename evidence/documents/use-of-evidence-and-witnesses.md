## Types

- **Real (physical)** — objects: DNA, weapons.
- **Documentary** — written material, and audio, photographic and video recordings.
- **Witness testimony** — first-hand, eyewitness or expert, given on oath; lying is **perjury**.

A witness is taken through **examination-in-chief** by the party calling them, then
**cross-examination** by the opponent, which tests reliability and credibility, then, if permitted,
**re-examination** to clarify. Limits apply to leading questions and to attempts to revive memory.

## Admissibility

Evidence must be **relevant**, **legally obtained**, **direct** (not hearsay, subject to exceptions
such as admissions) and **not opinion** (unless the witness is an expert). Improperly or illegally
obtained evidence may still be admitted if the court exercises its discretion in the public interest.
A **voir dire** — a trial within a trial, in the jury's absence — settles admissibility.

## Use it for

- **Balance**: every admissibility rule is a decision to accept a less complete picture in exchange for
  a fairer process. Write it that way rather than listing the rules.
- The **reform direction is one-way** at the moment: the *Evidence Amendment (Tendency and Coincidence)
  Act 2020* (NSW) and s 272.9(5) of the *Criminal Code Act 1995* (Cth) both move ground toward the
  prosecution in child sexual abuse matters.
- **Victims and witnesses** — cross-examination is the mechanism that makes testifying hard, and it is
  also the mechanism that catches the unreliable witness. That tension is the essay.
