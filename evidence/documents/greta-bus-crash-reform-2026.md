A coach carrying wedding guests rolled at the Branxton interchange near Greta on **11 June 2023**:
**10 dead, 25 injured**. This one tragedy runs through nearly every part of the criminal justice
system, and then past it into a corporate prosecution and a safety investigation.

## Charged versus resolved

| | Charged / argued | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Most serious charge | 10 × manslaughter, max **25 years** each | 10 × dangerous driving occasioning death, basic max **10 years** |
| Number of charges | **89** offences | Guilty pleas to **35**; manslaughter withdrawn |
| Injury charges | 25 counts of bodily harm by misconduct | 9 dangerous driving causing GBH; 16 furious driving causing bodily harm |
| Sentence | Families feared a light sentence | **32 years, non-parole 24**, Judge Roy Ellis, 11 Sep 2024 |
| Appeal | Double counting; manifestly excessive | **Dismissed unanimously, 14 Nov 2025** |

At sentence, **33 victim impact statements were read and 9 tendered**. The appeal court found the
sentencing judge had properly weighed them.

## The facts that drove it

Recorded speed **56.48 km/h** on the overpass, slowing to 52.60 km/h entering the interchange,
against a rollover threshold of about **47 km/h**. The driver admitted taking **350 mg of tramadol**
against a 200 mg daily maximum; OTSI found impairment likely. A previous employer had stopped him
driving in June 2022 after a report noting opiate dependence — and **no industry-wide driver record
system existed**, so nothing reached the regulator or his next employer.

## Beyond the individual

- **Corporate liability**: the NHVR prosecuted **Linq Buslines**, fined **$650,000** in Gosford Local
  Court in August 2026; two directors convicted and fined **$12,000 each**.
- **Systemic reform**: OTSI's report (31 July 2026) made **41 recommendations** — 34 to named
  organisations, all accepted or accepted in principle, and 7 to the wider industry. The government
  accepted all 21 directed to Transport for NSW.
- **Civil action**: survivors, families and insurers are suing the transport department over the
  roundabout, also naming Linq, the driver and engineering companies.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.4 (charge negotiation)** — the best available Australian example, and unusually complete:
  the ODPP Prosecution Guidelines require charges reflecting provable criminality and **consultation
  with victims**, with a s 35A certificate filed under the *Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Act 1999*.
  A victim's mother's statement criticising the DPP was read **in NSW Parliament** on 15 May 2024.
- **crime.4.4 (the victim in sentencing)** — 42 victim impact statements, a 32-year sentence, and
  families who still say the conduct deserved manslaughter. Useful for whether victim participation
  delivers victim satisfaction.
- **The penalty gap** — manslaughter 25 years; dangerous driving occasioning death 10 years, or 14
  aggravated. Yet the total sentence exceeded any single maximum because the counts were accumulated.
- **crime.1.2 / ls.3.2 (fault and civil liability)** — the same facts produce a criminal conviction, a
  corporate fine and a negligence suit, each with a different standard.
- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.2 (conditions and agencies of reform)** — a non-judicial investigator (OTSI) as the
  reform agency, with survivors pressing for medical reporting, medication lists and seatbelt change.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **feature story told mainly through victims**. It **omits the appeal
result** — the sentence is final — and gives little DPP or defence reasoning. The "six of 35 wearing
seatbelts" figure is the ABC's; OTSI says only that not all passengers wore belts or were reminded
to. Neither the appeal judgment nor the Linq judgment was read, so the quoted word "flippant" about
Linq's reference check is unconfirmed (other reports have "wholly inappropriate"), and no plea
discount is reported. Reports do not say whether any count was charged in aggravated form.
