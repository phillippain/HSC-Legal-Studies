- **Circle sentencing** began at **Nowra Local Court in 2002**. An Aboriginal offender who has pleaded
  or been found guilty sits with the magistrate, Elders, the victim and lawyers to agree a sentence.
  The first was **Robert Bolt**, who had 58 prior convictions.
- **BOCSAR (2008):** no evidence of reduced reoffending against a matched control group.
- **BOCSAR CJB 226 (2020):** participants were **9.3 percentage points less likely to be imprisoned**,
  **3.9 points less likely to reoffend** within 12 months, and took about 55 days longer to reoffend
  (the reoffending result was not robust in every model).
- **Walama List** (District Court, 2022, Judge Dina Yehia): an Elder-led sentencing conversation and
  monitored plan for Aboriginal adults in the higher courts.
- **ABC, 27 Feb 2026:** over 30% of NSW prisoners are Aboriginal against 3.4% of the population;
  Walama places are limited.

## Use it for

- **Alternative methods of sentencing** (`crime.4.7`) — the main NSW example.
- **Effectiveness** — choose the measure: reoffending (mixed), imprisonment (lower), legitimacy and
  community involvement (strong).

## Caution

Circle sentencing is a method, not a lighter penalty; a circle can impose prison. The 2020 results are
associations, not proof of cause.
