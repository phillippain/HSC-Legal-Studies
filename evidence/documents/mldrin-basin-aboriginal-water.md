MLDRIN is a confederation of Traditional Owner Nations along the southern Basin, formed at a Yorta Yorta-hosted meeting in 1998 to speak for Country in water policy. Its Echuca Declaration (2007) defines **cultural flows**: water entitlements legally and beneficially owned by Nations, of a quantity and quality to improve the spiritual, cultural, environmental, social and economic conditions of those Nations.

## The law

- The *Basin Plan 2012* (Cth), made under the *Water Act 2007*, requires each water resource plan to identify the objectives and outcomes of Aboriginal people for water, to have regard to their values and uses, and to describe how they were consulted (Chapter 10, Part 14) — a duty to consider, not to allocate.
- Aboriginal organisations own well under 1% of water entitlements in the Basin (a 2020 Griffith University study put it at 0.17% in NSW); water is bought and sold as property under state law.
- The Commonwealth's $40 million Aboriginal Water Entitlements Program, announced in 2018, was criticised for years of delay before purchases began; the Basin Plan review due in 2026 is the next decision point.

## Caution

MLDRIN is an advocacy body and the cultural-flows concept is its position, not law. Check the current status of the Entitlements Program and the 2026 Basin Plan review before using figures.
