## Suspect Target Management Plans

Police were given lists of people out on bail and thought to be at risk of reoffending, then
monitored them: routine stops and searches, home visits, bail compliance checks, consorting warnings
and move-on directions.

The argument against is stated in one line and is the strongest available for this dot point:
**waiting for someone on a list to commit an offence removes the presumption of innocence**, which
the rule of law requires. After concerns raised by the **Law Enforcement Conduct Commission**, NSW
Police announced the practice would be discontinued by **December 2023**.

## The Youth Koori Court

Sentences are deferred for six months while the young person completes agreed steps such as
improving school attendance, and the court meets again to see whether imprisonment can be avoided.
A **BOCSAR 2022** evaluation found participants were **40% less likely** to receive a custodial
sentence at finalisation than Aboriginal young people sentenced through the ordinary pathway, and
those who did reoffend were **84% less likely** to receive a custodial penalty at re-conviction.

Against that: **no statistically significant reduction in reoffending**, and a **guilty plea is
required** to attend at all — which narrows who can reach a just outcome through it.

## Strip searches and children, under LEPRA

| Age | The rule |
|---|---|
| Under 10 | **No strip search** |
| 10–14 | An adult other than a police officer must be present — a parent or similar |
| 14–18 | The child must **agree who that adult will be** |

## Use it for

- **The rights of children when questioned or arrested** — the dot point with the least evidence
  behind it in most answers, and the one where the protections are most concrete.
- **Discretion**, in its least visible form: the decision about who goes on a list is made by police,
  reviewed by no court, and shaped the child's contact with the justice system more than any
  sentence did.
- **A program that works on one measure and not another** — the Youth Koori Court reduces custody
  without reducing reoffending. Pair with circle sentencing, where the same question got a blunter
  answer.
