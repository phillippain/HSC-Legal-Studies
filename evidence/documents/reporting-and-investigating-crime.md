A reference note.

## Reporting (`crime.2.2`)

Crime reaches police through **victims**, **witnesses**, **mandatory reporters** (child protection,
some professions), **Crime Stoppers** and anonymous information, and **police detection** itself.

**The dark figure of crime** is the part that never gets reported, and it is not evenly distributed.
Sexual assault, domestic violence and fraud are heavily under-reported; motor vehicle theft, which
needs a police report for insurance, is not. The reasons are consistent: fear of the offender, shame,
distrust of police, belief that nothing will be done, and — in domestic violence — fear of what
reporting will set in motion.

This is why **recorded crime statistics measure two things at once** — the incidence of crime and the
willingness to report it. A rise in recorded domestic violence can mean either. Say which you think,
and why.

## Investigating (`crime.2.3`)

**Gathering evidence** — crime scenes (LEPRA ss 88–98: four hours without a warrant, six in rural
areas, and not a dwelling), search and seizure on **reasonable suspicion** (*R v Rondo*), forensic
procedures under the *Crimes (Forensic Procedures) Act 2000* (NSW), and **DNA** (*R v White*).

**Technology** — CCTV, ANPR, phone and metadata, detection dogs (LEPRA ss 145–150, no individual
suspicion needed in an authorised place), and **digital evidence orders** compelling a password
(ss 76AA–76AQ).

**Use of warrants** — the ladder from the individual officer to a Supreme Court judge, with the
covert search warrant at the top.

## Use it for

- **Discretion at the front of the process.** Police choose whether to investigate at all, and no
  mechanism reviews a decision *not* to. Everything downstream — charge, plea, sentence — operates on
  what that choice lets through.
- **Reporting rates as evidence in a DV answer**, beside the ADVO figures and the media campaign
  record: awareness changed reporting, not compliance.
- **Balance** — every investigative power above is a power exercised on people not yet charged, which
  is why the LEPRA safeguards and the s 138 exclusion discretion sit underneath them.
