A single trial mapped across the Crime topic, which is what makes it useful — most cases reach two
or three dot points; this reaches ten.

| Concept | In the case |
|---|---|
| **Nature of crime** | Offences against the person — murder and attempted murder (*Crimes Act 1958* (Vic); the NSW equivalent is s 18 *Crimes Act 1900*) |
| **Elements** | *Actus reus*: serving the poisoned meal. *Mens rea*: intent shown by the separate meal, the search history and the deception |
| **Categories** | Serious indictable offence; multiple counts, tried in the Supreme Court |
| **Parties to a crime** | Sole principal offender — a clean application of liability |
| **Investigation** | Heavy reliance on **circumstantial evidence**: the food dehydrator, search history, statements |
| **Trial process** | Jury trial in the Supreme Court of Victoria; guilty verdicts; **sentencing broadcast live** |
| **Sentencing** | Life imprisonment with a **33-year non-parole period** |
| **Factors in sentencing** | Aggravating: multiple deaths, lack of remorse. Mitigating: hardship of solitary confinement |

## Use it for

- **Proving intent without a confession.** The strongest available illustration that the criminal
  standard can be met on circumstantial evidence alone — and the strongest available objection to it.
  Both halves belong in an answer.
- **Open justice**: sentencing broadcast live is a concrete, recent example of accessibility, and of
  the tension between transparency and a fair trial.
- **Post-sentencing considerations**: a 33-year non-parole period raises the question the public
  debate actually turned on — whether parole should have been available at all.

## Caution

This is a concepts mapping, not a judgment, and the case is **Victorian**, so the offence and
sentencing provisions are not the NSW ones a NSW answer should cite. Verify the sentence and the
non-parole period against the sentencing remarks before quoting them, and remember appeal rights may
still be on foot.
