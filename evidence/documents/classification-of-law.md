A reference note.

## Public law

The state and the individual. **Criminal law** — conduct prohibited because it harms the community.
**Administrative law** — the review of government decisions (*Kioa v West*). **Constitutional law** —
the distribution and limits of government power (*Communist Party Case*, *Kable*, the **Tasmanian Dam
Case**).

## Private (civil) law

Disputes between individuals. **Contract** — enforceable agreements, and the statutory power to
reopen an unjust one (*Khoshaba*). **Tort** — civil wrongs owed at large: negligence
(*Donoghue v Stevenson*), defamation, nuisance, trespass. **Property** — including Torrens title and
the statutory duty of care in the *Design and Building Practitioners Act*. Also family law,
succession, and industrial law.

## Criminal against civil, in three questions

| | Criminal | Civil |
|---|---|---|
| **Who brings it** | The state — the DPP or police | The party who suffered the harm |
| **Standard of proof** | Beyond reasonable doubt | On the balance of probabilities |
| **Outcome** | Guilty or not guilty; punishment | Liable or not; damages, injunction, order |

The same conduct can be both: an assault prosecuted by the state and sued on in tort; a building
defect that is a regulatory offence and a claim under s 37.

## Common and civil law systems

| | Common law (Australia, UK, US, India) | Civil law (France, Germany, Indonesia, most of Europe and Latin America) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary source** | Judge-made law developing case by case, alongside statute | A comprehensive **code** |
| **Precedent** | **Binding** — *stare decisis* | Persuasive at most |
| **Procedure** | **Adversarial** — the judge is an umpire | **Inquisitorial** — the judge directs the investigation |
| **Role of the judge** | Decides between what the parties put | Calls for evidence, questions witnesses |

Some systems are neither or both: **religious legal systems** such as sharia, and **mixed systems**.

## Use it for

- **Classification** (`inv.1.3 / dis.3.1`, `dis.2.3`) answered in the three questions above rather than as a list
  of branches — it is faster to write and harder to get wrong.
- **Common and civil law systems** (`inv.1.3`) with the point that matters: the difference is not which
  country has better law but **who is responsible for finding the facts**, which is the same argument
  made about the adversary system in Crime.
