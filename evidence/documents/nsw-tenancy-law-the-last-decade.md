## 2018 — *Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2018*

- **Minimum standards**: structurally sound, adequate lighting and ventilation, hot and cold water.
- **Faster repairs** to essential services such as water and electricity.
- **Rent increases limited to once every 12 months** in a periodic agreement.

## 2020 — domestic violence protections

- **Immediate termination** of a tenancy without penalty by a victim of domestic violence, on
  providing the prescribed evidence.
- **No liability** for property damage caused by a perpetrator during an incident.

## 2024 — *Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024*, in force 19 May 2025

No-grounds evictions abolished, pets presumed in after 21 days' silence, application fees banned,
a fee-free rent payment method required, and the **Rental Taskforce** created to enforce it.

## Use it for

- **A reform trajectory rather than a single reform.** Each amendment answers a different failure:
  2018 the uninhabitable property, 2020 the tenancy that trapped a victim with a perpetrator, 2024
  the eviction with no reason. Written as a sequence it is a far better answer than any one Act.
- **The 2020 amendment is the cross-topic evidence** — it belongs equally to Family (`fam.2.4`) and
  is the clearest case in the course of housing law and domestic violence law being made to work
  together.
- **Values and ethics**: what changed is not just the rules but the model — from a commercial contract
  between formally equal parties to a regulated relationship where one side's obligations are set by
  statute because bargaining power is not equal.
