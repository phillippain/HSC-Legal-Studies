The ***Law Enforcement (Powers and Responsibilities) Act 2002*** (NSW) came out of a **Wood Royal
Commission** recommendation that NSW police powers be gathered into one law. It was introduced in
2001, passed in 2002 and commenced on **1 December 2005**, and on its own it granted police very
little that was new. The point of this summary is what happened next.

## What has been added since

| | |
|---|---|
| **Emergency public disorder powers** | *Law Enforcement Legislation Amendment (Public Safety) Act 2005*, after the **Cronulla riots**. It carried a **sunset clause** so it would lapse once the riots were no longer a threat. Parliament made it **permanent** |
| **Move-on powers for intoxicated people** | Refusing the direction is finable |
| **Consorting** | *Crimes Amendment (Consorting and Organised Crime) Act 2012* — communicating four or more times with a convicted offender, **maximum 3 years**. The first person convicted was a man on a disability pension who could not read or write; his conviction was overturned and the law stayed |
| **Covert search warrants and gang powers** | |
| **Investigative detention** | *Terrorism (Police Powers) Amendment (Investigative Detention) Act 2016* — a terrorism suspect may be held **up to 14 days without charge**, questioned up to 16 hours a day, **from age 14** |
| **Shoot to kill** | *Terrorism Legislation Amendment (Police Powers and Parole) Act 2017*, after the **Lindt Café siege**, where police said they held off partly for fear of prosecution. Police may use lethal force once the Commissioner declares a "terrorist incident" |
| **Issuing AVOs** | Police gained the power in 2013 |

## Where the law is thinner than it looks

- **Consent searches have no safeguard.** People empty their pockets because they do not know they
  can refuse (***DPP v Leonard*** (2001)). The **LEPRA Amendment Act 2014** required an officer to
  prove they are police and give their name and station — but still **not** to say that the search
  is voluntary.
- **Arrest is supposed to be a last resort** (***DPP v Carr***), yet an Ombudsman inquiry found
  police appear to treat arrest as a way to *begin* investigating rather than to conclude one.
- **Police as decision-maker instead of court.** NSW has the **highest swearing fine in Australia**
  ($500 from 2014). In 2016: **1,836** criminal infringement notices for offensive language, and
  **167 adults and 145 children** before a court — Indigenous people were **17% of those adults and
  26% of those children**, against **3% of the NSW population**. All of this after ***Police v
  Butler*** (2003) held the word is not offensive under the Act, and after the **NSW Law Reform
  Commission** suggested in 2012 that the offence be abolished.
- **Civil remedies are the real check.** Unlawful arrest, detention or search sound in the torts of
  **wrongful arrest, false imprisonment, assault, battery and defamation** — damages after the fact,
  for people who can bring a claim.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.1 (police powers)** — the shape of the whole dot point in one page: a consolidating Act
  and a decade and a half of accretion. The **sunset clause made permanent** is the sharpest single
  fact for "the balance between individual rights and police powers".
- **chg.1 / chg.3 (conditions and mechanisms of reform)** — every one of these powers has a
  triggering event behind it: Cronulla, the Lindt Café siege, bikie gangs, terrorism. A clean
  demonstration that **reform follows crisis**, and that what is enacted in a crisis outlasts it.
- **Discretion into infringement notices** — the offensive language figures let you argue that
  moving an offence from a court to an on-the-spot fine shifts the decision from a magistrate to an
  officer, and that the burden of that shift is not evenly distributed.
- **Compare with the 2026 knife-scanning scheme**, which repeats the pattern exactly: trial, sunset
  clause, made permanent and widened.

## Caution

A **class-made summary**, written to teach a dot point, and it argues a case — the tone is plainly
critical of the expansion, and it should be used as a map of the legislation rather than as a
source in its own right. **Check every Act and figure against the primary source before quoting**:
the citation for *DPP v Leonard* is given only as a year, *Police v Butler* is a Local Court
decision ([2003] NSWLC 2, Heilpern LCM) and so is persuasive only, and the offensive-language and
Indigenous over-representation figures are attributed to BOCSAR and SMH reporting that is not named.
The summary stops in **2017**, so the knife-wanding scheme, strip-search reform and the Law
Enforcement Conduct Commission's work since are all missing. It also refers to the Police Integrity
Commission, which the **LECC replaced in 2017**.
