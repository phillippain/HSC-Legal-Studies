An infographic ranking world cities by the floor area **US$1 million** buys in **prime** residential
real estate, from Knight Frank's *The Wealth Report 2025*. The benchmark it opens with is a
**standard US parking space at 162 sq ft**.

## Where Sydney sits

| | Approx. floor area for US$1m |
|---|---|
| Monaco | 205 sq ft |
| Hong Kong | 237 sq ft |
| Singapore | 344 sq ft |
| London · New York | about 366 sq ft |
| Paris | 452 sq ft |
| **Sydney** | **about 484 sq ft (~45 m²)** |
| Miami · Tokyo | 624 sq ft |
| Berlin | 743 sq ft |
| **Melbourne** | **about 936 sq ft (~87 m²)** |
| Mumbai | 1,066 sq ft |

Greatest **rise** in buying power since 2014: London +43%, Monaco +5%, New York +2%.
Greatest **fall**: Dubai −59%, Miami −54%, Lisbon −51%.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — an international benchmark, which most answers lack. Australian
  affordability is usually argued with Australian figures; this puts **Sydney in the same band as
  Paris and ahead of Berlin, Tokyo and Miami**, which is a far stronger claim than "housing is
  expensive here".
- **The comparison that lands** — a million US dollars buys roughly **three car parking spaces** of
  Sydney apartment. Use it once, as the opening of an affordability paragraph.
- **Sydney against Melbourne** — roughly double the space for the same money, in the same country
  under the same federal tax settings. Useful against any answer that explains affordability by
  Commonwealth policy alone: **planning and land supply are state matters**, and the difference shows
  up here.
- **shel.1.2 / inv.1.4 (right to shelter, equality)** — "prime real estate" is the luxury end, so
  this is not where ordinary buyers are. Its value is as a measure of how far the top of the Sydney
  market has detached, which is the same pressure that pushes everyone down the chain.
- **inv.6.2** — a reminder that housing markets are international: the same capital chooses between
  Sydney, Dubai and Lisbon, which is part of why foreign investment rules appear in the Grattan
  recommendations.

## Caution

**A commercial infographic from a global real estate agency**, not research: Knight Frank sells
prime property, and *The Wealth Report* is marketing as well as data. "Prime" is Knight Frank's own
category (broadly the top 5% of a market) and is **not defined here**, so the figures describe the
luxury end and say nothing directly about median prices or rents — never present them as what a
first home costs. Values are **US dollars**, so the Australian figures move with the exchange rate
as well as with house prices. The file is a **picture with no text layer**, and the city labels sit
beside the bars rather than on them, so an exact value can be misread — **check any specific figure
against the report itself before quoting it**, and prefer the comparison (Sydney near Paris,
Melbourne near Madrid) to the precise square footage.
