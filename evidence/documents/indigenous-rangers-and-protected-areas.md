Since the first Indigenous Protected Area at Nantawarrina (SA) in 1998, Traditional Owners have voluntarily dedicated their land and sea country to conservation under their own management plans, funded by the Commonwealth. Ranger groups — employed through Aboriginal organisations, not government — do the work: cultural burning, weed and feral control, threatened-species monitoring, protection of sites, biosecurity and sea patrols.

## Scale

- **Indigenous Protected Areas**: more than 85 declared, covering tens of millions of hectares — around half of Australia's National Reserve System by area.
- **Rangers**: about 1,900 ranger positions in 2024; the Closing the Gap commitment is to double that to **3,800 by the end of the decade**, with $575.5 million over 2024–28 and an explicit target for women rangers.
- The programs are consistently reported as among the most effective Indigenous policies on both environmental and social measures (employment, health, retention of culture on Country).

## Use it for

- **inv.2.4** — responsibilities in caring for Country given modern institutional form: land and water management by the people whose Lore obliges it.
- **inv.2.2** — Elders and senior knowledge holders directing young rangers; the transmission of Lore as a job.

## Caution

Government funding and declarations do not create the responsibility; they recognise and resource one that exists under Lore. Numbers of IPAs and rangers change yearly — quote NIAA's current figures.
