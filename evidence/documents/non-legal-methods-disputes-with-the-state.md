A reference note.

| Method | How it works | Its limit |
|---|---|---|
| **The media** | Converts an issue with no forum into one a minister must answer | Episodic and uneven — it follows what is visible and dramatic |
| **Members of Parliament** | Constituent representation, questions, petitions, private members' bills, committee references | Depends on the member and on the government's interest |
| **Trade unions** | Collective bargaining, industrial action, test cases, representation in tribunals | Concentrated in some industries and almost absent from others |
| **Interest groups and peak bodies** | Expertise, submissions, campaigns, standing constituency | Influence tracks resources, and the best-resourced group is not always the one most affected |
| **NGOs** | Documenting, reporting, advocating, supplying the evidence base a reform needs | No coercive power at all |

## Worked instances in this finder

- **The media** produced the *Sydney Public Reserves (Public Safety) Act 2017* within weeks of the
  Martin Place encampment — **resolving the issue against the people affected**, which is the
  cautionary half of the story.
- **NGOs**: the **Tenants' Union** makes tenancy rights usable; **AbSec** and **Grandmothers Against
  Removals** hold a department to account where its internal mechanisms did not; the **Lone Fathers
  Association** changed the *Family Law Act* itself.
- **An investigation** — the *SMH*'s freedom of information data and anonymous caseworkers produced
  what departmental reporting did not.

## Use it for

- **Why non-legal methods matter most in disputes with the state.** Legal review is narrow (judicial
  review tests legality, not merit), slow and expensive. A person who cannot meet those thresholds
  still has a member of parliament, a journalist and an advocacy group.
- **Interdependence** — non-legal methods rarely resolve the dispute themselves; they move it to a
  forum that can. That is the sentence to write.
