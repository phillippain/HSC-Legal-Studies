A reference note.

## The common law (`hr.2.10`)

Rights developed by judges and presumed to continue unless a statute clearly removes them: the
**presumption of innocence**, the **right to silence**, **procedural fairness** (*Kioa v West*), the
**principle of legality** — that Parliament is presumed not to abrogate fundamental rights by
ambiguous words — and the rule that a person cannot be detained without lawful authority. *Dietrich*
is the clearest instance: no right to be given a lawyer, but a serious trial without one may be
stayed as unfair.

**The weakness is structural.** A common law right yields to a clear statute. The *Communist Party
Case* is the exception that proves it: only a **constitutional** limit stops Parliament.

## Courts and tribunals (`hr.2.11`)

The **High Court** on constitutional limits and the implied freedom of political communication;
**federal courts** on unlawful discrimination after AHRC conciliation fails; **NCAT** and state
tribunals on state anti-discrimination complaints — cheap and accessible, but **unable to exercise
federal jurisdiction** (*Burns v Corbett*), which is where a discrimination claim under a Commonwealth
Act falls out of reach.

## NGOs and the media in Australia (`hr.2.12`, `hr.2.13`)

Community legal centres, the Tenants' Union, AbSec, Shelter NSW, Amnesty's Australian section — advice,
test cases, submissions and campaigns. The media does the work no institution is obliged to do: the
*SMH*'s child protection investigation, the Marcus foster care case, the Telegraph's front page on
young offenders. Both are **non-coercive** and both have changed statutes in this finder.

## A charter of rights (`hr.2.14`)

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Rights are currently scattered and can be removed by ordinary legislation | Parliament, not unelected judges, should decide contested social questions |
| It would set a standard for new laws and a lens for interpreting old ones | The ACT, Victorian and Queensland Acts are **dialogue** models — courts declare incompatibility, Parliament may ignore it |
| Australia is the **only** liberal democracy without one | Rights would be frozen in the language of one generation |
| It would meet obligations already accepted under the ICCPR | Existing protections — common law, statute, the ombudsmen, a free press — are said to work |

## Use it for

- The **dialogue model** is the detail that separates answers: a declaration of incompatibility does
  not invalidate a law, which answers both the "judges would rule us" objection and the "it would fix
  it" claim.
- **Protection without a charter is protection by inference** — implied freedoms, presumptions of
  interpretation, and the goodwill of Parliament.
