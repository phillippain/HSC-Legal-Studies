A one-page map of where assisted reproductive technology law has moved. Useful because the dot point
rewards **naming an instrument and a date** rather than saying the law lags behind the science.

## The reforms

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **ACT** | ***Assisted Reproductive Technology Act 2024*** (September 2024) — licensing for ART providers, a **donor conception information register**, standards for gamete and embryo storage, with emphasis on donor-conceived people's access to their genetic origins. Amended by the ***ART Amendment Act 2025*** (March 2025) |
| **Queensland** | ***Assisted Reproductive Technology Act 2024*** (September 2024) — mandatory licensing, a donor register with **retrospective records up to 50 years**, a limit of **ten families per donor**, and enforcement powers for Queensland Health |
| **Western Australia** | Review of the *Human Reproductive Technology Act 1991* and the *Surrogacy Act 2008*, guided by a **Ministerial Expert Panel** |
| **NSW** | ***Abortion Law Reform Act 2019*** (commenced 2 October 2019) — decriminalised abortion, terminations to **22 weeks**, and beyond with two medical practitioners |
| **Commonwealth** | **April 2025** — infertility **redefined** so single women and LGBTIQ+ people can access **Medicare rebates** for ART |
| **Commonwealth** | **Maeve's Law** (*Mitochondrial Donation Law Reform Bill 2021*, passed 2022) — permits **mitochondrial donation**, making Australia the second country to legalise it, with a staged rollout through clinical trials |

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3 (surrogacy and birth technologies)** — the reform side of the dot point. Two states
  rewrote their ART statutes in the **same month**, which is as close as Australia gets to
  coordinated reform without a Commonwealth Act.
- **The right to know where you come from** — donor registers, and Queensland's retrospective reach
  back **50 years**, are the clearest evidence that the law now treats the **donor-conceived
  person's** interest as outweighing donor anonymity. That is a changing-values argument with a
  statute attached, and it pairs with *Masson v Parsons* on what a donor is.
- **chg.1 / chg.3 (conditions and mechanisms of reform)** — technology as the condition, and three
  different mechanisms visible at once: state Parliaments legislating, a **ministerial expert panel**
  reviewing, and the Commonwealth moving by **changing a definition** rather than passing an Act.
- **Access as a measure of effectiveness** — the Medicare redefinition shows a rule that excluded
  people by defining the problem narrowly, and the fix being definitional.
- **inv.5.1 / inv.5.2 (division of powers)** — ART is state-regulated, Medicare and mitochondrial donation are
  Commonwealth. Same field, two legislatures, and the patchwork that follows.

## Caution

A **class-made survey**, not an authority, and its value is as a **checklist of things to verify**.
**Check every Act, title and date against the legislation register before quoting** — short summaries
like this routinely garble a short title or a commencement date, and companion documents in this
library do (one calls Queensland's surrogacy statute the "Surrogacy Act 2008 (Qld)" when it is the
*Surrogacy Act 2010* (Qld)). The WA review is described as under way and may have reported since.
The abortion reform is included here because the file is about reproductive law generally; it is
**not** part of the surrogacy and birth technologies dot point and should not be used as though it
is. Nothing here is sourced or footnoted.
