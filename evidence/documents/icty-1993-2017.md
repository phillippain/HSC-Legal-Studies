The UN Security Council created the ICTY in **1993**, during the war, to try crimes committed in the
former Yugoslavia since 1991. It closed on **31 December 2017**; the Residual Mechanism (IRMCT)
finished its appeals.

| Figure | |
|---|---|
| Indicted | **161** — every one accounted for |
| Sentenced | **90** |
| Acquitted | **19** |
| Years | **24** (1993–2017) |
| Cost | More than **US$2 billion** |

## Three accused

- **Radovan Karadžić** (Bosnian Serb political leader): convicted of genocide at Srebrenica in 2016 and
  sentenced to **40 years**; the appeal increased it to **life (2019)**. Some department materials still
  give 40 years.
- **Ratko Mladić** (military commander): **life (2017)**, upheld on appeal in **2021**.
- **Slobodan Milošević** (former President of Serbia): **died in 2006** before a verdict.

## Use it for

- **One fact used both ways**: 161 accounted for shows accountability is possible; 24 years and
  US$2 billion show how slow and costly it is.
- **Precedent**: rape was recognised as a crime against humanity and a means of genocide; the
  tribunal's case law shaped the Rome Statute.
- **Enforcement**: arrests depended on Serbia's cooperation, which came largely through EU membership
  pressure, a point about **compliance** rather than law.
