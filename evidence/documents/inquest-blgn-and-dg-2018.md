A joint inquest into two sisters born to the same mother, AD. **BLGN died at three months in April
2014**, in her mother's care. **DG died at nineteen days in June 2015**, while **under the control of
the Minister for Family and Community Services**. Cause of death was undetermined in both, possibly
**Sudden Unexpected Death in Infancy**.

AD had a serious **methamphetamine addiction**, as did her mother, her ex-partner and his mother.
Evidence described **ice binges** during which mother and grandmother slept for days, leaving the
children unattended. Between **2010 and 2015** numerous reports reached the **Department of Family and
Community Services**: drug use in front of the children, association with criminal activity, neglect,
refusal of assistance, and unsuitable housing. A **Mission Australia caseworker**, as a **mandatory
reporter**, repeatedly told FACS the children were at risk of significant harm.

**None of the reports were acted on, because of the department's caseload.**

## Use it for

- **Care and protection** (`fam.3.4`) — the failure was not of law but of capacity, and here it is
  documented by a coroner rather than alleged. Mandatory reporting worked; the response did not.
- **The second death is the harder fact**: DG died while the Minister already had control. State care
  is not by itself protection.
- **The reform chain**: the **Wood Special Commission of Inquiry into Child Protection Services
  (2007–08)** produced the **"Keep Them Safe"** initiative, and these deaths followed it. Set that
  against the 2023 *SMH* investigation (9,000 unassessed reports) and **Family Is Culture** (12 of 126
  recommendations implemented) to argue that the reform stage that fails is implementation.
- A **coronial inquest** as a legal response in its own right (`ind.2.7`): it cannot punish or
  compensate, only find facts and recommend — which is exactly its strength and its limit.
