**YiSSA**, a 400-bed short-stay facility on Batten Road, Darwin, run since 2020 by the Aboriginal
community-controlled **Yilli Rreung**, housed people experiencing homelessness, medical travellers
(including dialysis patients) and family-violence survivors — **233 residents including 85 children**
in September 2026. The NT Government gave 90 days' notice in July after no agreement on the site's
price, rejected Commonwealth offers of $6.5m then $3.5m (conditional on Yilli Rreung running it), and
on **11–12 October 2026** closed it, bussing residents to a new **~150-place** facility at
**Batchelor, about 100 km south**, run by Mission Australia (reportedly chosen without tender). Only
**23–30** people had moved there in the first days; a federal minister said 109 were unaccounted for.

On **19 October** five Commissioners — Social Justice (Katie Kiss), Race (Giridharan Sivaraman), Sex
(Anna Cody), Disability (Rosemary Kayess) and Children's (Deb Tsorbaris) — wrote to the Chief
Minister seeking urgent talks about safeguards to avoid breaching human rights obligations and
Commonwealth discrimination laws.

## What the AHRC can do here, and where it stops

| Function | Source | Reach in this case |
|---|---|---|
| Promote discussion; inquire into acts inconsistent with human rights | *AHRC Act* s 11(1) | A **letter and media release** — no legal force |
| Conciliate unlawful discrimination complaints | Racial, Sex, Disability Discrimination Acts | Possible in principle against a territory government; untested here |
| Investigate human rights complaints (ICCPR, CRC, CRPD) and report to the Attorney-General | *AHRC Act* Part II | **Commonwealth acts only** — the NT Government is outside it |

The relevant rights are the right to adequate housing (**ICESCR art 11(1)**, CRC art 27, CRPD art
28), which UN guidance says is not met where housing cuts people off from health care, schools and
work. The release names none of them.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.4 / ind.2.7 (statutory bodies)** — the sharpest current example of the AHRC's
  **advocacy-not-enforcement** position, and of the Commonwealth/state gap in its complaint power.
- **hr.2.7 / hr.2.14** — economic and social rights (housing) have no domestic statutory home; the
  Commissioners could invoke only discrimination law. A charter-of-rights argument in one case.
- **shel.3.3 / shel.1.2 / shel.1.3** — homelessness and the (absent) obligation to provide shelter:
  NT homelessness is **564 per 10,000** against 48 nationally (2021 Census).
- **ls.2.9 / self-determination** — an Aboriginal-run service replaced by a non-Aboriginal operator
  without consultation; Danila Dilba, Aboriginal Housing NT, NAAJA and NAAFLS objected.
- **Competing perspectives** — NT: paying the asking price would waste taxpayers' money, Batchelor
  offers food, care, clothing, shelter and case management, dialysis patients stay near Darwin;
  critics: fewer beds for more money ($18.2m v $12.7m, Opposition figures), residents discharged to
  unsafe partners or communities without dialysis, the NT's own homelessness strategy undermined.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of **advocacy by a rights body, not a finding of breach**. The release has
no figures and does not state the NT's reasons; most background comes from one outlet (NIT), and
several numbers come from a federal minister opposed to the decision. Closure dates differ by a day,
capacity is given as 300 or 400 beds, and cost figures are the Opposition's. Whether a discrimination
complaint against the NT could proceed was not checked. Look for the NT's own statements and later
Batchelor numbers before relying on counts.
