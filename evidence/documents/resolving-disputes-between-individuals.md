A reference note. The worked instances — NCAT's caseload, the closure of the Community Justice
Centres, Family Relationship Centres — are recorded separately.

## Alternative dispute resolution

| Method | Who decides | Binding? |
|---|---|---|
| **Negotiation** | The parties | Only if they agree |
| **Mediation** | The parties, helped by a neutral facilitator who does not advise | Agreement only |
| **Conciliation** | The parties, helped by a conciliator who **does** suggest solutions and knows the field | Agreement only |
| **Arbitration** | The **arbitrator**, on the evidence | **Yes** — and enforceable as a judgment |

ADR is cheaper, faster, private and preserves the relationship. Its weaknesses are that it produces
**no precedent**, is **unsupervised**, and rewards the party with better information or more staying
power — which is why it is a poor fit where there is violence or a serious power imbalance.

## Courts and tribunals

**Tribunals** — **NCAT** for tenancy, consumer, strata, guardianship and anti-discrimination matters;
the **Administrative Review Tribunal** for Commonwealth decisions since October 2024. Low fees, no
need for a lawyer, informal procedure.

**Courts** — the **Local Court** to $100,000, the **District Court** to $750,000, the **Supreme
Court** with unlimited civil jurisdiction. Formal procedure, the rules of evidence, and **costs
usually follow the event**, which is the real barrier: losing means paying the other side's lawyers.

**Class actions** are the exception worth naming: they make a claim viable that no individual could
afford to bring alone.

## Use it for

- **The jurisdictional ladder** is a cost ladder. Where a person's dispute is heard depends on its
  value, and the protections available to them change at each step — the point most answers miss.
- **Mediation against arbitration** — the distinction that matters is *who decides*, and whether the
  outcome can be enforced.
- **Accessibility against justice**: an informal, cheap forum without lawyers is more accessible and
  gives a self-represented party less protection. Both halves are true, and saying only one is the
  usual middle-band answer.
