## The courts

The *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) created no-fault divorce and the **Family Court**. The **Federal
Circuit Court** followed in **1999** as a cheaper, simpler option, and circuited to regional areas —
real access, since it removed the need for country litigants and their lawyers to travel to a capital.

Then the numbers went the other way. In **2007–08**, 95.8% of family law matters filed in the Federal
Circuit Court were finalised within twelve months. By **2018** the Attorney-General's department
reported the backlog rising from **17,200 to 21,000** matters, and the median time to trial from
**10.8 to 15.2 months** in the Federal Circuit Court and **11.5 to 17 months** in the Family Court.
*The Australian* reported costs of about **$110,000** to resolve a case in the Family Court and about
**$30,000** in the Federal Circuit Court.

The two courts were **amalgamated into the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia**, ending the
overlapping jurisdiction that had produced delay, cost and inconsistency.

## Family Relationship Centres

Australia ratified the **CROC** in 1990; the best interests of the child became the paramount
consideration, and **FRCs** were introduced in **2006** — **65** of them nationally. An independent
mediator helps parties negotiate separation and parenting arrangements outside court. They provide
**free information, referrals and a first hour of dispute resolution**; for people earning over
**$50,000**, **$30 an hour** applies for the second and third hours.

## Use it for

- **Dispute resolution** (`fam.2.5`) with the comparison made in dollars and months rather than
  adjectives — $30 an hour against $110,000, one free hour against seventeen months to trial.
- **Why mediation is preferred in family law specifically**: the parties keep dealing with each other
  for years, and an adversarial hearing produces "winners and losers" in a relationship that has to
  continue.
- **Access as a moving target** — the Federal Circuit Court was created to improve access and had
  itself become a bottleneck within a decade. Reform does not hold still.

## Caution

The figures are from a teaching series written around 2018–19, which anticipated the merger taking
effect on **1 January 2019**. It did not: the **Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia** began
on **1 September 2021**. Use the FCFCA name and that date, and treat the caseload and cost figures as
2018 rather than current.
