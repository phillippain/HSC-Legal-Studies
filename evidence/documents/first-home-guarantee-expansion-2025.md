The First Home Guarantee lets a buyer purchase with a **5% deposit**, with the government
guaranteeing up to 15% so no Lenders Mortgage Insurance is payable.

## What changed on 1 October 2025

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit | 5% | **5% — unchanged** |
| Income caps | Singles ≤$125,000; couples ≤$200,000 | **Removed** |
| Price cap, Sydney | $900,000 | **$1.5 million** |
| Price cap, Melbourne / Brisbane | $800,000 / $700,000 | **$950,000 / $1 million** |
| Eligibility | First-home buyers within the caps | All first-home buyers, regardless of income |
| **Places** | about **35,000 a year** | **Still about 35,000 a year** |

The scheme sits under the ***National Housing Finance and Investment Corporation Act 2018*** (Cth)
(the body since renamed Housing Australia). **The Act did not change** — the expansion was done
through **policy levers and ministerial instruments**.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — the cleanest example in the topic of a measure that looks like it
  widens access and may not. Eligibility was broadened and the number of places was not, so the
  scheme now rations the **same 35,000 spots** among a much larger pool, and raising the Sydney cap
  to $1.5m points the help at people buying more expensive homes.
- **inv.3.4 (delegated legislation)** — an unusually tidy illustration. A significant change to who
  the Commonwealth helps into home ownership, made **without amending the Act**, by instruments made
  under it. Good for the advantages (speed, flexibility) and the criticisms (less scrutiny, no
  parliamentary debate) of delegated legislation.
- **chg.3.2 / chg.4.2 (executive methods, limitations of reform)** — reform by ministerial
  instrument, and a reform whose limits are built into its design.
- **Demand-side versus supply-side** — set directly against the Grattan analysis, which argues that
  measures increasing buyers' purchasing power without increasing supply push prices up. This scheme
  is exactly that kind of measure, which makes it a good test case rather than a settled answer.
- **Equity (inv.1.4)** — a scheme available to a buyer on any income, for a home up to $1.5m,
  against renters in housing stress who are not helped at all. Pair with the Anglicare snapshot.

## Caution

A **class-made comparison table**, not an official source, dated **25 August 2025** and describing
changes that were to start on 1 October — so it records the **announced** scheme, not necessarily the
one that operated. **Check the current caps, income rules and place numbers against Housing
Australia before quoting any figure.** The analysis in it ("primarily benefits higher-income
buyers", "may worsen" affordability) is the author's argument attributed loosely to "experts", not a
finding — treat it as a position to test, and find a named economist or a Grattan or Productivity
Commission analysis if you want to assert it. The document does not name the instruments that made
the change, so verify the delegated-legislation point before relying on it.
