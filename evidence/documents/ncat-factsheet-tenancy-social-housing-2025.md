A short official fact sheet from NCAT's Consumer and Commercial Division on tenancy and social housing applications.

## What it says

- After a party applies, both parties receive a notice of the hearing day, time and venue, usually within **4 to 6 weeks**.
- The other party receives a copy of the application and its attachments, so confidential material should not be included.
- Matters are heard in a **group list** alongside other matters, and parties are **asked to try to reach agreement** before the hearing proceeds. If the matter is not resolved at the first listing it may be adjourned to a formal hearing.
- Hearings are run **as informally as possible**, and parties are encouraged to present their own cases, with evidence such as the lease, rent records, bond information, condition reports, correspondence and photos.
- Parties must comply with Tribunal orders about providing evidence before the hearing.

## Use it for

- **ADR in practice** (`shel.2.7`): the first step in most tenancy disputes is a negotiated settlement in the group list, not a contested hearing. *Kelly v NSW LAHC* shows what that can lead to (a short consent hearing that was later hard to undo).
- **Dispute resolution mechanisms and effectiveness** (`shel.2.5`): informal, low-cost process, set against the pressure of a short group-list conciliation for an unrepresented tenant.

## Caution

The fact sheet **does not mention mediation, consent orders or the legislation behind them**, and it gives no fee amounts. Do not cite it for how conciliation is run beyond what is stated above. NCAT's site has separate conciliation and mediation pages that I could not read.
