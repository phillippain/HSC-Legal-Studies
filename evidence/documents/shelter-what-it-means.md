## The definition

**Shelter** is protection from the elements and a place to live — at its widest, the **adequate
housing** of **ICESCR art 11**, which CESCR General Comment No 4 reads as requiring legal security of
tenure, services, affordability, habitability, accessibility, location and cultural adequacy. Not
merely a roof: a home a person can stay in.

## The tenure ladder — and the protection attached to each rung

| Tenure | Governing law | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| **Registered proprietor** | *Real Property Act 1900* (NSW) — Torrens, indefeasibility | Near absolute |
| **Strata or community title owner** | *SSMA 2015* | Strong, plus by-laws made by neighbours |
| **Tenant** | *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW) | Substantial, and stronger since 2024 |
| **Boarder or lodger** | **Excluded by s 8(c) RTA**; *Boarding Houses Act 2012* covers registered premises only | Thin |
| **Land lease and retirement village resident** | Their own Acts | Particular and partial |
| **Social housing tenant** | RTA plus policy; review by the Housing Appeals Committee | Substantial, with allocation decisions outside the courts |
| **Person sleeping rough** | Trespass, move-on powers, public space law | **None** — the law regulates their presence rather than protecting their shelter |

## Use it for

- **The proposition the whole option turns on**: effectiveness tracks **tenure, not need**. The person
  with the most secure shelter has the most legal protection of it.
- **Definition as an argument.** Define shelter narrowly as accommodation and the law looks effective;
  define it as ICESCR art 11 does and affordability, location and security of tenure all come into
  scope, and it does not. Say which definition you are using, and why.
