## The weakness all three share — time

| Case | Internal objection stage | Tribunal stage | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Devine and Kitson | 10 months | 8 months | **18 months** |
| Glancey and Glancey | **20 months** | 8 months | **28 months** |
| Palgrave and Blackwall | 14 months | 11 months | **25 months** |

In every case the **internal** stage — the agency reviewing itself — took longer than the
independent review that followed. Meanwhile the scheme requires parents to notify a change of care
within days and to lodge a review application within 28.

## Use it for

1. **Enforcement here is administrative, not judicial.** No court appears in any of the three, yet
   the obligations are real and enforceable. Any answer that treats "the family law system" as
   meaning "the Federal Circuit and Family Court" is missing most of it.
2. **Outcomes turn on statutory preconditions, not on fairness** — which income figure is higher,
   whether both parents intended a payment in lieu, whether the Tribunal has jurisdiction at all.
3. **A set of three decided by one member in eight days** is unusually good evidence: the variation
   in outcome cannot be explained by who decided them.

## Caution

Three tribunal decisions are not a statistical sample, and pseudonyms mean the parties cannot be
traced. Use them for what a merits review *is*, and for delay, rather than for how common any
outcome is.
