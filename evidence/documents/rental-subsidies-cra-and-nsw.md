The **demand-side** half of the Australian response to rental stress, and the part most Shelter
answers leave out. Two governments, two instruments, and neither of them touches the rent.

## Commonwealth Rent Assistance (CRA)

A **non-taxable income supplement** paid by **Services Australia** on top of a qualifying income
support payment (JobSeeker, Age Pension, Disability Support Pension, Youth Allowance, Parenting
Payment) to people who **rent privately or in community housing** and pay rent above a minimum
threshold. It rises progressively once rent passes the base threshold, up to a **capped maximum
rate**, and is **indexed to CPI**.

Between 2023 and 2025 the Commonwealth delivered **staged increases above normal indexation** —
described as the largest boost in over 30 years. (Grattan puts the rise at **27% above inflation**
over two budgets, and notes that rents paid by recipients have grown nearly **1.5 times faster than
the maximum rate since 2001**.)

**The structural point: CRA is a demand-side subsidy and does not regulate rent levels.** Because it
is capped and indexed to general inflation rather than to rents, it falls behind whenever rents rise
faster than CPI. Advocacy groups argue for indexation to a **rental price index** instead.

## The NSW programs — Homes NSW

- **Private Rental Subsidy (PRS)** — covers the gap between **affordable rent** (around 25–30% of
  income) and **market rent**, is usually **time-limited** (up to about three years depending on the
  stream), and is **paid directly to the landlord**.
- **Rent Choice** — transitional assistance into private rental, with income-based contributions, for
  **young people, veterans, people escaping domestic and family violence**, and social housing
  applicants.
- **Temporary Accommodation** — short-term emergency accommodation for people experiencing
  homelessness.

Administration has been **consolidated under Homes NSW**, and the state has signalled expanded
Rent Choice eligibility, review of the time limits in high-cost areas, and **rental bond portability**
to cut the upfront cost of moving.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.2 / shel.3.4 — name the actual mechanisms.** "Governments provide rental assistance" is a
  weak sentence. "Commonwealth Rent Assistance, administered by Services Australia, is capped and
  CPI-indexed, while the NSW Private Rental Subsidy pays the gap between 25–30% of income and market
  rent directly to the landlord, for up to three years" is a strong one.
- **The best structural criticism in the Shelter option** — a subsidy **indexed to CPI** chasing a
  rent **set by the market**. It explains why assistance can rise 27% above inflation and still leave
  two in three renting retirees in poverty. Pair it with `smh-2025-renting-retirees-grattan`.
- **Demand-side versus supply-side (shel.3.1)** — subsidies raise what a tenant can pay; they do not
  create a dwelling, and may be partly **capitalised into higher rents**. Set this record against the
  Housing Australia Future Fund and the planning records for a complete picture of the response.
- **Federal cooperation (inv.6.4 / shel.t.cooperation)** — income support is **Commonwealth**, crisis
  and transitional housing is **state**. Neither can solve it alone, and the National Housing Accord
  is the attempt to coordinate them. A clean cooperative-federalism example.
- **Reactive, not preventative** — PRS and Rent Choice are triggered by being **at risk of
  homelessness**, and most are **time-limited**, so a household can face renewed insecurity when
  assistance ends. Good for "does the law meet society's needs?".
- **Targeting as substantive equality (shel.3.2)** — Rent Choice streams for DFV survivors, young
  people and veterans. Use it where a question asks about responses to particular groups.
- **Paid to the landlord** — PRS goes to the landlord, not the tenant. Worth a sentence on who the
  subsidy really protects.

## Caution

This is an **unattributed explainer in the library** and it must be treated as a lead, not a source.
Two specific problems. First, its **reference list is unusable** — entries such as "ABC News. (2025).
Analysis of rent assistance increases and rental pressures" are generic placeholders, not citations,
and the document reads as having been drafted with AI assistance; **verify every figure** before
using it. Second, its forward-looking sections ("forthcoming / proposed changes", indexation review,
expanded Rent Choice streams, bond portability) describe **policy discussion, not enacted law** —
never present them as current entitlements. The figures in the attached model paragraph (122,000
homeless in the 2021 Census, 170,000 households on social housing waiting lists, vacancy rates below
1%) are **plausible but unsourced here**; take them from the ABS, the Productivity Commission's
*Report on Government Services* and the relevant market data directly. **Rates, thresholds and
eligibility change every indexation cycle**: check Services Australia and Homes NSW for the current
figures rather than quoting this document. The "approximately 15%" increase it gives for 2023–25
differs from Grattan's "27% above inflation over two budgets" — they measure different things, so use
Grattan's framing and cite Grattan for it.
