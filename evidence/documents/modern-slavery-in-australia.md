## The continuum

**Fair work conditions** — decent work, fair treatment, labour rights respected, appropriate pay.
**Poor work conditions** — discrimination, underpayment, limited safeguards, labour rights not
respected. **Modern slavery practices** — restriction on freedom, severe violation of human rights,
deception or coercion.

The continuum is **dynamic**: where a situation sits on it moves over time, and can become severe.
That is the analytical point — the criminal law draws a bright line where the reality is a gradient,
so the offences catch the extreme and the enforcement gap sits in the middle.

## The offences

Modern slavery is an umbrella term for practices in **Divisions 270 and 271 of the *Criminal Code Act
1995* (Cth)**, including:

- **forced labour** — a person is not free to stop working or leave their place of work;
- **servitude** — not free to leave, **and** freedom significantly restricted; **domestic servitude**
  where that occurs in domestic work;
- **slavery**, **debt bondage**, **deceptive recruiting**, **trafficking in persons**, **forced
  marriage** and **organ trafficking**.

The **International Labour Organization** administers the international labour standards against
which national definitions are measured, and what counts as criminal exploitation differs between
countries — which is what makes supply chains hard to police.

## Use it for

- **A contemporary human rights issue** (`hr.3.1`) with precise legal content, rather than assertion:
  name the Division, name the practice, say which right it engages.
- **International crime** (`crime.6.1`) — trafficking and slavery are the clearest crimes against the
  international community with a domestic offence attached.
- The **continuum** is the idea to carry into an evaluation: underpayment and visa-tied work are not
  offences under Div 270, so the law's answer to most exploitation is industrial, not criminal.
