Eddie Koiki Mabo, a Torres Strait Islander working as a gardener at James Cook University, learned from historians Henry Reynolds and Noel Loos that under Australian law he did not own the land on Mer (Murray Island) that his family had held for generations. A 1981 land rights conference at the university led to the claim.

## The road

- **20 May 1982**: Eddie Mabo, David Passi, Sam Passi, James Rice and Celuia Mapo Salee file in the High Court seeking a declaration of their traditional ownership of Mer.
- **1985**: Queensland passes the *Queensland Coast Islands Declaratory Act*, purporting to extinguish retrospectively any Islander rights to the islands.
- **1988 — Mabo (No 1)**: the High Court holds the Queensland Act invalid as inconsistent with the *Racial Discrimination Act 1975* (Cth) — the case can continue.
- **1986–1990**: the Supreme Court of Queensland (Moynihan J) hears 67 days of evidence on the facts of Meriam land tenure; Eddie Mabo's own claim to particular land is doubted on the evidence, but the system of ownership is established.
- **21 January 1992**: Eddie Mabo dies of cancer. **3 June 1992**: the High Court (6:1) rejects *terra nullius* and recognises native title.

## Caution

The decision is about the Meriam people's title to Mer; it did not grant land to anyone else — the *Native Title Act 1993* created the process for other claims. The case is the Torres Strait Islander example in a syllabus that asks for both Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander experiences.
