A reference note.

## Developing recognition (`hr.1.2`)

The abolition of **slavery**; **trade unionism** and labour rights; **universal suffrage**; **universal
education**; **self-determination** and decolonisation; **environmental rights** and the rights of
**peace**. The pattern is consistent: a movement first, recognition second, enforcement last.

## The United Nations (`hr.2.2`)

| Organ | What it does | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **General Assembly** | One state one vote; declarations and resolutions | **Not binding** |
| **Security Council** | Binding resolutions, sanctions, authorisation of force | **Five vetoes** |
| **ICJ** | Disputes between states | Only where states accept jurisdiction |
| **ICC** | Individual criminal responsibility for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes | No police force; depends on state cooperation |
| **Human Rights Council and treaty bodies** | Universal Periodic Review, individual communications where a state has accepted them | Findings are **views**, not orders |

## Intergovernmental organisations (`hr.2.3`)

Regional bodies with real machinery — the **European Court of Human Rights** (individual petition,
binding judgments), the **Council of Europe**, the **African Union**, the **Organization of American
States**. Also the **ILO**, which sets the labour standards used to judge exploitation, and the
**Commonwealth of Nations**. Asia and the Pacific have **no regional human rights court**, which is
why an Australian has no equivalent of *McCann* or *Yordanova* to bring.

## NGOs (`hr.2.5`) and the media (`hr.2.6`)

**Amnesty International**, **Human Rights Watch**, the **International Committee of the Red Cross**,
**Médecins Sans Frontières**. They have **no coercive power** and work by **documenting, reporting and
shaming** — producing the evidence base that states and IGOs then act on.

The media does the same at speed and scale: the **Four Corners** food supply chain investigation put
child labour in Ghanaian cocoa and wage theft in Australian horticulture in front of the people who
buy the product. Its limits are that attention is **episodic**, follows what is **visible**, and can
be shut out by a state that controls access.

## Use it for

- **The narrowing** — recognition is effective, promotion is partly effective, **enforcement fails**.
  That is the structure to organise a whole answer around.
- **Sovereignty as the explanation, not the excuse.** Enforcement is weak because states consent to be
  bound and can withdraw consent; the answer that names the mechanism beats the answer that calls it
  disappointing.
