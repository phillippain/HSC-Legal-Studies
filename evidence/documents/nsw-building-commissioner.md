**David Chandler** was appointed the first NSW Building Commissioner in **2019**, with five decades
in the industry behind him, after Opal Tower and in the month Mascot Towers was evacuated.

## What was built

- The office grew from a small team into a **standalone regulator of more than 400 professionals**,
  launched as **Building Commission NSW in December 2023** — a single authority for building
  regulation, licensing, oversight and enforcement.
- About **1,400 proactive inspections** across metropolitan and regional NSW, on home building and
  residential apartments, reported alongside a **20% reduction in waterproofing defects** and a **7%
  decrease in structural defects** since 2021–22.
- A published **library of common defects**, so builders can identify and fix problems early.
- **Enhanced powers** to find defects *before* a building is finished and to require rectification in
  time — which in practice means the Commission can stop a developer obtaining an **occupation
  certificate**.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.8 (the role of government organisations)** — the best worked example in the Shelter option
  of a government response that is **institutional rather than legislative**: the Acts set the
  standards, but it was creating a regulator with staff and powers that changed anything.
- **Where the power actually bites** — withholding the occupation certificate. The developer cannot
  settle sales without it, so the leverage is commercial and arrives **before** the owners are
  exposed, unlike litigation after the fact. Contrast with *Brookfield Multiplex* and *Pafburn*,
  where the remedy comes years later.
- **Prevention against remedy (shel.2.5)** — set this record against the owner's position in a
  defects dispute. Which response actually protects a purchaser?
- **Resource efficiency** — 40 staff to 400 is the clearest "legal responses need resources"
  illustration in the topic. Pair with the $59 million annual running cost and the $700 million in
  annual defect costs from the 2025 SMH series.
- **ls.4.3 (mechanisms of reform)** — executive action as a mechanism alongside parliaments and
  courts.

## Caution

A **class-made summary**, not an official source. It reads as **promotional**: the achievements are
listed without any of the criticism that the 2025 *Shoddy Sydney* series documents, and the
improvement figures (20% waterproofing, 7% structural) come from **NSW Government reporting of its
own performance** — check them against Building Commission NSW's published data before quoting. Its
citations are to secondary sites (Sourceable, Flat Chat) rather than to the statutory instruments,
so **verify the Commission's powers against the legislation**, and note the summary does not say
which Act confers them. Chandler **retired in 2024** and was succeeded by **James Sherrard**, who
takes a visibly different view of whether a crisis remains — the summary does not mention either.
