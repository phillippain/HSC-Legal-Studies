## The scheme

NSW switched on the world's first **mobile phone detection cameras** in **December 2019**, with
warning letters for three months and fines from **March 2020**. Software flags likely offences and a
person reviews each image before a penalty notice is issued. From **1 July 2024** the same cameras
also detect **seatbelt** offences.

## The numbers (NRMA, September 2025)

| | January–July 2024 | January–July 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Phone fines | 102,000+ | 80,000+ (about $40 million) |
| Seatbelt notices | about 28,000 | nearly 70,000 |

Most seatbelt notices were for wearing a belt **incorrectly** rather than not wearing one. A phone
offence carries a fine and **5 demerit points** (10 in double-demerit periods).

## Use it for

- **Strict liability** (`crime.1.3`): the prosecution need prove only the act (holding or using the
  phone, r 300), not that the driver meant to break the law. The only defence is an honest and
  reasonable mistake of fact.
- **Driving offences** (`crime.1.5`): the most common offences in NSW, dealt with by penalty notice.
- **Situational crime prevention** (`crime.1.9`): raises the risk of detection. The fall in phone
  fines from 2024 to 2025 is consistent with deterrence, though fewer fines could also reflect
  camera placement.
- **Rights**: automated surveillance of every passing driver, and fines that weigh more heavily on
  people with low incomes.
