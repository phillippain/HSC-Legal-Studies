A summary, with an evidence table, of a BBC piece by **Claire Fenton-Glynn** (a Cambridge academic
who has written on international surrogacy). It is the global framing document for this part of the
course.

## The argument

- Surrogacy has grown worldwide on the back of **IVF**, changing social attitudes and people
  **delaying childbearing**.
- Two types: **gestational** (the surrogate carries an embryo that is not from her egg) and
  **traditional** (her own egg is used).
- **Child welfare risk** — the **Baby Gammy** case, where a child with Down's syndrome born via a
  Thai surrogate was allegedly left behind by the commissioning parents.
- **Exploitation of surrogates** — women in poorer countries drawn by money, sometimes under strict
  and unfair conditions, including requirements about behaviour and the risk of **going unpaid after
  a miscarriage**.
- **Cross-border legal chaos** — countries disagree about who the legal parents are, what rights the
  surrogate has, and what citizenship the child holds. Children can end up **stateless** or with
  parenthood unresolved.
- Inconsistency produces "**health tourism**": commissioning parents travelling to more permissive or
  commercial jurisdictions.
- The remedy proposed is **international regulation**, for example through the **Hague Conference on
  Private International Law**, setting consistent rules on recognition of parenthood, protection of
  surrogates and the welfare of children.

The attached table sorts the issues into **parenthood and legal recognition**, **children's rights
and welfare**, **surrogates' rights and exploitation**, and **cross-border legal challenges**, with
the mechanisms and an evaluation for each — including the honest conclusion that international
agreements "could help, but cultural/legal differences make them hard to negotiate".

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3 — the international dimension, which most answers omit entirely.** Australian surrogacy
  evidence is almost all domestic. This gives you the global structure: no treaty, eight regimes in
  Australia alone, and parents moving between jurisdictions to find a permissive one.
- **Two victims, not one (fam.t.values)** — the **surrogate** who may be unpaid after a miscarriage,
  and the **child** who may be stateless. Naming both is what separates a sophisticated answer from
  one that only sympathises with intended parents.
- **Baby Gammy as the anchor case** — pair it with the record on *Farnell & Chanbua* for the
  judgment, and use this for why that case changed the global conversation.
- **Why international law struggles here (hr.2.2 / inv.6.5)** — surrogacy touches family status,
  citizenship and morality, all of which states guard closely, so a Hague instrument is slow. Good
  for the limits of international law generally, not just on this issue.
- **"Health tourism" as a concept** — a single phrase that explains the Colombian surrogacy record,
  the sex selection record and the DPP referral record at once. Use it as the organising idea for a
  paragraph covering all three.
- **CROC links** — a child's right to **identity and nationality** (arts 7–8) and protection from
  **sale and exploitation**, against arrangements that can leave both unresolved.

## Caution

This is a **summary of a journalistic piece, not the piece itself**, prepared for teaching: the
evidence table and the "evaluation" column are the summariser's framing, and some characters have
been mangled in extraction. **Attribute it as a summary** and go to the BBC article, or better to
Fenton-Glynn's published work, before quoting. It is **undated on its face** and the underlying
article is from around **2019**, so nothing in it reflects developments since — Thailand, India,
Nepal and Cambodia have all restricted or banned commercial surrogacy for foreigners since, and the
Hague Conference's parentage/surrogacy project has continued. The **Baby Gammy** allegations as
reported in the media were **not all made out**: the Western Australian Family Court in *Farnell &
Chanbua* [2016] FCWA 17 found the parents had not abandoned him, so use this record for the
international argument and *Farnell & Chanbua* for the facts. "Selling babies" is the BBC headline's
framing, not a legal characterisation. No numbers in this summary are sourced; do not quote figures
from it.
