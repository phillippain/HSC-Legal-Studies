## Where an Australian's rights come from

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| **The Constitution — express** | s 41 (voting), s 51(xxxi) acquisition **on just terms**, s 80 trial by jury for Commonwealth indictable offences, s 116 (no establishment of religion), s 117 (no discrimination by state of residence) |
| **The Constitution — implied** | The implied freedom of **political communication** — a restriction on legislative power, not a personal right |
| **Statute** | Anti-discrimination Acts (Cth and NSW), the *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth), fair work and consumer law, the *Bail Act*, LEPRA safeguards |
| **Common law** | Procedural fairness, the presumption of innocence, the right to silence, legal professional privilege, the right of a person not to be detained without lawful authority |
| **International law** | ICCPR, ICESCR, CROC, CRPD — **not directly enforceable** here without implementing legislation |

**There is no national bill or charter of rights.** The ACT, Victoria and Queensland have human rights
Acts; NSW does not. That is the central fact of this dot point, and everything else follows from it:
rights are **residual** — a person may do anything not prohibited — and Parliament can narrow them by
ordinary legislation.

## Rights and responsibilities

Every right implies a responsibility, and the pairing is easier to argue with examples than in the
abstract:

- the right to a **fair trial** carries the duty to serve on a **jury** and to give evidence when
  summonsed;
- the right to **vote** carries **compulsory** enrolment and attendance at the polls;
- the right to **drive** carries licensing, registration and the duty of care to other road users;
- the right to **free speech** stops at defamation, contempt and incitement.

## Use it for

- **The nature of individual rights** (`inv.7.2 / inv.7.3`) — the answer that earns marks says *where* a right
  comes from, because the source determines how easily it can be removed. A common law right yields to
  a statute; a statutory right yields to a later statute; only a constitutional right resists both.
- **Rights and responsibilities** (`inv.7.1`) as a genuine relationship rather than a slogan: a right
  that no one has a corresponding duty to respect is not enforceable, which is the link to the
  enforceability criterion.
