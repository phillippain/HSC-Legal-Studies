The Uluru Statement from the Heart (2017) asked for a constitutionally enshrined Voice to Parliament. The Albanese Government put a single question: to recognise Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples by establishing a Voice that "may make representations to the Parliament and the Executive Government" on matters relating to them, with Parliament to decide its composition and powers (proposed s 129).

## The result

| | Yes |
|---|---|
| National | 39.94% (6,286,894 to 9,452,792) |
| States carried | 0 of 6 (NSW 41.0%, Vic 45.9%, Qld 31.8%, WA 36.7%, SA 35.8%, Tas 41.1%) |
| Territories | ACT Yes 61.3%; NT No |

Turnout was 89.9%. The Yes case had the government, the Greens and most Aboriginal organisations; the No case the federal Coalition (from April 2023), with a separate "progressive No" arguing the Voice did not go far enough.

## Use it for

- **inv.5.5** — the double majority in operation and the single strongest explanatory variable in referendum history: once the Opposition declared against it, the proposal followed the pattern of every contested referendum since 1901.
- **chg.2.1 / chg.4.2** — drivers (advocacy, public opinion) and the limits of change when the mechanism requires near-consensus; what the Uluru Statement's other asks (treaty, truth-telling) now rely on.

## Caution

The vote decided whether to change the Constitution, not whether a legislated Voice could exist (South Australia legislated one in 2023). Attribute explanations of the result (misinformation, detail, "if you don't know, vote no") to their sources — they are contested.
