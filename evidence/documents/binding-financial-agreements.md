The private alternative to a court-ordered property settlement: a **contract** about how property,
finances and superannuation will be divided if the relationship ends.

## The sections — a map worth memorising

| Stage | Married | De facto |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** the relationship | **s 90B** | **s 90UB** |
| **During** the relationship | **s 90C** | **s 90UC** |
| **After** it ends | **s 90D** (after divorce) | **s 90UD** |

## Validity — s 90G (marriage) and s 90UJ (de facto)

1. **In writing** and **signed by both parties**.
2. **Each party must receive independent legal advice** on the effect of the agreement and its
   advantages and disadvantages.
3. A **signed statement from each party's lawyer** confirming that advice was given must be included.
4. The agreement must not have been **terminated or set aside** by a court.

## The cases

- ***Thorne v Kennedy* [2017] HCA 49** — the High Court held a BFA **invalid for undue influence and
  unconscionable conduct**. The leading authority, and the one to cite.
- ***Hoult v Hoult* [2013] FamCAFC 109** — the agreement was **upheld** despite a claim of
  misunderstanding; the legal advice given was sufficient.
- ***Kostres v Kostres* [2009] FamCAFC 222** — legal advice must be **comprehensive and informed**.
- ***Ruane v Bachmann-Ruane* [2009] FMCAfam 1109** — set aside for **non-disclosure of assets**.

## Benefits and limitations

**For**: settles the question early and avoids litigation; gives the parties **control and certainty**;
saves time and cost when valid and properly executed.

**Against**: can be challenged for **fraud, duress, non-disclosure or unconscionable conduct**; may
produce **unfairness** where one party is economically disadvantaged; and the mandatory legal advice
is an **upfront cost**.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.3 — the autonomy answer.** Every property question can be improved by noting that the
  parties may **contract out** of s 79 altogether, and that the law lets them — then asking at what
  price. The section map above is the detail most students lack.
- ***Thorne v Kennedy* is the case to know (inv.1.5 / dis.2.1).** A High Court decision setting aside
  an agreement for **undue influence and unconscionable conduct** is the clearest statement that
  **freedom of contract has limits** where there is a gross inequality of bargaining power. Read it
  beside *Amadio* and `khoshaba-2006` — the same equitable doctrines, in a family rather than a
  banking setting.
- **The validity requirements are the law protecting people from their own agreements (inv.1.6)** —
  writing, **independent** advice for **each** party, and a **lawyer's certificate**. Formality as a
  safeguard. Then the counter-argument: those formalities make BFAs **expensive**, so the instrument
  is most available to the party who already has the assets.
- **Compare it with a parentage or consent order (fam.2.5)** — a BFA keeps the matter out of court
  entirely, whereas consent orders are made **by** a court. Good for the spectrum from private
  agreement to adjudication, and for resource efficiency.
- **Pair it with *Beitel & Beitel* [2026] FedCFamC1F 495**, where a s 90C agreement signed in 2008
  **never took effect** because no **separation declaration** under s 90DA was ever made. The
  validity requirements in this record are necessary but not sufficient: an agreement can be valid
  and still never operate.
- **De facto parity (fam.1.4)** — the mirrored Part VIIIAB provisions (ss 90UB–90UD, s 90UJ) mean de
  facto couples can do the same thing. Useful evidence of the law recognising relationships outside
  marriage.
- **Who a BFA really protects (fam.t.values)** — it is usually sought by the party with more to lose.
  *Thorne v Kennedy* is the case where that mattered.

## Caution

A **teaching sheet**, not a source: the section numbers and validity requirements are accurate but
should be **checked against the consolidated *Family Law Act 1975*** before being quoted, and the
case holdings here are one-line summaries — read the judgments, especially *Thorne v Kennedy*, before
relying on them. The sheet does not mention that **Part VIII was substantially amended by the
*Family Law Amendment Act 2024*** with effect from **10 June 2025**; the BFA provisions were not the
target of those amendments, but the surrounding property provisions were, so do not present this as
a complete account of the current law.

The cases are old — **2009, 2013 and 2017** — and the three lower-court decisions are **first
instance or intermediate appellate**, not the High Court. *Thorne v Kennedy* turned on **its own
extreme facts** (a very large disparity in assets and bargaining power) and does not mean BFAs are
generally vulnerable; most are never challenged. s 90G also contains a **saving provision** allowing
a court to treat an agreement as binding despite a technical failure, which this sheet omits. The
**PEARRJAM** evaluation at the end of the document is the summariser's judgement, not evidence —
make your own. Anyone contemplating a BFA needs **independent legal advice**, which is both the
legal requirement and the practical point.
