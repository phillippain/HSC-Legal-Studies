Excessive rent increases, repairs not done, a bond withheld — under the *Residential Tenancies Act
2010* a tenant's legal options all end at the **NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal**, an
independent body deciding landlord–tenant disputes on the law and the evidence.

The Tenants' Union reports that tenants **with a strong case and evidence can and do win** — a bond
withheld after an eviction without cause, recovered on challenge; an excessive rent increase
overturned in Sydney. It also notes that the Tribunal's residential work is **heavily subsidised by
renters themselves**, through the interest on bonds held by the Rental Bond Board, and that a
**departmental review of NCAT's role in tenancy disputes** was underway following the Union's
December 2024 submission.

## Use it for

- **Dispute resolution and the role of tribunals** — cheap, informal, no lawyers as a rule, which is
  the accessibility argument; and against it, a self-represented tenant facing an agent who appears
  at NCAT every week, which is the repeat-player problem in a sentence.
- **Who pays** — the bond interest point is unusual and memorable, and it is a real answer to a
  resource efficiency question: the forum is cheap for the state because renters fund it.
- **Non-government organisations** (`shel.2.9`) — the Tenants' Union does the work of informing
  tenants that the remedy exists, which is what makes a right on paper usable.
