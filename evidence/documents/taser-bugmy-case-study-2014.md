Wilcannia, 20 February 2011. Three officers came to arrest **Phillip Bugmy** at his grandmother's
house. He told them to go away and kept cooking. They waited, heard the door unlock, and went in.

What the **Taser Cam** recorded is why this case is worth teaching. Bugmy had a steak knife in his
hand and gestured with it for six to eight seconds; the magistrate found the movement "wholly
consistent with gesticulation". He let his partner take the knife. He was told to get down, and
knelt with his hands behind his head and his head bowed — and stayed there, defiant but **uttering
no threats**, for **more than a minute and a half**. Then Senior Constable Charman fired the taser
at him.

The paper opens on the cross-examination:

> **Q.** If he remained on his knees for two, three, four, 5 minutes without doing anything
> aggressive towards police you were going to Taser him?
> **A.** In this situation yes.

## What happened to everyone afterwards

| | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Assault occasioning actual bodily harm | No evidence offered, **dismissed** |
| Resisting police | **Dismissed** — no prima facie case, no evidence of opposition by force |
| Using an offensive weapon with intent | **Dismissed** — not satisfied beyond reasonable doubt |
| Intimidating police | **Dismissed** — s 138 objection upheld, all the evidence excluded |
| Phillip Bugmy | About **seven months in custody, bail refused**, before it was over |
| The complaint | Declined; Professional Standards Command found assault, excessive force and collusion **"not sustained"**; referred to the Ombudsman |
| The DPP | No reasonable prospect of convicting the officer; **no charges** |
| The civil claim | **Settled** in Bugmy's favour |

## Use it for

- **crime.2.1 (police powers)** — the reasonable-force question with a concrete fact pattern. LEPRA
  authorises force that is **reasonably necessary**; the paper walks the use-of-force framework the
  police themselves apply (a situational model rather than a linear continuum, criteria to draw and
  cover, criteria to discharge) and asks whether **passive non-compliance** can ever meet them.
- **crime.3.7 (use of evidence)** — the cleanest worked example of **s 138 of the *Evidence Act
  1995*** in the library: evidence obtained improperly was excluded and a charge collapsed with it.
  The paper traces that line through *Robinett v Police*, ***DPP v Carr***, *DPP v CAD*, *DPP v Coe*
  and *DPP v AM*.
- **The accountability chain, every link of which held** — criminal charges dismissed, internal
  investigation not sustained, no prosecution of the officer, and only the civil claim delivering
  anything. Use it against the proposition that oversight bodies make police answerable.
- **crime.2.5 (bail or remand)** — seven months on remand on four charges that were all dismissed.
- **Aboriginal people and policing** — written by the Aboriginal Legal Service about a client in a
  remote Aboriginal community. Pairs with the Balzan inquest and with circle sentencing as evidence
  on whether the criminal justice system works for Aboriginal people.

## Caution

A **conference paper written by the defence lawyer**, so it argues a position and selects the
material that supports it; it is not a judgment, and ***Police v Bugmy* is unreported**, so quote
Magistrate Dunlevy's findings as the paper reports them, not as a citable authority. It is from
**2014**: the police standard operating procedures, the taser rollout and the oversight bodies have
all changed since — the Police Integrity Commission it refers to was replaced by the **Law
Enforcement Conduct Commission in 2017**. The paper describes the Ombudsman review and the civil
settlement as recent at the time; their final positions are not recorded here. This is **not** the
same person as the appellant in *Bugmy v The Queen* [2013] HCA 37.
