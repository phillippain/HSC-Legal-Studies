Masood Zakaria, charged over the **20 October 2021** Guildford murders of Salim and Toufik Hamze,
asks the Supreme Court to **permanently stay** his prosecution as an abuse of process, arguing the
AFP's role in his return from Türkiye was unlawful. The hearing opened the week of 19 October 2026.
**Everything here is an allegation; nothing has been tested at trial, and the stay has not been
decided.**

## Formal extradition versus what is alleged

| | Formal extradition | Zakaria's return (as alleged) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | *Extradition Act 1988* (Cth) and treaties | Turkish **deportation** of a foreigner allegedly on a false passport |
| Who acts | Government to government, AGD the central authority | AFP Ankara office alerted Turkish authorities to his presence |
| Challenge | The person can contest surrender under the other country's law | Defence says he was removed while his deportation appeal was pending |
| Custody in transit | Officials of the two states | **Private contractors paid over $1 million** by the AFP |
| Lawfulness | Lawful if the Act is satisfied | Ierace J found **credible prima facie evidence** it breached Turkish law |
| Australian knowledge | Not in issue | Judge **not satisfied to the same standard** that the AFP knew |

He was detained in Bodrum in January 2023, held in immigration detention about **11 months**, and
landed in Darwin on **3 December 2023**. The defence expects to call up to **35 witnesses**,
including two AFP Commissioners. The 18 October report is that a key witness, an ex-soldier involved
in the escort, is uncontactable days before the hearing.

## The test, and why the 2025 ruling matters

A permanent stay rests on the court's **inherent power** to prevent its process being abused:
would continuing bring the administration of justice into disrepute? The applicant carries a **heavy
onus** and stays are granted only in exceptional cases.

- ***Williams v Spautz*** (1992) — a stay where a prosecution is brought for an improper purpose.
- ***R v Horseferry Road Magistrates Court; Ex parte Bennett*** (1993, HL) and ***R v Hartley***
  (1978, NZCA) — courts may stay where extradition procedures were bypassed.
- ***Moti v The Queen*** [2011] HCA 50 — the closest case: the High Court stayed a prosecution because
  Australian officials **knowingly facilitated** a deportation they knew was unlawful. **Official
  knowledge was central** — which is exactly the finding Ierace J did not make.
- ***Ridgeway v The Queen*** (1995) — the related, lesser remedy: excluding evidence of an offence
  procured by unlawful police conduct on public policy grounds.

A stay is **not an acquittal**: the accused keeps the presumption of innocence either way.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.1 / crime.3.6 (court jurisdiction, pre-trial process)** — the rare chance to show a
  pre-trial application doing real work, and that the court's own integrity is a legal interest
  distinct from guilt.
- **crime.2.1 / crime.6.2 (police powers, international cooperation)** — police acting outside formal
  extradition, and the accountability question raised by paying private contractors over $1 million
  to escort a prisoner.
- **ls.1.7 (rule of law)** — the government must obey the law to use the courts; set against the
  community's interest in trying serious crime. The sharpest essay line available on balancing rights
  of the accused against community interests.
- **Evaluating effectiveness** — the trial was vacated in 2025 while agency material was outstanding,
  and the hearing has slipped again. Delay here is itself evidence about the trial process.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **news report drawing mainly on defence court filings**, with **no
Crown response** and no comment from the AFP on the witness. The Heckel details rest on one source.
Reports name different contractors (Hart International and Sky Traders), and give inconsistent ages
(28 in 2023, 30 in 2025, 33 here) and travel details. The September 2025 judgment was **not read**,
only reporting of it, and "credible prima facie evidence" is not a final finding of unlawfulness. Do
not treat the claim that a stay would be a NSW first as established. Zakaria's alleged Alameddine
role is unproven.
