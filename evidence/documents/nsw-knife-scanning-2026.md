The NSW wanding trial began in December 2024 under a sunset clause. On **13 October 2026** Parliament
made it permanent and extended it, and the ministerial release followed on 14 October. The release
reports seizures; it reports no crime trend.

## What changed

| | Trial (2024) | Permanent (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Sunset clause, repeal due Dec 2027 | Permanent |
| Declaration | Up to 12 hours | Up to 24 hours |
| Who declares | Assistant Commissioner or above | Superintendent or above |
| Where | Transport, shopping, sporting venues | Plus major entertainment venues |
| Same-sex officer to scan | Required if practicable (s 45O) | Requirement removed |

Scanning needs a declaration tied to a weapon or serious violent offence in the area in the last
12 months (s 45G), but **no suspicion about the individual** (s 45K). Refusing to be scanned is an
offence carrying a fine up to $5,500.

## The trial's numbers

**59,228** people scanned across **406** operations, December 2024 to June 2026, producing **380**
weapons seized, **200** charges and **68** infringements or diversions — a hit rate **under 1%**.
One legal commentary reports 308 weapons rather than 380.

## Two search powers in one Act

| | Ordinary search (LEPRA s 21) — about the **person** | Knife wanding (Part 4A) — about the **place** |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Reasonable suspicion the person has stolen goods, weapons or drugs | Being in a declared area; **no suspicion threshold** (s 45K) |
| Who authorises | The officer on the spot | A senior officer, by written declaration (s 45F) |
| Where | Anywhere | Stations, shopping precincts, sporting venues, prescribed places (and, from 2026, major entertainment venues) |
| Duration | No declaration | Up to 12 hours (s 45I); 24 hours after the 2026 reform |
| Scope | Pat-down, outer items, metal detector; strip search possible (ss 30–31) | Hand-held scanner; if metal is indicated, produce the item and be rescanned (s 45M); a fuller search needs a separate Part 4 power |
| Refusal | — | Offence without reasonable excuse: 50 penalty units, about $5,500 (s 45N); **17 people charged** in the trial |
| Safeguards | Reasons, privacy (s 32); unlawful-stop evidence can be excluded (*R v Rondo*) | Least invasive method, detain only as long as necessary (s 45O); counts-only records (s 45P, cl 47A); ministerial review (s 45Q); sunset (s 45R, now repealed) |

**How an operation lawfully happens:** a qualifying offence at the place in the previous 12 months
(armed offender, serious violent indictable offence, or repeated weapon possession — s 45G) → written
declaration weighing likely effectiveness and effect on lawful activity → publication on the NSW
Police website (s 45H) → scans, including vehicles within two stops (s 45L). There is **no appeal or
review of a declaration**, and the records list no demographic fields. The Opposition's 2024
amendments to remove place and time limits were lost 36–48; in July 2026 it called for wanding in any
public place.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.1 (police powers)** — the clearest current example of a power that departs from LEPRA's
  usual reasonable-suspicion threshold, and of discretion exercised at declaration level rather than
  about a person.
- **crime.1.9 / crime.2.3** — situational crime prevention measured by detection, not deterrence.
  Griffith University's review of Queensland's *Jack's Law*, the model for the NSW scheme, found
  wanding may aid detection but found **no evidence it deters carrying or reduces violent crime**.
- **chg.1 / chg.3 (law reform)** — a tidy reform sequence: the 2024 Bondi Junction attack, the
  2024 Act, a DCJ statutory review reporting 23 June 2026, a coronial recommendation in February
  2026 to monitor the trial, then permanence. Useful for whether reform followed the evidence or the
  event.
- **Rights and the rule of law (inv.1.6, inv.1.7)** — declarations cannot be challenged, the threshold
  is unchanged, and as at September 2025 NSW Police did not record demographic data on who was
  scanned, so discriminatory impact cannot be measured. The ALS and the Justice and Equity Centre
  argue the risk falls on Aboriginal people and young people.
- **exp.6 (evaluating responses)** — set the seizure figures against the sub-1% hit rate and the
  absent crime-trend evidence.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** built around a **government media release written to promote the reform** —
not the legislation. The approving rank and the removal of the same-sex safeguard come from
commentary on the bill *as introduced*, so check the Act as passed on the NSW legislation website
before quoting section numbers. The widely cited "more than 15,000 knives seized since April 2023"
covers **all policing**, not wanding, so it does not measure this scheme. The claim that the
Coalition voted for the laws is unverified — check Hansard.
