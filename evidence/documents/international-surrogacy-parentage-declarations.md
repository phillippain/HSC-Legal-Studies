Every Australian state and territory except the Northern Territory has a framework for transferring
parentage after an **altruistic** surrogacy, and all of them criminalise **commercial** surrogacy to
some degree. Couples go overseas anyway, and then come home and ask an Australian court to say they
are the parents.

The tool they reach for is **s 69VA of the *Family Law Act 1975*** — a declaration of parentage that
is "conclusive evidence of parentage for the purposes of all laws of the Commonwealth". Whether it
is available depends on how it sits against **s 60H** (artificial conception) and **s 60HB**
(children born under surrogacy arrangements, where a state court order makes the child theirs).

## The same question, three answers

| | Arrangement | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| ***Carlton & Bissett*** [2013] FamCA 143 | **Altruistic**, South Africa, done lawfully there; a South African order made the triplets his "for all purposes" | **Declaration made.** Ryan J: "the only factors relate to what is in the children's best interests; there being no issues of public policy". He held the father could rely on the general presumptions despite ss 60H and 60HB |
| ***Green-Wilson & Bishop*** [2014] FamCA 1031 | **Commercial**, India | **Declaration made.** The agreement could not meet the Victorian requirements, so s 60HB could not apply — leaving a "**lacuna**". Johns J filled it: to refuse "would be to elevate public policy considerations… above a consideration of the welfare of children" |
| ***Bernieres & Dhopal*** [2017] FamCAFC 180 (**Full Court**) | **Commercial**, "Country T" | **Declaration refused, appeal dismissed.** s 60HB **covers the field**; the specific provisions prevail over the general presumptions; "it is not possible to discard the plain meaning of legislation where public policy considerations may not be seen to be in the best interests of the children affected" |

The Full Court conceded the "unfortunate result": for children of international commercial surrogacy
the parentage of the intended parents "is in doubt" — and in truth may not exist at all. **Being the
child's biological father is not enough.** The father's spouse has an even weaker claim.

## What follows for the child

Without legal parentage, the article notes, there may be trouble accessing **Medicare**, child care
rebates and family tax benefits; difficulty establishing eligibility for **child support** if the
couple separates; and no settled succession position.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3 (surrogacy and birth technologies)** — the central legal problem of the dot point, with
  the authority to prove it. Far stronger than asserting "the law has not kept up": here are three
  judges reaching three results and a Full Court saying the gap is Parliament's to close.
- **fam.1.2 / ls.2.6 (state and federal jurisdiction)** — surrogacy is **state** law, parentage for
  Commonwealth purposes is **federal**, and the seam between them is exactly where these children
  fall. Note the quirk the article records: in Victoria the offence attaches to the **surrogate**
  receiving a benefit, not to the intended parents commissioning the arrangement.
- **ls.1.4 / ls.1.5 (just laws, the nature of justice)** — a law that works as written and produces
  a child with no legal parents is the sharpest available example of the gap between legality and
  justice.
- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.3 (conditions and mechanisms of reform)** — the courts have now expressly declined
  to fix it by interpretation. The mechanism left is Parliament, and it has not acted.
- **Set it against *Masson v Parsons*** — the High Court reading "parent" by ordinary contemporary
  understanding in one context, while in this one the specific sections shut that reasoning out.

## Caution

A **practitioner's case study from 2018**, written for lawyers and now some years old: check whether
any of the Acts or the Family Law Regulations have changed, and note the Family Court of Australia
has since merged into the **Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (1 September 2021)**. It
analyses three cases only and does not claim to survey the field. The case names are anonymised in
the originals and one jurisdiction is given only as "Country T". *Bernieres* is the binding
authority of the three — do not cite *Bissett* or *Green-Wilson* as the current law on commercial
arrangements.
