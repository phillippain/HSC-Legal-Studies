A reference note.

## What it is

A body of law predating British arrival by tens of thousands of years — **oral**, **spiritually
based**, **kinship-structured**, tied to **country**, with **ritual and restitutive** rather than
custodial punishment, and **enforced by the community** rather than by a separate institution. It is
not one system: it differs between nations and language groups.

## Where Australian law recognises it

| Area | How |
|---|---|
| **Land** | *Mabo (No 2)* (1992) recognised **native title** at common law, rooted in the laws and customs observed at sovereignty and continuously since; the *Native Title Act 1993* (Cth) and *Wik* (1996) followed |
| **Sentencing** | **Circle sentencing** in NSW brings Elders into the sentencing of Aboriginal offenders. BOCSAR found **no evidence that it reduces reoffending** — 77.9% against a 76.5% control — which is the honest figure to use |
| **Youth justice** | The **Youth Koori Court**, and diversion under the *Young Offenders Act 1997* (NSW) |
| **Child welfare** | The **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Child Placement Principle**, and the **Family Is Culture** review — **12 of 126** recommendations implemented in five years |
| **Family** | Traditional marriage recognised for some purposes; kinship care arrangements |
| **Evidence and procedure** | Interpreters, cultural reports, and rules about ceremonial and secret evidence |

## The limits

Recognition is **partial and conditional**. Customary law enters only through a door Australian law
opens: a statute, a doctrine, a sentencing option. It cannot override statute, and the *Native Title
Act* itself defines and confines the right it recognises. Formal legal pluralism — two systems of
equal standing — does not exist here.

## Use it for

- **Relevance to contemporary Australian law** (`ls.2.10`), argued rather than asserted: name the door,
  then say how wide it is.
- **Effectiveness with evidence on both sides.** Circle sentencing has strong procedural and community
  justifications and a null result on reoffending. Saying both is what distinguishes a judgement from
  an opinion.
- **Self-determination as the reform direction** — the Family Is Culture argument is not for better
  departmental practice but for transferring the decision, which connects this dot point to native
  title and to the wider constitutional question.
