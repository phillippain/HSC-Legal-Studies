## Legal responses

Legislation, delegated legislation, case law, courts and tribunals, regulators with statutory powers,
international instruments once implemented. The test is **enforceability**: someone can be compelled.

## Non-legal responses

Media campaigns, NGOs and peak bodies, unions and interest groups, private industry practice,
education, individual and community action. The test is **influence**: they change what people do, or
what governments feel they must do.

The division is analytical rather than natural, and the strongest answers say so. **Community legal
centres** are non-government in governance and deliver legal services. **AFCA** and **EWON** are
external dispute resolution schemes established under statutory frameworks but run independently.
**TAAP** is a non-legal service funded by a legal mechanism — the interest on renters' bonds.

## Evaluating with the syllabus's own criteria

| Criterion | The question it asks | A worked instance from this finder |
|---|---|---|
| **Resource efficiency** | Is the response worth what it costs? | NCAT clearing **96.9%** of 52,608 applications |
| **Accessibility** | Can the people affected actually use it? | **$30 an hour** at an FRC against ~**$110,000** in the Family Court |
| **Enforceability** | Can it be made to happen? | The *Modern Slavery Act* — eight years of statements, **no penalties** |
| **Responsiveness** | How fast, and to what? | No-grounds evictions: promised **July 2023**, passed **Oct 2024**, in force **May 2025** |
| **Protection of individual rights** | Whose rights, and at whose cost? | Tendency and coincidence evidence — victims' access against the accused's fair trial |
| **Meeting society's needs** | Does it address the problem or its symptom? | 1,432 of 40,000 **HAFF** homes delivered |
| **Rule of law** | Is power exercised under law and checked? | *Communist Party Case*; *Kable* |
| **Has justice been achieved?** | For whom, on what measure? | One forced marriage prosecution in twelve years, **only possible because the victim died** |

## How to write the judgement

1. **Rank the responses** rather than listing them — say which carries most of the work and why.
2. **Concede the strongest counter-example** before you argue. An answer that admits NCAT's clearance
   rate before attacking its accessibility reads as informed; one that does not reads as one-sided.
3. **Name where nothing can help.** Where the problem is **scarcity** — housing supply, caseworker
   numbers — no right and no campaign resolves it, and saying so is a Band 6 observation.
4. **End on a differentiated verdict**: effective *here*, ineffective *there*, and the reason for the
   difference.
