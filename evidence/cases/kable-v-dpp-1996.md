The *Community Protection Act 1994* (NSW) applied to **one named person**, Gregory Wayne Kable, and
authorised the NSW Supreme Court to order his **continued detention** after his sentence had expired,
on the **balance of probabilities**, without any fresh offence.

## Held

Invalid. Because state Supreme Courts can be invested with **federal jurisdiction** under Chapter III
of the Constitution, a state parliament cannot confer on them a function **incompatible with their
institutional integrity** as courts. Requiring a court to imprison a citizen for what he might do,
by civil standards, on legislation aimed at him alone, was such a function.

## Use it for

- **Separation of powers** (`ls.2.7`) with a state twist — the doctrine is not written into state
  constitutions, and the *Kable* principle is how it reaches them anyway.
- **Post-sentencing considerations** in Crime (`crime.4.8`) — preventive detention did not disappear
  after *Kable*; it was rewritten as general schemes applying to classes of offender rather than to a
  named individual, and those have survived. The comparison is the analysis.
- **Just laws** (`ls.1.4`): a law directed at a single person, applied retrospectively to his liberty,
  fails almost every characteristic the syllabus lists — generality, known in advance, applied
  equally — which is a neat way to use Part I concepts on a real statute.
