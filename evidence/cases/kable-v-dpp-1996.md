Gregory Kable was sentenced in 1990 for the manslaughter of his wife (diminished responsibility).
In prison he wrote threatening letters to her relatives. As his release neared, Parliament passed
the *Community Protection Act 1994* (NSW), amended in passage so that it applied to **Kable alone**,
authorising the Supreme Court to order his **continued detention** for up to six months at a time if
satisfied, on the **balance of probabilities** and on reports not bound by the rules of evidence, that
he was more likely than not to commit a serious act of violence. Levine J ordered six months'
detention in 1995; a second application was refused.

## Held — 4:2

Invalid. Because state Supreme Courts can be invested with **federal jurisdiction** under Chapter III
of the Constitution, a state parliament cannot confer on them a function **incompatible with their
institutional integrity** as courts. Requiring a court to imprison a citizen for what he might do,
without a finding of guilt, on legislation aimed at him alone, made the court an instrument of the
executive's purpose.

## Use it for

- **Separation of powers** (ls.2.7) with a state twist — the doctrine is not written into state
  constitutions, and the *Kable* principle is how it reaches them anyway.
- **Post-sentencing considerations** (crime.4.8) — preventive detention did not disappear after
  *Kable*; it was rewritten as **general** schemes for classes of offender, which survived in *Fardon*
  (2004) and underlie NSW's *Crimes (High Risk Offenders) Act 2006*. The comparison is the analysis.
- **Just laws** (ls.1.4) — a law directed at a single person, applied to his liberty after his
  sentence, fails almost every characteristic the syllabus lists.

## Caution

"The *Kable* principle" is a later label, not language in the 1996 reasons. Some printed versions
interleave extracts from later cases; only the 1996 reasons are the judgment.
