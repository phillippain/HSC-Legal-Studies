A Malaysian national assigned female at birth who identifies as male arrived in 2017 and applied for
a protection visa. At a 2020 interview he named a loan shark and passport problems as the harm he
feared, and said his "dream" was to become a man but the procedures were too numerous. The delegate
refused the visa, recording — **wrongly** — that he "no longer wants to have a sex change". He could
not read the refusal email in English.

## How he ended up in the High Court

No merits review application was filed in time, so the Tribunal was closed to him. That had a
cascading effect: the delegate's decision became a **"primary decision"** under s 476(4), which
meant neither the Federal Circuit and Family Court nor the Federal Court had jurisdiction. His only
remaining avenue was the High Court's **entrenched original jurisdiction under s 75(v) of the
Constitution** — five years out of time.

In between: housemates asked him for $10,000 to help with a review; he moved to Cairns, then
Melbourne; he paid $500–$600 to a friend who filed an invalid application; he reached the Asylum
Seeker Resource Centre in December 2022.

## Held — application dismissed with costs

The Court proceeded **on an assumption favourable to the plaintiff** that he had identified the
unavailability of surgery as a harm [26], then asked whether the claim could have succeeded anyway.
It could not. "Serious harm" under s 5J(4)(b) and (5) means threats to life or liberty, significant
physical ill-treatment, or denial of access to basic services **where that threatens capacity to
subsist**. The affidavits did not explain several periods of delay, but the Court did not rule on
that because the substantive grounds failed [11].

## Use it for

- **s 75(v) as the last line.** Parliament can legislate privative clauses and time limits, but it
  cannot take away the High Court's original jurisdiction. This is the single best illustration of
  an entrenched constitutional protection in a country with no charter of rights — and of how
  little it delivered here.
- **Access to justice** made concrete: a missed time limit closed merits review, which closed two
  courts, which left only the hardest and most expensive forum in the country.
- **The limits of "persecution"** — a human rights claim that is real and a protection claim that is
  not are different things, and the distinction is statutory.
