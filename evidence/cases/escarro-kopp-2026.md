Two **separate parenting proceedings were consolidated** because they raised the same factual
issues. The children — **X, born 2019**, and **C** — are **half-brothers** with the same mother
(the first respondent, Ms Kopp) and different fathers, and they already lived apart: X with the
applicant, C with the second respondent.

The court-appointed family report writer, Mr E, identified **safety as the central issue** in both
reports, and also recognised the importance of X's relationships with both parents **and with his
half-siblings**, noting the boys enjoyed each other's company. The second respondent described
difficulty facilitating the mother's time with C, with supervised visits frequently cancelled.

The risk came from the **mother's partner**, Mr B, who was found to present a risk of physical or
psychological harm to both children, including harm from exposure to family violence.

## Orders

All prior orders discharged. For X: the applicant has **sole decision-making responsibility** for
major long-term issues; **X lives with the applicant**; X spends time with the mother as agreed in
writing. The mother is **restrained** from:

- bringing X into contact with **Mr B**;
- facilitating communication between X and Mr B;
- bringing X **within 200 metres** of Mr B's home; and
- **consuming illicit substances** in the 12 hours before, and during, time with X.

Equivalent arrangements were made in the consolidated proceedings for C.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.2 (consequences of separation — children)** — risk from a **new partner**, not from a
  parent. The mother keeps her relationship with her children; what is removed is the exposure.
  That is a more precise and more common scenario than the "dangerous parent" case, and it shows the
  Act operating on the household rather than on the parent.
- **fam.1.4 (alternative family relationships)** — half-siblings in two households with different
  fathers, and the Court treating the **sibling relationship itself** as a relationship worth
  preserving under s 60CC. Useful for how the law handles families that are not one couple and
  their children.
- **Consolidation (fam.2.5)** — two proceedings joined because the same facts drove both. A neat
  example of courts managing litigation so that connected children are not dealt with
  inconsistently, and worth comparing with *Hemswold*, where one judge reached different outcomes
  for two siblings in **one** case.
- **Restraints as a tailored remedy** — a 200-metre exclusion, a communication ban and a
  **12-hour sobriety condition** are narrow, specific and checkable. Good for arguing that
  enforceability depends on orders being drafted so a breach is obvious.
- **Pair with *Morse & Duarte*** on unacceptable risk from a new partner.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB applies. **52 paragraphs**, first instance, and this record follows the
orders and the family report writer's framing rather than the detailed findings about Mr B — read
the judgment before asserting anything about him, and note the findings are about **risk**, not
criminal guilt. The arrangements for C are in the consolidated proceedings and are only summarised
here. The mother's **illicit substance use** is addressed by a condition rather than by a finding
set out in this record. Do not generalise: a restraint against a parent's partner is a serious order
made on specific evidence.
