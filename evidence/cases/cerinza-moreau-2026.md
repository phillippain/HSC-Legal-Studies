Two children, **X born 2019** and **Y born 2020**, living mainly with their mother and spending
**five nights a fortnight** with their father. Both parents worked; both had close, loving
relationships with the children. **Neither parent argued the other was a direct risk of harm.**

The dispute was about the **adult relationship**. The mother said the father's conduct — confronting
her at day-care pick-ups, **yelling, filming her, following her to the car** — was a pattern of
**coercive control** that had damaged her ability to parent. The children told the family report
writer they had seen "**lots of fights**" at changeover and it made them scared.

A Domestic Violence Order was made in her favour in late 2022 and later expired. The father commenced
proceedings in November 2024 seeking equal shared parental responsibility; the mother filed a
**Notice of Child Abuse, Family Violence or Risk** in February 2025, and the father gave an
**undertaking without admissions**. In June 2025 she amended her response: she wanted to **relocate
to Melbourne**, more than 1,000 km away, where her family and friends were. Three-day final hearing,
March 2026.

## The reasoning

There is **no separate "relocation test"** — the court weighs the competing proposals against
**s 60CC** and asks which is in the child's best interests (*AMS v AIF* (1999) 199 CLR 160; *U v U*
(2002) 211 CLR 238). Applying ***Pickford & Pickford* (2024) FLC 94-230**, which requires a
**forensic examination of behaviour, context and impact** rather than acceptance of the victim's
assertion, the Court found the father **used decision-making as a mechanism of control** and had
**limited insight** into its effect.

> Requiring the mother to remain in City B and continue to co-parent in these circumstances "risks
> further undermining of her parenting capacity and fails to adequately prioritise the children's
> emotional security".

**Relocation allowed.** Decision-making is **joint** generally, with **sole decision-making on
schooling** to the mother. The father's relationship is protected by **the whole of Term 2 holidays
each year**, half of other holidays, **a weekend each term in Melbourne**, and twice-weekly calls.

## The second question — passports refused

The father sought passports so he could take the children to **his country of birth** to meet their
grandmother and connect with their heritage. The Court accepted the **cultural benefits were
significant** and still refused — because the **evidentiary foundation for assessing the risk was
absent**. It made **no finding that he would abduct the children**.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.2 — the best relocation case in the library, and the reasoning is unusual.** Neither parent
  was a risk to the children. The order was made because **the conflict itself**, and its effect on
  the mother's parenting capacity, mattered more than preserving week-to-week contact. That is a
  sophisticated use of "best interests" and a quotable paragraph at [100].
- **"No separate relocation test" is the point students get wrong.** Cite *AMS v AIF* and *U v U* and
  say so: relocation is decided by the ordinary s 60CC inquiry, which is why outcomes look
  inconsistent from the outside.
- **Coercive control inside a parenting case (fam.2.4)** — filming, following, confronting at
  day-care, and **using decision-making as a mechanism of control**. Read *Pickford*'s requirement of
  a forensic examination of behaviour, context and impact beside the Family Law Council's **systems
  abuse** recommendation: both are about control exercised through legitimate-looking processes.
- **The post-2023 model in operation (fam.3.2)** — no presumption of equal shared parental
  responsibility; decision-making **split issue by issue**, joint generally and sole on schooling
  (ss 61D(3), 61DAA). Compare the 28-day protocol in *Hemswold* and the 7/14-day one in
  *Sullivan & Warren*.
- **Refusing an order without blaming anyone (inv.1.6)** — the passports failed on an **evidence
  gap**, not a character finding. An excellent, precise illustration of how courts manage risk under
  uncertainty, and of the burden of proof in practice.
- **Culture against safety** — the Court accepted the cultural value of the trip and still refused.
  Useful for competing considerations under s 60CC, and for the honest observation that protecting a
  child can cost them something real.
- **The children's voices** — "lots of fights" at changeover, through a family report writer who was
  **cross-examined**. Pair with the s 60CC children's-views factor.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB of the *Family Law Act* makes publishing an identifying account an offence —
the father's country of birth is anonymised and must stay that way. The library document is a **case
study card**, not the judgment: the quotation at [100] and the chronology should be **verified against
the judgment** before use. A **Division 2, first-instance** decision, highly fact-specific — the
relocation was allowed on **these** facts and is not a precedent that conflict justifies relocation.
The father was **never found to be a risk to the children** and gave his undertaking **without
admissions**; the finding was about **control of the mother** and its effect on her parenting. Do not
write that the court found he abducted or would abduct anyone. The DVO had **expired** before the
hearing. *Pickford & Pickford* (2024) is an authority on how coercive control must be proved — check
it directly rather than relying on this summary.
