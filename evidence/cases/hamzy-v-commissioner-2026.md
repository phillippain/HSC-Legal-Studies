Bassam Hamzy is serving 40 years and 3 months for murder, conspiracy to murder and drug offences,
some committed while already in custody. Housed at the High Risk Management Correctional Centre, he
was designated an **extreme high risk restricted** inmate under cl 15 of the *Crimes (Administration
of Sentences) Regulation 2014* (NSW).

Self-represented, he sought declarations that **cl 94** — which lets the Commissioner require
visiting lawyers to pass a criminal record check — was constitutionally invalid, and that a decision
denying HRMCC inmates tablet access to call lawyers was legally unreasonable.

## Held — judicial review failed on the merits

Leave to appeal refused on five grounds and granted on one; the appeal was **dismissed in full**,
with costs. Clause 94 remains valid. The tablet restriction was not legally unreasonable.

| Principle | Substance |
|---|---|
| No absolute right to counsel of choice | Implied Ch III rights secure a **fair trial**, not an unfettered choice of lawyer — *APLA*; *Dietrich* |
| Implied freedom of political communication | Protects the system of communication generally, not a personal right — *Unions NSW*; *McCloy* |
| Practical and material effect | A regulation's validity turns on its effect when **correctly applied**, not on isolated misapplications |
| Legal unreasonableness | A decision needs an "evident and intelligible justification" — *Li*; discriminating by inmate risk class has one |

## Use it for

- **Post-sentencing considerations** — security classification is the dot point, and this is a
  judgment about it rather than a description of it.
- **How rights are protected in Australia without a charter**: implied constitutional rights, the
  common law, and judicial review of administrative decisions, all doing the work a bill of rights
  would otherwise do. He lost — which is the honest evidence for a Charter of Rights argument.
- **Balancing the rights of offenders and society** at the sharpest possible end: cl 15(3) exists
  for inmates posing "an extreme danger to other people", and the powers are still reviewable.
- **Access to justice** — a self-represented applicant, against the Crown Solicitor's Office, in the
  Court of Appeal.
