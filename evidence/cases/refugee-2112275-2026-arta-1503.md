A Tamil Malaysian woman from Pahang arrived in February 2020 and applied for protection, saying a
man who wanted to marry her had threatened to kill her. **A delegate refused her visa in August 2021
without ever interviewing her**, finding effective state protection was available in Malaysia.

Her history included family violence from her brother and husband, a forced marriage she opposed,
and a later abusive partner in Australia who was the subject of an intervention order before
returning to Malaysia after making death threats.

Her son, born in Australia in 2021 to a father from a third country, has **no passport and no birth
registration in either parent's country**. The Department belatedly added him as a dependant in
January 2025 and refused his application in August.

## The statelessness trap

The definition of "refugee" in **s 5H** requires a person to be **outside** their country of
nationality or former habitual residence. A stateless child born in Australia who has never left it
fits **neither limb** — which could mean the merits of his claim are never reached.

Malaysia's citizenship rules made registration uncertain and highly discretionary for a child born
out of wedlock to a foreign father, and **constitutional amendments that would improve the position
had received royal assent but still had not commenced more than twelve months later**.

Three hearings with Tamil (Malaysian) interpreters; evidence included an intervention order, witness
statements, ASRC letters and country research. **Decisions set aside and remitted.**

## Use it for

- **State sovereignty and human rights** at their sharpest: a child's legal existence depends on
  another country's discretion, and Australia's protection framework has no category for him.
- **A reform that passed and did not commence.** Royal assent is not commencement — the single
  clearest example in the collection of the gap between enacting a law and it having effect. Pair it
  with the coercive control offence, enacted in 2022 and commenced in 2024.
- **Gender as the basis of a claim**, and a delegate deciding a family violence claim without an
  interview — a procedural fairness point with a real consequence.
