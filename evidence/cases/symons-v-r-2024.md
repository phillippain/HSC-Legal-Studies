On 5 April 2017 four men dragged Clint Starkey from a car at the Peats Ridge Caltex and beat him in
"no more than six seconds"; he died nine weeks later. Adam Symons said he had seen "the barrel of a
gun". Seven accused were tried together in 2022. The judge (Fagan J) **refused to leave excessive
self-defence** (*Crimes Act 1900* s 421) to the jury: if self-defence was not disproved the verdict
was not guilty, otherwise murder. Symons, McDonald and a third man, Robertson, were convicted of
murder. **Legal aid delays** — senior counsel briefed in February 2023, aid for McDonald granted only
in February 2024 — held up their appeals by about 17 and 20 months.

## Held

- *Robertson v R* [2024] NSWCCA 99, the co-accused's appeal on the identical ground, had already
  succeeded 2–1. The Crown conceded its reasons "apply with equal force" and did not rely on the
  proviso.
- **Equal justice decides it**: "Equal justice requires identity of outcome in cases that are
  relevantly identical" (*Wong*). Where an appeal is materially indistinguishable from an earlier one
  on the same trial, the Court follows it unless there is a compelling reason to depart (*Kwu*).
- Time was extended despite a "thin… and rather unsatisfactory" explanation; both murder convictions
  **quashed**, new trials ordered. The jury must next time be offered the "middle course" of
  manslaughter.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.8** — the three outcomes of the NSW self-defence provisions in one picture: s 418 (complete
  defence, not guilty), s 421 (excessive self-defence, manslaughter), or no defence (murder).
- **crime.4.5 (appeals)** — leave out of time, equality before the law between co-accused, and why
  juries "may be glad to take a middle course" (*Gilbert*).
- **crime.3.5 (legal aid)** — the practical cost of aid delays, a generation after *Dietrich*.

## Caution

The ratio is **equal justice**, not a ruling on how s 421 works with joint criminal enterprise — the
Court declined to choose between the three approaches in *Robertson*; cite *Robertson* for that. The
sentences imposed in 2022 are not stated in the judgment.
