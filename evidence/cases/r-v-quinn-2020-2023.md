Hannah Quinn and her partner Blake Davis were at home in Forest Lodge in August 2018 when Jett McKee, armed with a gun and knuckledusters, tried to rob them. McKee fled, was followed into the street, and was killed by a blow from a samurai sword swung by Davis. Both were tried in the NSW Supreme Court in December 2020.

## What happened

1. **Directed verdict.** The Crown ran Quinn's murder charge as an agreement with Davis to kill or cause grievous bodily harm. Justice Natalie Adams directed the jury to find her **not guilty of murder**, as a matter of law there was no case of murder to consider. The jury still decided the other charges.
2. **Jury verdicts.** Davis was convicted of **manslaughter**. Quinn was convicted of being an **accessory after the fact** to manslaughter, for the days she spent with Davis before they surrendered to police.
3. **Sentence.** [2021] NSWSC 493 (7 May 2021) is reported as the sentencing decision: a **two-year community corrections order** for Quinn, her conduct assessed as well below the middle of the range for the offence (maximum five years).
4. **Appeal.** On 8 September 2023 the Court of Criminal Appeal, by majority (Bell CJ and Sweeney J, Wilson J dissenting), held the verdict **unreasonable** and entered an **acquittal**. The majority thought the jury ought to have had a reasonable doubt: the most persuasive inference was that Quinn believed Davis had acted reasonably to protect them, so she was not helping him evade arrest.

## Use it for

- **The adversary system and a neutral judge**: the Crown carries the burden of proof, the defence tests it, and the judge stops a case that is legally too weak to go to a jury.
- **Legal personnel**: the judge's role (law, not facts), the prosecution's responsibility for how a case is framed, and the independence of the DPP, which still has to justify a charge by evidence.
- **Appeals as a check**: the first-instance verdict was a jury's, yet it was set aside on appeal. Use it, with Higgins, to show appeal rights correcting errors; the dissent shows how finely balanced such calls are.
- **Burden and standard of proof**: the beyond reasonable doubt standard was applied twice, once by the judge on the murder count and once by the appeal court on the accessory count.

## Caution

- The directed verdict, the sentence and the appeal are three different decisions on different dates. [2021] NSWSC 493 is the sentencing judgment as reported; **check the neutral citations on NSW Caselaw** before quoting them. The directed verdict itself is not the subject of that citation.
- The summary rests on court reporting and the CCA's published outcome, not a reading of each judgment. The majority's reasoning turned on the facts, so do not present the case as a rule about accessories.
- A directed verdict takes a charge from the jury; it is not an acquittal on appeal, and the two should not be run together in an answer.
