Between 1980 and 1996 the Northern Territory built roads, water tanks and other public works over 53 lots in the town of Timber Creek, extinguishing or impairing the Ngaliwurru and Nungali Peoples' non-exclusive native title. The *Native Title Act 1993* (Cth) Part 2 Division 5 gives a right to compensation for such acts.

## Held

- **Economic loss**: 50% of the freehold value of the land (the trial judge had said 80%; the Full Federal Court 65%).
- **Interest** on that sum from the date of each act (simple, not compound).
- **Cultural loss**: $1.3 million, upheld as the trial judge had assessed it — compensation for the "sense of failed responsibility" and the hurt of loss of connection to Country, assessed as a whole, not lot by lot.
- Total about $2.53 million.

## Use it for

- **chg.5.5** — an outcome of the native title regime: a benefit (recognition has monetary consequences for governments) and a limitation (non-exclusive title valued at half of freehold).
- **chg.5.3** — a driver: compensation claims across Australia followed, including *Yunupingu*.

## Caution

Compensation is only available for acts after the *Racial Discrimination Act 1975* (31 October 1975) unless the *Yunupingu* principle applies (Commonwealth acts in a territory). The figures are for a small town block; large claims have since been negotiated rather than litigated.
