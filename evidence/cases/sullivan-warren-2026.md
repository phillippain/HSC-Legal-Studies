Proceedings begun in 2019 about a child born in 2018. The mother had alleged for years that the
father physically and sexually abused the child; the child made allegations to police across **four
s 93A interviews**. In **written submissions at the end of the trial the mother abandoned them**,
conceding "on the evidence before this Court, there can be no finding that the father has
physically or sexually abused the child".

Brasch J accepted that concession — and then said it was not the end of the matter.

## The risk ran the other way

The father and the **Independent Children's Lawyer** both argued the **mother** was the unacceptable
risk: emotional abuse by allowing the child to believe and tell people she had been abused, and
wilful non-compliance with court orders. Their case was that the only way the child could safely
have a relationship with both parents was to live with the father, and that the upheaval would be
**"less-worse" than the child going through life wrongly believing or wrongly telling people she had
been abused**.

The Court tested the allegations theme by theme. On the claim that the father choked her "when I was
a baby": there was no medical evidence, no hospitalisation, and the child could not recall things
that had happened at four and five — so an independent memory from infancy was "most unlikely". The
finding was that the mother was **adept at planting ideas in the child's thinking**.

## The framework applied

- **s 140 *Evidence Act*** governs findings about **past** facts; **risk is separate, future-looking
  and predictive**, and is not governed by the civil standard (following *Isles*, *Fitzwater*).
- Risk is a **continuum** — "the chance of it happening" — assessed on the **whole of the evidence**
  (*Bant & Clayton*), together with whatever might **mitigate** it to an acceptable level.
- **s 60CG** imposes a statutory imperative that a parenting order not expose a person to an
  unacceptable risk of family violence.

## Orders

**The child lives with the father**, who has **sole decision-making responsibility** for health,
education, religion and cultural upbringing, and living arrangements that would make time with a
parent significantly harder — subject to a **consultation protocol**: tell the mother in writing,
she has **7 days** to respond, he may decide **14 days** after notifying her, and must tell her the
outcome. The child is to see a named child psychologist.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.2 (consequences of separation — children)** — a change of residence is the most serious
  parenting order a court makes, and this one is ordered **against** the parent the child had always
  lived with, on the basis of psychological rather than physical harm.
- **fam.3.4 / the limits of "believe the child"** — the Court took four police interviews seriously
  and still found the allegations were "bare… devoid of content and context". Good, careful material
  on how courts weigh a child's account without either dismissing or simply adopting it.
- **Past fact against future risk** — the cleanest statement in this stack: *no finding of abuse* is
  not the same as *no risk*, and the two questions are answered by different methods. Use it
  wherever an answer needs to explain "unacceptable risk".
- **fam.2.5 (courts and dispute resolution)** — the ICL's role is decisive here; the child's separate
  representation is what put the alternative case. Also note seven years of litigation over one
  child.
- **Set against *Cadel & Galea*** — both decided in August 2026, both about a parent whose own
  conduct creates the risk, and both ending with the children living with the other parent.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**, and Pt XIVB makes publishing an identifying account an offence. The subject matter —
allegations of child sexual abuse, found not to be made out — demands precision: the Court made **no
finding that the father abused the child**, and equally made **no finding that the mother lied**;
the finding was that she promoted beliefs that were not supported and involved the child in
investigations. Do not write this up as "false allegations" or as "parental alienation", a term the
judgment does not use. A **first-instance decision on its own facts**, expressly noting that "each
case must turn on its own facts"; it is not authority that residence should change whenever
allegations fail.
