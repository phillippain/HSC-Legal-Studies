Mr Martin was tried in the **Local Court** over four days (June and December 2025; 241 pages of
transcript) on two child sexual offences (*Crimes Act* s 66DE(1)(a); attempted voyeurism, s 91J(6)).
Straight after closing addresses the Acting Magistrate gave **ex tempore reasons** and found both proved;
he was sentenced to imprisonment in April 2026, with an ADVO and a Registrable Person Order. He appealed
**as of right on a question of law** (s 52(1)).

## Held — Hamill J

- The reasons **never mentioned the onus or the standard of proof**, did not engage with the defence
  submissions, and did not explain why the accused's mother (a defence witness) was disbelieved —
  contrary to the *Liberato* direction on defence evidence.
- Giving reasons is a "necessary incident of the judicial process" (*Stoker v Adecco*); they must at
  least tell the losing party why they lost (*Beale v GIO*).
- The **DPP conceded error**. Convictions, sentences and orders were **set aside** and the matter
  **remitted to a different Local Court judge**. Guilt is **undecided**.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.5 (appeals)** — a Local Court appeal to the Supreme Court on a question of law, and the remedy
  of a retrial rather than an acquittal.
- **crime.3.6 (burden and standard of proof)** — the prosecution's onus must be visible in a judge's
  reasons when no jury sits; pair with *Woolmington*.

## Caution

A **class case summary**. The complainant is a child: keep to the procedure. Mr Martin is not acquitted;
the rehearing is still to come. Emmett J's earlier stay refusal ([2026] NSWSC 339) and the sentence length
are not in the file.
