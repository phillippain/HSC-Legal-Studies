The ACT enacted the *Marriage Equality (Same Sex) Act 2013* (ACT). Couples married under it within
days. The Commonwealth challenged the Act.

## Held

The ACT law was **wholly inoperative**. The federal *Marriage Act 1961* (Cth) was a **complete
statement** of the law of marriage in Australia, so the territory law could not stand beside it. The
marriages performed under it were void.

The part that matters more than the outcome: the Court held that **"marriage" in s 51(xxi) of the
Constitution is not confined to marriage between a man and a woman**. The Commonwealth Parliament
therefore *could* legislate for same-sex marriage — and in December 2017, after the postal survey, it
did.

## Use it for

- **Inconsistency and the division of powers** (`ls.2.6`) — a clean modern illustration of a federal
  law covering the field, with real human consequences attached (marriages valid one week, void the
  next).
- **Same-sex relationships** (`fam.3.1`) — the case is the hinge between the 2004 amendment defining
  marriage as between a man and a woman and the 2017 amendment removing that definition. It shows the
  courts clearing a constitutional path and leaving the moral choice to Parliament.
- **Mechanisms of law reform** (`ls.4.3`): the High Court settled *who could act*; a non-binding
  national vote settled *whether they would*. Two different mechanisms, sequentially, on one issue.
