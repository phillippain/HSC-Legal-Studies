The **Youth Koori Court** began as a pilot at Parramatta Children's Court in 2015 and expanded to
Surry Hills (2019) and Dubbo (2023). It is **not legislated**: it runs under Children's Court
*Practice Note 11*, adjourning a case under *Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987* **s 33(1)(c2)**
while an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander young person follows an **Action and Support Plan**,
reviewed every two to four weeks with Elders and services.

John (a pseudonym), a Wiradjuri young person who had been in the care and protection system, spent
eight months in the Youth Koori Court, stayed **offence-free** and met every goal of his plan, and was
in stable accommodation and work by his graduation on 16 June 2023.

## The sentence — Children's Magistrate Hayes

- **The Act** — s 6 sets general principles but "gives little guidance" on sentencing; the Magistrate
  treated *Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Act* s 3A as not applying in the Children's Court.
- **The common law** fills the gap — *KT v R* and *BP v R*: youth and rehabilitation; "punishment for
  children should be directed towards changed behaviour, not retribution".
- **Human rights** — CROC art 3 (best interests) is relevant to the discretion, not mandatory (*Re
  Tracey*).
- **Result** — less serious charges **dismissed** (s 33(1)(a)(i)); **bonds** for the more serious
  (s 33(1)(b)); no conviction that might harm his career. "Changing the environment makes more sense
  than removing people from the environment."

## Caution

The judgment does not state the offences, the length of the bonds or any conditions. Its reading of
s 3A is one magistrate's; the Youth Koori Court's value is in the process, so pair it with evaluation
data rather than treating one graduate as proof.
