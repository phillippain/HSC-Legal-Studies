X lived with her mother, Ms Seaton, and had not seen her father since 2017. Ms Seaton alleged he was
sexually abusing X. Expert evidence and a risk assessment found Mr Fogarty a **low risk of harm**, and
the court found Ms Seaton had been **interrogating and harassing X and coercing her** to believe her
father was doing "rude" things — **creating a rift** between them. The allegations were dismissed.

Ms Seaton's history included convictions relating to drug use and sex work in her twenties, a
completed detox program, and admissions of leaving her children unsupervised with men including a
neighbour and a taxi driver, **two of her children having been sexually assaulted**. Mr Fogarty's
history included drug and alcohol abuse and a 2015 conviction for cannabis possession and breach of
bail (a 12-month community corrections order); since then, clean random drug screens on three
occasions in 2018, several years of full-time work as a labourer, family support, a block of land and
completed parenting courses.

## Held

The mother's interference posed an **unacceptable risk of psychological harm**. It was in X's best
interests that the **father have sole parental responsibility**, with supervised time for the mother
once X had settled.

## Use it for

- **The changing nature of parental responsibility** (`fam.3.2`) — the presumption of equal shared
  responsibility under the **2006 Act** does not apply where it would not serve the child's best
  interests, and the **expanded definition of family violence** reaches *fear or risk*, not only acts.
- **Alienation as harm.** Most answers treat risk as physical. Here the risk was the coaching itself,
  which is the point worth making about what "best interests" has come to include.
- **CROC art 7** — a child's right to know and be cared for by their parents — is the international
  standard behind the presumption, and this case shows it being honoured by *removing* a parent from
  decision-making.
