A final ADVO barred a father from going where the mother or three children lived. The parents and the Independent Children’s Lawyer then agreed to interim orders: the mother leaves the family home and the children live there with him. On 28 September 2026 Simpson J gave short reasons under s 69ZL, found the orders in the children’s best interests, and declared them inconsistent with the ADVO under ss 68P and 68Q. These are interim consent orders made on the papers, not a final decision.

## Use it for

- Domestic violence: how a family violence order and parenting orders interact under ss 68P and 68Q.
- Separation and children: interim living arrangements for three children, decided on best interests.
- Courts: an Independent Children’s Lawyer, lawyers for both parents, and an order made on the papers.
- Cooperation: the Court called the orders the product of co-operative parenting.
- Effectiveness: safeguards of lawyers explaining the orders and a liberty to relist on short notice.

## Cautions

This is an interim consent decision made on the papers, not a final parenting determination; a final hearing was vacated and the next listing is February 2027. The file cites no earlier cases. Inconsistencies: the reasons say weekly family therapy but order 7 requires attendances at least once per fortnight; some orders say ‘husband’ and ‘wife’ for the father and mother; order 5 fixes weekly time while order 4 leaves time to written agreement. The ADVO’s own terms are not in the file beyond the two restrictions described. Care line: family violence and a family in crisis; keep to the legal test and the s 68P steps.
