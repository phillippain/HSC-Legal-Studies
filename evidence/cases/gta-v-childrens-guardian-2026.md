GTA was arrested in 2019, aged 14, and charged with one count of **intentionally sexually touching
a child under 10** and three counts of **indecent assault of a person under 16**. The three
complainants were his younger female cousins, aged 7, 10 and 11, and the alleged incidents happened
at their grandparents' house. He was **13 or 14** at the time of the alleged conduct.

**Every charge was dismissed.** The three indecent assault charges fell in October 2020 on a **no
prima facie case** application: because he was under 14, the prosecution had to rebut the presumption
of **doli incapax** and could not. On the remaining charge he was found **not guilty** in November
2020 — Children's Magistrate Haskett found BG's evidence unreliable on crucial details (photographs
showed she was wearing overalls, not the shorts she described, and no watch), and so there was a
reasonable doubt. "Suspicion is not enough."

He has never been charged with anything since. He completed a plumbing apprenticeship and a
Certificate IV, and is fully licensed. He needs a clearance because his employer's **contracts with
schools, hospitals and universities require one**.

## Why he was refused anyway

In November 2024 the Children's Guardian refused him a clearance. Dismissal of the charges is not
the end of the inquiry, because:

- s 30(1)(a) covers "any **matters** that caused a refusal", not only proven offences; and
- under *M v M* (1988), as applied to this Act in *BKE*, *Tilley* and *CXZ*, the Tribunal may be
  unable to find an allegation proved **and** unable to dismiss it as groundless — and then assesses
  **risk from the possibility** that the conduct occurred.

Simpson AJA's statement of the trade-off in *CXZ* is the single most useful line in the case: a
person entirely innocent may be refused a clearance, because "the legislature preferred the risk of
injustice to an applicant to risk to the safety of children".

## The review outcome

Neither party bears an onus on a s 27 review. A clinical forensic psychologist assessed GTA as **low
risk** on the RSVP, in both her own report and a supplementary report written on the Respondent's
instruction to **assume the allegations were true**. The Tribunal could not put the likelihood of the
allegations being true higher than **low or very low**, found no real and appreciable risk, and was
satisfied both the **reasonable person test** and the **public interest test** were met — including
the public interest in a person being able to work. Decision **set aside**; clearance **granted**.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the preventive arm students almost never write about. Care and
  protection is not only removal and the Children's Court; it is also a scheme that screens every
  adult who works with children, and this is what it looks like from the inside.
- **crime.5 (young offenders) — doli incapax in operation.** Three charges collapsed because the
  prosecution could not prove a 13-year-old knew the conduct was seriously wrong. Pair with *RP v
  The Queen* and *DPP (NSW) v NW* for the rule, and use this for the consequence.
- **The limits of an acquittal.** A dismissal ends the criminal matter but not the administrative
  one, because the questions differ: proof beyond reasonable doubt of past guilt, against present
  and future risk on the civil material. An excellent paragraph on why "not guilty" does not mean
  "no risk" — and on the cost of that to a person who may be innocent.
- **Child protection vs individual rights (fam.t.values)** — the *CXZ* trade-off, stated by a judge,
  that the legislature deliberately chose to risk injustice to adults rather than risk to children.
  Quotable, and it cuts both ways in an evaluation.
- **inv.1.4 / accessibility** — he was **self-represented** against the Crown Solicitor, and the
  review took from March 2025 to August 2026. Seven years from the allegations to a clearance.
- **Law reform (fam.t.reform)** — and then this pathway was abolished: see the record on the WWC Act
  and the 2025 amendments. GTA only got a hearing because of a transitional provision.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**, with a s 64(1)(a) non-publication order covering every name — the applicant, the
complainants and the family. Do not write anything that could identify them. The charges were
**dismissed and he has always denied the conduct**; the Tribunal made **no finding that it
occurred**, and this record must not be used as though it did. A first-instance **tribunal**
decision, not a court, and decided on the **pre-amendment** law under transitional provisions, so it
is history for the scheme as it now stands. The allegations are of sexual offences against young
children — handle the detail carefully in class, and summarise rather than read out.
