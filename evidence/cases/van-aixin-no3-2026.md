A separated couple with a seven-year-old, property proceedings still running. In August 2025 the
wife left the former matrimonial home for a twelve-month lease at about **$3,259 a week — paid by
the husband's mother**. Maintenance of $4,000 a month was ordered in December 2025. In April 2026
her claim for **$61,435.55 a month** was dismissed (she had not shown she could not support herself
adequately) and a **dollar-for-dollar litigation funding order** was made. Given notice to vacate by
10 August, she asked him to guarantee a new lease, pay the bond and fund rent of up to $3,501 a
week, and booked serviced accommodation at about $3,744. The costs notices filed on the hearing day
projected her total costs at about **$1,003,478**; both sides' mediation costs at about $187,136.

## Held — 4 September 2026

- **s 72(1)**: a spouse must maintain the other "if, and only if" that other cannot support
  themselves adequately. "The inability of the applicant to support herself adequately must be
  established. It is not inferred merely because the other party has a higher income or access to
  greater resources" (at [61]).
- What changed: the rent-free arrangement ended, and the child needs housing near her school
  (**s 75(2)(c)**). But "the purpose of interim maintenance is not to require the husband to
  reproduce a standard of accommodation funded by a third party during or after the marriage"
  (at [67]). She searched only between $1,500 and $3,000 a week; he produced three-bedroom listings
  from about $575 to $1,050.
- Her own resources (**s 75(2)(b)**): she is 34, healthy, educated, employed, holds an $18,000 bond —
  and **owns an apartment**: under *Residential Tenancies Act 1997* (Vic) **s 91ZZA** a landlord may
  give 90 days' notice to move in; "it is not apparent why the wife cannot give the tenant notice to
  vacate and then occupy the apartment" (at [72]).
- **Orders**: maintenance **$6,000 a month** (up $2,000); lease guarantee, bond and rent funding
  refused; the enforcement application dismissed — the payment left his account on 28 April, two
  days before the funding order took effect, so no contravention; by consent, disclosure extended
  and mediation brought forward to before 1 December 2026. No costs order — but "the anticipated
  costs bring into stark relief the need for the parties to critically assess the costs of this
  litigation" (at [96]); appearing to hear a judgment "will generally take at most 10 minutes",
  costed at $2,500.

## Use it for

- **Effectiveness in money** — the sharpest cost evidence in the collection: about $1,003,478
  projected, $187,136 for mediation, a $24,000-a-year increase. Three numbers in one sentence.
- **fam.2.3 / fam.2.5** — spousal maintenance as the interim face of separation; a court that funds
  one side's litigation through a dollar-for-dollar order so wealth cannot decide the outcome, brings
  mediation forward, and then comments on the cost.
- **shel.3.1 / shel.2.2 (affordability, leasing)** — leasing law decides a family law case: a notice
  to vacate, a bond, serviced accommodation and an investment apartment; rents of $3,744, $3,501,
  $3,259 and $575–$1,050 a week in one judgment.
- **fam.1.5** — the child's housing is a statutory factor in its own right (s 75(2)(c)), not an
  afterthought.
- **fam.t.compliance** — an enforcement application that failed on two days' timing: orders operate
  prospectively.
