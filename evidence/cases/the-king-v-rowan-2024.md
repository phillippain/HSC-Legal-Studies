"Anna Rowan" (a pseudonym), a woman with a mild intellectual disability, lived from 1992 with a
violent and controlling partner, JR, on whom she was financially and socially dependent. She was
charged with thirteen joint sexual offences against two of their daughters between 2009 and 2015, in
which JR was the principal and she was present or assisted. Before her retrial the judge ruled there
was "no factual basis" for **duress**; she was convicted on twelve counts and sentenced to 7 years
5 months. The Victorian Court of Appeal quashed the convictions; the Crown appealed.

## Held — Crown appeal dismissed

- **The burden runs both ways**: the accused bears an *evidential* burden to raise duress; once raised,
  the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that she did not act under it.
- **The threat** — duress (*R v Hurley* [1967] VR 526) needs a threat of death or serious harm if the
  accused fails to act, but it may be express or implied — "whether it is formulated in words or left
  unsaid" — and need only be **operative at the time**, not repeated before each act.
- **Applied** — pervasive violence, intimidation and control raised a reasonable possibility of an
  implied threat of serious violence to her or the children if she refused. Duress should have gone to
  the jury; the retrial stands.
- **Edelman J**, alone, would go further: a demand is not essential; "A focus exclusively upon threats
  may be an unjust, unprincipled and unreasonable approach to human psychology."

## Use it for

- **crime.3.8 (complete defences)** — the leading Australian statement of duress, and the common-law

- **fam.2.4 / coercive control** — the criminal law recognising the effect of a pattern of control, not
  just a single threat; set beside NSW's coercive control offence (*Crimes Act* s 54D, 2024).
- **crime.3.6** — another defence the Crown must *disprove* once raised.

## Caution

A Victorian case: one count turned on Victoria's statutory duress (*Crimes Act 1958* s 322O), the rest
on common law. Edelman J's wider view is his alone. The facts involve child sexual abuse — keep
discussion at the legal level. The retrial outcome is not in the judgment.
