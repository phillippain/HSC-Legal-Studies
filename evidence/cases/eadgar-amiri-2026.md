De facto, never married; two children. A **Binding Financial Agreement** executed 11 April 2018 —
he retained a property and paid her $200,000. In early 2020 she moved back in because of financial
hardship, sleeping in a child's bedroom: separate bedrooms, separate finances, separate social
lives, her own address on her licence, mail redirected, single parent pension kept, no contribution
to mortgage or household.

She applied in November 2024 to set aside the Agreement, asserting the relationship had resumed. Her
solicitors ceased acting; she self-represented; a **s 102NA** order (the Family Cross-Examination of
Parties Scheme) was made but not taken up; she filed no trial material and missed three court
events. The trial proceeded **undefended**.

## Held

A declaration under **s 4AA** that no de facto relationship existed after 11 April 2018 — s 4AA
requires a "relationship as a couple living together on a genuine domestic basis", and at [58] the
cohabitation was "of convenience rather than a resumption of a de facto relationship". Costs fixed
at **$4,500**.

## Use it for

- **Alternative family relationships** — the headline use, and a clean contrast with the legal
  requirements of marriage, which need no such inquiry.
- **Changing community values**: de facto partners have had access to the *Family Law Act* property
  regime in NSW only since 1 March 2009.
- **Law reform**: s 102NA (2018/19) and s 114UB (from 10 June 2025).
- **Access to justice** — she lost her s 102NA protection by refusing instructions, and the
  undefended hearing shows what self-representation costs in practice.
