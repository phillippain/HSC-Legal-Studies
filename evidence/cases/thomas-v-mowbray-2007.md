## Facts

**Joseph Thomas** trained at an Al Qa'ida camp in Afghanistan in 2001. His 2006 convictions were quashed by the Victorian Court of Appeal on 18 August 2006 because admissions made in Pakistan were wrongly admitted. On **27 August 2006** Mowbray FM made an **interim control order** at the AFP's request: a midnight to 5am curfew at home, reporting to police three times a week, and limits on communication and travel.

## Held — 5 to 2 that Subdivision B of Division 104 is valid

- Supported by the **defence power** (and, for Gleeson CJ and Gummow and Crennan JJ, the external affairs power).
- Not inconsistent with **Chapter III**: the court is doing judicial work when it decides whether an order is reasonably necessary, appropriate and adapted to prevent a terrorist act.
- **Kirby J** dissented on every question. **Hayne J** accepted legislative power but held the task is not judicial power.
- The formal order does not record the split: it is read from the reasons. Costs against Thomas.

## Use it for

- **crime.6.2**: a domestic measure against terrorism, and a test of how far prevention can limit liberty.
- **Rights against safety**: no conviction is needed, an *ex parte* interim order is possible and the standard is lower. Kirby J's dissent supplies the counter-argument.
- **crime.1.5**: the preventive, pre-offence approach to terrorism, alongside *Lodhi*.

## Caution

Thomas's convictions were quashed: do not call him a convicted terrorist. The order was imposed while a retrial was possible. Check Division 104's later history before teaching the present scheme. The file has no hearing date or CLR citation.
