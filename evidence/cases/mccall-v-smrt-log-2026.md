During a civil trial over a Hunter Valley property held as tenants in common, Ms McCall spent four days
being cross-examined. The other side subpoenaed texts, emails and screenshots between her solicitors and
counsel. She claimed **client legal privilege** over a large portion; her opponents argued it had never
attached, or had been lost.

## Held — Hammerschlag CJ in Eq

- **The client must establish privilege** (*Evidence Act 1995* s 118, advice; s 119, litigation; s 131,
  settlement); the other side must then show it was lost. The judge **read the documents himself**.
- Most texts stayed protected. But **three** messages or parts did not:
  - two whose **substance she had revealed in her own evidence** — privilege is lost by voluntary
    disclosure (s 122);
  - one passage whose dominant purpose was to **benefit the solicitors**, not to advise her.
- The judge flagged, without deciding, a possible concern under s 125 (communications in furtherance of
  an offence or civil wrong) and the duty not to coach a witness under cross-examination.

## Use it for

- **inv.4.3 (the adversarial system)** — rules of evidence decided by the court, not the parties; the
  reason lawyer-client confidentiality exists, and its limits.
- **dis.2.6 (legal personnel)** — the lawyer's duties to the court in a live trial.
