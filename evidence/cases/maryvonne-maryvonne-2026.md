Two New Zealand-born parents separated in July 2026 in a remote Northern Territory town. The mother sought urgent interim orders to relocate with their two-year-old child to New Zealand. On 17 September 2026 Taglieri J refused: housing was available, key facts were disputed and untested, and no risk of harm was shown. The orders are interim, pending expert evidence and a final hearing.

## Use it for

- Divorce and separation: interim parenting orders after separation, decided under the s 60CA and s 60CC best interests test.
- Parental responsibility: joint decision-making on major long-term issues, with where the child lives and time with each parent.
- Courts: an urgent interim hearing on affidavits, with no cross-examination and no expert evidence yet.
- Cooperation: parents must communicate in writing and try for joint decisions; the court retains the case for case management.

This is an interim (interlocutory) decision on an urgent application, not a final parenting judgment: the court expressly kept the case for expert evidence and a final hearing.
