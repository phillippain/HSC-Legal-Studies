A Fijian man, in Australia since 2020, sought protection after a 2012 redundancy and health events in 2022. The Tribunal
accepted his evidence.

## Two tests, one standard

| Test | Law | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Refugee (s 36(2)(a)) | Real chance of serious harm for a Convention reason — not remote or far-fetched, even well below 50% (*Chan Yee Kin*) | No one targeted him for race; he could earn a living |
| Complementary protection (s 36(2)(aa)) | Real risk of significant harm — the same standard (*SZQRB*) — unless faced by the population generally (s 36(2B)(c)) | Lower healthcare standards are a risk the whole population faces |

The refusal was **affirmed**. His work, tax record and community ties in Australia were **not relevant** to the legal test.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 / hr.2.9** — the limits of Australia's protection obligations; a narrow legal test leaves economic and health
  hardship outside protection.
- **State sovereignty** — Australia decides who stays, within its treaty obligations.
