Robert Fardon was sentenced in 1989 to 14 years for rape, sodomy and assault. Weeks before his
sentence expired in June 2003, Queensland passed the *Dangerous Prisoners (Sexual Offenders) Act
2003*, which lets the Supreme Court order the **continuing detention** or supervision of a prisoner
serving a sentence for a serious sexual offence if satisfied, on acceptable, cogent evidence to a high
degree of probability, that there is an **unacceptable risk** of a serious sexual offence. White J
ordered his detention "for an indefinite term for control, care and treatment". He argued the Act
breached the *Kable* principle.

## Held — 6:1, the Act is valid

- *Kable* is about **institutional integrity**: public confidence is "an indicator, but not the
  touchstone" (Gummow J). Kable's Act targeted one man; this is "a general law authorising the
  preventive detention of a prisoner in the interests of community protection" (Gleeson CJ).
- **A real judicial process** — the Attorney-General bears the onus, the rules of evidence apply, a high
  standard of proof, reasons, appeal and annual review.
- **Protective, not punitive** — protection is already a purpose of sentencing and indefinite sentences
  already exist, so "it is difficult to maintain a strict division between punitive and preventive
  detention".
- **Kirby J, dissenting**: "Punishment is punishment and that is what the continued imprisonment
  ordered in the appellant's case is in law as well as effect."

## Use it for

- **crime.4.8 (post-sentencing)** — the constitutional basis for continuing detention and extended
  supervision orders, including NSW's *Crimes (High Risk Offenders) Act 2006* (which the judgment does
  not mention).
- **Kable → Fardon** — the same question with the opposite answer: what makes a detention law valid.
- **Balancing** — community protection against the principle that punishment follows only a crime
  proved, which is Kirby J's whole dissent.

## Caution

The judges describe Fardon's history differently; quote the orders, not the background. The UN Human
Rights Committee later found the detention breached ICCPR art 9 (2010) — not in this judgment.
