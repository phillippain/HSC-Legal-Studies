Ms Yin married Mr B in a foreign country in **1999**. She left him, and in **2014** married Mr Landon
in Australia. Mr Landon later applied for a **decree of nullity**, arguing the marriage was never
valid because Ms Yin was still legally married to Mr B.

## Held

The marriage was **void**. Both the *Marriage Act 1961* (Cth) and the *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth)
provide that a marriage is void where either party is, at the time, **lawfully married to another
person**. A decree of nullity was granted.

## Use it for

- **The legal requirements of a valid marriage** (`fam.1.3`) with the distinction most answers miss:
  a **decree of nullity** declares that a valid marriage never existed; a **divorce** ends one that
  did. Different tests, different consequences for property and status.
- **Recognition of foreign marriages** — an overseas marriage was effective enough in Australian law
  to invalidate a later Australian one, which is a clean example of private international law reaching
  into family law.
- Beside the other requirements: **real consent**, **age** (18, or 16–17 by court order), not within a
  **prohibited relationship**, and the **prescribed formalities** — an authorised celebrant, the
  notice period, two witnesses, and the words required by s 45.
