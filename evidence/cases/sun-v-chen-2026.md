Ms Sun engaged a building company, later in administration, to build three houses. She claimed its
director Mr Chen was personally liable for defective work under the *Design and Building
Practitioners Act 2020* (NSW). Mr Chen and his ex-wife Ms Yu had divorced and entered a financial
agreement dividing their assets: the family home went to Ms Yu, who was raising their children
there, and his interest was transferred to her in March 2024 — **the same day Ms Sun commenced
proceedings**.

## Held — Ms Sun failed on every ground

| Issue | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Transfer made "with intent to defraud creditors", s 37A *Conveyancing Act 1919* (NSW) | Not satisfied — the divorce and separation were genuine and neither knew of the intended claim |
| Financial agreement entered into to defeat a creditor, s 90K(1)(aa)(i) *Family Law Act* | Not established |
| Purchaser in good faith, s 37A(3) | **Made out** — refinancing the mortgage and giving up marital claims are valuable consideration |
| Cross-claim that Mr Chen held on trust for Ms Yu | Dismissed |

Principles applied: intent may be **inferred** from circumstances (*Marcolongo v Chen*; *Cannane*);
valuable consideration can be non-monetary; reconstructed oral evidence is unreliable and
contemporaneous documents are preferred (*Watson v Foxman*); the creditor bears the onus on intent
and the transferee on good faith.

## Use it for

- **Property on relationship breakdown, from outside.** Every other family case in the collection
  is two parties dividing a pool. This one asks what a division does to **a third party** — and
  answers that a genuine settlement is not undone because a creditor would prefer it were.
- **Shelter as well as Family**: the family home, joint ownership, mortgage refinancing, and who
  bears the housing risk after separation. A clean cross-topic piece for "the role of the legal
  system in protecting those securing shelter".
- Note the DBP Act appearing again — the same statutory duty as *Pafburn*, this time as the debt
  someone was trying to escape.
