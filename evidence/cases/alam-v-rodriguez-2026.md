Ms Alam sought an **apprehended violence order** against the manager of her unit block. In the Local Court
(27 February 2026) she was **unrepresented**; the magistrate offered an adjournment three times, but she
chose not to call her mother (who needed an interpreter). The application was dismissed with costs. She
appealed to the District Court; three ex tempore decisions followed on one day.

## The three decisions

| | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **No 1** [2026] NSWDC 387 | Leave to call fresh evidence? (ss 18(2), 19; s 19(6): was she represented?) | **Granted** — fresh evidence is exceptional, but she had been unrepresented and the AVO scheme matters |
| **No 2** [2026] NSWDC 388 | Was stalking, intimidation, assault or damage proved **on the balance of probabilities**? | **No** — the mother's evidence could not plausibly fit her daughter's account; appeal dismissed |
| **No 3** [2026] NSWDC 389 | Costs? | The case was so lacking in credit it was **frivolous and vexatious in law**: **$2,800** costs |

## Use it for

- **crime.3.6** — AVOs are civil orders decided on the **balance of probabilities**, not beyond reasonable
  doubt; contrast a criminal charge.
- **crime.3.7 / crime.3.5** — witnesses, interpreters and fresh evidence, and the cost of self-representation.
- **fam.2.4 / access to justice** — the AVO process for a non-domestic relationship.

## Caution

A **class case summary**. The allegations were **not proved**: teach the procedure, not the facts.
*Charara* is printed with two different citations in the judgments; the Local Court costs amount is not in
the file.
