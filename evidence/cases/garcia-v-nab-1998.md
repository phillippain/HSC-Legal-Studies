Jean Garcia signed a $270,000 guarantee of her husband's company debts on 25 November 1987 in under a minute with no explanation. It was secured by an "all moneys" mortgage over the Wahroonga family home that she did not realise stood behind it. The bank demanded about $327,000 and claimed possession.

## Held

The High Court **allowed her appeal** and restored the trial orders. The joint judgment (Gaudron, McHugh, Gummow and Hayne JJ) **explained and followed** *Yerkey v Jones* as an application of **unconscionability**: it is unconscionable to enforce against a volunteer surety who did not understand the transaction where the creditor gave no explanation and knew of no independent advice. Kirby J reached the result by a gender-neutral modified *O'Brien* rule; Callinan J applied *Yerkey*. The **Consumer Credit Legal Centre (NSW)** appeared as amicus curiae.

## Caution

This is a mortgaged home securing someone else's debt, not a purchase. Three separate sets of reasons reach the same orders, so the ratio is not unanimous. The file gives no hearing date, CLR citation or Act section numbers.
