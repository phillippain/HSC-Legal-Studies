An **interim** decision (25 September 2026) about a girl, Y, aged 11, taken by her father to "Country B" in
**February 2026** without her mother's knowledge or consent. The parents separated finally in October 2024
(pseudonyms; the country is not named in the file). The mother filed in July 2026; the father did **not attend**
the hearings of 29 July or 21 August 2026. A defended interim hearing is listed for **4 December 2026**.

## The two gates to jurisdiction

| | Gate 1 — citizenship | Gate 2 — habitual residence |
|---|---|---|
| Provision | *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) s 69E(1)(b) | ss 111CC and 111CD(1)(e) |
| Why it applies | Y is an **Australian citizen** | Country B is **not** a party to the Child Protection Convention, so residence is needed as well |
| Result | Met | Born and raised in NSW, at school here since kindergarten, family here; ties to Country B too weak to lose habitual residence |

## Held — 25 September 2026

- **Jurisdiction**: s 111CD(1)(e) is conjunctive — citizenship **and** habitual residence (*Kurtis & Aldo* [2025]
  FedCFamC1F 621; *LK v Director-General* (2009) 237 CLR 582).
- **Risk**: Y is at risk of harm from being deprived of a relationship with her mother, siblings and extended
  family (s 60CC; s 60CG).
- **Orders**: the father must return Y to Sydney by **4 pm on 30 October 2026**; on return she lives with the
  father, time with the mother as agreed; a **Child Impact Report** (s 62G) and reports from DCJ and NSW Police
  (s 67ZBD). Moving Y to the mother's primary care was **not** ordered at this stage.
- **Case management**: the court warned the father of an arrest warrant or an undefended hearing.

## Use it for
