The wife and her late husband married in **April 1996** and made a **financial agreement under s 90C
of the *Family Law Act*** on **15 July 2008**, during the marriage and after a brief separation. By
mid-2007 she had decided to separate but **could not afford alternative accommodation**. They were
never divorced. When he died they were **living as husband and wife**.

She sought a declaration that the agreement was of no force and effect, with **Supreme Court of
South Australia** proceedings running in parallel over his estate under the *Inheritance (Family
Provision) Act 1972* (SA), the interested parties including the children of his deceased sibling.

## Held — declaration granted

**Section 90DA** is the hinge. A binding financial agreement, so far as it deals with how property is
to be divided **on separation**, does not take effect until a **separation declaration** is made. The
declaration must be **in writing**, signed by at least one spouse, and state that the parties have
separated and are living separately and apart — and it **cannot be made until they are in fact
separated**.

Stewart J found the parties **married in 1996 and never divorced**, were **not separated** at the
husband's death, and that **no separation declaration was ever made** — no evidence anyone signed
one, contemplated signing one, or took any step towards it. So the operative provisions of the
agreement **never became operative at all**. The alternative arguments (rescission, abandonment,
termination) did not need to be decided.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.3 (consequences of separation — property)** — the best illustration in the library that a
  binding financial agreement is a **conditional instrument**. Students write about BFAs as though
  signing one settles the property question; here one sat dormant for eighteen years and then did
  nothing, because a formality was never completed.
- **fam.1.3 / fam.2.1 (marriage, divorce)** — the legal meaning of **separation** doing real work.
  The wife had *decided* to separate in 2007 and stayed because she could not afford to leave.
  Deciding to separate is not separating, and the financial reality of being unable to leave is what
  kept the marriage legally on foot. A sharp, humane point about law and economic power.
- **Form as substance (inv.1.2)** — s 90DA imposes a writing-and-signature requirement, and the
  consequence of ignoring it is total. Good for whether legal formality protects people or traps
  them.
- **fam.1.2 (state and federal jurisdiction)** — a Commonwealth family law question running
  alongside a **state** succession claim over the same estate, with the family law answer shaping
  what the Supreme Court has to work with.
- **Pair with *Pratley*** — two very different routes to dividing property, one by agreement and one
  by s 79, and here the agreement route simply failed.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB of the *Family Law Act* makes publishing an identifying account an offence.
A **short, first-instance decision of 43 paragraphs** on particular facts: it does not hold that BFAs
are generally ineffective, only that **this** one never became operative. The Court expressly did
**not** decide the alternative contentions, so do not cite it on rescission or abandonment. The
related South Australian estate proceedings were **unresolved**, so this is not the end of the
dispute. Check the current text of ss 90C and 90DA before quoting them — Part VIIIA has been amended
since 2008.
