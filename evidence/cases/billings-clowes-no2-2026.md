Married 2011, separated early 2023. X born 2015. Last unsupervised time June 2023. Four counts of
common assault against the father were **dismissed**, as was the final ADVO application. By trial,
contact was video calls of **30–60 seconds**.

**X was never interviewed or observed by the Court Child Expert for either family report** — the
mother would not permit an individual interview. The judge recorded that he had been "deprived of
important evidence".

## Held

- Family violence allegations **not established**, but **not** found to be deliberately false.
- Cocaine use in 2024 established; continuing use and dependence **not** established (four negative
  hair tests across 2025).
- Mother presently unable to support the father–child relationship — the Court Child Expert's
  unequivocal opinion, accepted.
- **X to live with the father**, sole decision-making subject to a duty to consult; ex parte
  recovery order; a 3-month moratorium on maternal time with s 68B injunctions, then supervised
  time with **no automatic progression**.
- ICL costs application dismissed — s 114UC(2) bars an order against a legally aided party.

## The reasoning that matters

At [290]–[310] the court weighed the **probability, gravity, duration and manageability** of the
competing harms. Change of residence: serious short-term grief, highly likely but mitigable.
Staying: permanent loss of the father–child relationship, "already materialising", not manageable
because a further supervision order "would repeat an approach which has already failed".

At [308]: "The change of residence is not ordered to punish the mother, to reward the father, or
because every allegation made by the mother has been shown to be deliberately false."

Per *Dever & Serano* [2025] FedCFamC1A 202, a finding of false or misguided allegations does **not
by itself** justify a change of residence.

## Best evidence uses

1. **Best interests where every option harms the child** — the four-part risk comparison is a
   ready-made structure for a 25-mark answer.
2. **The child who was never heard** — the strongest available point on the limits of the family law
   system and **CROC art 12**.
3. **Enforceability** — successive interim orders produced seven visits and then nothing for 22
   months; the court needed police.
4. **Unproven is not false**, and what non-attendance costs a party.
5. **Delay, cost, access** — two years; the father's costs notice showed **$69,300** before criminal
   defence, testing and supervision.
