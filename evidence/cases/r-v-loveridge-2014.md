On the night of 7 July 2012 Kieran Loveridge, 18, drunk and a month into a probation order for an
earlier assault, punched five strangers in Kings Cross over about an hour. One of them, **Thomas
Kelly**, 18, who was walking along on his phone, fell, struck his head on the pavement and died two
days later. Campbell J sentenced Loveridge to **6 years** for manslaughter and **7 years 2 months** in
total, non-parole 5 years 2 months.

## Held — Crown appeal allowed

- **General deterrence** got only a passing reference. "Particular significance is to be given to
  general deterrence in sentencing young offenders who commit serious crimes of violence on a public
  street"; alcohol-fuelled violence in public "calls for an emphatic sentencing response".
- **Specific deterrence** was underweighted for an offender on probation; intoxication did not
  mitigate; the attacks were not spontaneous but a course of conduct whose seriousness "grew
  incrementally".
- **No tariff**: "it is not meaningful to speak of one-punch or single-punch manslaughter cases as
  constituting a single class of offences".
- The Court declined its residual discretion and resentenced: **13 years 8 months, non-parole 10
  years 2 months**.

## Caution

The judgment does not mention s 25A (the one-punch offence), the lockout laws or mandatory sentencing;
link them as context, not as part of the decision.
