After a husband was charged with assaulting his wife and the couple separated in January 2024, he sold the family home to Ms Mandel, his long-term partner outside the marriage, and moved large sums overseas. In a final judgment the husband did not contest, Christie J set the transfer aside under s 106B on 15 September 2026, gave the wife all the Australian assets, and gave her sole decision-making for their child.

## Use it for

- Separation and property: s 106B and s 79 applied to a home, joint property and overseas assets, with sole decision-making for the child.
- Non-compliance: non-disclosure, a hidden sale and a husband the court expects will not comply with orders.
- Parental responsibility: sole decision-making for the mother because of violence, a breach, distance and a blocked contact.
- Domestic violence: the assault and a breached protection order shaped the s 79(5) and parenting findings.
- Courts: injunctions, a caveat, a third party joined and adverse inferences in an undefended hearing.

## Cautions

This is a final first-instance judgment, not an appeal, and the husband did not take part. The file is inconsistent on dates: [6] says the transfer was registered in June 2024, [43] says registration did not occur in early April 2024, and order 10 refers to a transfer dated April 2025; [74] says he stopped taking part on 16 April 2025 but [82] says 18 March 2025. Paragraph labels are doubled (the second is real). Care line: an assault and a breached protection order; keep to the legal test.
