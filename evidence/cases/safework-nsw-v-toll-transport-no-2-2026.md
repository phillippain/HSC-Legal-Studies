On 20 December 2022 a Toll Transport worker's ankle was crushed freeing a jammed pallet with a
crowbar on a live conveyor at Banksmeadow; there was no safe procedure for clearing jams. The company
pleaded guilty to breaching ss 19(1) and 32 of the *Work Health and Safety Act 2011* (NSW). Chin J
set a starting fine of **$950,000**, reduced it by **15%** for a plea given late, and fined the
company **$807,500** on 26 October 2026, with costs and half the fine to SafeWork NSW (maximum at the
time: $1,860,843).

## Held

- The risk was foreseeable and the fixes (a written procedure, training, lock-out) were cheap, so
  culpability was significant.
- A failure to implement an inexpensive administrative control "is highly unlikely to be less serious"
  than failing to implement a costly higher-order control; the judge declined to place the offence on a
  scale of seriousness.
- **13 prior convictions** (1999 to 2025) were aggravating under s 21A(2)(d): "a continuing resistance
  to the requirement for a proactive approach"; specific deterrence carried considerable weight.

## Use it for

- **Sentencing and punishment** (crime.4): a fine as the penalty for a corporation, the purposes of
  punishment, aggravating and mitigating factors and the plea discount; also Year 11 punishments.
- **Compliance and effectiveness:** 26 years of repeated breaches set against a fine below half the maximum.
- Do not say the offence was held "Category 2" (the catchwords do; the body does not). The judgment never
  mentions totality.
