Michael Anderson signed a site agreement at Kincumber Nautical Village on 3 January 2020. The site fee rose each year by **CPI plus 3.75% plus a share of the operator's cost rises**. In November 2023 the operator gave notice of a rise from $242 to $268 a week from 7 December 2023. His Tenants' Union solicitors applied to NCAT on 19 March 2024.

## Held

The agreement was a **standard form contract** and the fee term was an **unfair term** under ss 23 and 24 of the Australian Consumer Law: it caused a significant imbalance (the formula "double dipped" on inflation), the operator did not prove it was reasonably necessary, and it was not an excluded **upfront price** term. The $268 notice was invalid, so fees overpaid since 7 December 2023 were refundable. NCAT cannot declare a term void but treated it as void for restitution.

## Use it for

- **Other shelter types** (`shel.2.4`): a residential land lease community, where residents own the home and pay for the site.
- **Affordability** (`shel.3.1`): a fee formula that rises faster than inflation for residents on fixed incomes.
- **Effectiveness and reform**: consumer law succeeded where the Act's fixed method rule had failed (*Kincumber v Morris*), and the 2024 amendment limits fixed calculations to one element but does not reach existing agreements.

## Caution

The decision applies the Australian Consumer Law, not the land lease Act. NCAT **refused a refund for other home owners** (s 68(2)(b)) for lack of evidence, so only Mr Anderson got a remedy. The operator's director died before cross-examination, so his evidence carried little weight. The file's para 33 date ("3 January 2023") looks like a slip for 2020.
