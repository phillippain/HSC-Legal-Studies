Parents of four children separated but kept living in the same house, both working shifts. Child
Support first set care at 50/50 — "typical" for parents separated under one roof — then adopted a
Centrelink decision of 80% mother, 20% father. He objected, was disallowed, and sought review; both
parents were heard by audio link, unrepresented.

## Held — set aside: mother 67%, father 33%

- **s 50** — the care percentage must match the care actually given. The Child Support Guide's
  50/50 suggestion is a guide, and "the Tribunal is not bound to follow it".
- ***Polec & Staker*** [2011]: care includes who **pays for** the child's needs. The father paid the
  home loan every fortnight and some bills, and did breakfast, the school bus and evenings on shift
  days. The mother did 80–90% of school mornings, meals and appointments; six to eight weeks at the
  grandparents' home pushed care her way.
- Both 67% and 80% fall in the **66–86% band** (76% cost), and 33% and 20% in the **14–34% band**
  (24% cost). So "there is no change to the rate of child support".

## Use it for

- **fam.3.2** — parental responsibility inside one home: shift rosters, grandparents as carers, and
  financial support recognised as care.
- **fam.2.1** — "separated under one roof": separation without moving out.
- **Law reform / effectiveness** — banded cost percentages trade precision for predictability and
  create cliff edges; the father won on the facts and gained nothing.
- **Shelter** — one house, two separated parents, four children. Use with care on affordability:
  the decision attributes the arrangement to shift work, not cost.

Short reasons, and the care period under review ends in June 2025 — later changes were outside it.
