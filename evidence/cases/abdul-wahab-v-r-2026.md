## Held

Two decisions on 27 October 2026, taught separately.

- **No 1 (recusal):** after the hearing the Court emailed 14 proposed historical facts, then announced it would not use them. The apprehended bias test (*Ebner*; *DPP v Smith* [2024] HCA 32) was applied and the application refused: the step was an attempt at procedural fairness put to both sides, and any error was self-corrected.
- **No 2 (appeal):** the appeal by rehearing was dismissed. Reasonable excuse is judged objectively, in four stages, from the standpoint of an ordinary member of the community; the Crown negatived it beyond reasonable doubt. Section 93ZA was valid: its burden on political communication passed structured proportionality (suitable, necessary, adequate in balance).
- The sentence (a conditional release order with no conviction) was not appealed.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.5 / crime.3.6** — an appeal by rehearing, the Crown's burden, and a recusal application as a limit on judicial process.
- **crime.t.reform** — s 93ZA has since been repealed: a hate-symbol law tested, then changed.
- **inv.1.6 / dis.1.5** — the three-step bias test and procedural fairness in a real recusal.

## Caution

No 1 is not authority that judges may research after a hearing: the judge treated his step as possibly an error he corrected. Describe the placard only in the judgment's words. Care: antisemitism and the Israel-Gaza conflict; keep to the legal test. Not in file: the proposed facts, costs and any further appeal.
