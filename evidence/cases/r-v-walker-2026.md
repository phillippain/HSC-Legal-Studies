Walker, 25, used a hammer to force the door of a home where the victim had sought refuge, knowing
people were inside, and later acted to stop her giving evidence. He pleaded guilty in the Local Court
to **aggravated break, enter and intimidate** (*Crimes Act* s 112(2): maximum 20 years, SNPP 5), an act
**intending to pervert the course of justice** (s 319: maximum 14 years), and a related intimidation
charge (*Crimes (Domestic and Personal Violence) Act* s 13(1)) sentenced under a s 166 certificate.

## The sentence

- **25% discount** for the Local Court plea (s 25D(2)(a)); each indicative sentence discounted, then
  aggregated (s 53A).
- **Objective seriousness: lower end** for both offences — spontaneous and de-escalated — though the
  threat to use the hammer "was clearly implicit". Deterrence paramount for perverting justice.
- **Aggravating**: on conditional liberty with a warrant outstanding (s 21A(2)(j)). **Mitigating**: the
  plea, no planning.
- **Bugmy** applied: in custody from 14, drugs from 12, sexually assaulted in juvenile detention.
- **Result**: aggregate **5 years, non-parole 3** (60%, special circumstances), backdated to 23 January
  2026.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.3 / fam.2.4** — sentencing for domestic violence with every step visible: maximums, SNPP,
  s 21A, *Bugmy*, s 5 and aggregate sentencing.
- **The justice system as a victim** — the perverting count protects the witness's evidence, not just the
  witness. Pair with *R v Billings* ("public justice" murder).

A **class evidence summary** of one judge's first-instance remarks, which may be appealed. Keep the
victim's circumstances at the level the judgment records.
