A jury convicted Anthony Large of two sexual offences and an assault occasioning actual bodily harm on
his partner (28 March 2025); he received an aggregate **5 years** with a 3-year non-parole period
(5 June 2025). The complainant is protected by s 578A of the *Crimes Act 1900* (NSW). On 26 October 2026
the Court of Criminal Appeal dismissed the conviction appeal and allowed the sentence appeal,
resentencing him to **3 years 4 months** with a 2-year non-parole period.

## Held

- **Conviction:** the test is a material irregularity that could realistically have affected the jury
  (*Nudd*, *Brawn*), not counsel's incompetence. Counsel did not put to the complainant that she had
  lied about a seven-minute taxi trip (*Browne v Dunn*; *Hofer*) or ask for her recall. Stern JA and
  Dhanji J saw rational forensic choices; Fagan J called them "conspicuous errors" but found the point
  immaterial.
- **Sentence:** the judge found the offending "consistent with an ill-judged impulsive decision" he was
  more prone to given his brain injury, but did not consider the effect on general and specific
  deterrence: "one of the rare cases where serious injustice was occasioned".
