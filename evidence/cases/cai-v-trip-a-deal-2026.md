A couple booked a 29-day Antarctica cruise through Trip A Deal (June 2024). The company sent a visa reminder on 19 December
2024; he applied the next day, was refused boarding in Buenos Aires because the physical visa had not arrived, and joined the
ship later. Self-represented, he claimed refunds and expenses under the **s 60** guarantee of due care and skill.

## The three questions

| Issue | Held |
|---|---|
| Did Trip A Deal supply booking services only? | Yes — its terms made visas the traveller's job |
| Did it breach s 60 or its contract? | No evidence of any lack of care or skill; the missing visa, not the booking, caused the loss |
| Was the case proved on the balance of probabilities? | No — the applicant carried the burden and did not meet it |

Application **dismissed**. NCAT had earlier found it had no jurisdiction over the cruise line (September 2025).
