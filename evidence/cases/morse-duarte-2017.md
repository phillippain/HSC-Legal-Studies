The parents separated around September 2013; the three children lived with their mother under
**equal shared parental responsibility**. In October 2013 Mr Tolman moved in with her, and they had
two more children. He had a criminal history including **allegations of sexual assault and offences
against minors**. In 2014 **Family and Community Services placed the children with their father**,
where they settled into routines and improved markedly at school.

## Held

The *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) allows the court to weigh **actual and unacceptable risks of family
violence**, with the child's best interests paramount. The court found Mr Tolman posed an
**unacceptable risk** and that the mother **could not protect the children from it**. The existing
order was discharged, the **father was given sole parental responsibility**, and the mother was
allowed **supervised** time, with contact with Mr Tolman **strictly prohibited**.

## Use it for

- **Dealing with domestic violence** (`fam.2.4`) where the risk comes from a **third party**, not from
  a parent — and the finding that matters is about the mother's **capacity to protect**, not her own
  conduct. That is the hardest and most examinable form of the unacceptable-risk test.
- **The interaction of the state and federal systems**: a state child protection agency placed the
  children; the federal family court then made the orders. Good for `fam.1.2`.
- Set beside *Kendrick*: two cases, two years apart, both displacing shared parental responsibility
  because of who else is in the household.
