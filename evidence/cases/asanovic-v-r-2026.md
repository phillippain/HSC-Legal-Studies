Asanovic ran a methylamphetamine supply operation over six weeks in 2020 — 68 individual supplies,
and 4,912 g found in a neighbour's garage (nine times the large commercial quantity). The District
Court imposed an **aggregate 12 years, non-parole 8** (special circumstances; 66%), including a count
of participating in a criminal group. He appealed on one narrow ground: the judge had counted the same
conduct twice in fixing the indicative sentence for the criminal group offence.

## Held

- **Error found.** Where offences share significant factual overlap, punishing both in full is double
  counting contrary to *Pearce v The Queen* (1998) — the Crown's test that one offence must "wholly
  comprehend" the other was rejected. The judge never addressed the overlap.
- **But no lesser sentence warranted.** Once error is shown, *Criminal Appeal Act* s 6(3) requires the
  Court to decide for itself what sentence is warranted, including on post-sentence evidence. Custody
  records showed 91 days' segregation and a fresh plea to supplying 19.72 g of methylamphetamine
  **inside custody** — which sat badly with his letter of remorse and gave specific deterrence more
  weight.
- Stern JA would have imposed the same sentence; Rigg and Coleman JJ would have gone slightly higher.
  Leave granted, appeal **dismissed**.
