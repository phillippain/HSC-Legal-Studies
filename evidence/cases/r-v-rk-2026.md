Between 30 June and 8 August 2024 RK, then **14**, did online research and sent messages about an attack on a marathon —
no plan, date or materials. The AFP arrested him at home on 7 August 2024; bail was granted on very strict conditions and
eased after full compliance. He pleaded guilty to acts in preparation for a terrorist act (maximum **life**), with two
further offences on a s 16BA schedule.

## The three questions

| Issue | Held |
|---|---|
| How serious was the offending, judged on its own (*Muldrock*)? | At the lowest level of objective seriousness |
| How do terrorism sentencing principles interact with youth? | Youth carries less weight for serious offending (*Alou*), but earlier principles are not rigid formulae (*Hoolash*) |
| Was full-time imprisonment the only appropriate sentence (s 17A)? | No — remorse genuine, prospects of rehabilitation excellent |

**Order**: a 3-year community correction order from 9 November 2026 — Youth Justice supervision until 18, then community
corrections; one mobile phone, no encrypted apps, devices available on demand. A continuing detention or extended
supervision order could be sought later (Division 105A).

## In the media (ABC, 9 November 2026)

Sarah Gerathy's ABC report ("Boy who planned marathon terror attack at 14 avoids jail") notes that comparable NSW cases for
under-18 s 101.6 offenders produced head sentences of **7.5 to 16 years** (Public Defenders table, updated October 2025),
though their facts differ. [Class evidence summary of the ABC report](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1E1eqzA1H0CXMGhBfOSGSDZRqSSoyHFwB/view).
