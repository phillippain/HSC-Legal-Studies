Dominic Pullen, 23, drove after drinking and using cannabis, slid into a parked semi-trailer in a
road-works zone, trapped his friend's leg — breaking it — and tried to run. He pleaded guilty to
dangerous driving occasioning grievous bodily harm and failing to stop and assist (maximum 7 years
each). The District Court imposed an aggregate **15 months, served as an Intensive Correction Order
(ICO)**. A month later the new ICO scheme (*Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Amendment (Sentencing
Options) Act 2017*) commenced.

## Held — Crown appeal allowed, but still an ICO

- 15 months was **manifestly inadequate**: below the *Whyte* guideline for dangerous driving, and
  failing to stop was wrongly put "well below" mid-range. The residual discretion was not exercised in
  his favour despite his progress.
- Resentenced to an **aggregate 3 years** — still within the 3-year limit for an ICO for an aggregate
  sentence (s 68(2)).
- Under **s 66** "community safety" is the "paramount consideration", and "community safety is not
  achieved simply by incarcerating someone": supervision, work and treatment addressed his risk of
  reoffending better than prison. The s 3A purposes "become subordinate". Served by ICO with 650 hours
  of community service.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.6 (types of penalties)** — what an ICO is: imprisonment served in the community, with
  conditions — and how the 2018 reforms (from NSWLRC Report 139) replaced suspended sentences.
- **crime.4.7 (alternatives)** — community-based penalties as a deliberate policy choice.
- **Evaluation** — a "win" for the Crown that left the offender out of prison; BOCSAR's ICO research
  is the effectiveness evidence.

## Caution

The judgment does not state a "last resort" hierarchy; the ICO conditions regime has changed since
2018 — check the current Act. Pair with *R v White* (2025) for a Community Correction Order.
