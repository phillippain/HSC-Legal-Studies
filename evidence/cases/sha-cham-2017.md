Mr Sha married Ms B in 1997. In 2011, while still married, he met Ms Cham. From early 2012 they were
in a sexual relationship; at his request she stopped working, they spent more time at her home
including overnight stays, and in August 2012 they entered a **financial agreement** making provision
for children they planned to have. She conceived by **IVF** and gave birth to his daughter in mid-2013,
after which he paid her **$2,500 a month**. The relationship ended late in 2013; his divorce from Ms B
was finalised in 2014. In 2015 a court found a **de facto relationship existed between 2012 and 2013**
and made orders accordingly. He appealed, arguing there had been no bona fide domestic relationship.

## Held

Appeal dismissed. The circumstances met several of the factors in **s 4AA of the *Family Law Act
1975* (Cth)**: a sexual relationship, financial dependence, a degree of mutual commitment to a shared
life, the care of a child, and — though the relationship was not registered — a **financial
agreement**. The financial agreement was upheld.

## Use it for

- **Alternative family relationships** (`fam.1.4`) — the statutory test, applied. s 4AA asks about the
  **nature of the relationship**, not about a checklist met in full: duration, common residence,
  sexual relationship, financial interdependence, property, commitment to a shared life, care of
  children, reputation and public aspects.
- **A de facto relationship existing alongside a marriage.** The Act permits it, and this case is the
  clean authority for it — useful whenever an answer assumes the categories are exclusive.
- **Access to the federal system**: since 2009 de facto partners bring financial disputes in the
  federal courts on the same footing as married couples. The consequence of the finding here was that
  the agreement and the property regime applied at all.
