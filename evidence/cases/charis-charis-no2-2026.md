Final property orders on **31 October 2025** divided the assets roughly equally and gave the husband
seven days to elect: pay **$62,859,077** in cash or transfer shares of equal value. He elected cash
on 7 November — and paid nothing. A month of argument over wording and his "ability to comply"; a
deadline of 19 December; silence. In cross-examination in January 2026 he accepted he had
**USD$42,942,710.65** available, did not transfer it, and gave no evidence of where it went. His
appeal was dismissed; a High Court special leave application was pending; **no stay was ever
granted**.

## Held — 27 August 2026

- **Power**: s 105(1) — "all decrees made under this Act may be enforced by any court having
  jurisdiction under this Act" — discretionary, informed only by what has happened since the order,
  refusable only if inequitable (*McMillan*; *Suris (No 2)*). He did not dispute the power, did not
  say enforcement was inequitable, and never applied to vary or discharge.
- "It was undisputed that the husband has not paid the wife a single dollar since the final orders
  were pronounced" (at [28]). The "marked level of detachment" found at trial had become "the
  languid and almost indolent approach the husband has taken to complying" (at [31]).
- "Court orders must be complied with, unless discharged. The court should be jealous to ensure
  compliance" (at [30]). Without the orders sought, "the prospect of compliance… is remote".
- **Orders**: a declared debt of **$67,320,349.30** (interest of $4,461,272.30 under s 117B, then
  **$17,824.42 a day**); the **wife appointed trustee for sale** of his company interests and both
  properties with sole carriage; **caveats**; a **registrar may sign** for him (s 106A); he must give
  vacant possession 28 days before settlement; **restrained from leaving Australia** with both
  passports held by the Registry (s 114UB) — "the restriction of a person's freedom of movement is a
  serious step" (at [38]) — with liberty to apply on three business days' notice. Days later, on an
  urgent application, his name went on the airport watch list. Allowed to him: 60 days before his
  home can be listed, because the children spend time with him there.

- **fam.t.compliance** — the whole case: an order, a conceded breach, and the Act's machinery when a
  party simply will not pay. Learn the list — trustee for sale, caveat, s 106A signing power,
  travel restraint — not just the outcome (HSC 2020 and 2025 compliance questions).
- **fam.2.5 / inv.4.1 (courts and the hierarchy)** — trial, appeal, special leave, enforcement, then
  an urgent application heard within days: the hierarchy and its speed both on display; and the
  rule that an appeal does not stay an order unless a stay is granted.
- **fam.2.3** — the last stage of a property dispute: not how the pool is divided but whether the
  division can be made to happen. Legal responses at their strongest and their slowest: powerful
  tools, ten months late.
- **hr.3.1 / hr.2.11** — freedom of movement restricted by a civil court to secure payment, weighed

- **shel.1.1 / shel.2.1** — two properties and a home: who may sell, who must leave, and when,
  decided as an enforcement question.
