In 2017 a delegate cancelled Mr Da Silva's partner visa on character grounds. In January 2019 the
**Administrative Appeals Tribunal reviewed that decision on its merits and gave the visa back**.
Five and a half years later the Minister personally overrode the Tribunal using **s 501BA(2)** of
the *Migration Act 1958* (Cth) — a power that sets aside a tribunal outcome, **expressly switches
off the rules of natural justice**, and from which there is **no merits review**.

Mr Da Silva could not argue the merits. He could only ask the Federal Court whether the power had
been exercised lawfully. He ran four grounds. Three failed.

**The fourth succeeded.** The Minister assumed that in five and a half years nothing about the
applicant's children had changed. In fact he had a fifth child, born October 2023, and a sixth on
the way. Certiorari issued; the Minister was ordered to pay costs.

## The timeline that makes it teachable

| | |
|---|---|
| 25 Jan 2019 | The AAT revokes the cancellation — *merits* review: the Tribunal remakes the decision |
| 29 Jan 2019 | Four days later the Department flags the case for possible referral under s 501BA |
| 4 Apr 2019 | The *Herald Sun* names him among those whose cancellations the Tribunal overturned |
| Jan 2023 | Direction 99 changes how decision-makers weigh ties to Australia |
| 24–25 May 2024 | Knowing an article is coming, the Minister's office asks for a summary of everyone named in the 2019 piece; *The Australian* then publishes a critique of the Minister over Direction 99 |

## Use it for

This is the richest single case in the collection, because it is four dot points at once:

1. **Separation of powers in motion** — an executive power to override an independent tribunal.
2. **Merits review against judicial review** — the difference decided the shape of the whole case.
3. **CROC inside an executive decision** — the best interests of children, and the ground that won.
4. **A real test of what protects rights without a Charter** — natural justice was switched off by
   statute, and what remained was judicial review of a factual assumption.

Note also the **media as an agency of pressure**: the case moves each time a newspaper publishes.
