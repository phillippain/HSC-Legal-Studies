Clare Nowland, 95, with dementia and using a walking frame, was walking the Yallambee Lodge aged-care
home in Cooma with kitchen knives before dawn on 17 May 2023. Staff called for an ambulance and
police were notified. At about **5.10 am**, in the nurses' office, Senior Constable Kristian White —
an officer of twelve years' experience — activated the taser's warning arc, said "Nah, bugger it", and
fired. She fell, struck her head and died on 24 May 2023.

- **15 October 2024**: application for a **judge-alone trial refused** — media coverage had not been
  shown to make a jury trial unfair.
- **27 November 2024**: the jury found him guilty of **manslaughter** (whether by unlawful and
  dangerous act or criminal negligence is not known).
- **28 March 2025**: a **two-year Community Correction Order** with 425 hours of community service
  (maximum for manslaughter: 25 years). The Crown sought full-time custody.
- **30 July 2025**: the Court of Criminal Appeal dismissed the DPP's appeal that the sentence was
  manifestly inadequate.

## The sentence — Harrison CJ at CL

- **Objective seriousness: lower end.** Her age and frailty engaged "a community expectation that
  violence will not be occasioned to people in that category", but he was on duty, nothing was
  premeditated, and his overestimate of the threat "serves merely to explain its commission".
- **The victim and the family** — at least ten relatives read victim impact statements of grief and
  anger; they were heard, but did not decide the sentence.
- **The offender** — no priors, remorse accepted from his letter, depression and PTSD, a lost career.
  Specific deterrence was unnecessary; general deterrence had only a minor role.
- **Penalty** — "the law in New South Wales contemplates the imposition of a Community Correction
  Order, even for offences of manslaughter"; prison "would in my view be disproportionate".

## Use it for

- **crime.4.4 (the victim)** and **crime.4.6 (penalties)** — a CCO for manslaughter, against the
  family's anger and the Crown's submissions: the role of the victim, and its limits.
- **Juries** (crime.3.9) — the contrast with *Dawson*, where judge-alone was granted.
- **Police use of force** (LEPRA s 231) and accountability: police are not above the law.

## Caution

The sentencing judgment never mentions media, politics or "public trust" — those angles come from
commentary. The appeal result is from news reports (The Nightly, 30 July 2025), not the judgment.
Earlier versions of this card gave the time as about 2 am; the judgment puts the tasering at
5.09.59 am.
