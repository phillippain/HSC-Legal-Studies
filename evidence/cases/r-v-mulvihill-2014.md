Rachelle Yeo was found dead in her North Curl Curl home in July 2012 with fatal stab wounds. Paul
Mulvihill, a former lover whose behaviour had become obsessive after she ended their affair, was
arrested the next day. He pleaded not guilty, claiming self-defence.

## Held

The Supreme Court jury convicted him of murder. In sentencing, the judge was **not satisfied beyond
reasonable doubt that Mulvihill went to the apartment intending to kill**, but was satisfied from the
placement and nature of the wounds that the attack itself was intended to kill. He was sentenced to
**29 years with a non-parole period of 22 years** — short of the life maximum precisely because
premeditation was not proved.

## Use it for

- **Court jurisdiction**: murder is a strictly indictable offence, so the Supreme Court has original
  jurisdiction; an appeal would go to the NSW Court of Criminal Appeal in its appellate jurisdiction.
  The Local Court's role is the committal, testing whether a prima facie case exists before court time
  is spent.
- **The standard of proof in sentencing** — beyond reasonable doubt governs aggravating facts too, not
  only guilt. That is a precise point most answers miss.
- A **domestic violence homicide** to set beside the coercive control material: the escalation was
  visible to family and friends before the killing.
