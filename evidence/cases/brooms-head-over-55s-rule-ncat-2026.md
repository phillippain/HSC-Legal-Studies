Brooms Head Holiday Park, on Crown land managed by **Clarence Valley Council**, has **52**
residential sites (30 permanent, 22 long-term casual). On **28 April 2026** park management gave
permanent residents 30 days' notice of a new community rule: occupants of residential sites must be
**at least 55**. Council said existing agreements were unaffected; residents said it would stop
their homes being sold to, or inherited by, anyone younger, and that the justification shifted —
first "aligning with current legislation", then industry (CCIA) advice the CCIA said it never gave.

A resident, **Fiona Stewart**, representing herself with the residents' van group, applied to NCAT.
Heard **3 September**; decided **12 October 2026**.

## Held (as reported)

- The rule was **not fair and reasonable** under **s 86** of the *Residential (Land Lease)
  Communities Act 2013* (NSW) and was **set aside** under **s 95**.
- The Tribunal could identify **no clear and coherent purpose**; it weighed the harm to the
  **marketability and saleability** of residents' homes, noted sites could not pass to children under
  55, and that the park's **residents committee had not been consulted** (s 90).

## The pathway for a community rule

1. Operator amends the rules — must be fair, reasonable and clearly expressed (**s 86**).
2. 30 days' written notice; residents committee consulted first (**s 90**).
3. Resident or operator applies to NCAT (**s 95(1)**).
4. NCAT may set aside, amend, modify or uphold the rule, or stay its start (**s 95(2)–(3)**).
5. Internal appeal to the Appeal Panel — as of right on a question of law, otherwise with leave
   (usually 28 days; 14 in residential proceedings); beyond that, only the Supreme Court.

**Age rules are not automatically invalid.** In March 2020 NCAT set aside a 55+ rule at Tweed
Billabong as unfair and discriminatory, and in December 2020 the **Appeal Panel reinstated it** for
new residents. Brooms Head therefore turns on its facts — the shifting purpose and the missed
consultation — and could be appealed.

## The wider dispute

This is one of two fights at the Clarence Valley parks, under two different Acts. In **March 2025**
council gave **136** holiday-van owners across four parks termination notices under the *Holiday
Parks (Long-term Casual Occupation) Act 2002*, which requires a primary residence elsewhere (vacate
by 30 June 2025), saying tourist demand had grown and the sites are public land; owners said some
had lived there full-time for years and feared homelessness; a council motion for a full compliance
audit was defeated 6–3. **About 80** of those cases were still before NCAT at October 2026 (seven
resolved). Land lease communities house **over 40,000** people in NSW and are promoted as low-cost
housing for older people; the Tenants' Union argues mixed parks should have rules that bind
everyone, not only residents.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of **news reports, not the written reasons**, which were not found on NSW
Caselaw. The ABC report leans to the residents' side (council did not comment) and **blurs the 2025
holiday-van terminations with the separate 2026 age rule**; the 80-case figure is the residents'
group's, and site counts vary by source. Section numbers come from the Act as passed in 2013 — check
the current consolidation, amended in 2024. Council may still appeal.
