Faheem Khalid Lodhi, a 36-year-old Sydney architect with no prior convictions, was convicted by a
jury of three **preparatory terrorism offences** and acquitted of a fourth:

| Count | Offence | Conduct | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Criminal Code* s 101.5 | collecting two maps of the electricity supply system | 15 years |
| 2 | s 101.6 — doing an act in preparation | the Deltrex Chemicals price-list enquiry | **life** |
| 3 | s 101.4 | possessing a 15-page handwritten Urdu manual of poisons, explosives, detonators and incendiary devices | 15 years |

## Held — 20 years, 15-year non-parole period

Whealy J found the intention was to bomb part of the electricity supply system to advance violent
jihad and intimidate the government and the public, but that **no target, bomber or method had been
settled** — the planning was "at a very preliminary stage" — and was not satisfied of an intention to
kill. Culpability was "at a high level, although falling short of the worst category of case" [55].
Good character carried little weight; no allowance for remorse, because innocence was maintained
[74]. A modest allowance was made for segregated "one out" custody, with a recommendation that his
classification be reviewed early [88].

Twenty years on count 2 (22 April 2004 – 21 April 2024), ten years each on counts 1 and 3 wholly
concurrent, and a single non-parole period of **15 years** — three quarters of the aggregate, the
floor fixed by **s 19AG**, inserted by the *Anti-Terrorism Act 2004*. Release on parole is a matter
for the **Attorney-General**, not a court.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.2 (the elements of crime)** — the outer edge of the criminal law: the acts are lawful in
  themselves and the mens rea does the work, because the legislation exists "to intercept and prevent
  a terrorist act at a very early or preparatory stage" [51].
- **crime.4.3** — a complete sentencing judgment in one case: purposes of punishment, objective
  seriousness, subjective factors, totality and concurrency.
- **Discretion and its limits** — the judge weighed everything, then had the non-parole proportion
  fixed for him by statute, and parole handed to a minister.
- **hr.3.1 / rights against community protection**, argued openly. Answering a *Daily Telegraph*
  editorial ("Shed no tears for this terrorist"), the Court said: "we sacrifice our essential decency
  if we fail to treat him or her as a fellow human being" [86], and "the war against terror will be
  over and we will have lost" [87].
- **Effectiveness, doubted by the judge himself** — stern penalties may not deter the fanatical, so
  the protection they give may be "a fragile or uncertain one". On general deterrence: "Such crimes
  are hard to detect; they are likely to be committed by members of our own community and often by
  persons of prior good character and favourable background" [91].
- **Law reform** — the 2002 package after September 11, then the *Anti-Terrorism Act 2004*, applied
  to an offence that did not exist before 2002.

## Caution

A **sentencing** judgment, not a trial: the findings must be consistent with the jury's verdicts,
facts adverse to the offender had to be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and the acquittal on count 4
was left out of account. The sentence was upheld on appeal (*Lodhi v R* [2007] NSWCCA 360), and the
principles are now collected in *R v Alou (No 4)* [2018] NSWSC 221 at [165]–[171]. Twenty years on,
use it for the reasoning rather than as current practice.
