## Held

Chapter III of the Constitution confines the exercise of **federal jurisdiction** under ss 75–76 to
courts. A **state tribunal that is not a court** — NCAT is not — therefore cannot determine a matter
between residents of different states, or a matter arising under a Commonwealth Act. The NSW Court of
Appeal reached the same conclusion weeks later in *Attorney General (NSW) v Gatsby* [2018] NSWCA 254.

## What it did to shelter disputes

- NCAT **dismissed interstate landlords' applications** for arrears and possession.
- **Tenants who had moved interstate lost the forum** for their bond claims.
- Anti-discrimination complaints under Commonwealth Acts — the *Disability Discrimination Act 1992*
  (Cth), for instance — fell outside the Tribunal's reach.

Parliament responded with **Part 3A of the *Civil and Administrative Tribunal Act 2013* (NSW)**,
allowing affected proceedings to move to the Local or District Court without a further fee.

## Use it for

- **The role of a forum defined by constitutional structure rather than by the needs of the
  parties.** Nothing about the dispute changed; only the parties' addresses. It is the clearest
  example in the option of a jurisdictional rule producing an access problem.
- **Cross-topic evidence** — the same case serves Preliminary on Chapter III, the separation of
  powers and the court hierarchy, which makes it efficient to learn.
- **Volume against consequence**: *Burns v Corbett* resolved no shelter dispute at all, and
  redetermined which forum tens of thousands of parties could use.
