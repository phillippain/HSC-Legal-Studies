**Rajendra Sharma** was charged with **wounding his wife with intent to murder** (alternatively intent to cause grievous
bodily harm) and breaching an AVO, after an assault on 23 June 2024. A delusional fixation about her fidelity had
developed from 2021. Crown and defence agreed the **mental health impairment** defence was established, and the
matter was heard by a judge alone.

## Held

- On two psychiatrists' evidence, Noman SC DCJ was satisfied **on the balance of probabilities** that a delusional
  disorder meant Mr Sharma **could not reason about whether his act was wrong** (s 28).
- **Special verdict** (s 31): "**act proven but not criminally responsible**" — covering the AVO breach too.
- **Orders**: detention (ss 33–34) and referral to the **Mental Health Review Tribunal**, which decides release. The
  victim's statement was received.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.8 (defences)** — a complete defence that turns on mens rea and capacity, proved by the accused on the
  balance of probabilities.
- **crime.1.2** — why criminal responsibility needs a guilty mind.
- **Balance** — community protection and the victim's interests through detention and Tribunal oversight rather than
  punishment.

## Caution

A **class case summary**. A special verdict is **not a conviction**; the diagnosis is unsettled. The victim and son are not
named. Family violence context: teach with care.
