A husband and wife who married in 1998 and separated in 2023 after about 25 years; four adult
children; a rural pool of land, water licences, cattle and a crop, partly held through a company as
trustee. A jointly appointed **single expert** valued the trust interest at $3,067,007 and both
sides adopted it — only $117,000 of goodwill was actually in dispute, and their joint balance sheet
put the pool between **$6,503,641 and $7,088,128**. After five sitting days the trial judge found
the pool at **$12,581,558**, nearly double what either party had asked him to find.

## Held — appeal allowed by consent, but only after error was found

Consent alone cannot dispose of an appeal: the Court still had to identify appealable error, and
did. The May 2026 final orders were **set aside**, the matter **remitted for re-hearing before a
different judge of Division 2**, the October 2026 final hearing vacated, and costs certificates
granted to both parties under the *Federal Proceedings (Costs) Act 1981* — public funds meeting the
cost of the court's own error. No decision at all was made on how the property should be divided.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.5** — the court hierarchy doing its job: Division 2 trial, Division 1 appeal, remitter.
  Note the appeal was determined **in chambers on the papers** in about fourteen weeks.
- **fam.2.3** — the four-step property process only works if step one, identifying the pool, is
  right.
- **Effectiveness** — the system caught a $6m error, at the price of five wasted trial days and a
  fresh hearing three years after separation.
- **Procedural fairness** — a judge cannot make an order nobody asked for without hearing the
  parties on it; compliance here is owed by the Court.

## Caution

A short appellate judgment about error, not about how much anyone should receive — the division
question is still open. Summary prepared for class from the judgment; quotes checked against the
judgment text.
