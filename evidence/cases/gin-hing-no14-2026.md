Parenting litigation over one child has run since 2010. A father representing himself argued that a judge’s case-management comments created an apprehension of bias. On 14 September 2026 Johns J held bias was not established and the delay told against him, but called the application finely balanced, expected an appeal, and recused herself on prudential grounds. This is an interlocutory decision, not a parenting decision.

## Use it for

- Courts and dispute resolution: case management, an Independent Children’s Lawyer, counselling orders, appeals and recusal.
- Effectiveness: a child in litigation for most of her life, with delay, repeated applications and a costs order.
- Cooperation: counselling ordered by consent in 2025 but the parties were not meaningfully engaged.
- Compliance: the 2024 family report order was not complied with and the father’s conduct was criticised.
- Separation and children: a parenting dispute about where a child lives and spends time after separation.

## Cautions

This is an interlocutory decision about who should hear the case, not a parenting decision, and the judge expressly did not find apprehended bias; she recused on prudential grounds. The header says the hearing was ‘on the papers’ but para 32 records a hearing on 15 May 2026, and a solicitor is listed for the applicant though the father was self-represented. Order 3 dismisses paragraphs 1 to 5 of an application that lists nine items of relief. Paragraph labels are doubled (the second number is real). Care line: a child in a 16-year high-conflict dispute; use X, and keep to the legal test.
