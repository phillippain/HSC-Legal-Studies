Tasmania proposed to dam the Gordon River below the Franklin. The Commonwealth had the area listed
under the **World Heritage Convention** and passed the *World Heritage Properties Conservation Act
1983* (Cth) to stop the dam. Tasmania argued the Commonwealth had no power over a state's land use.

## Held

Valid, 4–3, under the **external affairs power, s 51(xxix)**. Where Australia has entered a treaty in
good faith, the Commonwealth may legislate to **implement its obligations**, even on a subject that is
otherwise a state matter. Where the resulting Commonwealth law is inconsistent with state law,
**s 109** makes the state law inoperative to the extent of the inconsistency.

## Use it for

- **The division of powers** (`ls.2.6`) — the single best case for showing that the division is not
  fixed by the text alone. Residual powers shrink as the Commonwealth's treaty-making grows, without
  a referendum.
- **How international law becomes Australian law** (`ls.2.14`): a treaty is **not** part of domestic
  law until implemented by statute, but the act of ratifying it can supply the *power* to implement.
  Both halves of that sentence matter.
- **Federalism as a live political question** — a state government with a mandate was overridden by a
  federal government with a treaty. Whichever side is argued, it is a real example of the
  Constitution distributing not just powers but conflicts.
