RP was convicted of sexual offences against his younger brother, committed when RP was between
about **11½ and 13**. He was of **very low intelligence**. The NSW Court of Criminal Appeal dismissed
his appeal.

## Held

Appeal allowed; convictions quashed. To rebut the presumption of *doli incapax* the prosecution
must prove beyond reasonable doubt that the child **knew the conduct was seriously wrong in a moral
sense**, as distinct from naughty or mischievous. It cannot rely on the wrongness of the act itself,
and evidence of the child's development and surroundings matters. The younger the child, the
stronger the evidence needed.

## Use it for

- **Age of criminal responsibility** (`crime.5.1`): the test NSW codified in s 5 of the *Children
  (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987* from 1 March 2026.
- **Law reform**: BOCSAR (CJB 268, 2025) linked RP to a collapse in proven outcomes for 10–13-year-olds,
  which led to the 2025 review and the 2026 codification.

## Caution

RP stated the common law. Proceedings commenced from 1 March 2026 apply the statutory s 5, which
also allows rebuttal on the facts of the offending alone.
