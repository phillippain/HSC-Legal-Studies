Carr was arrested after a verbal exchange with police, charged with offensive language, resisting
arrest and assaulting an officer. The arrest was not needed to secure his attendance at court — his
identity and address were known.

## Held

Smart AJ held that the **power of arrest should not be used where proceedings by summons or court
attendance notice would achieve the same result**. Arrest is reserved for the purposes that make it
necessary: securing attendance, preventing the continuation of an offence, preserving evidence,
protecting safety. The charges that flowed from the arrest itself fell away with it.

## Use it for

- **Arrest and charge** — the clearest statement that the *existence* of a power is not a reason to
  use it. This is the case to pair with s 99 LEPRA, which now codifies the point by requiring arrest
  to be "reasonably necessary" for a listed purpose.
- **Discretion**: a case where the courts, not the legislature, disciplined police choice — and then
  Parliament wrote the discipline into the statute. Useful for a law reform paragraph that shows both
  directions of influence.
- **Charges generated by the arrest** — resisting and assaulting police arise only because the arrest
  happened, so an improper arrest manufactures offences. That is the argument for the last-resort rule.
