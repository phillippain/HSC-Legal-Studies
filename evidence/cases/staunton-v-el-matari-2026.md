In 2017, aged 18, **Isaac El Matari** travelled to Lebanon intending to join Islamic State; after returning he prepared
for terrorism and foreign incursion offences and was convicted in 2021. His sentence expired on **1 November 2026**. A
senior AFP member sought an **interim control order**; he did not oppose it.

## Held — interim order made (29 October 2026)

- He had a **terrorism-related conviction** (s 104.4(1)(c)(iv)).
- Every condition — where he lives, who he mixes with, **electronic monitoring** (ss 104.5A–104.5D) — and their combined
  effect was **reasonably necessary and reasonably appropriate and adapted** to protect the public.
- The scheme is **protective, not penal** (*Thomas v Mowbray*); past conduct is the best guide to risk. Confirmation hearing:
  **17 December 2026**.

- **crime.4.8 (post-sentencing)** — Commonwealth control orders beside state supervision orders (see *Mustapha*, McKenna).
- **Rights** — liberty limited for what a person might do, decided on the balance of probabilities.
