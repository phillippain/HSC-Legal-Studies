## Facts

**Dragan Vasiljkovic**, born in 1954 in what was then Yugoslavia, migrated to Australia in 1969 and became an Australian citizen in 1975. He was arrested in **January 2006** on Croatia's request for his extradition over alleged offences in **1991 and 1993**, including torture and the murder of civilians and prisoners of war (Knin, Glina and near Benkovac). The allegations were never tested in this case.

## Held — 4 to 1 (Kirby J dissenting)

The High Court answered the three questions in the special case **No**:

- **Part II of the *Extradition Act 1988* (Cth)** is not invalid because it allows detention and surrender without judicial testing of the evidence: extradition is an executive act and does not need a judicial finding of a prima facie case.
- The **Extradition (Croatia) Regulations 2004** are supported by the **external affairs power** (s 51(xxix)), so a treaty is not needed.
- Kirby J would have held Part II invalid as applied to a citizen and returned the matter to a single Justice.

## Use it for

- **crime.6.2 (international measures)**: extradition as the main tool for bringing a suspect to another state's courts, and the limit it puts on state sovereignty.
- **Rights against enforcement**: the dissent is a ready counter-argument that citizens are exposed on an untested "no evidence" scheme.
- **Law and politics**: the executive, not a court, decides surrender.

## Caution

The case decides the **validity of the scheme**, not whether the war crimes allegations are true. The file has no hearing dates, CLR citation or footnotes. Check later history before teaching what happened to Vasiljkovic in Croatia: it is not in the judgment.
