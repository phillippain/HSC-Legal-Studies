Two parents of a **13-year-old and a 5-year-old** (pseudonyms), in a long-running case. They separated in September–October 2023; **consent final orders of 11 November 2024** gave shared responsibility and week-about care from March 2025. The father has not spent time with the children since **21 May 2025**. The mother applied under **s 65DAAA** to reconsider the orders; the interim defended hearing was on 10 September 2026.

## The test and the answer

| Question | Answer (Anderson J, 2 October 2026) |
|---|---|
| Is there a **significant change of circumstances**? | **Not** the alleged threats (April and May 2025): the allegations were vague, and the mother had consented to the orders and urged compliance. The change was the **breakdown of cooperation** and no time with the father. |
| Is reconsidering in the children's **best interests**? | Yes — reconsideration allowed |
| What **interim** protections? | Conflict between the parents was "the greatest risk" (at [53]) |

## Interim orders

- Earlier final orders discharged in part; the **mother has sole parental responsibility** and the children live with her.
- **Supervised time** on alternate Saturdays through a contact centre.
- The father is restrained from coming within **100 metres** of the home and school (s 68B injunctions, which are not subject to the paramountcy principle).
- Case management: a **children's lawyer** (s 68L(2)), a family report, agency disclosure (s 67ZBD) and a final hearing on **19 April 2027**. The judge found the parties themselves had prolonged the litigation (33 documents, 24 orders, 33 subpoenas — at [70]).

No findings were made on the mother's allegations of threats.
