Two children, Y (2012) and X (2014), had lived week-about after a long history of cooperative
parenting. From August 2023 the father asserted firmer discipline and boundaries with Y. In March
2024 Y raised concerns about the father's household, including exposure to adult material and
accessible needles. In **May 2024 Y stopped spending time with his father altogether**. X continued
week-about, happily.

## Findings uncomfortable for both parents

The judge accepted the father had **inadvertently** exposed Y to adult material on at least one
occasion, and that needles were probably accessible at times — **but not to the extent claimed**.
She found Y had used those genuine incidents as "a convenient basis to resist the proper discipline
and boundaries" the father was asserting [99].

She also found the mother's willingness to "indulge Y's behaviours and protestations as to feeling
'unsafe'" had **perpetuated the divide**, and that she failed to progress therapy [99], [101].

Ordered: a **six-phase, therapist-supervised** rebuilding of the relationship, with an Independent
Children's Lawyer appointed and **formal undertakings** from both parents to support the children's
relationship with the other.

## Use it for

- **The views of the child**, s 60CC(2)(b) and **CROC art 12**, at their hardest. A child's views
  must be considered, and the weight depends on maturity, understanding and whether the view is
  authentically the child's own (*Re G* (2000) FLC 93-025). Here the court heard a 14-year-old
  clearly and declined to follow him. Pair it with *Billings & Clowes (No 2)*, where the child was
  never heard at all: together they cover both failures of art 12.
- **An ICL represents the child's best interests, not the child's instructions** — the distinction
  students most often get wrong.
- **Undertakings** as an enforcement tool short of an order.
