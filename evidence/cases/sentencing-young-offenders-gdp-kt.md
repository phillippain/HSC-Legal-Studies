- **R v GDP (1991)**: a 14-year-old's custodial sentence for extensive property damage was set aside
  for probation; for young offenders, rehabilitation is generally the primary consideration.
- **R v Pham and Ly (1991)**: deterrence and retribution 'do not cease to be significant' for older
  teenagers who commit serious crimes.
- **R v KT (2008)**: considerable emphasis on rehabilitation, allowing for immaturity; but where a
  young offender has engaged in **'adult behaviour'** (use of weapons, planning or premeditation, an
  extensive record), the need to protect society can reduce the weight given to youth.

## Use it for

- **Penalties for children** (`crime.5.4`) and **why young offenders are treated differently**: the
  courts' sliding scale between rehabilitation and protection.

## Caution

The GDP facts (a 14-year-old; about $550,000 of damage) come from the department's textbook; the
Judicial Commission handbook cites GDP for the principle but does not restate the facts.
