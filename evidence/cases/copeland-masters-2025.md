A father paying child support for two children, with **less than 14% care**, paid **$409.38** of
school fees straight to the children's college in April 2024, having told the mother's lawyers in
2023 that he would pay half the fees and that "these contributions will be disclosed to Services
Australia". Child Support refused to credit it three days after he asked, relying on the parents'
50/50 agreement. (Note the year: this is a **2025** decision, unlike the rest of the pack.)

## Held — decision set aside, the payment credited under s 71C

The Tribunal worked the ladder in order:

- **s 71** cannot apply — the payment went to the school, not to the payee.
- **s 71A** cannot apply — a third-party payment needs **both** parents to intend it as child
  support, and the mother refused.
- **s 71C** does apply — school fees are a **prescribed payment** under **reg 19** of the *Child
  Support (Registration and Collection) Regulations 2018*. Where the payer has under 14% care and
  has paid at least **70%** of the liability, up to **30%** is credited **regardless of intention**.
  He was up to date.
- **s 71D** discretion not used: the Tribunal was not satisfied the mother had shown he agreed to
  pay his half **in addition to** child support.

Set aside and remitted. Not decided: the parents' wider school-fee dispute, still before the Family
Court, and whether his half should be measured against her **discounted** bill — she holds a health
care card and receives a 70% fee discount, so "half the fees" means different things to each parent.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.5 / fam.3.2** — child support is parental responsibility expressed in money, and this is
  the cleanest worked example of it being discharged **in kind** rather than in cash.
- **fam.2.5** — merits review doing real work: an administrative decision remade cheaply, without
  lawyers, by a tribunal rather than a court.
- **Shelter link** — reg 19's prescribed payments include the payee's **rent, bond, utilities, rates
  and home loan repayments**: child support redirected to keeping a roof over the children.
- **Access to justice** — the mother has post-traumatic stress disorder, said the hearing would be
  "too triggering", asked for an advocate and had none available. She stayed a party, was questioned
  **in writing** and given extra time: procedural fairness adapted to the person in front of it.
- **Effectiveness** — free and accessible, but **thirteen months** from objection to objection
  decision, and the parent most in need of help could not take part.

## Caution

A short tribunal decision on a narrow point, and the sum is small — its value is the **rule**, not
the amount. A family violence intervention order is in place and the Family Court matter continues,
so nothing here settles the parents' dispute. Pair it with *Brigham and Hibler* (where no payment
had been made at all) and *Swynnerton* for the scheme as a whole. Summary prepared for class from
the decision.
