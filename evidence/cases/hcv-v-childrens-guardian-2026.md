In 2002, aged 19 and a college student in Queensland, HCV pleaded guilty to one count of **unlawful
and indecent assault** (s 352, *Criminal Code 1899* (Qld)). The complainant was 17. **No conviction
was recorded** and he was fined $500. The sentencing judge put the offence "towards the lower end"
of the range.

That is still a **disqualifying offence** under Schedule 2 of the WWC Act, and a "conviction" for
the Act's purposes includes a **finding of guilt with no conviction recorded** (s 5(1)). So 24 years
later, when he applied for a clearance so he could **help out at his ten-year-old son's football
club**, he was a **disqualified person** and was automatically refused.

He maintains his innocence and says he entered a **"plea of convenience"** to avoid a defended
hearing, on strong advice that he had good prospects of acquittal. The Tribunal did not let that go
unanswered: on *Meissner v R*, a guilty plea is "an admission of all the elements of the offence"
whatever the plea-maker's reasons.

## The test

A disqualified person could apply for an **enabling order** (s 28). It is **presumed** that they
pose a risk to the safety of children, and the **applicant** must prove the contrary (s 28(7)). A
"risk to the safety of children" means a **real and appreciable** risk — not fanciful or theoretical,
but a risk "greater than the risk of any adult preying on a child" (*Commission for Children and
Young People v V*). Risk assessment is **protective, not punitive** (*FZ*). Section 30(1) sets out
eleven mandatory factors; s 30(1A) adds the **reasonable person test** and the **public interest
test**.

## Held — presumption rebutted, clearance ordered

The Tribunal accepted the offence was objectively serious: an indecent assault on a young woman who
was intoxicated and asleep. But: the circumstances were at the lower range and involved two college
students close in age; he desisted immediately and apologised; it was an isolated incident; **24
years** had passed with no relevant criminal history; and his circumstances were stable, supported by
one unchallenged reference from the football club president who knew about the offence. Risk of
repetition **very low**. Both s 30(1A) tests satisfied, including a public interest in parents who
are not a risk volunteering in children's sport.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the preventive, screening side of child protection, and the
  cleanest available example of **automatic disqualification** meeting an **individual review**.
- **Read it beside *GTA*.** Same scheme, opposite starting points: HCV **pleaded guilty** and carried
  a presumption he had to rebut; GTA was **acquitted** and carried no presumption at all. A perfect
  pair for showing how the burden of proof shapes an outcome.
- **crime.3 / crime.4 — what a plea costs later.** A plea entered in 2002 to save time and money
  produced, 24 years on, a statutory bar on coaching his son's team. Outstanding evidence that
  **charge negotiation has consequences the accused is not told about**, and that "no conviction
  recorded" does not mean no record.
- **"Protective, not punitive" (fam.t.values)** — the phrase to use whenever a question asks whether
  a measure punishes or protects. The Tribunal is not sentencing him again; it is assessing present
  risk, which is why time and conduct since matter so much.
- **The reasonable person and the public interest** — two tests that must *both* be satisfied even
  after risk is disproved. Useful for arguing that the Act errs on the side of caution.
- **inv.1.4 / responsiveness** — applied 17 June 2025, heard 31 March 2026, decided 5 August 2026.
  Over a year for a volunteer clearance, and the pathway no longer exists (see the WWC Act record).

## Caution

**Pseudonym**, with a s 64(1)(a) non-publication order over every name in the proceedings. A short
**first-instance tribunal** decision (71 paragraphs) on its own facts — it sets no principle and
does not mean other disqualified people will succeed. The Tribunal's reasoning records that he gave
limited concessions and **has not undertaken any counselling**, so this is not a rehabilitation
story. The underlying facts are a sexual assault on a sleeping, intoxicated 17-year-old; the
judgment includes the police brief in detail, and it should be summarised, not read out. Decided on
the **pre-23 September 2025** law under a transitional provision: s 28 enabling orders have since
been abolished.
