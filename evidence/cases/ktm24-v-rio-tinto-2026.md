**KTM24** (a court-ordered pseudonym), funded by Omni Bridgeway, complained to the **AHRC** in February
2024 for herself and women who worked at Rio Tinto's Australian workplaces from 2003 to 2024, citing the
Broderick, WA parliamentary and *Respect@Work* reports. Conciliation failed, the complaint was
terminated in December 2024, and she filed a **class action** (Federal Court Pt IVA) the next day; a twin
case was filed against BHP. Rio Tinto's own 2021 review had found 28% of women reported sexual
harassment. The companies applied to strike out the claims before trial.

## Held — applicants "largely successful"

- **Rejected**: group members need not be aggrieved by the *same* acts (s 46P); dozens of sites over 20
  years still share "a common core" of women, workplaces and centralised policies, so a class action
  is apt; branded shirts plus site control gave fair notice of agency; new bullying particulars were
  within the complaint.
- **Accepted**: **victimisation** (s 47A) and a group "sexual assault" claim were struck out because
  they were never in the terminated AHRC complaint — the court can hear only what the Commission did.
  Parts of the group definition and some negligence particulars were struck, with leave to replead.
- Rio Tinto and BHP pay the applicants' costs.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.9 / hr.2.11** — the *Sex Discrimination Act* enforced through the AHRC gateway and then the
  Federal Court, and the class action as a way to carry many women's claims at once.
- **hr.3.1** — a contemporary issue where corporate culture reviews, the *Respect@Work* positive duty
  (s 47C, 2022) and litigation all meet.

## Caution

A **strike-out** judgment on pleadings and jurisdiction: **no allegation against Rio Tinto or BHP has been
proved or disproved**. The survey figures are from company-commissioned reviews, not the court. A class
evidence summary.
