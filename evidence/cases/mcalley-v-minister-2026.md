A woman who fled Cambodia and worked for the United Nations has held an Australian passport for
decades. A 1994 Cambodian birth certificate records her date of birth as **30 September 1964**, and
the same date appears on her passport, marriage certificate, migration and citizenship documents and
driver licence. In February 2025 the Cambodian Ministry of Interior issued a **new** birth
certificate recording **1962**, and a Phnom Penh court order purporting to amend the year followed
six weeks later.

A delegate refused to give her a notice under **s 37(2)** of the *Australian Citizenship Act 2007*
(Cth) confirming she is a citizen. Section 37(4) says the Minister **must not** give the notice
unless satisfied of the person's identity.

## Held — decision affirmed

Satisfaction is "a state of mind — an actual persuasion of the occurrence or existence of the thing
in issue" (*Makasa*), reasonably based on the totality of the facts. It is **binary**: satisfied, or
not.

Departmental policy (CPI16 and the National Identity Proofing Guidelines) assesses identity through
**documents, life story and biometrics**. Here the biometric pillar was neutral — no verifiable
biometric data pre-dated her arrival — so everything turned on the first two. Policy is applied
unless there is a cogent reason not to (*Re Drake (No 2)*).

## Use it for

- **Resolving disputes with the state** (Preliminary ind.2): internal decision, merits review, and a
  tribunal that reconsiders *de novo* and makes the correct or preferable decision.
- **Policy as quasi-law.** CPI16 is not legislation, and it decided the case. Pair it with Ministerial
  Direction 110 in *Samuelu* for the two ways executive instruments shape rights without passing
  through Parliament.
- **Is this justice?** She is, on the evidence, who she says she is; the statute asks whether the
  decision-maker is *satisfied*, and the answer was no. A clean example of a lawful decision that is
  hard to call a just outcome — which is exactly what the syllabus asks students to distinguish.
