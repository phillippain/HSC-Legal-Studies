X, born 2016, was conceived through **egg donation** by Ms E, the partner of a friend of the
father's. At fertility counselling the parents and Ms E agreed to be "open" with X about her donor
conception — but never defined what openness would mean in practice.

After separation in 2023 the father built a close relationship between X and Ms E's two children, at
times describing them to X and others as her "sisters". The mother opposed this, fearing it would
confuse X and undermine her own role, and wanted X's understanding of her conception story deferred
until a psychologist assessed her as ready.

**Neither parent argued the other posed a risk of harm.**

## Held — final orders, largely by consent

Joint decision-making retained, without the final say the mother sought. The existing **five nights
a fortnight** with the father continues rather than progressing to equal shared care. The mother's
injunctions limiting contact and language about Ms E's family were **mostly refused** in favour of a
permissive, child-led arrangement with psychological support. The father was **not** ordered to
complete a parenting course.

## Use it for

- **The best interests test does not resolve itself.** Section 60CA makes best interests paramount
  and s 60CC supplies the checklist, but here it required a judge to choose between two good parents
  who disagreed in good faith. That is the honest answer to "is the law effective at protecting
  children" — it is effective at deciding, which is not the same thing.
- **Donor conception after the fact**: an agreement made at fertility counselling, never defined,
  became the dispute nine years later. A concrete law reform point — written agreements and
  independent advice, the same recommendation *Wilkerson & Croxford* makes.
- **Equal time is not a starting point** any more. The father sought 7/7 and got 5/9, under the
  post-2024 framework with no presumption to lean on.
