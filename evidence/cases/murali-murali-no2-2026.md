Together from December 2012; their only child born in 2020. In November 2022, during an argument,
the father hit himself on the head with a kettlebell in front of the family; separation followed
within weeks. An argument before the child's third birthday party in early 2023 ended with the
mother leaving, reporting to police and obtaining an interim intervention order; the child has not
seen his father since. Mediation in May 2023 produced an agreement she says he never followed; she
relocated interstate; he consented without admissions to a final intervention order; in August
2024 he **pleaded guilty to persistently breaching it by text**, was fined, and attended a Men's
Behaviour Change Program — in which, a mandatory reporter said, he remarked that he knew where she
was and could abduct the child (denied; she relocated again; police took no action). The Court asked
the state child protection department to intervene (**s 91B**); after **twenty reports over three
years** it declined by letter.

## Held — 9 September 2026

- **Proof and prediction are different questions**: past events must be proved on the civil standard
  with "actual persuasion" (*Evidence Act* s 140; *Warner v Hung (No 2)*); future risk "may be merely
  a possibility" and need not be proved on the balance of probabilities (*Isles & Nelissen*).
- **The admissions established family violence** — the self-harm incident, verbal abuse, sexual
  coercion — "without more". But "it is reductive to simply conclude that, because the father
  perpetrated certain acts of family violence, his denials in respect of other allegations should be
  rejected… [that] would amount to little more than lazy judging" (at [66]). The wider allegations
  were **not** accepted.
- **The risk method** (*Chiles & Petrenko*): identify the feared event, gauge likelihood and
  magnitude, ask whether a condition makes it acceptable. "Any form of unsupervised time would
  expose the child to an unacceptable risk of harm"; "the presence of a supervisor reduces the risk
  from one that is unacceptable to one that is acceptable."
- **Built around the mother's capacity**: sole carer of a six-year-old with autism; orders she could
  not sustain would harm him. The ICL's graduated plan was rejected; the family report writer's
  recommendation adopted — enough to keep "a thread of connection".
- **Orders**: the child lives with the mother, who has sole decision-making (by agreement); the Court
  declined even to require that she notify the father of major decisions — "the question becomes
  whether such an order would confer any benefit on the child as distinct from a benefit to the
  father" (at [92]); supervised time at a contact centre at the father's expense: 30 minutes in
  November 2026, 45 in January 2027, then **one hour each March, June, September and December** —
  four hours a year; no approach or communication at changeover; the ICL discharged.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.4** — family violence established by **admission** rather than contest, and a careful
  refusal to let the admissions decide everything else. "Lazy judging" lifts an answer above "the
  law believes victims" or "the law doubts victims".
- **fam.1.5 / crime.3.6** — the benefit of a relationship weighed against safety and resolved at
  four hours a year; the civil standard on facts against a lower threshold on risk — a precise
  point about standards of proof.
- **fam.3.2** — parental responsibility reduced to nothing on one side, with notification refused.
- **fam.3.4** — twenty reports, a casework response, and a decision not to intervene: the state
  looking and stepping back.
- **Legal and non-legal, all tried** — mediation, a behaviour change program, intervention orders,
  a criminal penalty: none produced safe contact (HSC 2015, 2023).
- **fam.t.values** — disability treated as central: the child's autism and the mother's capacity
  shaped the orders.

## Caution

A **class-made summary**. Most of the mother's allegations beyond the admissions were **not
accepted** — do not present them as findings. The abduction remark rests on a mandatory reporter's
account the father denies and the Court's treatment of it is not reproduced here. The orders are
what each parent can comply with, not a statement of what the father deserves. Pseudonyms apply.
The citation was taken from the summary and not independently re-checked here.
