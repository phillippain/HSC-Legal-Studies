A mother and father of **two very young children** (pseudonyms) separated finally on 1 November 2024. By **consent orders of 18 December 2025** the father's time was supervised and he was to undergo urinalysis and hair-follicle testing. On 17 April 2026 Judge McGinn discharged supervision and restrained cutting of the father's hair; a **positive June 2026 test** was disclosed and supervision was reinstated by agreement. **Professor Christie** was appointed single toxicology expert by consent on 31 July 2026. The interim hearing was on 24 August 2026; judgment on **23 September 2026**.

## The three questions and answers

| Issue | Curran J |
|---|---|
| Leave for the father's **own toxicologist** (Family Law Rules rr 7.08, 7.11)? | **Refused** — no affidavit, no contrary opinion, no special reason, issue not complex; a single expert is the rule |
| Weight of the single expert's **untested** interim opinion? | Untested "but it cannot be ignored" (at [102]); the father's claim that he misunderstood the 3 cm hair restraint was rejected |
| Can supervision ameliorate the risk? | Yes — an **unacceptable risk** to very young children if the father is impaired; supervision ameliorates it |

"The children are very young and accordingly are vulnerable. They are too young to act protectively if the father was impaired" (at [76]). Potential risk was balanced against "the children's need to maintain their close and important relationship with their father" (at [129]).

## Orders (interim)

- Supervised time at a contact centre, the father paying.
- **Hair-follicle tests** by 25 September 2026 and every three months, with no hair cut below **3 cm**; results to the mother within 24 hours.
- The mother may request up to **six urine tests a month**.
- **Supervision ends** after **two consecutive negative scalp tests three months apart**.
- Changeovers through a nominee, to reduce the mother's anxiety.
- Next listing: **4 December 2026**.
