The Khoshabas, a couple with limited English and limited financial experience, mortgaged their home
to fund an investment. The lender advanced on the **value of the security alone**, without
considering whether the borrowers could service the loan — "asset lending".

## Held

The NSW Court of Appeal reopened the transaction under the **Contracts Review Act 1980 (NSW)**,
which allows a court to grant relief where a contract is **unjust** in the circumstances at the time
it was made. Lending against the home of unsophisticated borrowers with no attention to their capacity
to repay, and no insistence on independent advice, can make the contract unjust even where there is
no misrepresentation.

## Use it for

- **Purchasing** (`shel.2.1`) — the shelter option is usually written as a tenancy topic, and the
  statutory protection of *purchasers and mortgagors* is the part most answers skip. The Contracts
  Review Act is the provision to name.
- **The limits of "freedom of contract"**, and the reason shelter is treated differently from other
  markets: losing this contract means losing the home.
- Pair it with ***Commercial Bank of Australia v Amadio*** for the difference between the general
  equitable doctrine of unconscionable dealing and a **statutory** power to reopen an unjust contract
  — the statute is wider and does not require proof of a special disadvantage exploited.
