NZYQ is a **stateless Rohingya man** born in Myanmar who arrived by boat in 2012. After serving a prison
sentence for a sexual offence against a child, he was taken back into immigration detention in 2018. In
2020 he was found to be a **refugee**, but was refused a visa. No country would take him.

## Held

- **Orders 8 November 2023; unanimous reasons 28 November 2023.**
- On the words of the Act, ss 189(1) and 196(1) did require his detention — that part of *Al-Kateb* stood.
- But the sections were **invalid as they applied to him**: under Chapter III of the Constitution only a
  court can punish. Detention by the executive is valid only for a legitimate, non-punitive purpose.
- **The test:** detention is unlawful where there is **no real prospect of removal becoming practicable in
  the reasonably foreseeable future**.
- The constitutional holding in *Al-Kateb v Godwin* (2004) was **overruled**.

## What happened next

About **350** people were released. Parliament passed new laws within weeks (visa conditions, new
offences, and later powers to pay third countries to receive people). See the related records.

## Caution

NZYQ did **not** end mandatory detention or set a time limit. Detention for processing or removal is still
lawful. The decision rests on the Constitution, not the Refugee Convention or the ICCPR.
