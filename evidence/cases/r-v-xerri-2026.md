After a separation, the respondent's emails were read aloud from a family device (20 November 2023); he took the
device and grabbed an arm. He was convicted in the Local Court of **common assault** and contravening an **ADVO**. On
appeal the District Court held the emails were **property**, so he could rely on **self-defence of property**
(s 418(2)(c)), and acquitted him. The DPP had four questions stated to the CCA.

## Held

- On the **limited facts** found, the emails were **information, not property**: property is definable, identifiable by
  others and has some permanence (*Ainsworth*); mere information is not (contrast Bitcoin, *Yeates* (Vic 2025), and online
  game tokens, *Lakeman* (UK 2026)).
- Reading them aloud was not **interference** with property.
- The acquittal was **quashed** and the appeal **remitted** to the District Court; the other two questions were not answered.
  The Court stressed the ruling is confined to the facts stated.
