Quy Huy Hoang, a mathematics tutor, was tried in the District Court on twelve counts of sexual
offences against five children. Evidence had been given that he held no Working with Children Check.
After the jury agreed on eight counts and went home for the night, one juror — a former teacher —
**Googled the requirements for the Check** and found the legislation dated from 2013. The foreperson
reported it next day. The judge found misconduct but **took ten verdicts first** (eight guilty, two not
guilty) and only then discharged the juror; the remaining jurors convicted on two more counts.

## Held — unanimous

- **Jury Act 1977 (NSW) s 68C** makes it an offence for a juror to make an inquiry to obtain
  information about any matter relevant to the trial — including internet research. What matters is
  the **purpose** of the inquiry (information about a matter in evidence), not the juror's **motive**:
  curiosity is no answer. The Court of Criminal Appeal majority had been wrong to hold otherwise.
- **s 53A(1)(c)** imposes a **mandatory duty** to discharge a juror once the court is satisfied of
  misconduct. "The trial judge was required to immediately discharge the juror before taking the ten
  verdicts."
- Verdicts taken while she sat were verdicts of a **jury not properly constituted**; the proviso
  cannot save them. Convictions on counts 4 and 6–12 set aside, new trial; the two convictions
  reached after her discharge stand.
