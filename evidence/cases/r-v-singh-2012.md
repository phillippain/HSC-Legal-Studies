Chamanjot Singh slashed his wife's throat with a box cutter after she threatened to leave him. He raised
**provocation**, which reduced murder to manslaughter, and received a **six-year** term.

## What happened next

The sentence produced community and media outrage — "Six years for killing sparks call for law
review", *SMH* (2012). The victim's family said justice had not been served. The defence, as it then
stood, allowed conduct that fell well short of a criminal act by the deceased to reduce murder to
manslaughter, and it was being used in precisely the situation it least fitted: a man killing a
partner who was leaving him.

Parliament responded with the **Crimes Amendment (Provocation) Act 2014 (NSW)**, which confined the
defence to **extreme provocation**.

## Use it for

- **Partial defences** — the clearest illustration that a partial defence is not an acquittal but a
  re-labelling, and that the label carries the sentence with it.
- **Law reform driven by public and media pressure**, with all four stages visible: a decision, an
  outrage, a review, an amendment. Most reform paragraphs assert this cycle; this one shows it.
- **The moral and ethical standards of society** as a theme — the objection was not that the law was
  misapplied, but that the law as applied did not match what the community thought murder was.
