Joshua Higgins was convicted in the Local Court on three counts of assaulting his wife, and appealed
to the District Court in April 2013.

## Held

The appeal judge noted what was **missing**: the victim had said in her statement that she
photographed her injuries, but the photographs were never produced; her mother, who arrived shortly
after one of the alleged assaults, was never called. On each count the judge entertained a reasonable
doubt, "because of the very high standard required of the criminal law before someone can be found
guilty". The convictions were quashed and Higgins acquitted on all three charges.

## Use it for

- **Standard of proof** shown working rather than stated. The magistrate was satisfied; the District
  Court was not; the standard is what decided between them.
- **The victim's position in a domestic violence prosecution** — the acquittal turned on evidence the
  prosecution did not lead, not on a finding that nothing happened. Strong for the argument that
  "beyond reasonable doubt" protects the accused at a measurable cost to complainants, which is the
  honest version of the balance question.
- **Appeals** (crime.4.5) — a District Court appeal from the Local Court is a rehearing, which is why
  the evidentiary gaps could be revisited at all.
