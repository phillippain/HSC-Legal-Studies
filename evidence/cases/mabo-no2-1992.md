The High Court held that the common law of Australia recognises **native title** where a connection
to land under traditional laws and customs has been maintained and the title has not been
extinguished by an inconsistent grant. The doctrine of **terra nullius**, on which the assumption of
sovereignty had rested, was rejected as a basis for denying pre-existing rights.

## Use it for

- The **mandatory** law reform in action study (chg.5): terra nullius as the obstacle, the **High
  Court** as a mechanism of reform, and Parliament's response the following year.
- **Courts as a mechanism of law reform**: this is the clearest Australian example of the judiciary
  changing the law where Parliament had not. It is therefore also the best example for the tension
  between judicial law-making and the **separation of powers**.
- **Customary law**: it recognised Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander law as a **source** of
  rights the common law will enforce, without incorporating it wholesale.

## Caution

Mabo recognised native title; it did not grant land rights generally, and it left the extinguishment
question to later cases and statute. Say what it did, not what it is often assumed to have done.
