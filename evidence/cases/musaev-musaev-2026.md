A wife began property proceedings, but her husband died in 2025 before they finished. They continued against his executors. His will purported to forgive a $2,412,344 loan owed to him by a family trust. On 21 September 2026, deciding a preliminary question only, Jarrett J held the forgiveness works as a legacy, the debt belongs to the estate, and her pending claim has priority.

## Use it for

- Divorce and separation: property proceedings begun after separation, and what happens to a claim when a party dies.
- Courts: Division 1 ordered one question decided first, since it could decide whether the case was worth continuing.
- Law reform and just outcomes: s 79AA(8) keeps a spouse’s claim alive so a will cannot defeat it.
- Effectiveness: the statute protects the claim, but the property split is still undecided in the file.

## Cautions

This is a first-instance ruling on a separate preliminary question (r 10.11(1)(d)), not a final property settlement: no division of property is stated in the file, and the parties agreed the estate had no other assets of sufficient value. The wife is not a creditor and holds no interest in the debt; her priority comes from s 79AA(8) itself. The file has some slips: the catchwords cite ss 46 and 51 of the Succession Act but the reasons cite ss 45, 52 and 56; Love is dated 1989 and 1993; the order declares once but [38] says ‘declarations’; the month of death is not given. No costs order appears. Care line: a death in the family; keep to the legal test.
