In ***Al-Kateb v Godwin*** (2004) the High Court held 4:3 that indefinite immigration detention was
valid. In ***NZYQ*** (28 November 2023) it overruled that constitutional holding: detention with **no real
prospect of removal** is punitive and breaches **Chapter III**; about **350** people were released. Mr
Abdel-Hady, detained until 8 November 2023, sued for **false imprisonment**; the Commonwealth admitted his
detention was unlawful from 28 July 2022 (when he became medically unfit to fly) but argued its officers
had a defence because they were following *Al-Kateb* and a statutory duty to detain.

## Held — 7:0

- **No defence.** Sections 189(1) and 196(1) never authorised detention of that kind, so officers who
  relied on *Al-Kateb* had no lawful authority. The Commonwealth is vicariously liable.
- The Court answered **one question of law** in a special case; **damages have not been decided**.
  Past false imprisonment awards range from nominal to hundreds of thousands of dollars; the
  government is "considering" the judgment and an advocate expects a compensation scheme.

## Before and after

| | *Al-Kateb* (2004) | *NZYQ* (2023) + *Abdel-Hady* (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Constitution | Indefinite detention valid | Breaches Ch III where no real prospect of removal |
| Officers | Treated as lawful | No defence for following the old law |
| Detainee's remedy | None | False imprisonment claims open |

## Use it for

- **hr.2.11 / hr.2.8** — the separation of powers protecting liberty, and a common law remedy (the tort of
  false imprisonment) enforcing a constitutional limit.
- **hr.3.1** — pair with the Ombudsman's wrongful-detention findings and the political response to the
  NZYQ cohort (curfews, monitoring, preventive detention).

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of an academic explainer**, checked against the High Court's judgment
summary. How many people were unlawfully held is unclear and no official costing exists. Not every
released person will have a claim.
