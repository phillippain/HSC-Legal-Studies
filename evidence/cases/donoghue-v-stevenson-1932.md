May Donoghue drank ginger beer bought for her by a friend; the opaque bottle contained the remains of
a decomposed snail. She had **no contract** with anyone — her friend had bought the drink — so on the
law as it stood she had no claim.

## Held

The manufacturer owed her a **duty of care**. Lord Atkin's **neighbour principle** — you must take
reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions you can reasonably foresee would injure persons so closely
and directly affected by your act — created the modern law of negligence. Australia adopted it in
*Grant v Australian Knitting Mills* [1936] AC 85 (underwear, sulphites, dermatitis), decided by the
Privy Council on appeal from the High Court.

## Use it for

- **Common law and precedent** (`ls.2.1`) — judges made a general duty out of a case about a bottle,
  which is exactly what "the common law develops case by case" means. *Stare decisis* binds; it does
  not freeze.
- **British origins** and the mechanics of reception: an English decision became Australian law
  through the appellate structure of the time, and the Australia Acts 1986 ended that. Good for
  showing that the *source* of a rule and the *authority* of a court are different questions.
- **Private law** (`ls.3.2`) — the standard illustration of tort as a civil wrong owed to the world at
  large rather than to a party you contracted with.
