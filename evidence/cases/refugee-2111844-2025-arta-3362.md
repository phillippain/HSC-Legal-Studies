A Chinese national arrived on a student visa in February 2008, aged about 17. His family practised
Catholicism through unregistered "house church" meetings, which are illegal in China. He claimed
authorities raided his parents' home in May 2018, detained them for two weeks, and demanded he
return for interrogation about importing religious materials.

| | |
|---|---|
| Mar 2011 | Student visa lapses; he lives unlawfully in Australia |
| Jul 2018 | Applies for a protection visa — **ten years** after arriving, seven after the visa lapsed |
| Aug 2021 | A delegate refuses |
| Oct 2024 | The AAT is abolished and becomes the ART |
| Oct 2025 | The ART **affirms** the refusal after two hearings |

## Why the claim failed

1. **Not a genuine practising Catholic** — he had attended church fewer than ten times in 17 years in
   Australia, where Catholic worship is completely safe; he had never read the Bible, could not name
   his church at the first hearing, and prayed only when in "a very bad state".
2. **No Chinese authority interest** — no evidence that Chinese authorities had tracked or contacted
   him in 17 years.

The framework: *Migration Act 1958* (Cth) ss 5H, 5J and 36 implement the Refugees Convention.
s 36(2)(a) requires a **well-founded fear of persecution** on a Convention ground; s 36(2)(aa)
provides complementary protection where there is a real risk of torture, the death penalty, or cruel
or degrading treatment.

## Use it for

- **How a human right is actually tested.** Freedom of religion is in the ICCPR and the UDHR. In an
  Australian tribunal it became a question of fact about a particular person's practice, decided on
  credibility. That is the gap between having a right and proving one.
- **Delay cuts both ways**: a ten-year gap before applying weakened his claim, and the case still
  took seven more years to resolve.
- The **AAT becoming the ART** in October 2024 is a live law reform students can date precisely.
