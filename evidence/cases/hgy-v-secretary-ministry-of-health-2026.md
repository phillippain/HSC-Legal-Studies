An existing employee of a State-operated health service applied for a further position within the
organisation and was **required to disclose his former names during the recruitment process** —
exposing him to the risk of disclosing his transgender status, because those names were gendered.

Unlawful **transgender discrimination in employment** under ss 38A, 38B, 38C and 53 of the
*Anti-Discrimination Act 1977* (NSW). Four hearing days in May 2026.

## Held — complaint upheld

**$35,000**, payable within 60 days. The Tribunal also considered **aggravated damages** (by
reference to the respondent's conduct of the proceeding), an **injunction** restraining
discriminatory conduct, and an **apology**.

Authorities include *Australian Iron and Steel v Banovic* (1989-90) 168 CLR 165, *Commonwealth Bank v
HREOC* (1997), *Elliott v Nanda* (2001), *Sklavos v Australasian College of Dermatologists* [2017]
FCAFC 128, and *Giggle for Girls Pty Ltd v Tickle* [2026] FCAFC 64.

## Why this is the most important discrimination case in the collection

Because it is the one that **worked**. On 11 August 2026 the same Division refused leave in three
complaints — *Fowler* (victimisation), *Haji Zadeh* (race) and *Jones* (disability) — none of which
reached a hearing. Five weeks later, after four hearing days, this one produced a finding and an
award.

Use the four together and the argument writes itself: the *Anti-Discrimination Act* **can** deliver a
remedy, and what separated this complaint from the others was evidence of a **specific requirement**
(disclose your former names) that a comparator would not have faced — the thing the other three could
not establish. That is not a criticism of the complainants; it is a description of what the Act asks
of them.

## Use it for

- **Statute law protecting rights**, with a measurable outcome rather than an aspiration.
- **Indirect discrimination**: a neutral requirement applied to everyone, with an unequal effect on
  one group — the clearest worked example in the collection.
- **A Charter of Rights argument, both ways**: this right was protected without one, and it took four
  hearing days and a tribunal to do it.
