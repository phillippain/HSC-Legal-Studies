## Facts

Police stopped **Bunning** for speeding and drove him to a station for a breath test (reading **0.190**) without the statutory grounds. The magistrate excluded the result as unfair. The Western Australian Full Court reversed that.

## Held — 4 to 1 (Murphy J dissenting on disposition)

The High Court (Barwick CJ, Stephen, Jacobs, Aickin JJ; Murphy J dissenting) held unlawfully obtained evidence is **admissible subject to a discretion** that weighs the public interest in convicting offenders against the public interest in not condoning unlawful police conduct, not merely fairness to the accused. The breach was a **mistake**, not a deliberate disregard, so the evidence was admitted and a conviction directed.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.3**: the common-law rule on improperly obtained evidence, and why the discretion looks at police conduct.
- **Discretion**: the court's power, and its limits.
- Contrast with **s 138** (*Kadir; Grech*): the statute now governs in NSW.

## Caution

The evidence was **admitted, not excluded**. The discretion is applied fully only by Stephen and Aickin JJ. It pre-dates the *Evidence Act 1995*. No hearing date in the file.
