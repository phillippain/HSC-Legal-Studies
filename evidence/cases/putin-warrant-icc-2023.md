On **17 March 2023** the ICC issued warrants for **Vladimir Putin** and **Maria Lvova-Belova** for the
war crime of **unlawfully deporting and transferring children** from occupied Ukraine to Russia (Rome
Statute art 8(2)(a)(vii)). Ukraine had accepted the Court's jurisdiction; Russia is not a party.

## Why no arrest

- Russia will not surrender its President, and the ICC **cannot try anyone in their absence** (art 63).
- Putin visited **Mongolia (September 2024)** and **Tajikistan (October 2025)**, both states parties
  obliged to arrest (arts 86–89). Neither did. In **May 2026** the Court referred Tajikistan's
  non-compliance to the Assembly of States Parties, which has no power to compel.
- In **August 2025** he met President Trump in Alaska; the US is not a party, so had no duty.
- Russia convicted ICC officials **in absentia** in December 2025.

## Use it for

- **Sovereignty and enforcement**: the clearest example that the ICC depends on states.
- **Selectivity**: compare Duterte, surrendered by his own government.
- **Children's rights in armed conflict** (CROC; Geneva Convention IV) for Human Rights.
