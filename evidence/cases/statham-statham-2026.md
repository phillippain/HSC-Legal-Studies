In 2015–16 the husband bought company shares, secured by a loan, that carried the **right to occupy an apartment** where
the wife and children lived; from 2021 the wife paid the loan. In early 2023 the husband became **bankrupt** and the
shares vested in his **trustee**; the bank sued for possession. The wife sought property orders, and days before the
hearing the husband's agent registered a **charge** on the shares.

## Held — Judge Colquhoun

- It was **just and equitable** to alter property interests even though the main asset had vested in the trustee.
- Using a **two-pool** approach, the wife's contributions to the shares were assessed at **100%**: the shares were
  **transferred to her** and she takes over the loan.
- The court could not order the non-party agent to remove the charge, so it ordered the **husband** to remove it. His
  adjournment request was refused.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.3** — property orders under s 79 and the Family Law Act's power to bind a bankruptcy trustee (s 90AE).
- **Contributions** — financial contributions (loan payments) and housing for the children.
