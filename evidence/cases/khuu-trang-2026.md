Living together 2012, married 2016, separated March 2024. The wife commenced proceedings for a
property settlement under **s 79**. Earlier orders restrained both parties from disposing of property
or transferring funds. **Despite that, the husband sold a luxury motor vehicle worth $500,000** and
said it belonged to his company and the proceeds had gone overseas to repay a business debt.

## Held

Campton J found the husband was the **legal and beneficial owner** of the vehicle and had failed to
give proper financial disclosure. He was ordered to pay the $500,000 into the wife's solicitor's
trust account pending the property proceedings.

Legislation: *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) ss 71B, 79; FCFCOA (Family Law) Rules 2021; *Duties Act
1997* (NSW).

## Use it for

- **Economic abuse as family violence.** No physical violence was alleged. Disposing of assets
  against a restraint, moving money offshore and withholding disclosure are post-separation
  financial control — which the reformed Act now requires a court to consider when dividing
  property.
- **Why the reform was made.** The conduct here is exactly what the amendments to the *Family Law
  Act* on family violence, coercive control and economic abuse in property settlements were aimed
  at. Use the case as the *reason for* a reform rather than an application of it.
- **Injunctions and enforcement** — the restraint was breached and the court's answer was to claw
  the money back into a trust account rather than to punish. Pair with *Merran & Salter*, where
  contraventions were proven and no penalty followed.
