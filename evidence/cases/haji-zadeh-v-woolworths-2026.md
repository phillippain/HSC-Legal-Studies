On 20 August 2025 Mr Haji Zadeh asked a Woolworths employee at Ermington for help finding an item.
He alleges that **because of his appearance or his accent** the employee refused to serve him and,
"in extreme violence way", told him to "go fuck yourself". He asked for a manager, was told there was
none in the store, left his details, and was never contacted.

ADNSW identified it as **race discrimination in the provision of goods and services** — ss 7, 19 and
53 of the *Anti-Discrimination Act 1977* (NSW). On 24 December 2025 the President's delegate declined
it as lacking in substance, on six grounds:

1. He did not establish that Woolworths **refused him a service** because of his race.
2. **The parties agree a staff member used offensive language towards him** — but beyond the
   assertion, he did not show this was a refusal of service, or that it happened because of his race.
3. He did not show he was served on **less favourable terms** than a person of a different race in
   circumstances not materially different.
4. No requirement he could not comply with because of his race.
5. **"Manner of service is not covered under the Act."**
6. The respondent gave reasons for its actions that do not equate to race discrimination.

**Leave refused** under s 96(1). Both parties were self-represented.

## Use it for

- **The comparator problem.** Direct discrimination requires showing that someone of a different race
  would have been treated better in circumstances not materially different. A shopper alone in an
  aisle has no comparator, and rarely any witness. That is the single biggest practical limit on the
  Act, and this case states it in six lines.
- **The gap the Act leaves**: rudeness, however extreme, is not discrimination unless a service was
  refused or provided on worse terms *because of* a protected attribute.
- Pair with the **27% of renters and 32% of Aboriginal renters who report discrimination** against
  the AHRC's complaint numbers: this is what happens to a complaint that is made.
- The pathway from complaint to leave is set out in ***Fowler v Forestville RSL***.
