A daughter born 14 November 2006, in a child support case opened in April 2021 with care recorded at
**0% / 100%** (father / mother). On **7 March 2024** she was discharged from a residential medical
clinic to her father's home; he notified a change of care the next day — four nights a week, then
five, then 72%, then "closer to 50/50", then 33%. A delegate found no change; his objection was
disallowed; on 30 September he notified full-time care; the case ended when she turned **18** on
14 November 2024. A second delegate again found no change, a second objection failed, and on first
review the Tribunal affirmed both. On second review the mother took no part but filed written
submissions.

## Held — 16 October 2026

- **No onus**: "My task is to make the correct or preferable decision on all of the evidence now
  before me"; "There is no formal onus on either parent to prove a case in the manner of adversarial
  litigation." Findings rest on the best evidence available.
- **Both reconstructions rejected**: no diaries, shifting accounts, the child's three statements in
  conflict with the father's own March reports; a video relied on by the father was undated,
  unattributed and tied to no particular night.
- **A bed is not the test**: a 17-year-old staying with friends or a boyfriend is not automatically in
  either parent's care — nights away "do not make every night away from a parent automatically a
  night of care by the other parent". Spending and transport showed involvement, not nights.
- **Pattern, not timetable**: an irregular arrangement can be a pattern of care; the accepted care
  after discharge and the father's own "closer to 50/50" report showed neither parent had a
  substantially greater share. **50% / 50% from 7 March 2024** — moving from a nil cost percentage to
  50% engaged mandatory revocation (s 54F) — "not an allocation of half the unresolved nights to each
  parent or a compromise between competing contentions".
- The September 2024 claim of full-time care **failed**: the mother's October messages showed her
  still caring for the child. Second decision affirmed.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.5 / ind.2.6 (dispute resolution; merits review)** — four decision-makers before a final
  answer, all outside the courts, with a self-represented father on the telephone and an absent
  party heard on paper. Pair with a court case: a tribunal carrying **no onus of proof** against a
  court deciding on the balance of probabilities — the Member's own words make the contrast.
- **fam.1.5** — a near-adult's independence breaks a model built on where a child sleeps.
- **fam.2.3 / shel.3.1** — child support as the last financial consequence of separation, ending on
  the eighteenth birthday; assessed at nil or at half, it changes what each household can afford.
- **Effectiveness in time** — two and a half years to decide eight months of care that ended before
  the first review was heard: effective in form more than substance (HSC 2018 Q28(b)).
- **fam.t.compliance** — notification is a legal duty, and the father's own contemporaneous
  notifications became the best evidence against his later case.
