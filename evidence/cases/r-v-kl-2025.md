KL, aged 14, had joined a Mt Druitt youth gang and begun carrying a knife after being stabbed himself
at 13; in 2021 he held his friend Darcy Moore as he died from a stabbing, and both psychiatrists dated
his **post-traumatic stress disorder** to that night. On 11 April 2022, in a ten-second
confrontation at the **Royal Easter Show**, he stabbed Uati Faletolu, 17, once in the heart. He
offered a plea to manslaughter; the **Crown refused it**. His 2025 jury trial ended in a **hung jury**
after two weeks, and the retrial was heard by **Garling J sitting alone**.

## Held — not guilty of murder, guilty of manslaughter, on three independent grounds

1. **Intention not proved** — the Crown's gang-revenge case and KL's later boasting videos were
   "nothing more than boasting or bragging by an adolescent, who is psycho-socially immature"; the act
   was "an impulsive, thoughtless, immediate reaction". Murder failed on intent alone.
2. **Excessive self-defence not excluded** (*Crimes Act* ss 419, 421) — it was reasonably possible KL
   genuinely believed stabbing was necessary; he conceded the response was unreasonable, which makes
   it manslaughter.
3. **Substantial impairment** (**s 23A**, the accused proves it on the balance of probabilities) — PTSD
   substantially impaired his self-control, and applying "the standards of the community which, as I
   am hearing this trial as a Judge alone, I represent", he should not be condemned as a murderer.

- **crime.3.8 (partial defences)** — the clearest current NSW example of both statutory partial
  defences, with the burden named on each (Crown on s 421; accused on s 23A).
- **crime.3.9** — a judge-alone retrial after a hung jury; the judge consciously standing in for the
  community. Compare *Dawson*.
- **crime.5 (young offenders)** — age, immaturity and trauma carried into the law of murder itself;
