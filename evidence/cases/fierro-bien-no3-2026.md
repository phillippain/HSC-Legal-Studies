A man struck off the Roll of Lawyers in 2015 began parenting proceedings in 2020 about his
**half-sister**, born 2008, suing his father and stepmother. Consent orders in May 2021 gave him no
contact unless she initiated it. Five months later he applied to vary them, and the litigation ran
across three registries for six years — four contempt applications, a contravention application,
appeals and a remitter. All three parties appeared in person. He then applied for the judge to
**disqualify himself** for apprehended bias.

## Held — recusal refused

The test is whether a **fair-minded lay observer** might reasonably apprehend that the judge would
not bring an impartial mind, and the applicant must articulate a **logical connection** between what
he complains of and a feared departure from deciding the case on its merits. He did not. *QYFM*
requires that observer to be placed "in a contemporary setting": "Uncritical attitudes of the past
cannot be assumed to be those of the present."

Adjourned: his application to amend the contempt applications, the show cause hearing, and both
respondents' applications for **vexatious proceedings orders under s 102QB**. He obtained no
substantive relief of any kind — his parenting case had ended when his half-sister turned 18 and was
"no longer amenable to the jurisdiction of this Court", keeping it alive being "an exercise in
futility".

## Use it for

- **fam.2.5** — court process as the subject matter: docket management, impartiality, the ICL, and
  what a court can do about a litigant who will not stop.
- **Cooperation** — consent orders in 2021 did not end the conflict; s 102QB is the law's last
  answer when cooperation never arrives.
- **Effectiveness** — six years of litigation produced no change to the 2021 orders: the sharpest
  example in the collection of a legal response that cannot resolve a family dispute.
- **Access to justice / rule of law** — the right to a fair hearing before an impartial tribunal,
  applied to both sides: shutting out the respondents' reply would itself have denied procedural
  fairness.
