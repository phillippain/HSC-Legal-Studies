Mary Willis inherited two houses in a Rozelle street from her father decades ago; one was tenanted
until January 2000 and managed by an agent, who paid rates and arranged maintenance, until 2014.
Yael Abraham — a solicitor, real estate agent and foster mother featured on *Australian Story* in
2009, with a $7 million Queensland home — heard of "apparently abandoned" houses from her brother,
began storing furniture there in 2011, and claimed the property under adverse possession when Willis
sued for possession in 2023. Her eldest child lived there part-time from 2012 and full-time from 2016.

## Held — Elkaim AJ

- **Not possession of the right kind.** Using the house "as a storeroom does not substantiate …
  adverse possession" because it was not being used "as a residence"; the house "did not have a
  functional bathroom in 2012 and probably not until 2016". Physical possession was taken, at the
  earliest, in **2016** — so the 12-year period under the *Limitation Act 1969* (NSW) had not run.
- **Not open.** "To the passerby, the appearance of occupation was not apparent": the front windows
  were never mended and the roof repaired only in the last five years. Abraham "deliberately, took no
  action to display her occupation to the outside world" — possession "continued by stealth".
- **Not without permission.** She told the agent in 2023 that Willis "gave us the keys" and that she
  had spoken to Willis in 2011 — a claim of permission is "contrary to the requirement for open
  possession".
- **Rates.** In 2024, with proceedings on foot, she "telephoned the Inner West Council and pretended
  to be [Willis], and arranged to pay the outstanding rates" — a "particularly strong inference" that
  she paid "in order to bolster her claim for possession". Judgment for Willis; cross-claim dismissed
  with costs.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 / shel.1.4 (squats)** — the three elements of adverse possession spelled out by their
  failure: continuous possession *as a residence*, **openly**, **without permission**. Pair with
  *Rogers* (Gymea Bay) where all three were present for fifty years.
- **shel.2.1 (registration, protection for owners)** — Torrens title holds against a claimant who
  cannot prove the full period; the owner's agent paying rates and managing the property was evidence
  the owner retained control.
- **shel.t.compliance / rule of law** — a lawyer impersonating the owner to improve her case, and a
  court drawing the inference against her.
- **Context** — the SMH notes "a string of adverse possession cases in Sydney in recent years" (a
  Redfern "dunny lane", Bankstown, Gymea Bay): empty houses in an unaffordable city are being
  contested.
