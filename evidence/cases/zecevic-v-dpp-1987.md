On 16 July 1983 Fadil Zecevic argued with his tenant Harold Triebel in the Ascot Vale units they
shared. Zecevic said Triebel stabbed him in the chest and threatened to "blow your head off"; fearing
a shotgun in Triebel's car, he went inside, loaded his own gun, came back and shot Triebel dead. The
trial judge **withdrew self-defence from the jury**, and Zecevic was convicted of murder.

## Held — appeal allowed, new trial

- **One question replaces six.** The six propositions from *Viro v The Queen* (1978) had proved
  impossible to explain or apply. The question is "whether the accused believed upon reasonable
  grounds that it was necessary in self-defence to do what he did" (Wilson, Dawson and Toohey JJ).
  Proportion, retreat and who started it are evidence on that question, not separate rules.
- **The belief must rest on reasonable grounds**, judged from the accused's own position — an honest
  but unreasonable belief is not enough.
- **The burden is on the Crown**: once raised, the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt
  that the act was not done in self-defence. The judge had no right to take even a weak plea from
  the jury.
- **No half-way house.** The *Howe*/*Viro* rule that excessive force reduced murder to manslaughter was
  abandoned (Deane and Gaudron JJ dissenting on this point).

## Use it for

- **crime.3.8 (complete defences)** — the common-law test that NSW codified in *Crimes Act 1900*
  **s 418** (2001), with **s 419** putting the burden on the Crown.
- **Law reform by Parliament reversing the courts** — NSW restored the half-way house the High Court
  abolished, as **s 421 excessive self-defence**. *Symons v R* (2024) and *R v KL* (2025) show s 421 in
  use.
- **crime.3.6** — a defence the accused only has to raise; the Crown must disprove it.

## Caution

A Victorian appeal decided at common law; in NSW cite ss 418–421 for the current law and *Zecevic*
for where the test came from. The outcome of the retrial is not in the report.
