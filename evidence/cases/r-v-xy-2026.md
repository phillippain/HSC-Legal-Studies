XY was sentenced in July 2026 for sexual offences against two children between 2002 and 2007,
including a 2007 aggravated sexual assault (*Crimes Act* s 61J: maximum 20 years, SNPP 10). The case had
gone through three trials. The judge imposed 4 years 6 months (non-parole 2 years) for the s 61J
offence, with heavy concurrency and backdating, so that the new sentence added **under five months**
of actual custody (effective total 5 years 6 months, non-parole about 3 years). He was anonymised under
the *Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act* s 15A. The DPP appealed.

## Held — appeal dismissed

- A Crown appeal must show error (*House v The King*); "manifest inadequacy" means below the range
  justly open, not merely lenient — the Court "might itself have imposed a heavier sentence, but that is
  not the test" (*Lowndes*).
- The Crown conceded "compelling aspects" of the subjective case and **did not challenge** the findings
  on deprivation (*Bugmy*), delay, three trials, broken custody or the "very generous" special
  circumstances. With those findings standing, the sentence was within the broad margin.
- Payne JA and Roberts J: the case was a poor vehicle for general guidance on sentencing.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.5 (Crown appeals)** — only the DPP or Attorney-General can appeal a sentence as too lenient,
  and such appeals should be rare; set beside *Loveridge* (Crown won) and *Pullen* (won, still an ICO).
- **Totality and concurrency** — how backdating and concurrency can make a sentence look shorter than it is.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary**. The offending involved children — keep to the sentencing principles. The
judgment gives two different start dates for the earlier sentence (2023 and 2024); 2023 fits the
arithmetic.
