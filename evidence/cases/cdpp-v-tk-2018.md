## Facts

**TK**, aged 16 to 17, repeatedly broke into a large American corporation's network over about 16 months and pleaded guilty to two Commonwealth offences (ss 477.2(1) and 478.1(1) *Criminal Code*). The corporation referred the matter to the FBI. TK was 19 at sentencing.

## Held

Section 20C of the *Crimes Act 1914* (Cth) is **permissive**, so the Children's Court could sentence under the *Children, Youth and Families Act 2005* (Vic), which excludes general deterrence and puts rehabilitation first, instead of s 16A. The prosecution's contrary argument was refused. With excellent prospects of rehabilitation, an early plea and cooperation, TK received **no conviction** and a probation order for just over eight months.

## Use it for

- **crime.6.1**: cybercrime across borders (US victim, FBI referral, AFP).
- **crime.5.4**: penalties for children, and the primacy of rehabilitation.
- **Contrast** with *Soyke v R* (an 18-year-old sentenced with strong emphasis on deterrence).

## Caution

A **Victorian first-instance** decision applying Victorian law, not binding across Australia. The file has inconsistent warrant and detection dates and no numerical plea discount. The child is identified only by initials: keep it that way.
