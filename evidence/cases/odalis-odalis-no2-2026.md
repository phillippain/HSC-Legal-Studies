A mother in a parenting case ended her lawyers’ retainer on 30 August 2026, days before a five-day trial. On 31 August the trial was vacated, partly because the Family Report predated overnight time with her. On 15 September 2026 Gill J dismissed her objection to a subpoena for her psychologist’s notes and let the father’s lawyers, not the father, inspect them. This is a procedural judgment, not the final decision.

## Use it for

- Courts and dispute resolution: vacating a trial, a subpoena, an Independent Children’s Lawyer and a Court Child Expert.
- Separation and children: parenting arrangements decided on best interests, with an updated family report.
- Domestic violence: the s 102NA scheme and protected confidences where family violence is alleged.
- Effectiveness: fairness for a self-represented mother, set against the father’s wish to end the case.

## Cautions

This is an interlocutory judgment about a vacated trial and a subpoena, not the final parenting decision; the court did not decide where the children will live. Inconsistencies in the file: Order 1 dates the Notice of Objection 14 August 2026 but [20] says 21 August; [14] says the application was made in September 2026 but [1] and [15] say 31 August; [29] says the mother obtained a Family Violence Order against the father, while [7] describes an order naming the mother as defendant; the header lists counsel for the applicant and a litigant in person as respondent, yet the reasons show the mother unrepresented and the father with counsel (Order 6 also calls the applicant ‘himself’). Paragraph labels are doubled (the second number is real). Care line: family violence allegations, a past assault charge and therapy notes; keep to the legal test and omit clinical detail.
