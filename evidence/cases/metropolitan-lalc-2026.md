Metropolitan Local Aboriginal Land Council is incorporated under **s 50** of the *Aboriginal Land
Rights Act 1983* (NSW), and under **s 36** it holds Lot 2 DP 1293455 in Willoughby **in fee simple**
— the fullest form of ownership Australian law recognises. The land is eight tennis courts with
buildings, surrounded by developed residential suburbs.

## The catch in s 36

Land transferred under the ALRA is held **subject to any existing native title rights**, and **s 42**
prevents the Land Council from dealing with it — selling, leasing, mortgaging or developing — unless
a determination has been made that native title **does not** exist.

So the owner of the land must go to the Federal Court and **prove a negative**. It filed a
non-claimant application under s 61(1) of the *Native Title Act 1993* (Cth). Unopposed.

## Held — negative determination made

- **Unopposed does not mean automatic.** "The question for the Court remains the same whether the
  application is unopposed or contested" — has the applicant discharged its burden of proof (*Mace*
  at [44])? An application "can be successfully opposed on the basis that the evidence adduced is
  insufficient (even where no affirmative evidence is adduced)" (*Harris*, Full Court).
- Raper J **did not accept** the Land Council's argument that no recorded Aboriginal sites within 50
  metres, plus heavy urban development, showed connection had not been sustained: development goes
  to **extinguishment**, which was not raised, and **"Aboriginal heritage and native title are
  distinct concepts"**, so the absence of heritage sites is not determinative [43].
- The State Minister is a party under **s 84(4)** in a *parens patriae* role, acting "to look after
  the interests of the community generally" (*Munn*), and must be satisfied as to the cogency of the
  evidence [7].

## Use it for

- **The sharpest irony available in the native title material.** *Mabo* recognised rights that
  survived; thirty years on, an Aboriginal organisation has to extinguish the possibility of those
  rights in court before it can use land it already owns outright. That is a law reform argument
  about the interaction of two statutes, and it is much stronger than the usual "claims take too
  long".
- **Purchasing and dealing with land** in the Shelter option, from a direction no student expects:
  a title that cannot be dealt with is a limit on what ownership means.
- **Heritage and native title are different things** — a distinction students routinely collapse.
