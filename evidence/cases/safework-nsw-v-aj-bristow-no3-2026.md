On **5 August 2022**, during restoration of a heritage school façade, two heavy sandstone blocks tipped forward after the
brick parapet holding them down had been removed; the stonemason, subcontracted through a specialist business, died at the
scene. After a judge-alone trial (from 17 March 2026), Taylor J found the company **guilty** of a Category 2 offence under
s 32 — exposing a worker to a risk of death or serious injury.

## The three questions

| Issue | Held |
|---|---|
| Did being principal contractor decide the duty? | Not decisive — the s 19 duty turns on the business's own conduct |
| Was the risk reasonably practicable to prevent, judged without hindsight, and could it rely on a specialist subcontractor? | Yes, preventable; reliance on a specialist did not discharge its duty |
| Did failures that began before the charge date expose a worker to risk? | Yes — no accident is needed to prove the risk |

Three of five pleaded failures were proved. The cause of the fall remains unknown.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.3** — a strict-liability regulatory offence, with a company as offender (crime.1.7).
- **crime.3.6** — the prosecution must prove each particular; two failed.
- **Compliance** — courts push duty holders to manage risk before harm occurs.

A **verdict only**: no penalty yet. Directions for sentencing were listed for 19 November 2026.
