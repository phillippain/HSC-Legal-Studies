William Bugmy, an Aboriginal man from Wilcannia with a childhood marked by violence and alcohol abuse,
assaulted a prison officer, causing the loss of an eye. The Court of Criminal Appeal increased his
sentence on a Crown appeal, reasoning that the weight of his deprived background lessened with time
and repeated offending.

## Held

Appeal allowed (the matter was sent back to the Court of Criminal Appeal). The effects of profound
childhood deprivation **do not diminish with the passage of time** or repeated offending, and are to
be given full weight. But there is no separate approach for Aboriginal offenders: **Aboriginality
alone is not a mitigating factor**, and deprivation must be balanced against deterrence and community
protection.

## Use it for

- **Mitigating factors** (`crime.4.3`) — moral culpability and individual justice.
- **Aboriginal over-representation** — Aboriginal adults are about 34% of NSW custody (BOCSAR, June
  2026); pair with circle sentencing (`crime.4.7`).
- The Moore Park defence (2026) relied on *Bugmy*.

## Caution

Do not say *Bugmy* gives Aboriginal offenders lighter sentences. It is about deprivation, applies to
anyone, and the weight may be reduced where protection of the community dominates.
