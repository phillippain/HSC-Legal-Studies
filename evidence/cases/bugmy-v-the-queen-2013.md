William Bugmy, an Aboriginal man from Wilcannia who grew up amid alcohol abuse and violence, began
offending at 12 and was moved from juvenile detention to adult prison at 18. On remand at Broken Hill
he threw pool balls at correctional officers; one struck Mr Gould, who lost the sight of his left eye.
The District Court gave an effective non-parole period of 4 years 3 months. On a Crown appeal the
Court of Criminal Appeal raised it to **5 years** — without first deciding whether the original
sentence was manifestly inadequate — reasoning that the weight of his deprived background lessened
with time and repeated offending.

## Held — appeal allowed and remitted

- The Court of Criminal Appeal had to find error (manifest inadequacy) before it could resentence.
- The effects of profound childhood deprivation **do not diminish with the passage of time** or
  repeated offending, and are to be given full weight in assessing moral culpability.
- But there is **no separate method for Aboriginal offenders**: the Court declined to follow the
  Canadian *Gladue* approach or take judicial notice of systemic Aboriginal disadvantage.
  Aboriginality alone is not a mitigating factor, and deprivation must still be balanced against
  deterrence and community protection.

## Use it for

- **Mitigating factors** (crime.4.3) — moral culpability and individual justice; cited in *R v Billings*
  (2026) and the Moore Park sentence.
- **Aboriginal over-representation** — Aboriginal adults are about 34% of NSW custody (BOCSAR, June
  2026); pair with circle sentencing and the Youth Koori Court (*R v John*, crime.4.7 / crime.5.5).
- **Crown appeals** (crime.4.5) — the appeal court must identify error first.

## Caution

Do not say *Bugmy* gives Aboriginal offenders lighter sentences. It is about deprivation, applies to
anyone, and the weight may be reduced where protection of the community dominates. The High Court did
not set the final sentence.
