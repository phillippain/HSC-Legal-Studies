A long marriage in which the parties built **a highly successful business together**. After
separation the husband drew large sums from it, earned significant income and **ran up substantial
tax debts**.

## What the Court did with the debts

| | |
|---|---|
| The husband's **tax liability** | The wife **should not share it** — excluded from the pool |
| **Interest** on the unpaid tax | Treated as **waste** |
| **Contingent liabilities** | Considered as such, not as present debts |
| **Capital gains tax** | Distinguished between tax that has **crystallised** and **potential future CGT**, which is weighed rather than simply deducted |
| **Post-separation conduct** | Taken into account in the adjustment |
| **Outcome** | **58–42 in favour of the wife** |

The orders also required the husband to **indemnify the wife** against claims arising from her
having been a director and secretary of the family company, from any alleged indebtedness to the
company or the family trust, from the Deputy Commissioner of Taxation, and from guarantees she had
given — and to transfer his interest in two ACT properties to her within 120 days.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.3 (consequences of separation — property)** — the record that shows property division is
  **not a 50/50 split of what is left**. Contributions, future needs, post-separation conduct and the
  treatment of debts all move the number, and here they moved it to 58–42.
- **Waste** — the concept students most often miss. Where one party's conduct after separation
  destroys value, the Court can treat the loss as that party's alone. Interest on tax the husband
  chose not to pay is a clean example: the wife contributed nothing to it and is not made to carry
  it.
- **Capital gains tax** — a genuinely technical point made usable. Tax that would only be payable
  *if* an asset is sold is **contingent**; the Court weighs the likelihood rather than deducting it
  automatically. Useful for arguing that a "fair" split depends on what is counted as an asset.
- **Indemnities as an enforcement mechanism (fam.2.5)** — the orders do not just divide property,
  they protect the wife from company, trust, tax and guarantee claims that would otherwise follow
  her out of the marriage. Good evidence that orders have to be **enforceable and complete** to be
  effective.
- ***Stanford*** and the just-and-equitable question sit behind all of it; pair with *Connor &
  Cosgrove* on contributions.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB applies. This is ***(No 9)*** — at least nine judgments in one property
dispute, which is itself evidence about cost and delay, but it also means this decision rests on
findings made in earlier judgments **not in the library**. The reasoning is heavily
fact-specific: the 58–42 outcome is **not a benchmark**, and no percentage from one case should be
transplanted into another. The CGT and waste discussions are technical; check *Rosati*, *Biltoft* and
*Kowaliw* (the authorities behind these principles) before stating any rule in general terms. The
judgment applies the *Family Law Act* as amended, so be careful quoting section numbers against
older textbooks.
