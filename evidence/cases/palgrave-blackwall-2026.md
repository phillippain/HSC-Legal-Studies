From July 2014 Services Australia recorded the mother as providing **100%** of the child's care. On
16 May 2024 the father telephoned Child Support to say that since March 2023 the mother had had no
care at all: the girl was at boarding school and "potentially supporting herself". **On that call
alone**, an officer decided neither parent had provided care from 4 March 2023 — which reduces the
assessment to nothing. On objection the date moved back to 1 February 2023 and the finding was
otherwise upheld.

The mother's evidence was different. The family lives in a remote town where children routinely
leave home for school. Between February and April 2023 the daughter lived with her older siblings in
a house **the mother rented and paid the bills for**, so she could attend a school; the siblings
drove her. It did not work; she came home and studied online. In August 2023 the mother enrolled her
at a boarding school, paying a $4,000 enrolment fee and about **$80,000 a year** in fees — the very
fact said to prove she had no care of her daughter.

The father allegedly asked the girl to meet him in Brisbane, took her to a Centrelink office and
told her to claim a payment and say she was independent of her mother. She refused. He produced
**no documents** and did not attend the hearing.

## Held — decision set aside and substituted

No change in care; delegated care to siblings and then to a boarding school did not break the
mother's pattern of care. The application was 3½ months out of a 28-day window: **special
circumstances** found. **100% care restored and backdated**, reversing two years of nil assessment.

## Use it for

- **Procedural fairness** at its most concrete: a decision that removed a parent's entire
  entitlement was made on one phone call, without documents, and stood through an internal review.
- **Evidence beats assertion** — and the party who did not attend lost. Pair it with *Billings &
  Clowes (No 2)* and *Eadgar & Amiri*, where non-attendance cost the absent party too.
- **Delegated care**: paying for a child's care is providing it. A useful corrective to the
  assumption that "care" means physical presence.
- Twenty-six months from the father's phone call to the decision correcting it.
