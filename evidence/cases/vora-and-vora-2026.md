Parents of two young children had **equal-time consent orders** (23 April 2024). After a case
assessment conference, a magistrate suspended them (25 September 2025) and on 8 May 2026 made
**interim orders**: the children live with the mother and see their father only in professionally
supervised time, pending a **single expert** report. The father appealed on nine grounds. Schonell J
dismissed the appeal and his application to add evidence on 20 October 2026.

## Held

- A discretionary decision under s 60CC is **presumed correct** until a *House v The King* error is
  shown; a different view on weight is not enough.
- **Procedural fairness** concerns the hearing, not the outcome. He was heard on 11 February and
  30 March 2026; his "real complaint" was the outcome.
- Reasons for a cautious stopgap **need not address every fact**; the path of reasoning was clear.
- Even success would only lead to a rehearing awaiting the same expert report (no utility).

## Use it for

- **Courts and dispute resolution** (fam.2.5): case assessment conference, Independent Children's
  Lawyer, single expert, litigants in person and the appeal path, with timeliness and accessibility
  as the effectiveness criteria (more than seven months from suspension to interim orders).
- **Year 11 procedural fairness** (inv.1.6): a clean statement that fairness protects the hearing
  and not the result.
- Care: mental health history and months without contact with a parent; teach the legal test only.
