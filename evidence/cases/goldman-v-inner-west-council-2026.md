Clementine Goldman drove sweepers, tippers and rubbish trucks, first for a labour hire company at
Inner West Council's depot, and was one of only a few female drivers there. She missed out on a
permanent Team Member Driver position in March 2024 — told her lack of garbage truck experience was
the main reason — got that experience, and applied again in August 2024. Male candidates were
notified of their interviews on 3 August; she was told at **4am on the morning of the interview**,
7 August, and had to work her shift first. Her medical assessment details were shown to her on a
supervisor's phone rather than emailed. The full-time jobs went to men; she was offered a casual
driver position and put on an eligibility list. She sought $30,000 for hurt and humiliation and
$9,755.73 in economic loss.

## Held — both complaints dismissed

- **ss 24(1)(a), 25(1)** of the *Anti-Discrimination Act 1977* (NSW): the question is whether she
  was treated **less favourably than a comparator** — here "an applicant who possesses the same or
  materially similar skills, experience and work record … but who is not a woman" — **on the ground
  of** her sex. Under **s 4A**, sex need only be *one* operative reason, not the dominant one
  (*Banovic*, *Waters*, *Purvis*).
- There was "no smoking gun": no witness said or wrote anything about her sex, so the case was
  **circumstantial**. Following *Arumugam* and *Jones v Dunkel*, an inference of discrimination
  requires a fact that "positively suggests" it — otherwise it is conjecture.
- The Tribunal accepted the Council's explanations: the short notice was a supervisor realising late
  that she should be interviewed, and he also placed her on the eligibility list, which is
  "inherently unlikely" conduct for someone trying to keep her out. She was agreed to have performed
  well at interview, and the medical assessment point could not be shown to matter.
- The proceeding "is not concerned with general fairness in the process" — only with whether it
  miscarried **because of sex**. The victimisation claim against the supervisor was abandoned on the
  second day and dismissed.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 / hr.2.9** — the complaint that failed, and why: the standard of proof for an inference,
  not the absence of unequal treatment. The Tribunal accepted she had less notice than the men.
- **Compare *HGY v Ministry of Health*** (same Senior Member, eight days earlier), where a
  discrimination complaint succeeded and drew $35,000. Together they are the sharpest pair in the
  collection on **accessibility and enforceability** of anti-discrimination law.
- **hr.2.11** — merits-style fact finding in a tribunal: two members, two hearing days, submissions
  closed almost three months before the decision.
- **Evaluation** — "less favourable treatment" and "on the ground of sex" are two separate hurdles,
  and the second is where most employment complaints fail.

## Caution

A dismissal is not a finding that nothing unequal happened — the Tribunal expressly accepted the
shorter notice, and decided only that sex was not shown to be a reason for it. Names in parts of the
evidence are suppressed under s 64 of the *Civil and Administrative Tribunal Act 2013*. First
instance, and appealable.
