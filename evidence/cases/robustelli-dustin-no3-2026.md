Three siblings with complex needs — **X (14), Y (13) and Z (almost 11)** — were exposed to neglect and family violence. There had been **28 risk-of-harm reports** to the welfare agency (DCJ) between 2014 and 2023, and removal into care was a real prospect. Pseudonyms are used throughout.

## How it unfolded

|---|---|
| 19 Apr 2023 | Consent orders: Y and Z with the mother, X with the father |

| 18 Sep 2026 | Judgment and final orders |

## Held — 18 September 2026

- **Framework**: each child's best interests are paramount; the **s 60CC(2)** considerations are not ranked but weighed together — "the synthesis of all of the applicable considerations" decides the outcome (at [13]).
- **Safety is wide**: it covers harm from violence, abuse and neglect and looks **forward** from past conduct. The benefit of a relationship counts only where it is safe — "does not contemplate consideration of the benefits of a relationship where such is not safe" (at [19]).
- **Shared responsibility**: carers with limited capacity were supported by the agency. The judge found the combined work of DCJ, the paternal grandmother and the mother meant "the looming prospect of the children being placed in care has not crystallised" (at [100]).
- **The father**: the risk, which includes a risk of **lethality** (threats recorded in hospital records), requires that any time be approved by DCJ **and** professionally supervised (at [103]). X's wish to see him is respected.
- **The children's lawyer** was praised; the court did not defer the lawyer's discharge by about 12 months.
