Married 2011, separated January 2024. Ten real properties held personally and through a group of
trusts and companies. Net pool: wife $14,938,133 / husband $17,237,600.

The husband sought release from a consent restraint so he could grant his solicitors a charge over a
property securing past and future fees. **Charge permitted** — reversible, "modest" protection, and
it does not defeat her claim. Sale of another property refused.

## The headline evidence

Wife projected **$1,979,971** to the end of the final hearing — "an extraordinary sum" at [56];
husband **$1,206,222**; **combined over $3,186,000**, roughly a fifth of the pool. She had already
applied over $1m of pool funds to legal fees.

At [61] the figure "ought to compel serious reflection"; at [64] "intractable conflict can be
measured in the extraordinary legal costs".

## Use it for

**Resource efficiency and accessibility** — the strongest cost evidence in the Family option, and
the answer to any question that assumes a well-resourced litigant gets justice. Pair it with
*Eadgar & Amiri*, where a woman slept in her ex-partner's child's bedroom and the costs order was
**$4,500**: the same court, the same Act, opposite ends of the wealth spectrum.

**Authorities worth naming:** *Stanford v Stanford* (2012) 247 CLR 108 (the just and equitable
threshold); *Bevan & Bevan* (2013) FLC 93-545; *Hickey* (2003) FLC 93-143; *Strahan* (2011) FLC
93-466.
