A woman applied on 13 March 2026 for a decree of nullity because her husband was still married to Ms F when they married in mid-2024. He agreed the marriage should be void but said he did not know. Campton J, giving ex tempore reasons in a final first-instance decision on 29 September 2026, did not accept his evidence, declared the marriage null and void, and ordered him to pay $2,190 costs.

## Use it for

- Marriage: the Marriage Act defines marriage and makes a second marriage void while the first exists.
- Nature of family law: Commonwealth marriage law and a decree of nullity, which is not a divorce.
- Compliance: a false ‘never validly married’ declaration; the court could not find if it was intentional.
- Effectiveness: a registered marriage was still void, and a nullity decree cannot be made by consent.

## Cautions

A final first-instance decision with ex tempore reasons; the nullity claim was agreed, so the real dispute was costs. A nullity decree is not a divorce: it declares the marriage never valid. The court could not find whether the false declaration was intentional or reckless ([15]). The order says ‘B Venue’ but [19] says ‘B Recreational Centre’; the catchwords cite s 44, the reasons s 44(1A). Only Axon is cited. Care line: deception and relationship breakdown; keep to the legal test.
