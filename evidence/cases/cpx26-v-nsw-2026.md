A mother — **self-represented** — and her child alleged that their social housing did not accommodate
their disabilities: a steep access route, flooding, water ingress, mould and other defects. They
sought urgent release from the lease, suitable temporary housing and priority for permanent housing.
An occupational therapist recommended timely relocation and raised safe-access concerns. **Homes NSW
filed no evidence responding to that report.**

## Held at the interim hearing

| Issue | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Accessibility and reasonable adjustments | **Arguable** — a serious question to be tried |
| Urgent housing orders | **Refused** — broad, imprecise positive obligations, and competing demands for finite housing |
| Expanded discrimination claims | **Limited** — they did not sufficiently correspond to the terminated AHRC complaint (AHRC Act s 46PO(3)) |
| Victimisation | Not established for interim purposes |
| Identity protection | **Partly granted** — pseudonyms necessary for safety |
| Next steps | **Mediation ordered**, plus evidence on the therapist's report and alternative accommodation |

**s 25** of the *Disability Discrimination Act 1992* (Cth) is the accommodation gateway; ss 5 and 6
define direct and indirect discrimination, including a failure to make reasonable adjustments.

## Use it for

- **Discrimination and social housing at once**, which few cases do.
- **The exam distinction that matters**: refusing urgent orders did **not** dismiss or finally
  determine the discrimination proceeding. Students routinely read an interim refusal as a loss.
- **Resources shape remedies without excusing the state.** The Court said resources did not place
  the State outside the law, but they shaped the assessment of interim relief. That sentence is the
  honest answer to "is the right to adequate housing enforceable in Australia".
- **Access to justice**: a self-represented applicant with a disability, against the State, in the
  Federal Court, on an interlocutory application.
