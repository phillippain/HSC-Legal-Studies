The couple met in 1996 and married in 1998. Before the marriage the respondent had told the applicant
that he enjoyed dressing as a woman but **identified as male**. They had two children. In **2013** the
respondent disclosed that she had suppressed a female identity since the age of six; with her wife's
support she began hormone therapy in **2014** and had **gender reassignment surgery in 2017**.

The wife told the court she was glad she had married the respondent and intended to stay married — but
they sought a **declaration of nullity** so they could **remarry as a same-sex couple**, now that the
law allowed it.

## The law that had just changed

The **December 2017** postal survey: **79.5%** of eligible Australians responded and **61.6%** voted
yes. The *Marriage Act 1961* (Cth) was amended by the **Marriage Amendment (Definition and Religious
Freedoms) Act 2017**, redefining marriage as **"the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others,
voluntarily entered into for life"**.

## Held

Nullity refused. The question was not whether a same-sex marriage is valid but whether **mistaken
gender identity** made this one void. It did not: at the time of the marriage the respondent
**identified and lived as male**, so there was no mistake as to identity. The transition did not erase
the history, the marriage **remained valid**, and they could not remarry.

## Use it for

- **Recognition of same-sex relationships** (`fam.3.1`) from an angle nobody expects: a couple who
  *wanted* the annulment, refused it, and left legally married after one party's transition — which
  means Australian law already contained married couples of the same sex before 2017, and did not
  disturb them.
- **The requirements of a valid marriage tested at the moment of the ceremony** (`fam.1.3`), not
  afterwards. Nullity looks backwards to formation.
- **Law reform by plebiscite** — the figures are precise and quotable, and the mechanism (a
  **non-binding** national survey followed by legislation) is worth naming for `ls.4.3`.
