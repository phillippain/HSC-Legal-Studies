Parenting proceedings begun in 2023 were listed on 6 February 2026 for a five-day final hearing from 14 September. At a Mention on 9 September a self-represented father orally asked to vacate it, saying he needed time with a new legal team. He had not paid his lawyers’ retainer. Curran J refused in ex tempore reasons: no proper basis, delay would distress the children, and court time is a public resource. This was an interlocutory ruling only.

## Use it for

- Courts and dispute resolution: case management, a Mention, an Independent Children’s Lawyer and an urgent Legal Aid conference.
- Effectiveness: timeliness, cost and efficient use of public resources, against one party’s call for fairness.
- Non-compliance: filing directions were not met and reports were not paid for despite orders.

## Cautions

This is an interlocutory, ex tempore ruling on an oral adjournment, not a final parenting decision; the court did not decide where the children live and the file does not say what happened at the trial. The catchwords say the application was made without notice; the order is dated 9 September 2026 but the reasons are certified on 29 September 2026. Paragraph 15 says the matter was listed ‘at my motion yesterday’. The file calls Curran J’s authority ‘French J’ for AON, and cites only two cases. Care line: the Independent Children’s Lawyer reported the children’s distress and a claim of being stalked; this was not in evidence and the judge made no finding. Keep to the legal test.
