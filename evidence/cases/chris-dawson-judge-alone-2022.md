Lynette Dawson was last heard from on 8 January 1982. Her husband Christopher, a teacher at Cromer
High, said he had dropped her at a bus stop and that she rang him the next day — both later found to
be lies — and within days his 17-year-old former student JC moved in. Two inquests (2001, 2003)
pointed at him, but no charge came until after the case was reopened and *The Teacher's Pet*
podcast (2018) drew national attention. He was charged in December 2018. A permanent stay for delay
and publicity was refused in 2020, and the trial was heard by a **judge alone**.

## Held — Harrison J, 30 August 2022

- No body, no cause of death, no witness: a **wholly circumstantial** case, in which guilt must be
  **the only rational inference** open on the evidence.
- The Crown proved beyond reasonable doubt that Lynette was dead and had not left voluntarily, so
  every later "sighting", phone call and bankcard purchase relied on by the defence had to be false.
- Dawson's motive (his relationship with JC) and his lies left the judge "in no doubt": guilty of
  murder.
- Under *Criminal Procedure Act 1986* **s 133** a judge sitting alone has every power of a jury but must
  **give reasons** and take into account every warning a jury would have received — on lies (a lie
  shows consciousness of guilt only of the offence charged), hearsay, identification and the
  forensic disadvantage of forty years' delay (*Evidence Act* s 165B).

Sentenced on 2 December 2022 to **24 years, non-parole 18**. Conviction appeal dismissed: *Dawson v R*
[2024] NSWCCA 98. NSW "no body, no parole" laws followed in October 2022.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.9 (juries and verdicts)** — why judge-alone trials exist and what they cost: no jury, but
  written reasons that can be scrutinised. Contrast *R v KL* (judge-alone after a hung jury) and
  Kristian White (judge-alone application refused, 2024).
- **crime.3.6 / crime.3.7** — the standard of proof applied to circumstantial evidence, and how a
  judge must warn *himself* about unreliable evidence.
- **Media and the presumption of innocence** — publicity reopened the case and endangered the trial.

## Caution

The verdict judgment never cites s 132 or explains the judge-alone order, which was made separately;
the arrest date differs between the verdict (12 December 2018) and sentence (6 December 2018)
judgments. The 2024 appeal result is not in the verdict judgment. Earlier versions of this card gave
the verdict date as 31 August — the judgment is dated 30 August 2022.
