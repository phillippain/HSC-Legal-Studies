Muldrock, an offender with an intellectual disability, was sentenced for a child sexual offence that
carried a **standard non-parole period** (SNPP). The NSW Court of Criminal Appeal had treated the SNPP
as the starting point for an offence in the middle of the range and adjusted from there (the
'two-step' approach from *R v Way*, 2004).

## Held

Appeal allowed. The SNPP is **one guidepost** among the many factors the court must weigh; the court
does not begin with it and work up or down. Sentencing is an **instinctive synthesis** of all the
relevant factors (*Markarian v The Queen*, 2005).

## Use it for

- **Statutory v judicial guidelines** (`crime.4.1`) — Parliament set the SNPPs (2003), and the High
  Court read them so that judicial discretion survived.
- **Factors** (`crime.4.3`) — the offender's cognitive impairment still had to be weighed.

## Caution

*Muldrock* did not abolish SNPPs; they remain in the Act and judges must still refer to them.
