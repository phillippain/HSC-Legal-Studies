Derek Muldrock, 30, with an intellectual disability (a full-scale IQ of 62), pleaded guilty to sexual
intercourse with a child under 10 — maximum 25 years, **standard non-parole period (SNPP) 15 years**.
The District Court gave 9 years with a non-parole period of only 96 days (time served), so he could
receive treatment in the community. On a Crown appeal the Court of Criminal Appeal applied the
"two-step" approach from *R v Way* (2004) — start from the SNPP as the sentence for a mid-range
offence and adjust — and raised the non-parole period to **6 years 8 months**.

## Held — unanimous

- *R v Way* was **wrongly decided**. The SNPP is **one of two legislative guideposts**, beside the
  maximum penalty; the court does not begin with it and work up or down.
- Sentencing is an **instinctive synthesis** of all the relevant factors (*Markarian v The Queen*,
  2005), with reasons given for departing from the SNPP.
- **Intellectual disability** can make general deterrence and retribution less weighty, because the
  offender is a less appropriate vehicle for deterring others.
- Appeal allowed; remitted to the Court of Criminal Appeal to resentence.

## Use it for

- **Statutory v judicial guidelines** (crime.4.1) — Parliament set the SNPPs (2003), and the High
  Court read them so that judicial discretion survived. Compare *Hurt* (2024) on a mandatory minimum.
- **Factors** (crime.4.3) — the offender's cognitive impairment still had to be weighed.

## Caution

*Muldrock* did not abolish SNPPs; they remain in the Act and judges must still refer to them. The
offence involved a young child — keep discussion at the level of principle.
