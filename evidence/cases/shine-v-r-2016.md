Stephen Shine, a 58-year-old nursing student with an untreated psychotic illness, came to believe his
friend Ms Kim was in danger from her husband. On 19 April 2014 he went to their Lidcombe home and
stabbed Mr Lee in the neck as he lay in bed beside his three children, then slashed his face with a
second knife. Arrested at the scene, he said "I tried to kill him." He **delayed his plea while
psychiatric reports were obtained** — the last, in December 2014, said a mental illness defence was
open — and pleaded guilty to wounding with intent to murder about twelve months after arrest.

At first instance he received a **20% discount** and **12 years with a 7 years 6 months non-parole
period**; the judge found he "knew what he was doing was wrong" without explaining why the
psychiatrist's contrary opinion was rejected.

## Held on appeal

- **The general rule** (*R v Borkowski* [2009] NSWCCA 102): the reason for a late plea is usually
  irrelevant — a plea not yet entered has less utilitarian value, so the discount falls.
- **The exception** (*Haines v R* [2016] NSWCCA 90): where the delay is genuinely caused by the
  investigation of a mental illness defence, the plea's value remains high. Here the delay "can fairly
  be attributed to the applicant's mental illness": **25%** was right.
- The judge also erred on the mental illness findings and on rehabilitation. Resentenced to **9 years,
  non-parole 5**.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.4 (pleas)** — the discount is the engine of the guilty-plea system; this case shows its
  size and the fight over when it is earned.
- **Efficiency and desert come apart** — discounts are justified by resource savings and remorse, but
  here the full reduction was awarded for a delay caused by illness.
- **Since 2018** the *Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Act* s 25D fixes the discounts by stage (25% / 10%
  / 5%) for indictable matters — *Shine* explains the discretion that scheme replaced. *Billings* (2026)
  shows the discount cut from 25% to 20% under s 25F(2).

## Caution

Largely superseded for indictable offences by the 2018 Early Appropriate Guilty Plea scheme; cite it
for the principle and the summary matters it still governs.
