Shine, a nursing student, developed delusional beliefs that his friend Ms Kim was in danger from her
husband. He went to their home and stabbed Mr Lee in the neck, back and face while he slept in the
same room as his three children, causing life-threatening injuries. He admitted the attack to police
and said he was protecting Ms Kim, then **delayed his plea while awaiting psychiatric assessment**.

At first instance he was convicted of causing grievous bodily harm with intent to murder. The judge
accepted the psychiatric report but observed that Shine "knew what he was doing was wrong", applied a
**20% discount** and imposed **12 years with a 7 years 6 months non-parole period**.

## Held on appeal

Shine was entitled to the **full 25% discount** — the delay was explained by the wait for the report,
not by tactics — and the sentencing judge **erred in not giving weight to the psychiatrist's findings
on culpability**. The sentence was reduced to **9 years with a non-parole period of 5 years**.

## Use it for

- **Pleas and charge negotiation** — the discount is the engine of the guilty plea system, and this
  case shows both its size and the fight over when it is earned.
- **The tension in plea discounts**: they are justified by resource efficiency and by remorse, but here
  the reduction was awarded for a delay caused by illness. Efficiency and desert come apart.
- **Mental illness at sentencing** rather than as a defence — the report failed to produce an acquittal
  but succeeded in reducing the sentence by three years.
