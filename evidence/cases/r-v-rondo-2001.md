Police stopped Rondo because a young man was driving an expensive car in a manner they thought did
not fit. The search that followed turned up cannabis. The question was whether the suspicion that
justified the search was **reasonable**.

## Held

The Court set out the test still used for every warrantless search power in NSW:

- reasonable suspicion is **less than a reasonable belief but more than a possibility** — a hunch is
  not enough;
- there must be **some factual basis** for it, though hearsay and material that would be inadmissible
  at trial can supply that basis;
- it is judged **objectively, on what the officer knew at the time** — the fruits of the search cannot
  retrospectively justify it.

The search was unlawful and the evidence excluded.

## Use it for

- **Police powers** — the threshold that sits underneath ss 21, 23, 31 and 36 of LEPRA. Every
  discussion of stop and search should name it.
- **The invisible power**: *Rondo* is the point at which discretion becomes reviewable. It is also the
  case that connects to **over-policing** arguments — a suspicion formed from what a person looks like
  driving is exactly what "some factual basis" is meant to exclude.
- **Enforceability** — failing the test does not void the prosecution automatically; it exposes the
  evidence to the **s 138 *Evidence Act 1995* (NSW)** discretion. The remedy is discretionary, which is
  the standard criticism.
