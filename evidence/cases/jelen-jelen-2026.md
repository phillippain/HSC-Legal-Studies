Married 2006, two children, separated 2020–21, divorced October 2024. The husband, 51, trades
through his own company; its income fell from $413,123 (FY2023) to $226,718 (FY2024). He sought
**$500 a week for four years** — up to $104,000 — from his former wife, 49, who earns $135,000. His
agreed needs came to $1,864 a week, the largest item **$1,000 a week in rent**. The rental
application he lodged told the agent a different story about his income from the one he told the
Court, and a subpoena produced what disclosure had not.

## Held — application dismissed

- **s 72(1)(c)** is a gateway: the applicant must prove he is **unable to support himself
  adequately** before the Court has any discretion to exercise.
- **s 71B** — the duty of full and frank disclosure. It is enforced here not by punishment but by
  **evidentiary consequence**: because he had not disclosed, the Court could make no findings about
  his finances, so he could not get through the gateway.
- All other applications dismissed; costs left to be decided in chambers on the papers.

## Use it for

- **Compliance** — the cleanest available example that non-compliance defeats the non-complier, not
  just the other side. Pair with *Vacek & Novosel (No 7)*, where the sanction was costs.
- **fam.2.3** — spousal maintenance as the part of separation that outlives the divorce: litigated
  22 months after the divorce order and five years after separation.
- **Changing values** — a female primary earner defending a maintenance claim by her former
  husband, under deliberately gender-neutral drafting.
- **Shelter** — housing pressure produced the evidence that decided the case; rent was the largest
  item in his claimed needs.

## Caution

The trial only ran because it was transferred to Division 1 on the day — a resourcing point the
judgment makes, not a rule. Parenting and property were resolved by consent; only the money went to
trial. Summary prepared for class from the judgment; quotes checked against the judgment text.
