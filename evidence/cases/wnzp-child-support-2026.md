Two daughters, born 2012 and 2016. Care was recorded at **14% / 86%** (mother / father) from
December 2023. The arrangement changed on **22 March 2024**; the mother notified Child Support the
next day claiming 37%. The Registrar recorded 37/63, the father objected (28/72), the Tribunal
affirmed 28/72 on first review, and the mother sought a second review. From September to October
2024 the father worked in Pakistan for six weeks, leaving the children in his home with his
fiancée under a power of attorney, paying all costs and calling every second day.

## Held — 16 October 2026

- **Count the nights**: the two calendars differed by only two nights across the year — **116** to
  the mother, **249** to the father (22 March 2024 to 21 March 2025).
- **Absence is not abandonment**: applying *Polec & Staker* [2011] FMCAfam 959, the father kept the
  care while overseas — "I am not satisfied that the existence of a Power of Attorney establishes a
  transfer of care for child support purposes"; "counting nights does not itself resolve whose care
  the children were in when a parent was physically absent" (s 54A).
- **Revocation by discretion**: the cost percentages would not change, so mandatory revocation
  (s 54F) was unavailable; the s 54H discretion was used because an accurate record would result.
- **Court-ordered care cannot be recorded instead**: s 51 allows an interim determination
  reflecting parenting orders, but before **29 March 2024** para 53(1)(c) barred one after a
  revocation — *Child Support Registrar v CMU23* [2024] FCA 109 binds. The care changed **seven days
  too early**.
- Result: **31% / 69%** from 22 March 2024 — and cost percentages **unchanged at 24% / 76%** under
  the s 55C table. "No finding is made on whether care was withheld."

## Use it for

- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.3 (law reform)** — a transitional provision with a date on it: Parliament repealed
  the bar that let a parent benefit from withholding court-ordered time, but only for changes of
  care on or after 29 March 2024. HSC 2019 Q27(b) in one dated example.
- **fam.2.5 (dispute resolution outside the courts)** — Registrar decision → objection → first review
  → second review, with sworn oral evidence by phone and video; accessible and free, and two and a
  half years long.
- **fam.1.5 / fam.3.2** — "delegated care is still care": parental responsibility exercised through a
  fiancée, a grandfather and a power of attorney.
- **fam.t.compliance** — parenting orders said one thing, the care that happened said another, and
  child support must follow the second; the Tribunal is explicit that this "does not amount to
  approval of the withholding of care, if that did occur".
- **ind.2.6** — merits review: the Tribunal stands in the decision-maker's shoes and substitutes
  its own decision (*MDXJ v Secretary, DSS* [2020] FCA 1767: the Guide guides but does not control).
