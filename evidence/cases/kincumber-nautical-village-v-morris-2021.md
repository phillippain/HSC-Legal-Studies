Fifty-two home owners at Kincumber Nautical Village applied to NCAT on 15 October 2019 about a site fee increase calculated as **CPI plus 3.75% plus a share of cost rises** (Mr Morris's fee went from $227 to $242 a week from November 2018). On 3 September 2020 NCAT found the clause provided more than one fixed method, breaching s 66(2).

## Held

The Appeal Panel **allowed the appeal** on 14 September 2021. A "fixed calculation" under s 65 is one that can be **ascertained definitely**, and a formula adding several components is still one calculation. There was no breach of s 66(2), so **s 66(7)** applied and the term could not be challenged under the Act; the Tribunal had no jurisdiction.

## Use it for

- **Other shelter types and leasing**: how site agreements in land lease communities fix yearly fee rises (`shel.2.4`, `shel.2.2`).
- **Courts and tribunals**: an internal appeal on a point of statutory construction, and the limits of NCAT's jurisdiction (`shel.2.6`).
- **Contrast with** *Anderson v Kincumber* (2025), where consumer law succeeded on the same clause.

## Caution

The Panel did **not decide** the Australian Consumer Law unfair terms question. The file has typos ("RLCC") and the orders cite RC 19/47649 while the header says RC 19/48069. A later Supreme Court decision upholding this one is mentioned only in *Anderson*, not in this file.
