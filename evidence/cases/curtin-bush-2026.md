X, 11, lives with his mother under 2021 orders that allow overseas holidays on 30 days' notice. In
late June 2026 he told her he was going to Country C with his father — who had earlier spoken of
buying property and living there — and then said that was not right. On the evening of 6 July she
found his passport gone. A few minutes after 7pm the after-hours service reached the Duty Judge on
his train home; the father's phone went unanswered.

## Held

**6 July — ex parte, by phone (No 1).** A 16-minute hearing from 7:07pm. At 7:23pm, an injunction
restraining removal of X from Australia for 12 months "irrespective of authenticated consent", and a
request that the AFP place him on the **Family Law Watchlist**. The AFP "attended and prevented
travel". The flight was due at 7:39pm.

The judge first reminded the mother of her **duty of candour** — to tell the court what the absent
father would say. The likeliest story was a misunderstanding, and an order would be "a terrible
interruption of a splendid holiday"; the unlikely one, removal to a country outside the **1980
Hague Convention** with no treaty mechanism for return, "would likely be catastrophic". The balance
"strongly contended" for the order.

**23 July — the father heard (No 2).** He said he left written notice on the kitchen table and took
the passport in her sight; she denied both. No findings were made, but the risk remained —
sharpened by the father keeping X, who was not attending school. The order stays until **6 July
2027**; the judge urged mediation and suggested a Child Impact Report.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4** — the watchlist as a front-line child protection tool, and how fast the court can act.
- **fam.2.5** — an after-hours Duty Judge, an ex parte hearing by phone, a return hearing, then
  mediation encouraged.
- **State sovereignty** — without the Hague Convention, Australia can prevent removal but cannot
  compel a return: the limit of domestic family law at the border.
- **Effectiveness** — the law stopped a flight in 16 minutes; it could not return X to his mother or
  to school.

## Caution

Interim orders with **no findings** on the disputed facts or on the father's allegations about the
mother's care. Use it for process and risk, not for who was right. Two judgments combined in one
class summary, with quotes checked against the judgment texts.
