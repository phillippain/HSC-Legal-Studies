In July 2011 the Government signed an arrangement to send asylum seekers who arrived by boat to
**Malaysia**. The arrangement said it was **not legally binding**. Malaysia is not a party to the Refugee
Convention. Two asylum seekers on Christmas Island, one an unaccompanied 16-year-old from Afghanistan,
challenged it.

## Held (31 August 2011, by majority)

- Under s 198A the Minister could declare a country only if it was **legally bound**, by international or
  domestic law, to give asylum seekers access to effective procedures, to protect them while their claims
  were decided, and to protect recognised refugees. Malaysia was not. The declaration was **invalid**.
- An unaccompanied child could not be removed without the Minister's written consent as guardian.
- The Court gave no view on how Malaysia in fact treats asylum seekers.

## What happened next

In 2012 Parliament amended the Migration Act to remove those conditions, and offshore processing on Nauru
and Manus Island resumed.

## Use it for

- **exp.5.1 / exp.5.3** — domestic law giving effect to protection obligations, enforced by a court.
- **exp.6.1 and exp.6.2 together** — a court check that worked, then was legislated away.
- **chg.3.2** — a court decision as a trigger for legislative change.

## Caution

The case turned on the words of s 198A as it then stood. It did not decide that offshore processing breaches
the Refugee Convention.
