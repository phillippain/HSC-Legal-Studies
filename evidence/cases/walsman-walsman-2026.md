Married 2012, separated February 2021. X born 2015 with a neurological condition, later epilepsy, a
mild intellectual disability and an ASD/ADHD assessment; Y born 2018. A parenting plan put the
children **week-about**, and that ran for five years while disputes multiplied over schools, therapy,
extra-curricular activities and the role of the father's fiancée.

**By the hearing neither parent asked for joint decision-making.** Both accepted they could not
co-parent on major long-term issues, and each asked to be given sole responsibility.

## The pivotal moment

The family consultant recommended the **mother** have sole decision-making and that the children
live mainly with her. Under cross-examination she accepted that the benefits she had identified —
accountability, consistency, timely access to services, less conflict — flowed from **someone**
having sole responsibility, not from it being the mother.

When counsel reversed the "if" in her reasoning — if the *father* had sole responsibility, should
the children live with him? — she could not explain why her time recommendation should survive. The
judge found **"no rational basis"** for it [97].

Sole decision-making to the father; week-about time continues.

## Use it for

- **The changing nature of parental responsibility** after the 2024 reforms: responsibility is now
  *allocated*, and this shows a court allocating it against the expert's recommendation.
- **Decision-making and time are separate questions.** The children kept living week-about while one
  parent got the final say. Students routinely assume the two travel together; here the judgment
  turns on prising them apart.
- **Expert evidence is weighed, not followed.** A single expert's recommendation collapsed under
  cross-examination, and the court explained why. Good evidence for the adversary system doing what
  it is meant to do.
