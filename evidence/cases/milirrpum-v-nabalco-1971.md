The Yolngu clans of Yirrkala (whose 1963 bark petitions had already gone to the Commonwealth Parliament) sued to stop a bauxite mine on the Gove Peninsula, claiming the land was theirs under their own law.

## Held

- Blackburn J accepted on the evidence that the Yolngu had "a subtle and elaborate system highly adapted to the country in which the people led their lives" — a system of law.
- But he held he was bound by the doctrine of *terra nullius* as received in Australian law (*Cooper v Stuart* 1889): the relationship of the clans to land was spiritual, not proprietary, and the common law could not recognise it. The claim failed.

## What it drove

The loss triggered the Woodward Royal Commission (1973–74) and the *Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976* (Cth) — statutory land rights because the common law would not move — and the reasoning Brennan J overturned in *Mabo (No 2)* twenty-one years later.

## Use it for

- **chg.5.3** — a driver of change: a judicial defeat that exposed the gap and sent the Yolngu, and later the Meriam people, to parliament and back to the courts.
- **chg.5.2** — the doctrine of *terra nullius* operating on living people, not in the abstract.

## Caution

A Northern Territory Supreme Court decision, never appealed; it bound no other court and *Mabo* did not need to overrule it, only to reject its premise.
