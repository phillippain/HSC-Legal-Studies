Bale pleaded guilty at the first opportunity to supplying an above-indictable quantity of
methamphetamine (maximum 15 years) and a **commercial quantity of LSD** (maximum 20 years, standard
non-parole period 10 years), with two possession offences on a Form 1. Both parties agreed the
offending sat at the **lowest end** — drugs bought in bulk largely for personal use — and the Crown did
not oppose a non-custodial outcome.

## The sentence

- **25% discount** for the early plea (s 25D).
- **Subjective case**: no record, remorse, stable housing and work, admission to a withdrawal unit,
  counselling and Narcotics Anonymous, and leaving a violent, controlling relationship. Specific
  deterrence had "run its course".
- **s 5 gateway**: imprisonment only if no other penalty is appropriate — Crown, defence and judge all
  agreed it was not, despite the weight of general deterrence in drug cases.
- **Result**: a **two-year Community Correction Order** (s 8) with eight conditions under s 89, including
  inpatient withdrawal, drug treatment and abstention.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.6 (types of penalties)** — a CCO in a serious-sounding case; pair with *R v White* (CCO for
  manslaughter) and *R v Pullen* (ICO) for the community-based ladder.
- **crime.4.1 / crime.4.3** — maximum penalties and the SNPP as guideposts, the Form 1, the plea
  discount and mitigating factors in a few paragraphs. *Parente v R* (2017) ended the old rule that
  drug trafficking needed exceptional circumstances to avoid custody.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary** of brief ex tempore remarks: no case law is cited and the facts are
summarised. Because the Crown did not oppose a CCO, the penalty was not contested.
