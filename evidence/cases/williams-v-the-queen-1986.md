## Facts

Police arrested **Williams** at 6 a.m. on **17 May 1984** for burglaries at Scottsdale (Tasmania) but held and questioned him for hours about **26 other offences**. The trial judge excluded his confessions and he was acquitted of those counts. The Tasmanian Court of Criminal Appeal ordered a retrial.

## Held

The High Court **allowed the appeal** and set aside the order for retrial, so the acquittals stand. Three separate judgments:

- **Mason and Brennan JJ** and **Wilson and Dawson JJ**: detaining an arrested person in order to question or investigate is **unlawful**. Arrest is not an investigative tool, and the person must be taken before a justice as soon as practicable.
- **Gibbs CJ** held the Crown's appeal was not on a question of law alone and was misconceived.
- The sufficient basis for excluding the confessions is the unlawful detention.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.4 / crime.2.6**: arrest, detention and the rights of suspects. The limit on police time with a suspect is why statutory investigation periods now exist.
- **Balance** of effective investigation against the suspect's liberty.
- **Compliance by police**: the courts' response to unlawful conduct is to refuse to admit what it produced.

## Caution

The case pre-dates the Evidence Act s 138 and the statutory detention regimes (LEPRA Part 9). The printed header gives 1986 but a decision date of 26 August 1987: check before quoting a date. No hearing date in the file. Multiple judgments: teach the split, not one ratio.
