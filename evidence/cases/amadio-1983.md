Mr and Mrs Amadio, an elderly Italian-born couple with limited English, guaranteed their son's
company debts and mortgaged their property. They believed the exposure was limited to $50,000 for six
months. The bank knew the company was in serious trouble, and knew the parents were relying on their
son for information.

## Held

The guarantee and mortgage were **set aside** as **unconscionable dealing**. Where one party is under
a **special disadvantage** — age, language, lack of business knowledge, dependence on another for
advice — and the stronger party **knows or ought to know** of it, taking advantage of that position is
unconscionable and the transaction may be set aside.

## Use it for

- **Purchasing and securing shelter through credit** — most people acquire shelter through a mortgage,
  and this is the doctrine that limits what a lender may enforce against the home.
- **The equity/statute pairing**: *Amadio* is the common law standard; the *Contracts Review Act 1980*
  (NSW), applied in *Khoshaba*, is the statutory one. Showing that Parliament legislated a wider
  version of a judge-made rule is a Preliminary point (`ls.2.1`, `ls.4.3`) made with a shelter case.
- **Just laws** — the case is a clean illustration of formal equality (both parties signed) against
  substantive fairness (only one understood).
