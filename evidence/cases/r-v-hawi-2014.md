**Mahmoud ('Mick') Hawi**, a former president of the Comanchero Motorcycle Club's Sydney chapter, was convicted by a jury in 2011 of the murder of Anthony Zervas in the Sydney Airport brawl of 22 March 2009. He appealed and the Court of Criminal Appeal divided, so a retrial was ordered.

## Held

- On **23 June 2014** Harrison J granted bail (ABC News, 23 June 2014). Hawi's counsel applied under the new Act, which had commenced about a month earlier: **murder no longer carried a presumption against bail**.
- Justice Harrison accepted that some unacceptable risks existed but could be **"adequately mitigated by the imposition of appropriate conditions"**.
- Conditions: good behaviour; daily reporting to police; a curfew; no contact with Crown witnesses and listed people; no new passport; stay more than one kilometre from any airport or international departure point; a **$700,000 surety**.
- The Crown opposed bail, citing his active membership of the Comanchero club and a risk to witnesses (Sydney Criminal Lawyers, 6 July 2014).

## What followed

Hawi's bail and Fesus's a week earlier were two of the three high-profile decisions that preceded the Premier's review (Brown and Quilter, 2014). Hawi pleaded guilty to manslaughter on 4 September 2014 and was shot dead in 2018.

## Use it for

- **Law reform:** conditions can manage risk (the unacceptable risk test), but publicity drove reform.
- **Enforceability:** the conditions are detailed and enforceable; the surety is an incentive to comply.
