C was born in 2007. Her parents separated in 2009 and agreed on **equal shared parental
responsibility**, with C living primarily with her mother and spending alternate weekends and part of
the holidays with her father. Both re-partnered; the father's household included a stepson, E.

In **2014** it was established that **E's behaviour was a risk to C's safety**. Because E was **below
the age of criminal responsibility**, police could not charge him. The mother feared the father would
not protect C, and would conceal any further incident. She sought an order conditioning the father's
time on C never being in contact with E; the father sought contact subject to a safety plan.

## Held

**s 60CA** of the *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) makes the child's best interests paramount. The court
gave the mother **sole parental responsibility**, C to live with her, and the father time on alternate
Saturdays and in holidays **subject to an injunction prohibiting contact with E**.

## Use it for

- **The consequences of separation for children** (`fam.2.2`) — the presumption of equal shared
  parental responsibility is displaced by risk, and the order is shaped around the risk rather than
  around the parents' rights.
- **A risk the criminal law cannot touch.** E could not be prosecuted, so the only protective
  mechanism available was a family law injunction. That is a precise, unusual illustration of civil
  law doing what criminal law cannot (`crime.5.1`).
- Read with the **2023 Act**, which removed the presumption entirely: this case shows a court doing in
  2016 what the reform later made the default.
