In April 2022 **Michael O'Connell** drove off with Ms Jordan on the bonnet of his car; she fell and died. In a
circumstantial case, a **jury convicted him of murder** (reckless indifference to the probability of death); he was
sentenced to 15 years. In 2025 the **ACT Court of Appeal** (2–1, McCallum CJ dissenting) held the verdict
**unreasonable** and entered **manslaughter** (10 years). Both sides appealed.

## Three courts, three answers

| | Jury (2023) | ACT Court of Appeal (2025) | High Court (4 November 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verdict | Murder | Murder set aside; **manslaughter** entered, 2–1 | DPP appeal allowed **unanimously**; orders set aside |
| Reasoning | — | Jury's advantages could not resolve its doubt; speed critical | Majority failed to make full allowance for the jury's advantages (*M v The Queen*), and decided on a hypothesis not raised at trial |
| Substitution | — | Entered the lesser verdict the jury rejected | O'Connell's appeal on s 37O dismissed |
| Result | 15 years | 10 years | **Remitted**: the Court of Appeal must decide the unreasonable-verdict ground again |

The ABC headline says the murder conviction was "reinstated"; strictly, the unreasonable-verdict ground was **remitted,
not decided**.
