Two children, born 2011 and 2013. The father was **unrepresented**, applied orally on the first
morning of trial for an adjournment (refused), and had **not filed a trial affidavit** as directed —
so the Court proceeded under **r 10.27(1)(g)** of the Rules, hearing submissions from both parties
and from the **Independent Children's Lawyer**.

## The criminal history

- **Late 2011**, District Court of Queensland: guilty of **assault occasioning bodily harm, whilst
  armed, in company**.
- **Early 2023**, District Court of Queensland: pleaded guilty to various offences and was sentenced
  to **2 years and 6 months imprisonment** — including an offence under **s 315A of the *Criminal
  Code* (Qld), choking, suffocation or strangulation in a domestic setting**. By pleading guilty he
  admitted the conduct, and that it was associated domestic violence under the
  *Domestic and Family Violence Protection Act 2012* (Qld).
- **August 2023**: guilty of using a carriage service to menace, harass or cause offence.

The offences were against a **subsequent partner**, not the children's mother — which is the point
worth noticing: violence against a later partner is evidence of risk to these children.

Having heard him, Howard J concluded he **"shows no insight into the impact of his criminal
behaviour"**, and a report described his presentation as raising "significant concern in relation to
both risk and parenting capacity" while still showing "moments of warmth, affection".

## Orders

1. The children **spend no time with the father** and **have no communication** with him.
2. Leave under **s 114Q** to give the orders to relevant agencies.
3. **Injunctions under s 68B**: be of good behaviour towards the children; no contact by any means;
   not within **150 metres** of their residence; not within **50 metres** of the children; no
   contact through their school — **declared under s 68C(2) to be for their personal protection**,
   which is what makes them enforceable by arrest.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.2 (consequences of separation — children)** — the far end of the range. The *Family Law
  Act* starts from a child benefiting from a relationship with both parents; this is what it takes
  for a court to order **none at all**. Pair it with a shared-care case to show the spectrum.
- **fam.2.4 (domestic violence)** — three things at once: a criminal conviction for **choking**,
  which Queensland made a standalone offence precisely because strangulation predicts later
  homicide; **s 68B injunctions declared under s 68C(2)**, so police can arrest; and the finding
  that **lack of insight** is itself a risk factor.
- **Criminal and family law in the same facts** — the guilty plea did the evidentiary work in the
  family proceedings. Excellent for the relationship between the two jurisdictions, and for what a
  plea admits.
- **fam.2.5 / access to justice** — an unrepresented litigant who missed the filing directions, and
  the Court proceeding anyway under r 10.27(1)(g). Worth asking whether the outcome was right *and*
  whether the process was fair; the ICL is the counterweight.
- **crime.4.8 (post-sentencing considerations)** — the consequences of a sentence running on long
  after release, into a different court.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**, and Pt XIVB makes an identifying account an offence. This is **(No 2)** — the
adjournment refusal has its own reasons of 15 June 2026, not in the library. A **first-instance
decision on extreme facts**: do not generalise it into "violent parents get no contact", because the
Act requires an individual assessment and most cases produce supervised or limited time instead.
The criminal offences are described as the judgment records them; the details of the offending
against the later partner should not be elaborated. The children's views and the family report are
summarised only briefly here.
