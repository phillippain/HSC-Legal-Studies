## Facts

**Wei Tang** owned a licensed Melbourne brothel. Five Thai women had been "purchased" by a syndicate and held to a large "debt". She was convicted of five counts of **possessing** and five of **using** a slave under s 270.3(1)(a) of the *Criminal Code* (Cth). The Victorian Court of Appeal quashed the convictions ([2007], 29 June 2007).

## Held — 6 to 1 on the orders (Kirby J dissenting on jury directions)

- **Slavery** in Division 270 covers more than chattel slavery: it turns on the exercise of the powers attaching to the right of ownership.
- Division 270 is valid under the **external affairs power** (the slavery conventions).
- The prosecution did not have to prove Tang knew the source of her powers.
- Appeal allowed, convictions restored; the matter was remitted to the Court of Appeal on sentence.

## Use it for

- **crime.6.1**: slavery and trafficking in persons as a transnational crime that harms the individuals and the international community.
- **Domestic implementation** of international obligations by Commonwealth criminal law.

## Caution

Subject matter is distressing: keep discussion at the level of the legal test. The file has no hearing date, offence dates or sentencing reasons: the 10-year total sentence is known only from Kirby J quoting the Court of Appeal. Check later amendments to the slavery and trafficking offences before teaching current law.
