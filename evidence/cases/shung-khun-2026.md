Two brothers, X (born 2009) and Y (born 2014). The family moved to Australia in January 2020; the
father returned to China two months later. In 2021 the mother left the boys unsupervised on four
weekends and then for 6–7 days (she later completed a parenting course). On a family visit to China
in **July 2023** the father offered each boy a choice: X, 13, came back; Y, 8, stayed and was told
his mother would not trouble him again. The brothers were apart for **29 months**. In December 2025
the father brought Y to Australia, not knowing the mother had placed him on the airport watchlist;
in 2026 he took Y to the airport knowing they could not leave — an "avoidable and distressing
public confrontation" in which Y interpreted for the AFP. By July 2026 Y's anxiety scores were
extremely severe and he had messaged that he wanted to die.

## Held — 10 September 2026, after a four-day hearing with an ICL and a single expert

- Y's wish to return to China was "genuine and strongly held" — but discounted for his age, possible
  coaching and either-or thinking. It could not be determinative.
- "This case offers no easy solution. The evidence is finely balanced" (at [65]). What tipped it:
  "the mother's efforts to meet the needs of both children through education and ongoing
  psychotherapy" (at [69]) and the single expert's evidence that "unlike parenting relationships,
  sibling relationships have the potential to be lifelong sources of support and identity" (at [67]).
  The father showed limited insight — the airport episode, the denigration, the use of X as a "pawn".
- **Not a single case is cited**: 85 paragraphs decided on the statute and the evidence.
- **Orders**: both boys live with their mother, who has **sole parental responsibility** with
  **14 days' notice** to the father and consideration of his views; communication "at all reasonable
  times"; no denigration, including on social media; from 15 January 2027 the father may have up to
  7 days in each of three school holidays and 14 in summer; the father is **restrained from
  removing either child from Australia until he turns 18** and the AFP is *requested* to keep both
  on the **Family Law Watchlist**; family therapy; each party pays Legal Aid NSW $8,038.25 towards
  the ICL. Taking a child overseas while orders are in force is an offence (**s 65Y**), so the
  mother's own travel is expressly permitted on nine weeks' notice.
