- **1991–93:** eight women were attacked at knifepoint in Moore Park, the eastern suburbs and the
  inner west; media called the offender the "Night Stalker". The task force disbanded in July 1993
  after every suspect was eliminated.
- **2022:** a forensic review linked four assaults to one unknown male DNA profile; a national
  search pointed to a relative.
- **Feb 2024:** DNA from a fork and glass matched; the man, then 59, was arrested at Sydney
  International Airport and refused bail. He denied the offences. Suppression orders were lifted in
  May 2024.
- **23 Sept 2025:** guilty pleas to **13** offences in the Downing Centre Local Court (11 counts of
  aggravated sexual assault with a weapon, plus attempted aggravated sexual assault and assault
  with an act of indecency); **14** further offences on a **Form 1**; **nine** withdrawn (including
  armed robbery and detain for advantage). Charge counts ran 33 at arrest, 36 at plea.
- **19 Oct 2026:** at the sentence hearing before **Judge Pickering** a victim read her impact
  statement to him; the defence relied on a troubled upbringing and drug use; he addressed the court,
  apologised and admitted everything. Sentence is due in **December 2026**.

## Aggravating v mitigating, as reported

| | Towards a heavier sentence | Towards a lighter sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Weapon | Knife to the throat and ribs — s 21A(2)(c) | — |
| Planning | Judge: "premeditated knife-point rapes" — s 21A(2)(n) | Defence: the attacks gave him power he lacked as a child |
| Harm | VIS: decades of anxiety, terror, lost trust | Harm beyond the ordinary must be proved beyond reasonable doubt |
| Plea | Denied after arrest; resisted prosecution | Local Court pleas earn the mandatory discount |
| Remorse | Apology came 32 months after arrest | Apologised and admitted all — s 21A(3)(i) |
| Background | Judge called the defence position "fairly extreme" | Deprived upbringing, drug use (*Bugmy*) |
| Form 1 | 14 further offences add weight to deterrence and retribution | Sentence cannot exceed the principal offence's maximum (s 33(3)) |

The maximum for aggravated sexual assault (**s 61J**) in force in 1991–93 was **20 years**.

## Use it for

- **Historical offences** (`crime.4.1`, `crime.4.3`) — since 18 October 2022 (s 21B) courts use
  today's sentencing patterns, but the maximum penalty is the one in force when the offence was
  committed. Legal Aid NSW opposed the reform as risking harsh retrospective outcomes.
- **Victim impact statements** (`crime.4.4`) — harm described three decades on; s 30E requires a
  tendered statement to be considered; sexual-offence victims may read it in closed court.
- **Discounts and Form 1** (`crime.3.4`) — s 25D (25% for a Local Court plea, unless reduced for
  extreme culpability, s 25F); Form 1 offences are taken into account only if the offender admits
  them and asks (s 33(2)), and cannot be prosecuted later (s 35).
- **Investigation** (`crime.2.3`, `crime.2.4`) — familial DNA and covertly collected samples.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a news report of one hearing day, not a judgment: no sentence had been
imposed at 21 October 2026, the defence arguments are submissions, and the **Crown's submissions and
the agreed facts are not reported**. The 19 October article **gets the plea date wrong** ("last
month" — the pleas were September 2025) and describes the charges less precisely than the plea
coverage; published charge counts do not fully reconcile. The 25% discount depends on findings not
yet made. Check the remarks on sentence in December, and do not identify any victim.
