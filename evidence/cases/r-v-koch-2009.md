Koch severely beat his former partner. Police gathered extensive evidence and charged him with
**attempted murder**. The DPP then entered charge negotiations and accepted a plea to the much less
serious **malicious injury without intent**. He was sentenced to **four years**. The victim was **not
consulted**.

## The reform it produced

Public and media reaction — "Forced to take on the system", *SMH* — led NSW to amend the *Crimes
(Sentencing Procedure) Act 1999* through the **Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Amendment Act 2010
(NSW)**. Under **s 35A**, the prosecution must now **consult the victim** during charge negotiations in
prescribed matters.

## Use it for

- **Charge negotiation** — the standard advantages (speed, cost, a guaranteed conviction) set against
  what this case shows: crimes punished insufficiently, and a victim who learns the outcome rather
  than participates in it.
- **The role of the victim** (crime.4.4) — s 35A is a **consultation** right, not a veto. Whether that
  is meaningful is a good evaluative question, and the honest answer is that it changes the process
  and not necessarily the result.
- **Prosecutorial discretion**, which is largely unreviewable: no court ordered this outcome, and no
  court could have refused it.
