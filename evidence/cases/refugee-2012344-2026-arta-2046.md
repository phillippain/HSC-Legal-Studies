A divorced Sikh farmer from Punjab arrived with his adult son in October 2018 and applied for a
protection visa in January 2019: he was an Akali Dal leader facing attacks and false charges. At
interview in 2020 he said his lawyer had been "confused" — the real problem was cousins trying to
take his land. Refused in July 2020; review sought in August 2020. In October 2024 he spent 25 days
in India visiting his sister, and the same month the AAT was abolished and his review passed to the
ART. In 2025–26 new claims arrived by email — a false accusation over a village shooting, forming a
Khalistan party in Sydney — and at the hearing the claim became family association with a gangster
brother-in-law.

## Held — refusal affirmed, for him and for his son

- **ss 5H, 5J** (refugee test): political activism and the land dispute not accepted — the land
  claim was never mentioned at hearing, and he still owns and leases the land.
- **s 367A**: claims first raised on review without explanation attract an adverse inference. His
  account of a secret trip home was "contradictory, far-fetched, and totally unconvincing".
- DFAT country information (applied under **Direction No 84**): Sikhs in India face "a low level of
  official and societal discrimination and violence"; the chance of harm on return was "remote".
- **s 36(2)(aa)** complementary protection: the same facts and, under *MIAC v SZQRB* [2013], the
  same test — no real risk of significant harm (defined in s 5(1) by reference to **ICCPR art 7**).

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1** — the treatment of refugees seen from inside an individual claim.
- **hr.2.9 / hr.2.7** — protection obligations incorporated into the *Migration Act 1958*: the
  refugee test codified in ss 5H–5LA, ICCPR art 7 written into the definitions.
- **Effectiveness** — rigorous and fair (an interpreter, a full hearing, late material considered),
  but seven years from application to decision.
- **Sovereignty** — Australia decides whom it protects, through domestic law and DFAT's assessment.

## Caution

A decision that turns on **credibility** — it is not evidence that claims of this kind generally
fail. The 1951 Refugees Convention is the source of the refugee test but is not named in the
decision; students supply that link. Summary prepared for class from the statement of reasons.
