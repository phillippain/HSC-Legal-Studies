Ms Fowler and Mr Alecci, husband and wife, are members of a registered club. In **2020** Ms Fowler
complained to Anti-Discrimination NSW alleging sex discrimination and sexual harassment, primarily
against the club's chief executive; Mr Alecci acted as her advocate. That complaint **settled in
2021** under a confidential deed.

In late 2025 and early 2026 they complained again — Ms Fowler of discrimination on the grounds of
sex and marital status and of **victimisation** under s 50, Mr Alecci of victimisation. The President
accepted the victimisation complaints and **declined to accept the discrimination complaints under
s 89B(2)(a)** — a decision the Tribunal has **no power to review** (s 89B(4); *Wecker*). On 30 April
2026 the President's delegate declined the victimisation complaints as **lacking in substance**.

**Leave refused** under s 96(1).

## The pathway, which is the point

| Step | Provision |
|---|---|
| A person complains to the President | s 87A(1)(a)(i) |
| The President accepts it, and must then investigate | ss 89B, 90(1) |
| If satisfied it is **lacking in substance**, the President may decline it | s 92(1)(a)(i) |
| "Lacking in substance" means no factual basis, or **not reasonably arguable** | *Langley v Niland* (1981); *Chalker* |
| At the complainant's written request, it is referred to the Tribunal | s 93A |
| It cannot proceed **without the Tribunal's leave** | **s 96(1)** |

Section 96(1) is an **unfettered discretion** (*Jones v Ekermawi*; *Ekermawi*), exercised with regard
to the purpose of the Act and guided by the fact that **refusing leave finally determines the
complainant's rights** under the scheme. On a leave application the Tribunal may take the
applicant's evidence at its highest.

## Use it for

- **How statute law actually protects rights in NSW** — not by a court declaring one, but by a
  complaints pathway with two filters before a hearing. Every answer that says the
  *Anti-Discrimination Act* "protects" people should say what the protection requires.
- Decided the same day as *Haji Zadeh* and *Jones*: **three complaints, three grounds, three
  refusals**. Together they are the strongest available evidence on enforceability and access to
  justice in anti-discrimination law.
- Both applicants were **self-represented**, against a firm of solicitors.

## Caution

Refusing leave is not a finding that discrimination or victimisation occurred or did not — it is a
decision that the complaint was not reasonably arguable. Say that precisely, and the point about
access gets stronger rather than weaker.
