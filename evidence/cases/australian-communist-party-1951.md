The *Communist Party Dissolution Act 1950* (Cth) dissolved the Communist Party, allowed the
Governor-General to declare a person a communist, and **recited** that these things were necessary for
the defence of the Commonwealth. The recitals were the only evidence offered for the power.

## Held

Invalid, 6–1. A law cannot **bootstrap** itself into constitutional validity by asserting the facts
that would support it — whether a law is within power is for the **courts** to decide on the facts,
not for Parliament to declare. Dixon J's formulation is the one to quote: the rule of law **"forms an
assumption"** on which the Constitution is framed.

## Use it for

- **The rule of law** (`ls.1.7`) as something with legal force in Australia rather than a slogan:
  government is subject to the law, and the question of what the law allows is decided by an
  independent court.
- **The role of the High Court** (`ls.2.8`) and **separation of powers** (`ls.2.7`) — the case is the
  clearest example of the judiciary as a check on the legislature, and it was decided in a period of
  genuine public fear, which is the point. Rights protection is only tested when it is unpopular.
- Against **anarchy and tyranny** (`ls.1.8`): the Act is what the slide toward arbitrary government
  looks like in a democracy — passed by an elected parliament, in ordinary form, and stopped only by
  a court.
