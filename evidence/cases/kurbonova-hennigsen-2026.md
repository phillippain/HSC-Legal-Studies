Two Pakistani parents and their sons, born 2020 and 2022, all in Australia on temporary visas — the
children on their mother's student visa. Pakistani courts had already decided guardianship under the
*Guardians and Wards Act 1890*, while the father ran parenting proceedings here; there were interim
orders, an airport watchlist listing, and hearings missed on both sides of the world. A protection
visa claim was pending.

## Held — jurisdiction declined; the children may leave

- **s 69E** can confer jurisdiction on the Court and **Pt XIIIAA** still allows it to decline to
  exercise it. The children were **habitually resident** elsewhere; the pending protection visa
  claim "does not establish that the children are owed protection obligations, nor does it determine
  habitual residence".
- The **1996 Child Protection Convention** allocates jurisdiction between signatory states, but
  **Pakistan is not a signatory** — so the reciprocal machinery did not apply and the outcome rested
  on **judicial comity** and s 157 recognition of the foreign orders.
- Orders: the father's applications dismissed; under **s 65Y** the mother is at liberty to leave
  Australia with the children; the AFP asked to remove them from the **Family Law Airport
  Watchlist** forthwith. The interim orders and the child impact report ordered in November 2025
  went with them.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.2** — jurisdiction in its hardest form: two sovereign courts with power over the same
  children.
- **hr.2.1 / hr.2.3** — state sovereignty resolved by comity rather than treaty; compare
  *Curtin & Bush*, where the absence of the **1980** Hague Convention cut the other way.
- **Law reform** — the judge notes there is "little case law on s 111CD(1)(f)" and few cases about
  parents on student and bridging visas: migration patterns outrunning the drafted law.
- **Shelter** — "The mother rents, but there is nothing in that. She and the children have to live
  somewhere": renting treated as normal, and housing precarity tied to migration status.

## Caution

Delivered ex tempore, and about **which court decides**, not about what is best for these children —
the Australian interim orders were wiped away after months of litigation. Summary prepared for class
from the judgment; quotes checked against the judgment text.
