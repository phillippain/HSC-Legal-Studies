**Prabhjot Singh** was tried by a jury at Penrith (25 March–14 April 2025) and **acquitted of all three charges**. He applied for a **costs certificate** under the *Costs in Criminal Cases Act 1967* (NSW). At the hearing on **21 November 2025** there was **no interpreter**, and the application was dismissed; on **4 December 2025** the judge said he could not reopen it (*functus officio*). He pursued two routes, representing himself with a Punjabi interpreter: **judicial review** in the Court of Appeal and a **leave appeal** in the Court of Criminal Appeal. Both were dismissed on **5 November 2026**.

## What the Court decided

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Should the Court entertain judicial review when leave to appeal under *Criminal Appeal Act 1912* s 5F(3) was available? | **No.** Relief is discretionary and ordinarily refused where there is an "alternative and equally effective remedy" (at [42]); *Barr v DPP*, *Quach v NCAT*, *Makowska* applied |
| Did the lack of an interpreter deny procedural fairness? | **No.** He had counsel and a solicitor and "is bound by the forensic decisions made by his legal representatives" (at [113]); his everyday English let him raise concerns |
| Was any error material? | **No.** Costs, hardship and police conduct do not go to whether prosecuting was reasonable (at [119]); it was word against word |
| Could the judge reopen the decision? | **No.** Once the order was perfected there was "no statutory power" to reopen (at [139]); correcting typing errors is different |

**Orders**: the Amended Summons (judicial review) dismissed with costs; leave to appeal granted on grounds 1–4 only; the Notice of Appeal dismissed. A remittal to a different judge was not ordered.

## The test for a costs certificate

Under ss 2 and 3(1)(a) a certificate may issue only if a **fully informed prosecutor would not have found it reasonable** to prosecute. The Director-General, not the court, fixes the amount if a certificate issues (s 4), so **hardship is not part of the s 3 test**. An acquittal alone is not enough.

## Caution

A **class case summary**; the acquittals are not questioned and this is a costs and procedure decision. The charges were sexual offences against a former wife — **no detail is given and none should be added**. The header and paras 11 and 13 name the respondents inconsistently, some pre-trial dates in the file conflict, and Ch 4 of the Criminal Appeal Act is listed but not discussed. The Court did not decide the position of an unrepresented party. Check the judgment before quoting paragraph numbers.
