X, born 2017, was conceived through **home insemination** using donor sperm after his mothers
jointly decided to have a child. Ms Valeria carried and gave birth to him and — on the evidence, so
she could claim single-parent Centrelink payments — was listed as his **only** parent on the birth
certificate. Ms Ashtoreth was present at the birth, cut the umbilical cord, was called "Mumma" by
agreement, and was X's primary carer during the relationship.

After final separation in 2023 X lived primarily with Ms Valeria, whose circumstances deteriorated
sharply: periods of **homelessness** (a tent, later a converted shipping container), a school
attendance rate that collapsed to **11.8%** in 2024, and repeated difficulty administering his
medication. X has autism and ADHD and receives NDIS funding.

## Held — final parenting orders

- **Declared a legal parent under s 69VA**, on the joint decision to conceive, presence at the
  birth and day-to-day care — that is, on function and intention rather than on the register.
- **Joint** decision-making ordered, not the sole decision-making sought.
- X **stays with Ms Valeria**, whose circumstances had markedly improved by trial.
- The alleged **unacceptable risk of neglect was not established**.

## Use it for

- **Recognition of same-sex relationships** and **birth technologies** together: informal, at-home
  conception outside any clinic, and a legal parent recognised years later by a court.
- **Alternative family relationships** — the birth certificate was wrong from the day it was signed,
  for a welfare-payment reason, and it took a court to fix it. A good argument for a simpler
  administrative route.
- **Risk assessed at the date of trial, not at the date of the application.** Her circumstances had
  improved, and that mattered more than what they had been. This is the answer to any argument that
  a parent's past determines the outcome.
- **Shelter crosses into Family here** — homelessness as the reason a child's schooling collapsed.
  One of the few pieces of evidence in the collection that belongs squarely in two topics.
