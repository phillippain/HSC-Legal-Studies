In a pending parenting and property case, a self-represented mother filed five interlocutory applications. She sought to remove the Independent Children’s Lawyer, compel counselling, set aside consent orders for sale of the home, and gain maintenance, sole schooling decisions and money. On 22 September 2026 Jarrett J dismissed them, apart from limited mortgage orders. This is an interim decision, not a trial.

## Use it for

- Courts and dispute resolution: the role of an Independent Children’s Lawyer, and when a court refuses counselling and mediation.
- Cooperation and conflict resolution: the court had power to order counselling but held it unsuitable and unwanted.
- Divorce and separation: interim maintenance, the mortgage, the home and supervised time after separation.
- Parental responsibility: sole decision-making for schooling refused; one parent is not excluded merely for disagreeing.
- Effectiveness: interim processes protect fairness, but self-represented parties struggled to supply evidence.

## Cautions

This is an interlocutory (interim) judgment by a single judge at first instance, not a trial: disputed facts, the cryptocurrency, the offset account and long-term schooling were left for the trial. The file labels paragraphs twice (‘1. 3’); the real number is the second. The catchwords cite s 13(1) but the reasons cite s 13C(1). Paragraph 70 says the father lost his employment, yet paragraph 40 describes him as employed; the mother’s monthly claim ($3,000 or $13,000) is unexplained; and the contact-visit affidavit is dated both 2025 and 2026. No cases are cited. Care line: allegations about mental health and children’s distress; keep to the legal test.
