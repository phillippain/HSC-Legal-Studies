The case the surrogacy dot point has been waiting for.

**Ms Taggart** cannot carry a pregnancy. In early 2023 she and her husband **Mr Miller** entered an
**altruistic surrogacy agreement** with **Ms Gowden — Mr Miller's sister** — using an **anonymous
egg donor** and Mr Miller's sperm. All three did everything the law asks: **counselling,
psychological assessment, independent legal advice**, and approval from the **Patient Review Panel**
under the ***Assisted Reproductive Treatment Act 2008* (Vic)**.

In **January 2026** Ms Gowden sent a **text message** saying she was withdrawing from the surrogacy
and would raise the baby herself. **X was born in 2026.** The intended parents **found out
indirectly, through family**, and **had not met him** at the hearing. Ms Gowden was **breastfeeding**
and refused to hand him over. **All three parties are Aboriginal.** They live **6–7 hours apart**.

## The orders (interim)

- **Granted** — X to **live with Ms Taggart and Mr Miller** from 15 May 2026, with **joint
  decision-making responsibility** to both applicants.
- **Refused** — an **interim parentage order** for Mr Miller: **s 69VA is worded as a final order
  only**.
- **Ordered** — Ms Gowden to provide X's **certified birth certificate** within 7 days.
- **Conditions** — Mr Miller must produce a **negative hair follicle drug test**; until then
  **Ms Taggart must be in substantial attendance** when X is in his care.
- Ms Gowden retains **regular short supervised visits**. A **family report** was due September 2026
  and a **five-day trial** listed for 28 September 2026. An **Indigenous liaison officer** was
  appointed.

## The principles the Court applied

- **Best interests (s 60CA)** — paramount; the Court weighed X's safety, developmental needs,
  **cultural identity** and each party's capacity.
- **s 60CC**, including the **additional considerations for an Aboriginal child** — cultural
  identity, connection to country and community.
- **Interim versus final orders** — at an interim hearing the Court **cannot resolve disputed facts**;
  it makes temporary arrangements on the available evidence to protect the child until trial.
- **Unacceptable risk** (*Isles & Nelissen* [2022]) — risk assessment is a **predictive exercise
  separate from fact-finding**, so the Court must act on possible risks even without proof.
- **Surrogacy agreements are not enforceable as contracts.** Altruistic surrogacy is lawful; the
  Court decides by **the child's best interests, not the agreement**.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.3 — the single most important proposition in the topic, demonstrated.** "A surrogacy
  agreement is not enforceable" is a line every student writes. Here is what it **means**: every
  statutory safeguard was satisfied — counselling, assessment, independent advice, Patient Review
  Panel approval — and when the surrogate changed her mind by **text message**, none of it bound her.
  The intended parents had to litigate for a child they had never met.
- **Why the law is built that way (fam.t.values)** — a surrogate cannot be compelled to surrender a
  child, because that is the line between surrogacy and sale. The honest counter is in the same facts:
  the intended parents, the egg donor arrangement and the genetic father were all left without a
  remedy. Argue both.
- **s 69VA is a final order only** — a precise, markable point. The Court could decide **where X
  lives** immediately but could not declare **who his parents are** until trial. Parentage and
  parenting are different questions, which is the same gap that runs through
  `international-surrogacy-parentage-declarations` and *Masson v Parsons*.
- **Altruistic surrogacy inside a family (fam.1.4)** — the surrogate is the genetic father's
  **sister**, so the child's aunt gave birth to him. Read it with
  `telegraph-2025-surrogate-grandmothers`: keeping surrogacy in the family is what the law
  encourages, and it is exactly what makes a breakdown unbearable.
- **An Aboriginal child in a surrogacy dispute (fam.3.4 / hr.2.9)** — s 60CC's additional
  considerations and an **Indigenous liaison officer** appointed. A rare intersection, and a strong
  answer to how the law responds to cultural identity. Pair with `nit-2024-family-is-culture`.
- **Interim decision-making under uncertainty (inv.4.1)** — a newborn moved from the woman
  breastfeeding him to people he had never met, on **interim** evidence, with supervised visits back
  and a trial four months away. Use it for the limits of what a court can do quickly, and for
  *Isles & Nelissen* on predicting risk without proving facts.
- **Conditions as risk management (fam.2.2)** — a **negative hair follicle drug test** before
  unsupervised care. Specific, checkable, and the same technique as the sobriety condition in
  *Escarro & Kopp*.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB applies, and this is an unusually identifiable fact pattern — a brother and
sister, one surrogacy, one newborn — so **do not speculate about identities** and keep the discussion
to the legal issues. The library document is a **case study card**, not the judgment; verify anything
load-bearing, including the s 69VA point, against the decision.

**These are interim orders.** Nothing here is final: the **family report** and the **five-day trial
in September 2026** would decide parentage and long-term arrangements, and **the outcome is not
recorded** — check it before writing about what happened to X. The Court **resolved no disputed
facts**, so Ms Gowden's reasons, Mr Miller's **history of mental health issues and cannabis use**, and
Ms Gowden's **complex trauma history** are matters raised, not findings; the drug-test condition is
risk management, not a finding of current use. Ms Gowden has **six children** and was **breastfeeding
X**: the human weight of this is on all sides, and a classroom resource should say so rather than
casting anyone as the villain. The statutory scheme here is **Victorian** (*Assisted Reproductive
Treatment Act 2008* (Vic), Patient Review Panel); NSW's equivalents are the *Surrogacy Act 2010* (NSW)
and the *Assisted Reproductive Technology Act 2007* (NSW), with no Patient Review Panel — check the
scheme for the jurisdiction you are writing about.
