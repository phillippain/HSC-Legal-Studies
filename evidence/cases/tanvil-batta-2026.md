A divorced couple; the wife lives with their children, 11 and 13, in a strata apartment registered
in the husband's sole name. Three family violence intervention orders followed separation in 2022.
She alleges physical, verbal, financial, sexual and psychological abuse. In one week of September
2024 he moved $412,000 to his mother's account and left a new company out of his financial
statement; from August 2025 he stopped paying the mortgage. Then the owners corporation ordered the
balcony re-waterproofed within 28 days, and he applied — a second time — to have the home sold.

## Held — no sale, no transfer; the wife controls the home's upkeep

- ***Bearup & Bearup*** (1993): a pre-trial sale of the family home demands "the utmost caution".
- No need to sell: she had paid the mortgage since March 2026, the arrears of $7,896 were "relatively
  modest", and there was no threat of foreclosure. A sale would favour him, leaving "very little
  non-superannuation property to satisfy any claim of the wife" if he dissipated the proceeds.
- No transfer either: too much was disputed, and her $280,000 conditional finance could not
  discharge a $296,546 loan.
- **s 43(1)(ca)** — orders giving her control protect against economic abuse and "the threat of
  loss of accommodation for the wife and children" [79].

He pays the arrears within seven days and signs whatever she needs for the owners corporation,
trades and insurer; she pays the mortgage, levies and repairs, recoverable at trial.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.4** — property orders used as a family violence protection, without any finding on the
  allegations: the law acting on *risk*.
- **shel.2.1 and shel.3.1** — purchase finance at the point of separation: sole registered
  proprietorship, mortgage default, and a working single mother on about $90,000 who could not
  refinance.
- **shel.3.3** — family violence as a pathway to homelessness, and a court naming it.
- **Compliance** — the s 71B disclosure duty, an undisclosed company, a strata rectification notice
  with a refused extension.

## Caution

An **interim** decision with no findings on the violence; the final hearing may come out
differently. The legal remedy secured occupancy; it could not supply the money to keep the home long
term. Summary prepared for class from the judgment, with quotes checked against the judgment text.
