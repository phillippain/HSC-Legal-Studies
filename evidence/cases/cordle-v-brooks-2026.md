At a Wurgabup Rifle Club (WA) meeting on 7 November 2020, the Treasurer made an Australia Day remark
and another member replied; Mr Cordle, a member, complained they were racist. His complaint to the
**Australian Human Rights Commission** was terminated in November 2021 because conciliation could not
succeed, and he sued under *Racial Discrimination Act 1975* (Cth) **s 18C** (offensive conduct because of
race) and **s 18E** (vicarious liability), representing himself. One respondent, Mr Brooks, died in 2022.
The respondents applied for **summary dismissal**.

## Held — in part

- **Against Mr Brooks: dismissed.** The claim had no reasonable prospects once he died — relief against
  his estate was futile — so he was removed.
- **Against the club and the other two: continues.** The meeting was convened by the club and the
  remarks begun by its Treasurer, so s 18E liability was possible. Remarks about Aboriginal Australians
  can reach people of colour generally, and an applicant need not belong to the targeted group —
  applying the Full Court's 2026 *Hanson v Faruqi* test.
- Defective pleadings and delay were not enough; summary dismissal turns on substance.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.9 (statute law)** — the full civil pathway for racial hatred: conduct → AHRC complaint →
  termination → Federal Circuit Court; s 18C is unlawful, not criminal.
- **hr.2.11 / exp.6.2 (barriers to justice)** — nearly six years and no trial; a self-represented
  applicant against counsel and around $80,000 in claimed legal costs. Damages in comparable single-insult
  cases were $750 and $2,000.

## Caution

An **interlocutory** ruling: **no finding that s 18C was breached** has been made against anyone. The
$80,000 was asserted by counsel, not proved. A class evidence summary.
