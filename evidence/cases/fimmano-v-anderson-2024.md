A tenant of a Pyrmont studio complained to NSW Fair Trading about repairs and told his landlords' agent he would apply to the Tribunal. A termination notice under s 85 of the *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* (NSW) followed. NCAT declared it a **retaliatory notice** (s 115), ordered the landlords to provide an oven that reaches 200 degrees and to reinstate dimmer switches, and found the rent **excessive** under s 44 because facilities had been withdrawn (refund $2,687.27).

## Held

The Appeal Panel (Dr R Dubler SC and L Wilson, Senior Members) **refused leave**, rejected the new evidence and **dismissed the appeal**. An appeal is not a chance to run the case again. The landlords paid the tenant's costs of **$112.80** because the appeal had no tenable basis.

## Use it for

- **Leasing and protection of tenants**: repair duties (the oven and dimmers) and retaliation, with the tenant's Fair Trading complaint as the trigger (`shel.2.2`, `shel.2.3`, `shel.2.8`).
- **Courts and tribunals**: NCAT and its internal Appeal Panel, the leave test and the finality of litigation (`shel.2.5`, `shel.2.6`).
- **Effectiveness and compliance**: the remedies worked, but the oven fault dated from 2019 and the orders came in 2023.

## Caution

The file gives the notice date as both 20 May and 20 June 2023, and the excessive rent period as ending "24 October 2024" (an apparent slip). The reasons are joint, so do not attribute a view to one member. The case is about amenity, not affordability: s 44 does not test the tenant's ability to pay.
