A landholder pleaded guilty at the first opportunity to two counts of clearing native vegetation in a
regulated rural area (s 60N, *Local Land Services Act 2013* (NSW)), about 545.6 ha in May–June 2023 and
399.3 ha in September–October 2023, far beyond what he had notified. He also admitted a Form 1 offence
of knowingly damaging the habitat of a threatened species (s 2.4, *Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016*).

## Held — Pritchard J

- The harm was **of some seriousness but not substantial**, so the s 21A(2)(g) aggravating factor was
  not engaged; no practical measures were taken and he had complete control.
- The offending was **planned and for financial gain**; the s 60N offences were at the lower end of
  moderate objective seriousness.
- A 25% discount for the early plea took $120,000 and $180,000 to **$90,000 and $135,000**.
  Half of each fine goes to the prosecutor, he pays costs, and notices are published in four newspapers.
- General deterrence requires penalties that make "the financial cost ... of clearing native vegetation
  illegally outweigh the commercial gain".

## Use it for

- **crime.4.3 and crime.4.6** — factors in sentencing, and a fines-only outcome with ancillary orders.
- **dis.1.3** — s 60N is a strict-liability regulatory offence, yet planning still raises seriousness.
- **Compliance and effectiveness** — can a fine be large enough to deter a profit-driven offence?

## Caution

The judgment's area figures conflict (944.9 ha is the offence total). The offender's means to pay are
not in the file. The Form 1 offence is taken into account, not punished separately.
