The couple married in **2003**. They separated in 2010, reconciled in **January 2012**, separated
again in **May 2012**, applied jointly for divorce in **September 2012**, and then **reconciled again
in December 2012** and resumed living as husband and wife. A divorce order was made in April 2013 by
the Federal Magistrates Court. They appealed.

## Held

The divorce was **not valid**. Under the *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) a divorce requires that the
parties have lived **separately and apart for at least 12 months** with **no reasonable likelihood of
resuming married life**. These parties had not, and had demonstrated the capacity to reconcile. They
remained legally married.

## Use it for

- **Divorce** (`fam.2.1`) — the single ground, **irretrievable breakdown**, proved only by the
  twelve-month separation. This case shows the requirement is substantive, not a formality, and that
  the court will look at what actually happened rather than what the application says.
- **No-fault divorce** in practice: the court asked nothing about who was at fault, only whether the
  separation had lasted. That is the 1975 reform doing exactly what it was designed to do.
- **Separation under one roof**, and reconciliation of **less than three months** not breaking the
  period, are the two refinements worth naming beside it.
