An Indian man claimed he feared his in-laws; at the hearing he said he feared his wife's ex-partner, and raised a shooting not in
his written application. He also claimed stress that was not a diagnosed condition. A postponement was refused.

## What the Tribunal decided

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| The changed account | An invention |
| The late claim | Implausible — s 367A allows an unfavourable inference from a claim not raised before the delegate decided |
| Evidence | A neighbour's statutory declaration and a video never provided were given no weight |
| Refugee test / complementary protection | No real chance of persecution and no real risk of significant harm — the same standard (*SZQRB*) |

The refusal was **affirmed**. The Tribunal must follow Ministerial Direction 113 and DFAT country information.

- **hr.2.11 / hr.3.1** — a tribunal testing claims hard; credibility decides the outcome.
- **Year 11** — barriers to justice: evidence, language, delay and proof of a medical condition (exp.6.2), with a fair hearing,
  an interpreter and reasons.
