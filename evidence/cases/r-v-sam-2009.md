Gloria Mary Sam was born healthy in July 2001 and diagnosed with eczema that November. Her father
was a practising **homeopath but not a medical practitioner**. A nurse at the early childhood centre
noted the worsening eczema and advised a skin specialist; the advice was not acted on. In January
2002 a doctor at the clinic where the father worked called the rash **the most serious case he had
ever seen** and wrote an urgent dermatology referral. The parents did not take her. Evidence at trial
showed marks from head to toe and a child in constant pain. **Gloria died at nine months.**

## Held

Both parents were convicted of **manslaughter by criminal negligence** — non-parole periods of
**six years** (father) and **four years** (mother).

## Use it for

- **The rights and obligations of parents** (`fam.1.5`) at their outer limit: parental responsibility
  under the *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) includes the obligation to ensure a child receives **adequate
  medical treatment**, and failing it is not merely a family law matter — it is a crime.
- **Where family law and criminal law meet.** Parental autonomy over a child's medical care is wide,
  and it ends where the risk of death becomes obvious to a reasonable person. That is the line
  criminal negligence draws.
- Pair it with the **court-ordered treatment** cases (*Kiszko*, the Jehovah's Witness transfusion
  case) to show the two ways the law intervenes: authorising treatment in advance, or punishing the
  failure afterwards. The first protects the child; the second is too late.
