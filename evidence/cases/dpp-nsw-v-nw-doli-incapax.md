NW was taken into his grandmother's care in May 2014. He had a pattern of indecent assaults and
received more than eight months of specialised counselling for young people who sexually harm
others. During counselling he said that indecently harming someone was **"never the right thing to
do"**, promised not to do it again, apologised and cried. He reoffended.

In February 2015, at **13 years and 1 month**, he was accused of indecent assault on a child under
five. The magistrate found he **knew what he was doing was wrong**, so the presumption of *doli
incapax* was rebutted and he was held legally accountable.

## Why it matters

Children **under 10** cannot be held criminally responsible at all in NSW (s 5 *Children (Criminal
Proceedings) Act 1987*): the law presumes they cannot form *mens rea*. Between **10 and 14** the
presumption of *doli incapax* applies but is **rebuttable** — the prosecution must prove the child
understood that the act was seriously wrong, not merely naughty.

What rebutted it here was **the child's own words in a therapeutic setting**. That is the point
worth writing about: counselling designed to help him understand the wrongfulness of his conduct
produced the evidence that made him criminally responsible for it.

## Use it for

- **The age of criminal responsibility**, with the mechanism shown rather than asserted. Most answers
  state the presumption; few show it being rebutted, and fewer show what rebuts it.
- **Balancing rights**: a five-year-old complainant, a 13-year-old defendant, and a system trying to
  rehabilitate and to hold accountable with the same facts.
- Against the **UN Committee on the Rights of the Child's** recommendation that Australia raise the
  minimum age to 14 — this is the strongest case *for* keeping a rebuttable presumption rather than a
  bright line, and it should be met head-on rather than avoided.
