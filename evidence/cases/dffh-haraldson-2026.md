Two boys, six and four. Their parents married in Country B in 2017; a report of family violence in
late 2022 (an assault on the mother "while she was holding Y, who was an infant", coercive control)
sent the family to a refuge, and a final intervention order ran from early 2023 to early 2024. In
2025 police found the mother dead at home with both children there; the father was charged with her
murder and has been in custody since. Six out-of-home placements followed. The maternal grandmother
and uncle flew in, arranged the funeral, and were assessed as long-term carers by a departmental
officer who travelled to Country B. The Victorian department and the relatives filed together on
**20 July 2026**; the father, served and advised by legal aid, filed nothing.

## Held — 27 August 2026, 38 days after filing

- The father has **no parental responsibility**, no time and no communication; **s 68B** restraints
  bar him from the children's home, school or childcare and from contact "by any means whatsoever",
  including social media and gaming apps.
- **Parental responsibility to the grandmother and uncle** (s 61D), for major long-term and
  day-to-day decisions; the children live with them in Country B and may be removed from Australia.
- Under **s 11 of the *Australian Passports Act 2005*** travel documents may issue without a
  parent's signature; the orders must be registered in the Country B court within 60 days; the
  Department may give the orders to the Children's Court, the Passport Office, consulates, schools
  and health services.
- *Allesch v Maunz* (2000) 203 CLR 172: the opportunity to be heard is what must be given — "and he
  has had that". The Court need not wait indefinitely for a party who will not use it.

Still waiting at judgment: an Overseas Citizenship Card, about six weeks in processing — "what is
standing between the children being able to leave Australia and joining their family in Country B"
(at [95]).

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the issue in its starkest form: a state department as
  applicant, an emergency protection history, six placements in a year, kinship carers found
  overseas. The judge: "Sadly, but not surprisingly, the children are now on their sixth out of
  home care placement since the mother's death" (at [31]).
- **fam.1.2 / ls.2.6 (state and federal jurisdiction)** — the join made visible: the state
  investigated, removed, placed and assessed; only the Commonwealth court could allocate parental
  responsibility, authorise passports and permit departure. HSC 2024 Q27(a) exactly.
- **fam.3.2** — parental responsibility removed from a surviving parent and vested in a grandmother
  and uncle, covering religion, culture, identity documents and relocation.
- **Effectiveness** — the Critical Incident List as a court's own reform, no legislation required:
  "to get litigants in and out of the Court process as quickly as a child's best interests will
  allow" (at [92]). The delay that remained was administrative, not legal.
- **hr.2.7 / CROC** — s 60B expressly gives effect to the Convention; the children's rights reached
  them through statute, with citizenship and a consular card as the rights machinery.

## Caution

**Unopposed** — the father filed nothing, so no finding was contested; the case is authority for how
a court proceeds in that situation, not for any contested proposition. The children were too young
to express views ("this is not really about the children's views"). The murder charge is an
allegation; nothing here decides it. Pseudonyms under s 114Q(2) apply and Part XIVB makes
identifying publication an offence. Summary prepared for class from the judgment; the citation is
taken from the summary and was not independently re-checked here (AustLII and Jade are not
reachable from this session).
