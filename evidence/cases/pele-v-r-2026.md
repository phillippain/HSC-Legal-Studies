Pele was tried in October 2024 for specially aggravated kidnapping and aggravated break and enter
(*Crimes Act* ss 86(3), 112(3)). During deliberations the jury sent a note with four questions about
the element of acting "in company", in three sets of handwriting. Defence counsel argued the questions
mirrored Court of Criminal Appeal authority on joint enterprise and "had to have been" the product of
research; the prosecutor accepted research was "a possibility". The judge declined to examine the
foreperson and found the questions consistent with lay jurors grappling with the element. Pele was
convicted (aggregate 6 years 3 months, non-parole 3 years 9 months).

## Held — appeal dismissed

- Both sides accepted the High Court's **"double might"** test from *HCF v The Queen* [2023] HCA 35:
  might a fair-minded observer apprehend the jury might not have decided on the evidence?
- But the test needs **facts found on the evidence first**. Unlike *Hoang* (a juror's admitted Google
  search) or *HCF* (a juror who researched penalties), there were no extrinsic facts — no notes, no
  sheriff's inquiry — only the questions, which were consistent with ordinary reasoning. What the
  foreperson might have said was "mere conjecture".
- Chen J added that the appeal sought to outflank a finding it accepted.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary**. The jurors were never asked whether research occurred — the case decides
what the evidence showed, not what happened. Defence counsel argued *against* examining jurors.
