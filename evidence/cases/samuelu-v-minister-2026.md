A citizen of Samoa and New Zealand who came to Australia aged **17** in 1998. In December 2018,
criminal damage and unlawful assault in the context of family violence against his former partner —
fined $2,000 without conviction. In April 2021, while subject to an interim intervention order, he
committed aggravated burglary, criminal damage, threats to kill and contravention of the order, and
was sentenced to **2 years 9 months**.

## The machinery

- **s 501(3A)** — cancellation is **mandatory** where a non-citizen has a substantial criminal
  record and is serving a full-time sentence. No discretion at this stage.
- **s 501CA(4)** — the person may make representations; the Minister *may* revoke if satisfied the
  character test is passed or there is "another reason".
- **Ministerial Direction No 110**, made under s 499, binds the decision-maker and sets the primary
  considerations: protection of the community, family violence, best interests of children in
  Australia, community expectations, and ties to Australia.

He argued the Tribunal had weighed the **seriousness** of his offending as an adverse factor before
deciding how **likely** he was to reoffend — treating a protective power as a punitive one.

## Held — appeal dismissed

## Use it for

- **Post-sentencing considerations**: deportation is named in the syllabus dot point, and this is
  what it looks like. A man punished by a court, and then removed by the executive, for the same
  conduct. Whether that is a second punishment or a separate protective decision is exactly the
  argument the case turns on.
- **Delegated legislation doing the real work.** Direction 110 is not an Act. It is made under s 499,
  it binds decision-makers, and it structures how the discretion is exercised — a form of
  law-making that never passes through Parliament. Preliminary ls.2.5 rarely gets a better example.
- **Judicial review is not merits review.** The Full Court could not decide whether he should stay,
  only whether the decision was lawfully made. Students routinely conflate the two; this case makes
  the distinction impossible to miss.
- **Family violence crossing into migration law** — the Family option's dot point reaching a
  jurisdiction it never mentions.
