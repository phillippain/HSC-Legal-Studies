Raymond Hurt (ACT) and Enrico Delzotto (NSW), each with a prior child sexual abuse conviction, were
sentenced for possessing child abuse material obtained through a carriage service (*Criminal Code*
(Cth) s 474.22A, maximum 15 years). From **23 June 2020**, *Crimes Act 1914* (Cth) **s 16AAB** imposes a
**minimum of 4 years** for a repeat offender, reducible only for a plea or cooperation (up to 25% each,
s 16AAC). Both had their sentences increased on Crown appeals (to 4 years 10 months and 4 years 6
months) and argued the minimum should not bite because some downloading predated 23 June 2020, and
that a minimum only limits the court's power.

## Held — both appeals dismissed, unanimously

- **The conduct is the possession**; using a carriage service is a circumstance. So the minimum
  applied to possession after commencement, whenever the material was obtained.
- **A minimum is a yardstick.** Like a maximum (*Markarian*), it "informs the sentencing process by
  conveying the Commonwealth Parliament's view of the relative seriousness of the offence" — it
  represents "the least worst possible case warranting imprisonment against which the case before the
  court at the time can be measured" (Gageler CJ and Jagot J).
- The plea and cooperation exceptions do not detract from that role, and "the policy of imposing a
  statutory minimum sentence for an offence is a matter for the legislature, not the courts".

## Use it for

- **crime.4.1 (statutory guidelines)** — mandatory minimums and how they shape the whole range, not
  just the bottom; the counterpoint to *Muldrock*'s guidepost reading of SNPPs.
- **Discretion** — Parliament narrowing judicial discretion, and the High Court accepting it.
- **Law reform** — the policy debate about mandatory sentencing (compare the NSW one-punch minimum).

## Caution

Not a Crown-appeal principles case; the Crown appeals were in the courts below. The offending involves
child abuse material — keep discussion at the level of principle.
