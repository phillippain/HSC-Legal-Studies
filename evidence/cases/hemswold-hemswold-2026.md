Two children, **X born 2019** and **Y born 2022**. Y was an infant when the parties separated and
had been spending **professionally supervised time** with her father weekly, building a positive
relationship. X was refusing to see him. The mother did not want to force X, and was worried about
the children having **different arrangements**.

The father had undertaken psychological treatment to address his issues. The Court's assessment was
that he was **not yet safe enough** for either child to spend substantial **unsupervised** time with
him. A long interim history sits behind it: substantial interim hearing in February 2023, supervised
time, suspension of X's supervised time in September 2023, and a breakdown in the mother
facilitating visits from October 2024.

## The orders

- **Sole parental responsibility to the mother**, but with a built-in consultation obligation: **28
  days' written notice** of any major long-term decision and her preliminary views, consideration of
  the father's written views, and prompt advice of the decision.
- The children **live with the mother**; by consent their surnames are changed to a hyphenated form.
- **X spends time with her father in accordance with her wishes**, and the mother must take all
  reasonable steps to **encourage, support and facilitate** it.
- **Y spends time on a staged schedule**: every second Sunday, 10am–1pm for three months, then
  10am–3pm for three months, then 10am–5pm — with the **first stage professionally supervised** and
  **handovers supervised thereafter**, at the father's expense.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.2 (consequences of separation — children)** — the clearest example available that there is
  **no presumption of equal treatment between siblings**. The Act asks what is in *each* child's best
  interests, and here that produced two different answers in one household.
- **The children's views (s 60CC)** — X's refusal is respected without being made determinative: she
  sees him "in accordance with her wishes", *and* the mother is obliged to encourage it. That double
  obligation is a precise, quotable way of showing how courts weigh a child's wishes against the
  benefit of a relationship.
- **fam.2.4 (family violence)** — the middle of the range, between unsupervised time and the complete
  severance in *Melzer & Powles*. **Supervision is a risk-management tool**, not a punishment, and
  the staged schedule shows time expanding as risk reduces.
- **Sole responsibility with consultation** — compare the 28-day protocol here with the 7/14-day one
  in *Sullivan & Warren*. Both give one parent the decision while keeping the other informed, which
  is what replaced the repealed presumption of equal shared parental responsibility.
- **Cost as a barrier (inv.1.4)** — supervision is "at the father's expense". Worth asking what
  happens to a parent who cannot pay for it.

## Caution

**Pseudonyms**; Pt XIVB applies. A **Division 2** decision of **351 paragraphs** reduced here to its
outcome — the family violence findings, the expert evidence of Dr O and the children's views are
summarised only in outline, and the judgment should be read before relying on any finding about
either parent. The Court **did not accept** the father's claim that the mother reduced his time to
punish him, so do not write the mother up as obstructive. The change of surname was **by consent**
and is not a contested ruling. First instance, highly fact-specific: the staged schedule is a remedy
fitted to these facts, not a template.
