A boy of 8 and a girl of 5, living with their maternal grandparents since November 2023, when their
mother was hospitalised after a mental-health episode and methamphetamine use. Their father: a door
kicked off its hinges (2017), an assault on a transport worker with the elder child in the car
(2018), a "jealous rage" (2019), physical violence with charges later withdrawn after she recanted
(2020), an attack on her new partner in front of the children two months after separation (June
2023) — "he has lied about what occurred" — and taking the younger child from the school grounds
(September 2023: "I'm going to keep her for a couple months and see how you feel"). The children
have not seen him since. **Consent orders in May 2024** placed the children with the grandfather and
required the father to complete an accredited Men's Behaviour Change Program. Told in August 2024 he
was not eligible, he **swore twice** he was enrolled and participating and told the single expert he
had done three sessions; the grandparents learned the truth in October 2025 — after consenting to
further orders on the strength of it.

## Held — 1 September 2026

- **Six occasions** between 2017 and 2023 found to be family violence under **s 4AB**, some in front
  of the children; three other allegations (including a knife threat) not made out on the
  *Briginshaw* standard. A witness who lies about one thing is not disbelieved about everything
  (*Sayed & Rehmann*), but his evidence "contained deliberate falsehoods".
- The deception decided it: the expert's recommendation for contact depended on the accredited
  program — "you take away that leg of the argument, the argument, itself, topples". Two contact
  centres declined to supervise; the proposed aunt was unsuitable; a private twelve-week course was
  not the program ordered.
- The eight-year-old's consistent fear carried real weight — a toy called Protectosaurus that "isn't
  strong enough" before a session with his father (at [121]).
- "Ultimately, the legislation does not contemplate the benefit to children of relationships which
  are not safe for them" (at [172]); "Self-evidently, it is a very serious matter to make orders
  which will mean that children spend no time with one of their parents" (at [169]).
- **Orders**: parental responsibility **taken from both parents** and given to the grandparents, who
  are put "in the same legal position" as parents; the children live with them; the mother's time
  supervised by a grandparent with an **s 68B** restraint carrying a power of arrest; the father has
  **no time and no communication** — cards, gifts and letters at Christmas and birthdays, which the
  grandparents may inspect first; passports and overseas travel without either parent's consent.
  Costs: the father pays **$8,926.46** (one third of the ICL's costs) to Legal Aid NSW; the mother,
  on legal aid, cannot be ordered to contribute (s 114UC(2)(a)).

## Use it for

- **fam.3.2 (parental responsibility)** — the best case in the set for grandparents as carers, and
  for the examinable point: parental responsibility is **allocated by a court, not owned by a
  parent**.
- **fam.2.4** — six findings under s 4AB and the reasoning that exposure to family violence "is
  necessarily harmful to children" (at [146]); the expert's evidence on patriarchal attitudes, and a
  court treating denial of violence as a parenting deficit (**fam.t.values**).
- **fam.t.compliance** — non-compliance that changed an outcome: a court-ordered program never
  joined, sworn affidavits saying otherwise, consent orders obtained on a false premise.
- **fam.3.4** — kinship care **without a child protection order**: otherwise "the responsibility for
  that care would likely otherwise have fallen to the State".
- **Legal and non-legal together, and failing** — intervention orders, criminal charges, therapy,
  a behaviour change program, two contact centres: none produced a relationship that could be made
  safe (HSC 2015, 2023).
