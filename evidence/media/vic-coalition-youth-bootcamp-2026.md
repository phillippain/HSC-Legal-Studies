The Coalition would use the former **Dhurringile Prison** near Murchison (closed 2024; courts have spent **$2.06m** keeping it empty). No cost, capacity or opening date has been released.

## Two election promises

| Coalition bootcamp (15 November) | Labor therapeutic order (8 November) |
|---|---|
| Offenders aged 14 to 17 | Young offenders; no age range stated |
| Offences not specified | Theft, vandalism, property damage, minor assaults |
| Daily farm work, cleaning, maintenance, cooking, education | Drug and alcohol rehabilitation, life skills |
| Length not stated (Queensland ran 3–6 months) | Up to 12 months; $204m |

In Queensland's earlier program, 47 of 74 sentenced participants (**63.5%**) reoffended.

## What the evidence says (The Conversation, 16 November 2026)

A review of **32** studies found boot camps make no difference to reoffending (odds ratio **1.02** against comparison groups), and
**24 of 35** completers of Queensland's boot camp went on to reoffend. The article frames both parties' plans — the Coalition's
Dhurringile camp and Labor's $204m mandatory therapeutic order — as prevention relying on removal and detention, in contrast with
NSW's diversion-first *Young Offenders Act*. [Class evidence summary](https://drive.google.com/file/d/167440V6-AoUvZRtn8T4TcLz8q1im16j7/view).
