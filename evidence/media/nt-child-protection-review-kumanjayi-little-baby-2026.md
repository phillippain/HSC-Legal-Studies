**Kumanjayi Little Baby**, a young Aboriginal girl, went missing from an Alice Springs town camp in
April 2026 and was found dead five days later; a man has been charged. The NT Government commissioned
former NSW Police Commissioner **Karen Webb** and senior public servant **Greg Shanahan** to review the
Department of Children and Families' (DCF) dealings with the family. An interim report was tabled on
21 July 2026; the final report on **20 October 2026**.

## What the review found

| What the system should have done | What happened |
|---|---|
| Assess family-violence risk as part of child-protection risk | Violence in every notification, but no DFV risk assessment was ever done |
| Reassess and escalate when new violence was reported | Safety rated 2/10 more than once; never escalated |
| Assess promptly and close only when safe | The August 2022 case stayed open **229 days** "with no tangible improvement" |
| Check the adults relied on in a safety plan | No checks; one nominated adult had five threat-to-kill counts |
| Use protective options such as a DVO | None sought; the mother left to protect the children while being abused |

**14 notifications** over six years (the minister told Parliament 16, including two in pregnancy). The
review made no adverse findings against individual staff; **45 recommendations**, all accepted, for a
"system-wide reset". The minister ordered an audit of current safety plans.

## The reaction: "the full picture" is missing (24 October 2026)

| The Webb review | What critics say is missing |
|---|---|
| Conduct of the department only | Housing, health and justice failures (Sue-Anne **Hunter**, National Commissioner for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Children) — she wants a commission of inquiry |
| Practice, not legal powers | None of the 45 recommendations seeks legal change (**NAAJA**) |
| Child Placement Principle not mentioned (per Congress and NAAJA) | Culture, identity and kin not considered |
| Mother left to protect the children while abused | Focus on the mother may deter survivors seeking help |
| System-wide reset | Needs genuine partnership with Aboriginal community-controlled organisations (**Congress**) |

- **Senator Jacinta Nampijinpa Price**, the child's aunt, accused Child Placement Principle advocates of
  an ideological approach; the minister did not respond by deadline.
- The July 2026 *Every Child Matters* Act put Aboriginal placement principles (s 12C) under a universal
  principle (s 12B) and says the principles create no enforceable rights; AHRC commissioners warned it
  risks repeating Stolen Generations harms. About **90%** of NT children in out-of-home care are First
  Nations (Children's Commissioner, June 2026). The government also ordered an audit of safety plans
  and required WWCC clearances for people in a child's care.
- Pairs with **UNCRC arts 3 and 30** (best interests and cultural rights).

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — a statutory child-protection system failing a child despite
  repeated reports: the failure was in **practice and resourcing**, not the law's powers. Compare NSW's
  *Care and Protection Act 1998*.
- **fam.2.4** — child protection and domestic violence as one problem, handled as two.
- **Law reform** — the review sits beside the NT's contested July 2026 *Every Child Matters*
  amendments, which weakened the Aboriginal Child Placement Principle; and beside a long line of NT
  inquiries (*Little Children are Sacred* 2007, the 2010 Board of Inquiry, the 2017 Royal Commission).

## Caution

A class **evidence summary of news reporting**; the final report itself could not be retrieved, and
early figures differed (the ABC's preview reported 40 recommendations). A criminal charge is pending:
**say nothing about the accused**. The coverage includes few Aboriginal community-controlled
organisation voices. Treat the child's name and story with care in class.
