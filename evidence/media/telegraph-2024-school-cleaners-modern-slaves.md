In **April 2024** the NSW Anti-slavery Commissioner, **Dr James Cockayne**, wrote to several Minns
government ministers about the conditions of the **7,000 school cleaners** working under private
whole-of-government contracts, and encouraged them to consider employing those cleaners **directly as
employees of the Department of Education** instead.

Victims and whistleblowers in the NSW cleaning sector had approached him directly, and their accounts
suggested "**chronic under-reporting of exploitation**". He described **intimidation and threats,
deception, withholding of wages and abusive working conditions**; suppliers cutting costs by
**coercing workers into forced overtime** and threatening dismissal if they complained; **wage theft
or underpayment**; **unreasonable fees imposed on workers** for uniforms, transport and supplies; and
**deceptive recruiting**.

His conclusion is the line to quote:

> "Taken together these factors expose cleaners in NSW to forms of exploitation that surpass the
> threshold of forced labour based on the International Labour Organization's [definition] and
> suggest real risks of modern slavery."

Taxpayers should be "highly concerned" about modern slavery occurring under **NSW government-funded
contracts**. "Insourcing school cleaners would improve the institutional checks and balances on
abuses of power."

Under the cleaning services award a full-time school cleaner could be paid a minimum of **$24 an
hour**. The **United Workers Union** said a large proportion of the workforce comes from migrant
communities. A cleaner of 53 years, "Mary", said her colleagues with limited English were "afraid to
speak up … so they comply and do the extra work without pay". A spokesman for Education Minister
**Prue Car** said the government was "deeply concerned", blamed the previous government's "addiction
to privatisation", and said the contracts were under review.

## What happened next

In **June 2025** the NSW government committed to **bringing school cleaners' employment back within
the Department of Education**, which Dr Cockayne publicly welcomed. The change begins **from 2026**,
starting in the **Hunter and Central Coast**.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 — modern slavery in Australia, in a public school.** The most useful feature of this record
  is how ordinary the setting is. Not a farm, a brothel or an embassy: the cleaning of NSW
  classrooms, paid for by the state.
- **The state as the buyer of exploitation (hr.t.values)** — the exploitation occurred under
  **whole-of-government contracts**, so the government was indirectly funding it. A sharp argument
  that outsourcing moves the work without moving the responsibility.
- **A complete law reform sequence, all documented (chg.1 / hr.t.reform)** — a statutory officer
  identifies the problem → writes to ministers → a newspaper publishes it → a union campaigns → the
  government reviews the contracts → **insourcing is announced in June 2025 and begins in 2026**.
  Most reform evidence stops at "calls for change"; this one has an outcome, which makes it worth far
  more in an evaluation.
- **hr.2.4 — what an independent statutory authority can actually achieve.** Cockayne has no power to
  prosecute or to vary a contract. He wrote letters and spoke to a newspaper, and the policy changed.
  Use it to argue that influence, publicity and reporting are real mechanisms.
- **The ILO definition as a legal threshold** — he does not say "this is bad", he says the conduct
  **surpasses the ILO threshold of forced labour**. Good for showing a legal standard being applied
  to Australian working conditions.
- **Why victims do not complain (hr.3.1 / exp.6.2)** — migration status, limited English, fear of
  losing hours. The same under-reporting problem the UN Special Rapporteur identified, in a concrete
  workplace.

## Caution

A **tabloid report** of the Commissioner's correspondence, accessed through a NewsBank copy; the
letters themselves are not in the file, so the quotes are as reported. Cockayne's assessment is that
there are **"real risks of modern slavery"** and that conditions "surpass the threshold" — a
**finding of risk by a commissioner**, not a court finding, and **no supplier has been named or
convicted**. Do not write that NSW school cleaners *are* slaves. The **$24 an hour** award minimum
and the 7,000 figure are from **June 2024** and will have moved. The insourcing commitment is
**staged and partial** — Hunter and Central Coast from 2026 — so do not say every NSW school cleaner
is now a department employee; check the current rollout. "Mary" is identified by first name only at
her own request.
