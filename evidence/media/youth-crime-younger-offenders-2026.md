Police and the AFP warn of younger children involved in violent crime and recruited online by organised crime.
BOCSAR data says otherwise about volume: young people proceeded against to court are **down 8%** over 15 years and
stable over two. But detention rose **34%** (June 2023 to June 2025), and in March 2026 **175 of 220** (79.6%) young
people in detention were **on remand**, not sentenced. Proven outcomes for 10–13-year-olds fell from **76% to 16%**
(2015–16 to 2022–23).

## Headlines v the data

| Claim | What the sources show |
|---|---|
| AFP: a "tsunami of youth crime" | Given to a radicalisation inquiry; about extremism and sadistic online networks |
| 34% detention jump since bail rules began "April last year" | 34% is June 2023–June 2025; s 22C began April 2024 |
| Bail law covers any prior property charge | Only motor theft or serious break and enter, allegedly while on bail for one |
| School attendance the strongest predictor | By 15, suspension data dominates |
| Scotland has no child prisoners | No under-18s in young offender institutions; secure care still used |

## The pathway

Police contact → **diversion** (cautions, youth justice conferences) → bail (s 22C: "high degree of confidence") →
**doli incapax** for 10–13-year-olds (prosecution must prove the child knew the act was seriously wrong; s 5 from 1
March 2026) → Children's Court (in 2022–23, 53% of 10–13-year-olds had all charges withdrawn).
