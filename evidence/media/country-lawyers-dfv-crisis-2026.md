DFV is **40–50%** of community legal centre (CLC) work nationally and about **75%** in rural and remote areas. Funding
for CLCs and women's legal services rose **74%** to **$833m** for 2025–30, but a $15.8m request for regional DFV and
family law services was not funded. The article follows a Victorian program placing early-career lawyers in the regions.

## City v country

| | National / metropolitan | Regional and remote |
|---|---|---|
| DFV share of CLC work | About 40–50% | About 75% |
| Coercive control incidents (NSW 2024–25) | Greater Sydney 2.3 per 100,000 | Regional NSW 5.9 per 100,000 |
| Service reach | WDVCAS statewide by phone | A Mid-North Coast centre "could double and still not meet need" |
| Workforce incentives | HELP debt relief for rural doctors, nurse practitioners, remote teachers | None for regional CLC lawyers, despite NLAP review Rec 26 |

## The pathway depends on lawyers

Police can seek a provisional ADVO (s 25) and must apply when all protected persons are children (s 48(3)); the court
applies the s 16 fear test on the balance of probabilities. CLCs handle family law, housing and financial abuse. NSW's
coercive control offence (s 54D) produced **297 incidents but only 9 charges** in its first year.
