In a QLRC-funded survey, **57%** said a mother slapping a child's face was guilty of assault, yet **65%** said
"not guilty" across all physical punishment scenarios. Smacking has fallen from **64.2%** of parents aged 65+ to
**14%** of 16–24-year-olds. **70 countries** have fully prohibited corporal punishment of children.

## The defences compared

| | Who is covered | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Qld now — s 280** | Parents, carers, teachers, "masters" | Reasonable force for discipline, correction, management or control; "reasonable" undefined |
| **NSW — s 61AA** | Parents and people they authorise | Unreasonable if to the head or neck, or harm lasts more than a short period |
| **Qld proposed — Rec 16** | Step 1: parents only, common assault only; teachers removed | Step 2 (three years on): protection or management only, **not punishment** |
| **New Zealand — s 59 (2007)** | Parents | No force for correction; police need not prosecute trivial cases |
| **Scotland 2020, Wales 2022** | — | Defence abolished: children protected from assault like adults |

The QLRC made **36 recommendations** after consulting 786 stakeholders, including court-based **diversion** for
parents (Rec 17) and awareness programs (Rec 18). They are **not binding**; at July 2026 Queensland had not legislated.
