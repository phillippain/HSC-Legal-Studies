Police allege five men were hired to carry out a contract killing outside a Revesby daycare centre,
with **seven "kill cars"** staged across Sydney and a loaded Glock, a GoPro and a cardboard sign
seized at the arrest. Iese Esera, 21, applied for bail in **Campbelltown Local Court** and was
refused.

## The bail decision, which is the useful part

His lawyer argued the case was **weak**, that the proceedings would be **significantly delayed**
because of their complexity, that he had a **limited criminal record**, and that his family would
supervise him around the clock under a **stringent home detention condition**. The prosecution
opposed release on the seriousness of the allegations.

**Magistrate Lisa Graham refused bail, finding Esera "had not demonstrated his ongoing detention was
unjustified."**

That wording is the **show cause** requirement in **s 16A of the *Bail Act 2013*** working: for the
serious offences listed in s 16B the accused must show cause why detention is not justified, and
only if that is done does the court go on to the ordinary unacceptable-risk assessment.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.5 (bail or remand)** — a short, recent, concrete illustration of the **reversed onus**.
  Most students can state the unacceptable-risk test; far fewer can explain that for some offences
  the accused must clear a prior hurdle, and this gives you the magistrate's own words for it.
- **The arguments on each side**, which are exactly the s 18 considerations: strength of the
  prosecution case, length of time likely on remand, criminal history, and the conditions proposed.
  Worth noting the conditions offered were strict and still did not succeed.
- **crime.1.7 (parties to a crime)** — "contract criminals" alleged to have staged vehicles for
  someone else's shooting: accessorial liability and the reach of conspiracy and organised crime
  charges, with an alleged $6,000 fee.
- **Balancing rights (crime.t.balance)** — remand for people not convicted of anything, against
  community safety in an alleged murder plot. Pair it with the Chief Justice's statement on bail and
  with the Guardian piece on children refused bail, where the same test produces a very different
  argument.

## Caution

**Every allegation here is untested** — none of the five had entered a plea, and the quoted
intercepted messages and the $6,000 boast are police allegations reported before trial. Treat the
accused as innocent and attribute all of it to the police case. The article is a **tabloid exclusive
written for its crime drama**, not a law report: it gives the magistrate's conclusion in one line
and no reasons, and does not name the section. A **16-year-old** is among those charged, so say
nothing that would identify a child. Do not cite this for the content of the *Bail Act* — cite the
Act.
