Jake and Callum Robinson and their American friend Carter Rhoad were killed camping at Punta San
José around **27–28 April 2024**; their bodies were found in a disused well on 3 May, each shot once
in the head. After a month-long trial before a single judge in Ensenada, three men were convicted on
**9 October 2026** of aggravated homicide, violent robbery and vehicle theft, and sentenced to **158
years nine months each**, plus reparation.

## Mexico and NSW side by side

| | Baja California | NSW |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides guilt | Judge alone, oral accusatory trial | Jury by default; judge alone only by agreement (s 132) |
| Murder penalty | Aggravated homicide **30–60 years** (Penal Code art 126) | Maximum **life** (*Crimes Act* s 19A); 20-year standard non-parole period |
| Longest sentence | Prison 3 days to **60 years** (art 26) | Life must be imposed where culpability is extreme (CSPA s 61) |
| Multiple offences | Penalties **added** — 158 years nine months here | Whole episode informs culpability for each count |
| Reparation | Victims' **constitutional** right (art 20 C); a judge cannot waive it | Victim impact statements; separate compensation schemes |

Reparation ordered: **1,980,728 pesos** for the Robinsons (about AU$154,500) and 549,550 pesos for
Rhoad.

## The system behind it

Mexico's **2008 constitutional reform** replaced written, semi-inquisitorial procedure with
adversarial oral hearings, with the *Código Nacional de Procedimientos Penales* published in 2014 and
national implementation due by June 2016. A **control judge** supervises the investigation and binds
the accused to trial. A co-accused woman took an **expedited proceeding**, pleaded guilty, received
**20 years** in November 2025, and then testified at trial. The defence has **ten days** from
notification to appeal (CNPP art 471); appeals generally cannot re-weigh the evidence (art 468), and
the appeal court may confirm, modify or revoke, or order acts repeated (art 479).

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **news report, not the judgment**. The ages differ between outlets (the
ABC gives 31 and 32; Reuters and AAP give 30 and 33) and the **compensation figures rest on the ABC
alone**. The Penal Code as published caps prison at 60 years including for multiple offences, and no
report explains the gap — so do not state how long will actually be served. The claim that one man
was a hit man with Sinaloa cartel links is an allegation attributed to trial evidence. The defence
intends to appeal, so the outcome is not final. NSW appeal time limits, victim impact statement rules
and Mexican life-sentence rules were not checked.
