Between **1 and 12 October 2026** police allege a series of incidents on the Narrabeen Lagoon
walking trails on Sydney's Northern Beaches — women walking or running in daylight touched or
spoken to by a youth on an e-bike — plus one in Cromer on 7 September. Strike Force Mexvo released
CCTV on 14 October. On **17 October** a **13-year-old boy** attended a police station with his family,
was arrested and charged with **eight counts of sexual touching** and **three of behaving in an
offensive manner** (11 charges, 11 alleged incidents, complainants aged 11 to 54), granted
**conditional bail**, and listed for the **Children's Court on 4 November 2026**. **Every incident is
an allegation; no plea has been entered.**

## Adult v child on the same charges

| | If an adult | A 13-year-old |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal responsibility | Presumed | **Doli incapax**: Crown must prove beyond reasonable doubt he knew the act was *seriously wrong*, not merely naughty (*RP v The Queen*) |
| Identity | Publishable | **s 15A** *Children (Criminal Proceedings) Act 1987* — no naming |
| Court | Local, then District Court on indictment | **Children's Court**, summarily (ss 28, 31) |
| Bail | Show-cause may apply | No show-cause under 18; s 22C's youth test covers only 14–18 and property offences |
| Maximum | Sexual touching, s 61KC: **5 years** | Control order capped at **2 years**, last resort (s 33) |
| Diversion | Not available | *Young Offenders Act* cautions and conferences — but sexual offences are largely excluded |

## The reform debate it lands in

- **Oct 2025** — the NSW doli incapax review (Bellew and Loy) kept the age at 10 and the high
  rebuttal threshold, with seven recommendations including legislating the test.
- BOCSAR: proven outcomes for 10–13-year-olds fell from **76% to 16%** over seven years to 2023 —
  the figure that prompted the review.
- **1 Jul 2025** ACT raised the age to 14; **30 Sep 2025** Victoria to 12 and abandoned 14; NSW
  stays at 10. **1 Jul 2026** YOA changes: "non-denial" statements replace admissions, cautions
  limited to three in three years.
- Bar Association: protect the presumption unless the age rises to 14; Aboriginal Legal Service:
  early contact entrenches offending.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **report built on police statements**, with no defence, family or
legal comment. **Never name the child or any complainant.** The charge sections were not published
(s 61KC is inferred from the wording; touching of a child under 16 is a different offence, s 66DB,
10 years), the YOA sexual-offence exclusion comes from a law firm rather than the Act, and the
"4pm–6pm" pattern is approximate. Check the outcome after 4 November before using anything beyond
