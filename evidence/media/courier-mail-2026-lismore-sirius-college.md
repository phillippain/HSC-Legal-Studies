A short regional story with two useful pieces of evidence in it.

- **Record regional rents** — REA Group data released 20 January 2026: the median weekly rent in
  **regional NSW is $600**, up **5.3%** in twelve months; regional **units $530**, up 6%. (HILDA's
  regional median was $360 in 2024 on a different measure — the two are not comparable.)
- **Flood recovery as housing supply** — **Sirius College**, Southern Cross University's 27-unit
  student accommodation on Military Road, Lismore (21 two-bedroom and six three-bedroom
  apartments), was gutted in the February 2022 floods and rebuilt to be hosed out after the next one:
  epoxy floors, rendered brick, aluminium windows, no gyprock or carpet. Reopening February 2026.
- **The local argument** — Member for Lismore Janelle Saffin: student housing "takes pressure off
  the Lismore rental market"; the rebuild used 100% local contractors (Joel Jensen Constructions).

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — the regional figure students rarely have: the rental crisis is not a
  Sydney story. Pair with Anglicare's snapshot and HILDA for the trend.
- **shel.3.3 / shel.1.3** — Lismore as the place where **natural disaster** became a shelter issue:
  four years on, housing lost in 2022 is still being replaced, and the Tenants' Union digest records
  flood "pod" cabins being reused for key workers in Byron Shire.
- **shel.2.8** — a university (a public body) and a local member as actors in supply; the absence of
  any tenancy-law response in the story is itself a point — supply, not regulation, is the lever here.
- **Non-legal responses** — building standards adapted to climate risk as a practical, not legal,
  measure.

## Caution

A **News Corp regional story under two minutes long**, with the REA figures second-hand and no
counting of how many private rentals 27 student units would free. It says nothing about the
Northern Rivers Reconstruction Corporation's buy-backs or the Resilient Homes Program, which are the
larger legal responses to the floods — go to those for the government side. Check the college
actually reopened.
