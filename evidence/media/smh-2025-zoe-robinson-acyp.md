A profile of **Zoe Robinson**, the **NSW Advocate for Children and Young People** — the statutory
office behind the special inquiry into alternative care arrangements already in the library. It is
worth a record because it describes what the office actually **is**.

## The office

- An **independent statutory officer**, whose mission is to ensure children and young people have "a
  dedicated champion in the halls of government".
- The legislation defines children and young people as **anyone under the age of 25** — babies to
  young adults.
- **Budget $4.4 million. Staff of 23.** "I'd love more," Robinson says. "**We are stretched.**"
- She held the role for five years, with **2025 her last year**.

## What she did with it

- **Lived experience built into the office**: "I really fought for lived experience to be a
  meaningful thing in our space; [these days] we have a young person who **spent five years in
  custody** who works for us, there are people who have been in the care system too."
- In her first year she was "in **every youth justice centre every month**".
- After the **Black Summer bushfires**, her report found that **no specific programs for young people
  were built into disaster recovery planning**. When the **2022 Northern Rivers floods** hit she
  lobbied state and federal governments for recovery support officers dedicated to the **300+
  children** in temporary accommodation.
- Her diagnosis of the job: "**people don't take children and young people seriously all the time** …
  And I get to sit with children and young people and I realise the sensibility and the thoughtfulness
  that they bring to issues affecting them."

## The clash

In **2021**, when **Mark Latham** was pursuing a "parental rights bill" in the NSW Parliament aimed
partly at banning classroom discussion of gender fluidity, Robinson testified that **young people
themselves did not want such bans**. He attacked the adequacy of her research and "has called for my
office to go, for me to go, more than once". The article notes the clash prompted her to make sure
future reports would "comfortably weather even Lathamesque scrutiny".

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 / exp.2.2 — "authorities that advocate for the rights of young people", with a real one.**
  Name the office, the statute, the under-25 definition, the budget and the staff, and the answer
  stops being generic.
- **$4.4 million and 23 staff is the evaluation.** An independent advocate for every person in NSW
  under 25, with a budget smaller than a single school's. The sharpest possible resource-efficiency
  point: the mechanism exists, the capacity does not. "**We are stretched**" is the quote.
- **Independence is what makes it work, and what makes it vulnerable (hr.2.4 / dis.3.5)** — the office
  is designed to criticise government, and a member of Parliament publicly called for its abolition
  after it contradicted him. Excellent for the tension between independent statutory oversight and
  political accountability, and it pairs with the Anti-slavery Commissioner records, where the same
  structure (influence, no power) appears.
- **Participation as a legal principle (croc-1989)** — Article 12 of CROC gives children a right to be
  heard. Robinson's whole method, including employing people with lived experience of custody and care,
  is that right operationalised. Use it where a question asks how international obligations reach
  domestic practice.
- **Oversight only works if it is believed** — her response to being attacked was to raise her
  evidentiary standard. A quietly instructive point about how scrutiny shapes institutions.
- **Gaps nobody was looking for (fam.2.6)** — no youth-specific programs in disaster recovery
  planning, found because somebody independent went looking. That is what an advocate produces that a
  department does not.
- **Pair it with the care records** — `acyp-special-inquiry-aca-2024` is this office's major output,
  and `telegraph-2025-aca-ended` is what followed. Three records, one institution, a complete story of
  oversight producing change.

## Caution

A **"Lunch with" profile** — a sympathetic, personality-driven feature over a sandwich, not an
assessment of the office. Much of it is **biography** (To Kill a Mockingbird, death row appeals in
Texas, Deloitte) which is colour, not evidence; the legal content is the budget, the statutory
independence, the under-25 definition and the Latham clash. The **$4.4 million and 23 staff** are as
at February 2025 and will have changed. **Robinson's term ended in 2025**, so she is almost certainly
**not the current Advocate** — check who holds the office before writing in the present tense, and
cite the office rather than the person. Her account of the Latham exchange is **her own**, given in a
friendly interview, and he is not quoted in reply; report it as her account of a political attack.
The description of the "parental rights bill" is the journalist's summary of a 2021 Bill that **did
not pass** in that form. The office's governing statute, the *Advocate for Children and Young People
Act 2014* (NSW), is not named in the article — cite the Act, not this profile, for the office's
powers and functions.
