After a Crime Stoppers tip-off about violent extremist material online, the NT Joint Counter Terrorism Team
executed warrants in Darwin on **28 October 2026** and charged an unnamed teenager with three Commonwealth
offences: **s 101.5** (collecting or making documents likely to facilitate terrorism, max 10 or 15 years) and two
online-material offences, including **s 474.45C** aimed at 3D-printed firearm blueprints (max 5 years each).
Police said there was no information the teen could access or make a firearm. He was remanded; bail is listed
for **9 November 2026**. **All matters are allegations; he is presumed innocent.**

## Children v adults charged with terrorism offences

| | Adult | Child |
|---|---|---|
| Offences and maximums | Same | Same — no lower federal ceiling |
| Court | Adult courts exercising federal jurisdiction | Youth court; s 20C lets it treat the charge as a territory offence |
| Bail | s 15AA presumption against bail: exceptional circumstances | Same test; community protection paramount, child's best interests primary |
| Bail appealed | Prosecution notice stays the grant | Same — the Law Council says it should not apply to children |
| Sentencing | s 19AG limits discretion | Age, maturity and family weighed; the Law Council wants an s 19AG exemption |

13 youths aged 14–17 have been charged with extremist-material offences since 2024. The INSLM (2019) recommended
that terrorism charges carrying 15 years or more always be tried on indictment, not in children's courts.
