A Sunday Telegraph special report in which **five former and current staff** of **Life Without
Barriers** (LWB) — a non-government provider delivering out-of-home care **on behalf of the
Department of Communities and Justice** — alleged that at least **50 children and young people** in
its care were at serious risk of harm, with allegations of "serious neglect". LWB's own 2023–24
report records it cared for **5,966 children** with **1,691 carers and families**.

The central case study is "**Amy**" (not her real name), 16, in LWB care since 2022 after foster
placements and residential homes, with PTSD and suicidal ideation. Her January safety plan required
**24/7 supervision** because of repeated self-harm and significant suicide attempts, and recorded
that she had absconded on **six days in the past month**, each time exposed to drug and alcohol use
and unsafe individuals. Whistleblowers alleged staff were not following that plan, that Amy raised
her own concerns and nothing was done, and that children could buy drugs from dealers outside the
homes. The paper says it saw **texts in which workers mocked children**. A placement student alleged
workers were not making regular visits or entering homes to check on children.

A child protection advocate said the case was not isolated and that she was aware of hundreds of
children seriously harmed in residential and foster care. **LWB** responded that the "care and
privacy of children is our absolute focus" and that misconduct allegations were "taken extremely
seriously". The then **Families and Communities Minister, Kate Washington**, said the foster care
system needed "significant reform"; **DCJ** said it "continues to monitor LWB". The accompanying
**editorial** demanded an immediate full inquiry into the government's service providers.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection) — outsourcing.** The single best illustration that NSW does not
  run most out-of-home care itself: parental responsibility sits with the **Minister**, while the
  day-to-day care is delivered by **non-government providers under contract**. Ask the obvious
  question: who is accountable when the care fails?
- **Enforceability and oversight** — a safety plan requiring 24/7 supervision is a document, and the
  allegation is that nobody enforced it. A concrete answer to "how effective is the law in protecting
  children in state care?" that is not just "it is ineffective".
- **The non-legal response (fam.2.6 / fam.2.7)** — this story exists because **employees went to a
  newspaper**. Media and whistleblowers as accountability mechanisms where internal complaints
  ("I made formal complaints to the director and nothing was achieved") did not work.
- **Build a line of evidence** — put it with *smh-2024-foster-carers-quitting* (carers leaving), the
  **ACYP special inquiry** and *telegraph-2025-aca-ended* (the ACA), and *smh-2026-marcus-foster-care*
  (a damages claim over ignored reports). Four independent sources on the same system failing in
  four different ways is a much stronger paragraph than any one of them.
- **Pressure for reform (fam.t.reform)** — a minister conceding the system needs "significant reform"
  and a tabloid editorial demanding an inquiry: use it to show how media attention converts into
  political pressure, which is the first stage of the law reform process.

## Caution

**These are allegations**, not findings. No court, tribunal or inquiry had tested them; LWB denies
wrongdoing and no individual has been found to have breached anything. Write them as "whistleblowers
alleged", never as established fact, and do not name LWB as having neglected children. The source is
a **tabloid special report with an accompanying campaigning editorial** — the framing is
deliberately emotive ("suffer the children", "stop torturing kids"), the numbers ("at least 50",
"hundreds") come from the sources themselves rather than from any audit, and the sources are
anonymous and in dispute with the organisation. "Amy" is a pseudonym and the detail includes
**self-harm and suicide attempts**: handle it carefully in class, flag it before use, and do not
reproduce the specifics unnecessarily. The file in the library is named "Life Without Barriers
CPOC.pdf" but is a **newspaper clipping**, captured 13 May 2025; confirm the publication date before
citing it precisely, and note Kate Washington's portfolio may have changed since.
