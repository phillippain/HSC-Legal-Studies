A national broadcaster's investigative program combining original data journalism with ministerial
interviews. The **original dataset is the strongest single piece of evidence in it**: every
affordable-housing listing available in NSW over eight weeks, checked against the guideline.

## What the dataset found

- **Almost half** of the NSW listings analysed were **not 20% below the median** for that property
  type in that area, contrary to the guideline.
- **Some were priced above local median market rents.**
- A one-bedroom unit in Bondi Beach advertised at **$925 a week**; a two-bedroom in the same suburb
  at **$1,400**, leased at $1,150. Neither was affordable for any eligible household.
- **Swift Walk** (HAFF-supported): about **18 of 181** affordable properties still vacant **more than
  seven months** after completion, some priced above local median market rents.

## The Chimes collective sale — NSW Land and Environment Court

Anastasia Moesses, **80**, had owned her Potts Point unit for **44 years** and repeatedly rejected
the developer's offers. The owners corporation — controlled by the developers — applied to force the
remaining owners to sell. The Court found the sale of her home for **$1.4 million** was "just and
equitable". She refused to leave and was **evicted in January 2026**. Legal fees and a
court-appointed administrator consumed roughly **$200,000** of her proceeds. She now lives in a
hotel nearby.

**More than 180 objections and four supporting submissions** were lodged against stage one; the
concept design and early demolition works were approved regardless.

A second matter: **Landmark Group** appealed to the LEC on a **deemed refusal** after a council
failed to determine, within time, its application to end an affordable-housing period early. It
withdrew the application in 2026.

## The policy being measured

The **National Housing Accord** (2022) commits to **1.2 million homes by 2029**, including 20,000
affordable — state targets of NSW **3,100**, Victoria 2,546, Queensland 2,049. Latest projections
have Australia reaching the target **15 months after the deadline**.

## Use it for

- **Affordability and social housing**, with the rarest thing in the option: *compliance data on a
  scheme*, not just its targets. A target missed is one argument; a scheme delivering homes that are
  not affordable is a much stronger one.
- **Collective sale** as the sharpest illustration of whose interests strata law protects, and of
  what "just and equitable" costs an 80-year-old with 44 years of tenure.
- **Non-legal and quasi-legal mechanisms**: objection rights exercised in volume and overridden; a
  broadcaster doing the compliance checking a regulator did not.

## Caution

Journalism, not an audit — the sample is eight weeks of NSW listings, and the developers' right of
reply is part of the record (Time & Place's written response is published alongside). The Chimes
judgment is described without a citation; find it on NSW Caselaw before quoting it as a case.
