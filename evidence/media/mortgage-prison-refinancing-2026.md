The RBA's fifth 2026 rise (3 November) took the cash rate to **4.85%**, the highest since 2008. Refinancing to a new lender by
26–35-year-olds fell **14.2%** in the year to September, while it rose for over-55s. National home values are down **6.7%** from
the March 2026 peak, and a record **5.8%** of new loans were approved as serviceability exceptions (June quarter).

## Who can refinance v the mortgage prisoner

| | Can refinance | Mortgage prisoner |
|---|---|---|
| Profile | Older borrowers, low balances, long-held homes | Recent, younger first home buyers |
| Equity | Home has grown in value | High loan-to-value after price falls; may need mortgage insurance |
| Serviceability | Passes the new lender's test at product rate plus 3 points | Fails a test near 9.5% while still meeting current repayments |
| Legal route | A new contract: responsible lending assessment (NCCP Act ss 128–131) | Hardship request (National Credit Code s 72); complaint to AFCA |

Some lenders assess like-for-like refinances at a 1-point buffer as an exception to policy.
