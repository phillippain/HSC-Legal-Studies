A UNSW survey for Homelessness Australia, released before the mid-year budget update, found almost **two in three**
providers turning away people they would once have helped and **91%** reporting higher demand over two years.
Workers report offering **tents** and camping spots, and asking women fleeing violence whether their car locks.

## The numbers (AIHW 2024–25)

About **350 requests a day** went unassisted (about 129,000 a year), most often because no accommodation was
available. Family and domestic violence is the most common main reason for seeking help (27%). Of 90,400 clients
homeless when support began, **56,400** were still homeless when it ended; only **3.9%** of those needing long-term
housing got it. 95% of providers say social housing shortages limit them.

## Asks v commitments

| | Homelessness Australia asks | Current commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Social housing | 34,000 new social homes every year | 55,000 social and affordable homes in total; 7,200 complete |
| Income support | At least $600 a week | JobSeeker about $409 a week |
| Services | Substantially more funding | $400m a year under the national agreement, matched by states |
| Supportive housing | 20,000 permanent places for complex needs | None found |
| Crisis housing | — | $300m HAFF grants for about 500 homes by 2029 |
