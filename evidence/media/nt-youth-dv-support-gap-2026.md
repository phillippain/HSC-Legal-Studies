NT Police figures obtained by the ABC show **233 DV-related charges against under-18s** between March and September 2026; the most common was serious assault, then choking or strangulation, then threats to kill. Two teenage girls were allegedly killed by older partners at Galiwin'ku (July) and Alpurrurulam (October); men aged 34 and 26 have been charged with murder and are presumed innocent. The NTCOSS, AMSANT and KWILS warn of a **"massive" gap** in support for young victims in remote communities. **Charges are not convictions, and the data do not show victims' ages or whether they were partners or family.**

## Legal pathways for a remote young victim

1. **Disclosure** — every NT adult must report DV causing or likely to cause serious harm (s 124A).
2. **Police** — an urgent s 28 DVO on the spot, even if neither party agrees, with a four-hour hold.
3. **Court** — a DVO with safety paramount; a 14–17-year-old needs the **court's leave** to apply herself (s 52(3)).
4. **Charges** — choking in a domestic relationship (Criminal Code s 186AA, up to five years, no injury required); breaching a DVO is an offence.
5. **Support** — legal help (KWILS), clinics and safe accommodation should follow, but crisis accommodation already cannot meet demand.

*Section numbers come from the NT Law Handbook summary, not the Act itself.*

## Legal v non-legal responses

| | Legal | Non-legal |

| Early intervention | 233 charges | Circuit Breaker not offered remotely |
