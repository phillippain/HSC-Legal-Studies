Between 2023 and 2025, Bathla-linked buyers failed to complete three land purchases worth a combined **$130m**. In
***Ryan v UPG 322*** a $39.5m Box Hill contract, guaranteed by founder **Bhart Bhushan** as principal, was due to
settle on 18 April 2023; after the notice to complete and two extensions failed, the Supreme Court ordered
**specific performance** on 31 October 2023 — and the buyer missed that deadline too. Bathla entered **voluntary
administration** on 25 August 2026 across 542 entities, owing about **$3–3.6 billion**, with about **1,000** buyer
deposits held for unfinished homes.

## A seller's remedies when the buyer won't settle

| Remedy | What it gives | The Bathla limit |
|---|---|---|
| Specific performance | Court order forcing the buyer (and guarantor) to pay | Deadline missed anyway |
| Guarantor liability | Director liable as principal | Only as good as his assets |
| Terminate and keep deposit | End the deal and resell | Weaker market, maybe a lower price |
| Damages | Resale shortfall and losses | Unsecured once the buyer is in administration |
| Default interest | Interest on the late price | Buyer renamed and liquidated within a month |

Administration halts proceedings against the company without consent or leave (s 440D). Off-the-plan deposits must
sit in trust until settlement, but deposits paid directly to the developer are likely unsecured.
