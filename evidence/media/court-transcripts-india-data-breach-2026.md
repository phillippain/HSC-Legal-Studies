The federal courts outsource transcription. In **December 2014** the former national manager of the
contractor Auscript told both courts and the AFP that court audio had been sent to **India** for a
seven-month pilot, in breach of a contract banning offshoring without approval. The Family Court
replied about **two years later**, calling the breach "technical, minor and inconsequential". In
**February 2026** the ABC revealed that staff of e24, a Chennai subcontractor of the next provider VIQ
Solutions, had accessed thousands of court files; VIQ confirmed "unauthorised access by its global
personnel", went into voluntary administration and stopped providing services on 30 June 2026. The
courts told Senate estimates in May that up to 136 (FCFCOA) and 10 (Federal Court) files might be
affected and that CyberCX had been engaged.

## Promised v happened

| What was promised | What happened |
|---|---|
| Contracts barred moving court data offshore | Data accessed in India on at least two occasions |
| *Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013* (Cth): allocate within 14 days, investigate within 90 | The 2014 disclosures were answered about two years later |
| Court policy: consider notifying people of a data breach | By May 2026 no notices to affected litigants |

On 23 October 2026 the Coalition and the Greens urged the Attorney-General to order an independent
audit; the OAIC is examining a privacy complaint. *Family Law Act* s 114Q restricts publishing
identifying family law material.

## Caution

A class **evidence summary of investigative reporting** that leans towards the whistleblower's
account. Whether the then acting CEO was notified in 2016 is **disputed**; the number of transcripts
involved was still unknown. The courts say data stayed on encrypted Sydney servers. The FCFCOA's own
June 2026 notices on transcription services confirm the change of provider.
