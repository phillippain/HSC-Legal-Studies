Anglicare Australia tested **48,776 rental listings** (collected 14–15 March 2026) against the full-time adult award wage of **16 essential occupations**. "Affordable" means rent of no more than 30% of a single person's income. A nurse could afford **1.1%** of listings (558), a hospitality worker **0.7%** (338), and **no** listings were affordable for a hospitality worker in the NT. Ten of the 16 occupations could afford fewer than 1%; teachers fared best at 3.3%. Rents rose **40.6%** from July 2021 to 2026 against wage growth of **18.8%**.

Only three occupations (aged care workers, early childhood educators, teachers) improved, each by 0.1 percentage points.

## Challenging an excessive rent increase in NSW

1. **Notice** — written notice of the new rent at least 60 days ahead; most tenancies allow one increase in any 12 months.
2. **Check validity** — an invalid notice need not be paid; an invalid increase already paid can be reclaimed at NCAT within 12 months.
3. **Apply to NCAT** — within 30 days of the notice, under *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* s 44.
4. **Tribunal** — compares rents for similar premises; it does **not** consider the tenant's income.

Because the test is market rent, s 44 protects against outliers, **not market-wide rises** like those in the report. NSW limits the *frequency* of increases and ended no-grounds evictions in May 2025, but sets no cap on the *amount*.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a news report of an advocacy organisation's report; Anglicare campaigns for social housing and rent limits. The data are one March weekend of advertised listings and a single-earner benchmark, which overstates difficulty for multi-income households (a couple with two children on two minimum wages could afford 14.8%). The Cotality unit and house rent figures were not checked. NSW law is the example; other states differ. Check the full Anglicare report before quoting figures.
