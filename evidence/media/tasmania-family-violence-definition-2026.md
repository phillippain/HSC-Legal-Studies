After a Tasmanian man was charged (reported 30 August 2026) with murdering his mother, police said it
would not be treated as family violence — because the *Family Violence Act 2004* (Tas) covers only
**spouses and partners**. **The charge is an allegation.**

## Tasmania v Victoria

| | Tasmania (2004) | Victoria (2008) |
|---|---|---|
| Relationships covered | Spouse or partner, incl. significant relationships | Spouses, partners, relatives, people treated as family, carers |
| Excluded | Parents, children, siblings, relatives, carers | Few |
| Orders and support | Tied to the partner definition | Extend to family members |
| Data | Non-partner incidents not counted as family violence | Counted |

Tasmanian family violence incidents rose **3,936 → 6,488** (2020–21 to 2024–25, +65%). Police family
violence orders last up to 12 months; court orders on the balance of probabilities (ss 15–16); breach
penalties rise to 5 years. The criminal law still applies to the violence itself — the definition
governs orders, the Act's abuse offences, counting and funding. Compare **NSW**, whose *Crimes
(Domestic and Personal Violence) Act* covers relatives and carers, and the *Family Law Act* s 4AB.

## Reform

Discussion paper (Nov 2025) → reforms announced (May 2026, $82 million) → draft **Family Violence
Reforms Bill 2026** consultation (closing 4 October 2026), with a further 18-month tranche. The releases
do **not** say the definition will cover non-partner relatives.
