"Jack" entered state care in **2019**, aged 13, when caseworkers suggested a temporary placement away
from his mother Renee. "It was a gut-wrenching decision, and not one taken lightly … It was the most
painful thing I ever had to do," she said. She was told he would be cared for by professionals and
supported to return home.

For the **five years before his death** he was under the **parental responsibility of the Minister**.
He moved between state-run group homes, hotels and motels — some, his mother says, with mould and
asbestos — and slept on the street. He stopped going to school, had minimal supervision and was
exposed to drugs. He died in a fall in the Blue Mountains **the day after his eighteenth birthday**.

Before care he had been at a specialist school with a part-time job and a paediatric psychiatrist in
Orange, or living with his mother's former partner Mick in Dubbo with a job and an apprenticeship.

## The system around him

- **15,000 children and young people** are in the NSW child protection system. The overseeing
  minister, **Kate Washington**, has called it "**broken**" and says she has "**limited visibility**"
  over it. She called Jack's death "truly heartbreaking": "he had a right to feel supported and safe".
- Organisations providing **residential care** can be paid between **$119,000 and $504,000 per child
  per year**, depending on the level of therapeutic intervention.
- A **2024 Ombudsman report** found **one in three children had a substantiated allegation of abuse
  while in residential care**, but a **shortage of caseworkers** meant allegations were not fully
  investigated.
- A DCJ staff member described forms of residential care as a "**petri dish for the juvenile justice
  system**" with no structure from workers.
- In 2022 he was placed, for a 12-week emergency placement, in a group home in **Inverell — 600
  kilometres from Orange** — run by the not-for-profit **Pathfinders**, in a Supported Transition and
  Engagement Program for children with complex needs "where other care arrangements have been
  exhausted". His own child protection counsellor raised "significant concerns" that **none of the
  trauma-informed or therapeutic care advertised was being delivered**, and advised returning him to
  Orange "as soon as possible". The move also **discharged him from his mental health services and
  paediatric psychiatrist**.
- Back in the central west his care was outsourced to **Life Without Barriers**. In **2023–24** LWB
  recorded **$845 million in revenue and other income**, of which financial documents show
  **$93 million** was spent on service delivery costs; youth worker jobs advertise at **$36 an hour**
  and can be filled by TAFE students with relevant experience. In 2022 its CEO apologised after the
  **Disability Royal Commission** found it had failed to prevent violence and abuse against residents
  in its group disability homes.
- **Mick** was assessed by caseworkers as "totally dedicated to meeting all of Jack's needs" and as a
  **suitable permanent placement**. He quit his job, sold shares in his businesses and completed
  parenting and child psychology courses while waiting to be approved. Promised payments never
  arrived; LWB denied promising them but paid him a lump sum in January 2024 for the "**unacceptable
  delay**" in completing his assessment.

## Caution

**A child died. Handle this with care and flag it before use.** It involves **suicide attempts, a
fatal fall, self-harm, alleged assault of a child by a carer, and drug exposure**. The family asked
that the name and some details be withheld — "Jack" and "Renee" are as published — so **do not
attempt to identify them**, and the Herald published the piece to illuminate the system, which is how
it should be used.

Most of the account comes from **Renee's emails to DCJ and her recollection**: the assault, the two
suicide attempts, being found unresponsive, the wrong medication and the syringes are **her
allegations**, not findings. **DCJ said a number of issues "do not accurately reflect this incredibly
complex matter" but did not elaborate**; **Pathfinders** said children in its program receive a
"structured, fully supervised 24/7 routine" and were unaware of any counsellor's report;
**Life Without Barriers declined to comment**. There is **no coronial finding, inquiry or judgment**
reported here — write it as a family's account of failures, not as established fact, and do not name
either organisation as having caused his death. The LWB financial comparison ($845m income against
$93m "service delivery costs") is the journalist's reading of financial documents and the accounting
categories are not explained; treat it as reported, not as a measure of waste. Published **May 2025**
— check for an inquest, an Ombudsman follow-up or a departmental response before using it.
