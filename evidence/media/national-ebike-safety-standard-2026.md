Consumer affairs ministers backed national rules in July 2026; on 6 August the government directed the ACCC to develop an
ACL safety standard. ACCC consultation (nine options) runs **30 October to 30 November 2026**, with a recommendation to the
Minister possible in early 2027. The ACCC estimates e-bike injuries and deaths cost at least **$28m a year** (about $27m of
it valuing 23 deaths); Fire and Rescue NSW attended **786** e-micromobility fires (2022–2025).

## State patchwork v proposed national standard

| | Now | Proposed (consultation options) |
|---|---|---|
| What can be sold | No national e-bike supply rule | Only e-bikes meeting EN 15194 or another referenced standard |
| Power and speed | Varies; NSW still allows 500 W pedelecs, moving to 250 W | 250 W continuous, 25 km/h assistance |
| Batteries, chargers | Only NSW prescribes standards | A safety standard for separately sold batteries and chargers |
| Conversion kits | Not covered | Power limits plus pre-sale information |
| Tampering | Left to state road enforcement | Rules on tampering devices at supply; scope open |
| Riding | State road rules; NSW seizure powers from 21 August 2026 | Unchanged — the standard covers sale only |

High-powered e-bikes can currently be **sold** for private-property use even though they cannot be ridden in public.

## Use it for

- **Year 11 Changing the law** — reform responding to technology (chg.1.3) through executive methods: ministerial
  direction, regulator consultation, then a standard made by the Minister (chg.3.2).
- **Division of powers** — the Commonwealth regulates supply, the states regulate use.
