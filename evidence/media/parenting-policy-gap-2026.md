Professor **Daryl Higgins** says parents carry primary responsibility for children (**CRC art 18**) yet are "largely
invisible" in policy, which funds crisis systems: **$11.3 billion** on child protection and family support in
2024–25, **63.6%** of it on care services. The Child Maltreatment Study found **62.2%** of Australians experienced
maltreatment, and 65% of young people under youth justice supervision had child protection contact.

## Policies aimed at children v supports for parents

| | Aimed at children | Support for parents |
|---|---|---|
| National framework | Safe and Supported: cut abuse and neglect | Focus area 1: early support for vulnerable families |
| Early years | Early Years Strategy outcomes | Priority to empower parents; no stand-alone parenting strategy |
| NSW child protection | Acts once children are at risk | **Parent responsibility contracts** (s 38A): courses, counselling, treatment, up to 12 months |
| Family law | Shorter best-interests list (May 2024) | Presumption of equal shared parental responsibility removed |
| Income and leave | — | Parental Leave Pay **26 weeks** from July 2026; Parenting Payment to youngest child's 14th birthday |

## Use it for

- **fam.3.2 (changing parental responsibility)** — who carries responsibility, and how law and policy support or
  police it.
- **Prevention v crisis** — whether a preventive, parent-focused model would better protect children's rights.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of opinion**; the author leads the maltreatment study he cites and names no specific
program. His claim that Australia lacks policy for parents is overstated — the supports exist but are not coherent.
