The Medicare rebate turned on a definition of **infertility** framed around an inability to conceive
after a period of regular unprotected sex — so **solo women and LGBTIQ+ couples** could face the full
cost of a first IVF cycle, worth thousands.

The new definition is **"the inability to achieve a successful pregnancy based on a patient's
medical, sexual, and reproductive history, age, physical findings, diagnostic testing, or any
combinations of these factors"**. ANZSREI president Dr Alison Gee called it a significant step toward
"more equitable access to fertility care for same-sex and solo parents", after the society and the
peak bodies had pushed for the change for years.

**Surrogacy was not included.** Male same-sex couples therefore still miss out. One interviewee, a
transgender man, qualified only because he was classified as an **infertile male** — "if we had tried
to have a baby pre-transition I wouldn't have gotten the rebate, that doesn't seem fair."

## Use it for

- **Reform by definition rather than by statute.** No Act was amended; a clinical definition inside a
  benefits schedule was rewritten, and access changed for a whole class of people. It is a precise
  example of how much law sits below the level of legislation (`ls.2.5`, `ls.4.3`).
- **Changing community values** (`fam.t.values`) — the law catching up with who is understood to be a
  family, and doing it through the health system.
- **The gap that remains**: the exclusion of surrogacy means the reform equalises access for those who
  can carry a pregnancy and not for those who cannot. That is a sharp, checkable limit to put in an
  evaluation instead of a general claim about progress.
