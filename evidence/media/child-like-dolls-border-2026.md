Researchers who work with the ABF argue that border detections of **child-like sex dolls** carry
child-protection information and should prompt investigation, not just seizure. Home Affairs
detections reportedly fell from **103 to 29** between 2020–21 and 2022–23 (primary data not located).

## The law

| Offence | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Possession, Criminal Code (Cth) **s 273A.1** (added 2019) | **15 years** |
| Importing Tier 2 goods, Customs Act **s 233BAB(5)** | **10 years** or a fine up to $782,500 |

Test: the doll resembles a person under 18 and a reasonable person would consider it likely intended
for sexual use. Defences include lawful purpose; law enforcement and approved research are excluded.

## Border to court

Detection (intelligence-led) → classification against the statutory test → **search warrant** at the
delivery address, devices seized → **joint AFP–state police** examination for further offences →
charges in a higher court. In the **2026 Perth case**, an intercepted import led to charges including a
child-abduction plot and a sentence of **7 years 5 months**.
