Anko camera glasses — **$89**, 8MP stills, 1080p video, paired to the Chinese **HeyCyan** app — went on
sale at Kmart and Target on **28 July 2026** and sold out nationally in about a week. Ten weeks later
the product was gone from Kmart's website and the regulator had opened an investigation. **No law had
changed.**

## Ten weeks

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 28 Jul | On sale at Kmart and Target |
| ~5 Aug | Sell out nationally; **GetUp petition** (led by Gina Martin) launches — **43,000+ signatures** |
| 7 Aug | The **Attorney-General** asks the Privacy Commissioner to prioritise the issue |
| 10 Aug | **Electronic Frontiers Australia** calls for a sale and import ban |
| 25 Aug | **Brisbane City Council** bans covert filming at its pools |
| 27 Aug | Greens and crossbench announce an import-ban bill; the government says no to a ban |
| 22 Sep | **ABC testing** finds 12+ security flaws in the HeyCyan app |
| 7 Oct | **OAIC opens a formal investigation** into the app's developer, Shenzhen Qingcheng; the Kmart listing is removed |

## The legal gap

The ***Privacy Act 1988*** (Cth) binds **organisations and agencies, not individuals**, so the person
wearing the glasses is outside it — the Privacy Commissioner has said so. The statutory **privacy
tort** (Sch 2, in force 10 June 2025) is the only federal route against an individual, and it requires
the victim to sue and prove intent. NSW's *Surveillance Devices Act 2007* s 8 and *Crimes Act 1900*
ss 91K–M reach **private** premises and acts, not filming in public. The OAIC's $50m-per-contravention
penalty is aimed at a company in Shenzhen that has ignored every request.

## Use it for

- **ls.4.1 / ls.4.3 (conditions and mechanisms of reform)** — new technology as the classic condition,
  and a clean demonstration that the *mechanism* that matters, Parliament, has not moved: the ALRC
  recommended the tort in **2014** and it began in **2025**; the "tranche 2" *Privacy Act* reform
  agreed in September 2023 is still unwritten.
- **ls.4.2 (agencies of reform)** — a petition, an NGO, the media and a regulator all acted inside ten
  weeks. The media's security testing is what triggered the formal investigation.
- **ind.3.3 / ind.3.4 (enforcing rights, future directions)** — most people never learn they were
  filmed, so a right that depends on discovering the breach cannot deter misuse.
- **Federalism (ls.2.6)** — privacy is mostly Commonwealth, surveillance and voyeurism offences are
  state, and Brisbane used a council rule: no single parliament can close the gap alone.
- **lip.2.3 (evaluating responses)** — the sharpest line in the summary: *non-legal responses removed
  the product; only law reform can protect people from the next one.*
