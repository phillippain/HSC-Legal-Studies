Kariong Intake and Transit Centre (Central Coast) receives people refused bail before transfer to a
correctional centre. The Inspector inspected it in December 2025 and the 13 court cell complexes to June
2026; the report was tabled on **20 October 2026** with **four recommendations**.

## The standard v what was found

| Standard | Finding |
|---|---|
| CSNSW policy: pads or tampons on reception, more on request | Supplies very limited; **women went without for days**, some transported while menstruating |
| Transfer within **seven days** (s 72(3)) | **29** people held longer at Kariong (one for 11 days); **100+** across court cells |
| Cells built for stays of up to 24 hours | **Average stay 27 hours** |
| Bangkok Rule 5: free sanitary towels for women prisoners | Not met — but the UN rules are guidance, not binding law |
| Basic dignity (Mandela Rules) | Some denied outdoor time and soap; one moved without shirt or shoes |

**Why:** rising numbers refused **bail by police**. In June 2026, **46%** of NSW adults in custody were on
remand; Aboriginal people were **33.9%** of adults in custody.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.8 / crime.2.5** — conditions of remand for people **not yet convicted**, and the knock-on effect
  of bail decisions.
- **Effectiveness of oversight** — the Inspector can visit at any time and table reports in Parliament, but
  cannot enforce recommendations or investigate individual complaints; period products have been raised
  in earlier inspections too.
