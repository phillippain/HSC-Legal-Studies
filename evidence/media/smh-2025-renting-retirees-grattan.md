Australia's retirement income system assumes you own your home. This is the evidence of what happens
when you do not.

## The figures

- **Two in three** retirees who rent privately **live in poverty**. Three-quarters of retirees own
  their home, and most of those report feeling financially comfortable.
- **Half of retirees renting in the private market have less than $25,000 in savings** (as the SMH
  reports it); Grattan's own measure is that the poorest 40% of renting households aged **55–64 have
  less than $40,000 in net financial wealth**.
- Homeownership among the **poorest 40% of 45–54 year olds fell from 68% in 1981 to 54% in 2021**.
- About **a third of retirees who rent today live in social housing**, where rents are capped at
  **30% of income** — and, as Grattan's Brendan Coates says, "far fewer can expect to do so in future
  because there simply isn't enough social housing to meet demand".
- Over the past two budgets the Commonwealth raised the **maximum rate of Rent Assistance by 27%**
  above inflation — but **rents paid by recipients have risen nearly 1.5 times faster than the
  maximum rate since 2001**.

## What Grattan asks for

A further **50% increase for singles** (about **$53 a week**, $2,750 a year) and **40% for couples**
(about **$40 a week**, $2,080 a year) — enough to afford the **cheapest 25% of one- and two-bedroom
homes in capital cities**. Coates also supports measures to boost supply, but says the rent
assistance rise is the **immediate** step.

> "Today's low-income renters are tomorrow's renting retirees."

The article sets this against the policy context of the time: Labor's pledge of **1.2 million homes
by mid-2029**, a $10,000 incentive for construction workers, prefabricated housing, and a Coalition
plan to stop foreign investors buying residential property.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.2 — the long-run consequence of renting, not just the tenancy.** Most tenancy evidence is
  about an eviction or a rent rise. This is about what a **lifetime of renting does by the time you
  are 70**, and it is the single strongest "why does this matter" statistic in the Shelter option.
- **"Today's low-income renters are tomorrow's renting retirees"** is the quote. It converts a
  present problem into a projected one, which is what an evaluation of **responsiveness** needs.
- **Rent assistance as the test of effectiveness (shel.3.4)** — a payment raised **27% above
  inflation** and still falling behind, because rents grew **1.5 times faster** for two decades. A
  measurable, dateable gap between a legal response and the problem it addresses. Pair it with the
  record on Commonwealth and NSW rental subsidies.
- **The retirement system's hidden assumption (shel.1.3)** — the age pension and superannuation are
  calibrated to people who own their home outright. Owning or renting is therefore not just a housing
  question but a **retirement income** question, which is a sophisticated point few students make.
- **Social housing as the only real protection, and shrinking (shel.3.4)** — rents capped at **30% of
  income** for the third of renting retirees who get in, and no prospect of that share holding. Use
  it for whether the state's response is adequate rather than merely present.
- **Homeownership falling only for the poor (shel.2.1)** — 68% to 54% among the **poorest 40%**. The
  distributional detail is what makes it an equality argument rather than a generational one.

## Caution

A **newspaper report of a Grattan Institute report**: go to *Renting in retirement: Why Rent
Assistance needs to rise* (Coates, Bowes and Moloney, **9 February 2025**) for anything load-bearing,
and attribute the recommendations to **Grattan**, not to government. Grattan is a **think tank with a
policy position** — it has argued for higher rent assistance and for supply reform for years — so the
numbers are credible but the prescription is advocacy, and a balanced answer should note the fiscal
cost and the argument that rent assistance can be capitalised into higher rents. The two savings
figures are **different measures** ($25,000 as the SMH reports it; Grattan's own is net financial
wealth under $40,000 for the poorest 40% of 55–64 year old renters) — do not merge them. "Poverty"
here is a relative measure; check Grattan's definition before quoting it. Published **February 2025**,
before that year's federal election, so the party policies described are campaign positions that may
not have survived; check the current maximum rate of Commonwealth Rent Assistance and the status of
the 1.2 million homes target before using either. The PDF is a newspaper page and includes unrelated
weather coverage.
