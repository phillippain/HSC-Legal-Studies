On the 35th anniversary of the National Report, NITV reported Senator Lidia Thorpe's count of **at least 630** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander deaths in custody since April 1991, and her statement that the majority of the 339 recommendations "had not been properly implemented". The Australian Institute of Criminology's Deaths in Custody program, which the Commission itself recommended, publishes the official annual series and is the primary source for any year's figure.

## What the number measures

- Deaths in prison, police custody and police operations, of all causes — natural causes are the largest category in prison deaths, which is itself a finding about the age and health of the prison population.
- The rate of Aboriginal imprisonment has roughly doubled since 1991: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were about 14% of prisoners in 1991 and are about a third today, at more than 2,400 per 100,000 adults.

## Use it for

- **dis.3.7** — outcomes of the Royal Commission: the recommendations that were implemented (Custody Notification Service, cell design, the AIC monitoring program) and the one that was not (last resort).
- **chg.4.2 / exp.6.2** — limitations of reform by recommendation; a barrier to justice measured over 35 years.

## Caution

The 630 figure is a senator's tally, not an official statistic; the AIC series is the primary source and counts slightly differently. Use the trend, not a decimal.
