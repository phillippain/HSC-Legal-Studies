On **9 February 2026** a rally at Sydney Town Hall against the visit of Israel's President Herzog
ended in OC spray, arrests and hundreds of complaints. It took place under two special instruments:
a **Public Assembly Restriction Declaration** under the *Terrorism (Police Powers) Act 2002* s 23B
(made after the Bondi attack, later held **invalid** in *Jarrett* [2026] NSWCA 62) and a **major
event declaration** under the *Major Events Act 2009* (upheld by the Supreme Court that morning).

The LECC opened its investigation, **Operation Makalu**, in February. On **29 September** it rejected
the Police Commissioner's bid for wholly private hearings; on **19 October 2026** counsel assisting
opened the examinations in public.

## What the numbers say

- **823 complaints** reported — 556 to the LECC, 267 to NSW Police (ABC figures; the LECC publishes no total).
- **About 6,000** at the rally on the police estimate; the NSW Council for Civil Liberties says about 20,000.
- **27 arrested**, nine charged on the night (police). More than **1,000 hours** of CCTV reviewed by May 2026.
- Eight police witnesses were examined **privately** in June–July 2026 before the public opening.

## How a Part 6 investigation runs

1. Complaints → decision to investigate as a matter of public interest (Part 6, *LECC Act 2016*).
2. Footage, documents and witness accounts gathered; police cannot see what the public supplied unless the LECC discloses it.
3. **Private examinations** (s 63): operational officers and civilians; police up to inspector use pseudonyms.
4. **Public examinations** of senior commissioned officers about what the private evidence showed.
5. A **s 132 report to Parliament** — mandatory because public hearings were held; no date set.

Possible outcomes: briefs referred to the **DPP**, information referred to NSW Police for
**discipline**, or **systemic recommendations** on protest policing, training and command. Counsel
assisting said it plainly: not a criminal trial, not a corruption hearing, no findings yet.

## The legal standard being tested

Police may use only the force **reasonably necessary** to arrest or prevent escape (**LEPRA s 231**).
Neither a PARD nor a major event declaration expands that limit — which is why a declaration later
struck down still matters: it shaped the confrontation without changing what force was lawful.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **news report of an opening address**, not findings. No officer has been
found to have done anything wrong and every allegation is untested. The complaint totals are the
ABC's, the crowd figure is contested, and the article's pseudonym rank (below inspector) differs
slightly from the LECC decision (up to and including inspector). The article does not mention that
the PARD scheme was struck down in April 2026. Check the final s 132 report before drawing
conclusions, and never identify complainants or pseudonymous officers.
