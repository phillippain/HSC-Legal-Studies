A 2013 change to Victoria's **Open Courts Act** from "physical safety" to "safety" let courts suppress an
accused's identity on **mental health** grounds. After men whose names were suppressed were later
convicted of rape, media leaders and the Press Club pushed back; on **11 August 2026** the Premier
pledged to stop such orders protecting convicted rapists and the Department will consult on the law.

## Victoria now v proposed or NSW

| | Victoria now | Proposed / NSW comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Safety ground | "Safety" lets in mental-health claims | Tighten mental-health grounds |
| Interim orders | Can be made without deciding merits (s 20) | Strict time limits, as in NSW (s 12 of the 2010 NSW Act) |
| Duration | Must be stated; up to 5 years if tied to an uncertain event | Review how long orders last |
| Pseudonym orders | Outside the Act's notice rules | Bring them under the Act |
| Media notice | Three business days; courts must notify media | Enforce compliance |

Press Club study: **521 of 1,113** orders in 2023 were made in Victoria (contested as incomplete;
the Chief Justice called it misleading). Bosland says **99%** of orders are lifted on conviction, so a
conviction-only fix changes little. ***Hogan v Hinch*** (2011): open justice is not absolute.
