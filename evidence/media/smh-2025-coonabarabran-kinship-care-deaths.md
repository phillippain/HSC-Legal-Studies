Two boys, **aged six and seven** — one of whom **lived with cancer** — were found dead at a home in
**Coonabarabran**, a town of about 2,500 in central-western NSW, on Monday 5 May 2025.

Their grandmother **contacted a Department of Communities and Justice caseworker** to say the two
primary schoolers were dead. Two junior police officers forced their way into the home. They found
the six-year-old in a front bedroom, then the grandmother, who had **self-harmed**, then the elder boy
in another bedroom. She was taken to hospital in Orange **under police guard** and, as at publication,
**had not been charged**; Assistant Commissioner Andrew Holland said that on release "it is likely she
will face serious charges". A post-mortem was scheduled.

## The care history

DCJ became involved "due to concerns for their welfare several years ago". The boys were **removed
from their parents** towards the end of the COVID pandemic, **placed under the care of the Minister**,
and placed with their grandmother in what is called **kinship care**. She was their **sole carer**.
They had moved to Coonabarabran **eleven months earlier** from the Central Coast. Their biological
parents live elsewhere in the state and, Holland said, "were not doing well".

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 — kinship care, named and defined.** The *Children and Young Persons (Care and
  Protection) Act 1998* and the **s 10A Permanent Placement Principles** rank placement with a
  relative or kin **second**, above adoption and above parental responsibility to the Minister,
  because it preserves family and cultural connection. This is that preference in operation, and its
  limits: a **sole carer**, hundreds of kilometres from the children's previous home, with the
  Minister still holding parental responsibility.
- **Where does responsibility sit?** The children were **under the care of the Minister** and living
  with a relative. Use it to ask the question the dot point is really about: when the state removes a
  child and places them with family, **who is supervising, how often, and with what resources**?
  Note that the grandmother's first call was to a **caseworker**, not to police or an ambulance — the
  department was the family's point of contact.
- **The permanency hierarchy's trade-off (fam.3.2)** — kinship care keeps children with family and
  avoids the residential care described in the Jack record, but it can also mean one unsupported older
  carer. Set this record against `smh-2025-jack-died-in-state-care` and you have both failure modes:
  outsourced residential care, and under-supported kinship care. Neither is an argument for the other.
- **Isolation as a risk factor (fam.2.6)** — a town of 2,500, eleven months after a move from the
  Central Coast, a child with cancer, a sole carer. Services, respite and oversight are all thinner,
  and that is a structural point about regional child protection rather than a judgement about anyone.
- **What happens next, legally (dis.3.6)** — on these facts the likely sequence is charges, and a
  **coronial inquest** or a **DCJ / Ombudsman review** into the department's involvement. Good for
  the oversight mechanisms, and worth comparing with `inquest-db-2025`.

## Caution

**Two children died and nobody had been charged** at the time of publication. The grandmother is
named only as "the grandmother"; she was **in hospital under police guard**, **had not been charged**,
and is entitled to the presumption of innocence — "alleged murder" is the masthead's headline, not a
finding, and the **cause of death was not established** (the post-mortem had not occurred). Do not
state that she killed them. She had **self-harmed**, which points to a possible mental health crisis
with no assessment reported, and that possibility must not be presented as fact either.

This is **news reporting two days in**, sourced partly to a police press conference and partly to
unnamed "sources", and much of the care history is thin: the reasons for removal are given only as
"concerns for their welfare". **The outcome is not recorded** — check for charges, a trial, an
inquest, an Ombudsman or DCJ review, and the Child Death Review Team's reporting before using this,
because any of those would supply findings this article cannot. The children were **living,
identifiable minors** in a very small town, one with a diagnosed illness; **do not reproduce
identifying detail** and think carefully before classroom use. The record uses this for the
**structure** of kinship care and oversight, not for the facts of the deaths.
