An ABC lifestyle feature on taking in a housemate to cover costs (with an estimate of up to **13
million spare bedrooms** in Australian homes) explains the Queensland rules. The class summary adds
the NSW position, where the legal label decides whether tenancy law applies at all.

## NSW: three positions

| | Tenant or sub-tenant | Boarding-house resident | Boarder or lodger in a private home |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Law** | *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* | *Boarding Houses Act 2012* (5+ residents) | **Excluded by RTA s 8(1)(c)** — contract and common law only |
| **Bond** | Max 4 weeks, lodged with Fair Trading | Deposit up to 2 weeks | No duty to lodge; ask for a receipt |
| **Ending it** | Valid reason required since **19 May 2025**, 30–90 days' notice | Occupancy principles; reasonable notice | Notice per agreement or "reasonable", e.g. 7 days for weekly rent |
| **Rent rises** | Once every 12 months (since October 2024) | 4 weeks' notice | Set by the agreement |
| **Disputes** | NCAT | NCAT | Local Court, or NCAT General Division if run as a business |

A tenant who sub-lets part of the premises needs the landlord's written consent (s 74), which cannot
be unreasonably refused for part of the premises (s 75).

## Caution

The **article covers Queensland only**; the NSW comparison is the summary's own, built from legislation
and Tenants' Union factsheets (some dated 2023). It is a consumer-advice piece written for owners and
head tenants. The 13 million spare bedrooms is a modelled estimate (AHURI), not available housing.
