The 2026 Budget made the first change to **negative gearing** and the **capital gains tax discount**
since the discount was introduced in 1999 — the reform Labor took to the 2016 and 2019 elections,
lost, and dropped. The class brief sets out the mechanics, the perspectives and the dot-point links.

## What changes

| Measure | Before | From 1 July 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| **Negative gearing** | Rental losses deductible against *all* income, including wages | For **established homes bought after budget night**, losses only offset against residential property income or carried forward; new builds still fully deductible |
| **CGT discount** | 50% of the gain tax-free if held over 12 months | Replaced by **inflation indexation** with a minimum 30% rate on gains; gains before July 2027 keep the old treatment |
| **Grandfathering** | — | Properties bought before budget night keep the old rules |
| **Supply measures** | — | $2 billion infrastructure for up to 65,000 homes; $500 million to speed approvals; foreign-investor ban on established homes extended; ~$60 million over four years to house 4,300 homeless young people |

**Treasury modelling**: about **75,000** homes move from investors to first-home buyers over a decade;
supply falls by **35,000**; other measures add **65,000** — a net **+30,000**; rents "may rise
slightly".

## Perspectives

- **Treasurer Jim Chalmers**: the reforms "level the playing field for workers and first home
  buyers"; "we can't see these tax arrangements lock more and more people out of the housing market".
- **ACOSS**: "historic housing and trust tax reforms" — but "people with the least miss out again":
  little direct help for renters, welfare recipients or homeless people. **Mission Australia**:
  stronger intervention still needed on homelessness, rental stress and social housing.
- **Property investors and industry**: "Mess with negative gearing … watch what happens. That's one
  less rental property out there" (investor Jason Sukkar); fewer rentals, higher rents.
- **AFR**: "A wealth hit to woo next gens"; "The great Australian dream has become a great
  Australian tax rort" — and, in its editorial, "Labor's anti-Boomer measures won't make Australia
  better off overall". **Pitcher Partners**: "a seismic tax reset". **Westpac Economics**: the old
  settings encouraged leveraged investment.
- **Coalition**: higher rents, less investment, less supply.

## Caution

A **class compilation of news reports**, not the Budget papers — the figures are as reported, and
the legislation implementing the changes (and its passage through the Senate) should be checked on
aph.gov.au before stating the measures are law. The reforms start in **2027**, so no evidence of
effect exists yet; Treasury's modelling is a forecast. Quotes are attributed in the brief to their
outlets; find the originals before citing them in an essay.
