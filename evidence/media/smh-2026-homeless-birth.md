A woman living in a tent through her pregnancy went into labour still homeless. Homes NSW,
emergency accommodation and welfare support all existed; none reached her before the crisis.

## The evaluation the article supports

- **Protection of individual rights** — a pregnant mother and a newborn without secure housing,
  despite a welfare system built for exactly that. Rights to safety, shelter, health and dignity
  were not delivered.
- **Responsiveness** — the system recognises homelessness as a serious issue and intervenes **too
  late**. Partially responsive is the honest verdict, not unresponsive.
- **Accessibility** — social housing exists in principle; waiting lists and shortage mean a
  vulnerable family cannot practically exercise the right. Pair with **57,000 households on the NSW
  Housing Register**.
- **Resource efficiency** — crisis response costs more than prevention, and produces a worse result.

## Use it for

The strongest single human illustration in the Shelter option, and the one that bridges into
**Family** and into Preliminary **Law in practice** — a group suffering disadvantage, with legal and
non-legal responses that can both be named and judged.

An answer that pairs this with a statistic is doing what the syllabus asks: the case proves the
possibility, the Census and waiting-list figures prove the scale.

## Caution

One family's experience. It shows what the system can fail to do, not how often it fails. Always
pair it with the homelessness or social housing evidence sheet.
