New Street (est. 1998) works with 10–17-year-olds who have sexually harmed others, and their families. Among
16–24-year-olds, **18.2%** report being abused as children by an adolescent, against **11.7%** by an adult. Demand exceeds
supply: **204 of 414** referrals were accepted from 2023–24 to 31 March 2025, with gaps in rural areas.

## Criminal response v therapeutic response

| | Criminal justice | New Street |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Children 10+; under-14s presumed doli incapax | Ages 10–17, not criminally prosecuted |
| Entry | Police investigation and charge | Referral, mostly Community Services and JIRT |
| Diversion | Listed sexual offences **excluded** from YOA warnings, cautions, conferences (s 8) | Runs outside the courts |
| Focus | Proof beyond reasonable doubt, then sentence | Acknowledge, take responsibility, stop |
| Family | Limited role | Whole-family model |
| Victim | Complainant and witness | Restitution only when counsellors approve |
| Outcome | Conviction or acquittal | 89% of completers stopped by case closure (2014) |

Treatment runs six months to two years (the 2014 completer average was 734 days); 3% recurrence at three-month follow-up.

## Use it for

- **crime.5.5 / crime.5.1** — prevention and rehabilitation instead of punishment, and why the law presumes young children
  lack criminal capacity.
- **Moral and ethical standards** — community demand for punishment against what reduces reoffending.

## Caution

A **sympathetic feature** with no victim-survivor, police or prosecution voices. The 89% figure covers 71 completers only;
long-term recidivism was not assessed and a new evaluation is due in 2026.
