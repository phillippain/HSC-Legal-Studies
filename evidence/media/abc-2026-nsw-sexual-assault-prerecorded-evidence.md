Attorney-General **Michael Daley** was to introduce the reforms in the NSW Parliament on
22 September 2026. Three parts:

| Change | What it does | Already exists for |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-recorded evidence-in-chief** | Recorded police interviews with complainants and special witnesses usable as evidence-in-chief in adult sexual assault proceedings | children under 16 and people with a cognitive impairment |
| **Ground rules hearings** | A three-year pilot (2027–2030) at Parramatta, Wollongong and Dubbo District Courts: directions before the complainant's evidence on how, and on what, they may be questioned | child sexual assault proceedings |
| **Counselling records** | Accessible by the court only "when it is necessary", pending a University of Sydney review of the Sexual Assault Communications Privilege | — |

Complainants **still have to appear for cross-examination and re-examination** — described by some
witnesses as bruising and invasive — until a separate review next year considers wholly pre-recorded
evidence.

## The responses

- **Women's Safety Commissioner Hannah Tonkin**: complainants often give detailed accounts "years
  after their assault"; recording earlier lets them start rebuilding their lives sooner.
- **Fair Agenda**: a step forward, but "this is not what we've been asking for" — they want
  cross-examination and re-examination pre-recorded too, and prosecutors, not police, conducting
  the recorded interview, because the police purpose is investigation, not trial.
- **Full Stop Australia**: "a step in the right direction".
- A petition led by journalist Nina Funnell for counselling records to be kept from accused people
  gathered almost 15,000 signatures.

## Use it for

- **crime.3.7** — use of evidence and witnesses: how the rules for vulnerable witnesses are being
  extended from children to adult complainants.
- **crime.3.2** — the adversary system's core, oral cross-examination, left intact: the reform shows
  where the balance between the accused's right to test evidence and the complainant's wellbeing
  currently sits.
- **Law reform** — incremental reform driven by NGOs (Fair Agenda, Full Stop), a statutory office
  (the Women's Safety Commissioner), a petition and the media; three separate reviews still to come.

## Caution

A **proposal**, reported on the day of introduction — check whether it passed, and in what form,
before relying on it as law. The article reports the government's and advocates' positions; the
defence perspective on pre-recorded evidence and access to counselling records is not put.
