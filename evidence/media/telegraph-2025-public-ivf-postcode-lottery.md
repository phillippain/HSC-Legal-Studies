**South Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the ACT** provide **no IVF through their
public systems** and have **no dedicated bulk-billing private clinics** offering low-cost cycles.
Victoria has a public system — Hollie Bennett, 39, had her daughter Bonnie through it.

**RANZCOG** deputy chair for Tasmania **Dr Lindsay Edwards** said it was "**not equitable**" to deny
women reproductive health care based on where they live or what they earn:

> "As an obstetrician in Tasmania, we hear of women withdrawing funds from their super just to be
> able to afford access to a health service that is publicly funded one state away in Victoria. It's
> not fair to make women choose between having a family and financial security in their retirement
> years."

## The superannuation numbers

Applications for **compassionate release of superannuation** for IVF have climbed steadily:

| Year | Approved amount |
|---|---|
| 2018–19 | about **$36.2 million** |
| 2022–23 | about **$47.9 million** |
| 2023–24 | about **$64.1 million** |

In **2023–24** there were about **5,200 applications** and about **4,210 approved** (ATO data). Some
clinics charge up to **$12,827** for a standard cycle. Bulk-billing clinics exist in some states but,
as **Fertility Society of Australia and New Zealand** board member **Dr Manuela Toledo** noted, they
still have **upfront costs** and often do not offer the more complicated IVF procedures. RANZCOG
"believes public fertility care should be made available in every state and territory".

## Caution

A **News Corp analysis**, not an official dataset: the state-by-state map is the paper's own work and
public IVF programs **change with each state budget**, so verify which states fund IVF before
teaching from it — the figures are as at **May 2025**. The super figures are **ATO data on
compassionate release approvals**, which covers more than IVF alone in some reporting, so present
them as approvals for fertility treatment as the article does rather than as a count of IVF cycles.
The **$12,827** is an upper figure "some clinics charge" for a standard cycle, not an average, and it
is before Medicare rebates. **RANZCOG and the Fertility Society are professional bodies** arguing for
expanded public funding; that is a legitimate position but it is a position. There is **no counter-
argument in the article** on the cost to the public purse or on competing health priorities, and a
balanced answer needs one. Hollie Bennett and her daughter are named and photographed with consent.
