After arrest, NSW police may detain a suspect for up to **6 hours** (extendable once by 6). A judge weighs **8** factors before admitting improperly obtained evidence (s 138). The *Lie Detectors Act 1983* likely already bans neural lie detection, and India's Supreme Court (*Selvi*, 2010) held brain-test results inadmissible even if voluntary.

## Safeguards v neurotechnology

| Traditional safeguard | Neurotechnology risk |
|---|---|
| Caution (LEPRA s 122); no adverse inference from silence (Evidence Act s 89) | EEG recognition responses are involuntary, so silence may not protect a suspect |
| Admissions excluded unless circumstances make untruth unlikely (s 85) | Neither recognition tests nor neural lie detectors are scientifically proven |
| Exclusion of improperly obtained evidence (s 138) | No law expressly authorises or regulates the technology, so s 138 may let it in |
