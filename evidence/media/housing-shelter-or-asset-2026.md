An ABC business analysis arguing that Australians' fixation on housing is **necessity, not
obsession**: concentrated banking, mostly variable-rate mortgages, and decades of policy treating
housing as an asset class. Its one legal line is that housing **is not considered a human right in
Australia**. The summary builds the legal framework around that claim.

## The two framings

| | Shelter: housing as a right | Asset: Australia's settings |
|---|---|---|
| International law | **ICESCR art 11(1)** — adequate standard of living including housing; art 2(1) progressive realisation | Australia is a party; the right is not fully legislated |
| Domestic recognition | ACT right to housing passed September 2025, **commencing January 2027** | No state or territory human rights law yet reflects it |
| Security of tenure | UN standard: legal protection against forced eviction | NSW ended no-grounds evictions from **19 May 2025** |
| Affordability | Tenants protected from unreasonable rents; subsidies | **0.56%** of advertised rentals affordable to a single minimum-wage worker (March 2026) |
| Tax | UN Committee urges a rights-based national housing strategy | Negative gearing limited for existing homes bought after 12 May 2026 |
| Rate risk | Long fixed loans common overseas (5 years UK, Canada) | Most Australian fixes last **three years or less** |

## The numbers

**122,494** people homeless on Census night 2021 · **31%** of homelessness service clients needing
emergency accommodation did not get it (2024–25) · **308,000** homes built towards the Housing
Accord's 1.2 million target, now expected December quarter 2030. **None of these appear in the
article.**

## Use it for

- **shel.1.2 / shel.1.3 (right to shelter, obligation to provide)** — the set-piece answer. Australia
  ratified ICESCR, appeared before the **UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights on 17
  February 2026**, and was urged to adopt a human rights-based national housing strategy, build more
  social housing, strengthen tenant protections and enact a national Human Rights Act, with a
  24-month report-back. Concluding observations are **recommendations, not binding judgments**.
- **ls.2.11 / ls.2.14 (international law into domestic law)** — a clean demonstration that a treaty
  binds Australia in international law but binds no Australian court until Parliament implements it.
  Implementation here is **piecemeal**: tenancy law, supply targets, and one territory-level right.
- **The ACT model** — from January 2027, ACT authorities must **consider** the right in decisions and
  policy. Worth noting that the amendment created no new complaint mechanism, and that recognising a
  right does not itself build houses (progressive realisation).
- **shel.2.2 / shel.2.3 (tenants)** — the article is about borrowers; renters are the sharper case,
  and NSW's valid-reason evictions and yearly rent-increase limit are the legal response to measure.
- **lip.2.3 (evaluating responses)** — competing frames: the AHRC and the UN Committee on one side;
  the Property Council arguing the 2026 tax changes will cost 35,000 homes and that about 40% of a
  new home's cost is taxes and charges on the other.

## Caution

**Economic commentary, not a legal development**: the article reports no new law, case or report, and
contains **no statistics** — every figure above comes from a separate verified source and should be
cited to that source, not to the ABC. Remarks attributed to RBA chief economist Sarah Hunter, Ross
Gittins and AustralianSuper's Paul Schroder are second-hand, and the Schroder quote and the 2009
"People's Bank" proposal could not be independently verified. The claim that housing "is not a human
right in Australia" is **about to change in one jurisdiction** — check the ACT commencement. The
Census homelessness figure is 2021.
