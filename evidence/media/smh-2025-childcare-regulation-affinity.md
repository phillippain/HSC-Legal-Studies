The regulatory arm of child protection, which Shelter-style "is the law effective?" questions reach
and most Family answers never do.

**Frankie Scott's** daughter **Stevie** attended the **Spring Farm** daycare centre in south-west
Sydney between **2020 and 2023**; **Affinity Education Group** took the centre over in **2022**.
Scott says Stevie was often collected in a soiled pull-up that had not been changed all day, or
changed back into urine-soaked pants rather than the spare clothes provided, and that she would
"end up holding her urine for so long… and it's caused damage to her bladder". In late 2023 Stevie,
now six, was **diagnosed with chronic kidney disease**; medical notes record that **recurrent urinary
tract infections contributed**, along with medication issues and congenital factors. She has had
several surgeries and will most likely need a **kidney transplant** in her teens. Scott emailed the
centre in December 2022: "This is pure neglect of Stevie's basic needs."

## What the regulator found

A cache of internal regulatory documents was released following a **parliamentary order obtained by
NSW Greens MP Abigail Boyd**. The compliance notices issued to the Spring Farm centre record:

- a child made to drink their **liquid medication from a paint cup**;
- a site visit with **92 children in attendance and "nearly no childhood teachers"**;
- an educator who **dragged a child by both arms** to a bed, leaving fingernail scratches;
- an educator who **mopped around a child**, bumping a dirty mop against the child's legs and head;
- **a child walking out of the centre towards the gate**;
- an educator who **failed to intervene** while children were kicked and punched by other children;
- "**dangerous facilities**" — exposed nails, **blocked emergency exits**, and a build-up of litter.

On **5 May 2025** the **NSW Department of Education's Early Childhood Education and Care Regulatory
Authority suspended the centre's licence for three months**. Affinity called the suspension
"disappointing" given its work with the regulator, pointing to an improved **National Quality
Standards** rating and reduced staff turnover. The article notes it came days after an **ABC 7.30**
investigation that included footage of a worker at another Sydney Affinity centre **repeatedly
slapping a baby**.

**Deputy Premier and Minister for Education and Early Learning Prue Car** told Parliament the
government would improve the "quantity and the quality of services", and that an **independent review
of the NSW Early Childhood Education and Care Regulatory Authority**, launched in **February 2025**,
would report an interim finding later that month: "We will not hesitate in acting on recommendations
that ensure childcare in this state is safe and of a high quality."

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 — child protection as regulation, not only removal.** The *Care and Protection Act*
  responds **after** harm. This is the other half: licensing, ratios, qualified staff, compliance
  notices and the power to suspend. Pair it with
  `child-protection-working-with-children-act-2012` (screening the workers) and you have the whole
  **preventive** architecture, which almost no student writes about.
- **"92 children in attendance and nearly no childhood teachers"** is the quote. Staff ratios and
  qualification requirements are law, and this is what breaching them looks like.
- **Enforcement that arrives late (enforceability)** — Affinity took over in 2022, the mother
  complained in December 2022, the child was unenrolled in August 2023 and diagnosed in late 2023,
  and the licence was suspended in **May 2025**. A timeline is the most effective way to argue about
  responsiveness, and this one writes itself.
- **How the evidence became public (hr.2.13 / dis.3.3)** — a **parliamentary order** by a
  crossbench MP, plus an **ABC 7.30** investigation. Neither the regulator nor the operator published
  it. Excellent for the role of Parliament and the media as accountability mechanisms, and it is a
  concrete example of a **call for papers**, which is a real but rarely taught parliamentary power.
- **A suspension is not a remedy for the child (dis.2.7)** — the centre was suspended; Stevie needs a
  transplant. Use it to distinguish **regulatory sanction** from **compensation**, and to ask what
  civil action, if any, would be available and against whom.
- **Reform in train (chg.1.3)** — an independent review of the regulator itself, launched February
  2025. Note that the problem identified is partly the **regulator's** performance, not just the
  operator's.
- **Private provision of a public good (fam.2.6)** — a for-profit group running centres the state
  licenses but does not operate. The same structural question as outsourced out-of-home care in the
  Jack record.

## Caution

**Causation is contested and not established.** The medical notes record that recurrent UTIs
**contributed** to the disease **along with medication issues and congenital factors**; Affinity said
the allegations were "very serious" and "would need to be substantiated by medical evidence", and
suggested heat may have been a factor, pointing to the mother's own earlier interviews about heat
exacerbating Stevie's **existing kidney condition**. Do not write that the daycare centre caused the
kidney disease — the honest statement is that the mother alleges neglect, that medical notes record
UTIs as a contributing factor, and that the operator disputes it. No court or tribunal has decided
anything.

**Stevie is a living, identifiable six-year-old** whose medical condition and toileting are described
in detail, and the family consented to publication; a classroom resource should use the **regulatory
findings** and keep the child's medical detail out. The compliance notices are **regulator findings
of non-compliance**, which is the firmest evidence here — attribute them to the Regulatory Authority.
The **7.30 footage** concerns a **different centre** and is not evidence about Spring Farm. The
suspension was for **three months** from May 2025, so the centre's current status, the outcome of the
independent review (interim report due late May 2025) and any further regulatory action all need
checking before use.
