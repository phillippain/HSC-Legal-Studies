The **Brereton Afghanistan Inquiry** (2020) found credible information that **39** people were unlawfully killed in
23 incidents by **25** special forces personnel, made **143** recommendations and referred alleged offenders to the
**Office of the Special Investigator**. A separate inquiry into the **culture and command** of the SAS — written in
2021–22 and kept secret — had its executive summary published in October 2026; Andrew Hastie says it makes almost
**50 findings**. Chief of the Defence Force David Hammond told Senate estimates that holding senior leaders to
account is difficult.

## Brereton v the culture and command inquiry

| | Brereton (2020) | Culture and command (2026 summary) |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Individual soldiers and patrols | SASR culture, leadership and command over the whole war |
| Responsibility | Mainly patrol commanders; no evidence higher command knew | Spread up the chain; absence of effective leadership |
| Warning signs | Higher command should have seen unsustainable demands | High enemy deaths and few wounded ignored by headquarters |
| Throwdowns | Weapons planted to conceal killings | Some officers knew and did not act |
| Consequences | Two former soldiers charged with the war crime of murder | No senior officer prosecuted |

## From inquiry to trial

Inquiry → OSI and AFP investigation (with limited access to Afghan crime scenes) → brief to the CDPP; a Division 268
prosecution needs the **Attorney-General's consent** (s 268.121) → committal (Schulz, August 2025) → NSW Supreme
Court trial (war crime of murder: **life**). Under the Rome Statute **complementarity** principle (art 17), the ICC
acts only if Australia is unwilling or unable to prosecute genuinely.
