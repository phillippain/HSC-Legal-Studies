The government developer **Landcom** earmarked a former **WestConnex dive site on Parramatta Road in
Camperdown** — "a plateau of concrete, padlocked for the past two years", about 20 minutes from the
CBD and close to Royal Prince Alfred Hospital — as the **first site** to be developed under NSW's
**$450 million build-to-rent scheme**.

- At least **200 new apartments** for **essential workers** — teachers, nurses, police — at
  subsidised rents, with about **300 additional private and affordable homes**: roughly **500 in
  total**.
- The site was identified through the Minns government's **surplus land audit**.
- The state had announced about **400 subsidised apartments** for essential workers; the discounts
  were undecided but the Premier had suggested **around 20 per cent**.
- **This is a significant increase on the original plan.** In July the previous year the government
  said the land would be developed for about **100 apartments** "in partnership with the private
  sector" — an announcement criticised because it included **no social or affordable housing
  component**.
- The government had promised a **mandatory 30 per cent** social or affordable requirement on land
  identified in the audit, but later said that figure would be **spread across the total quantum of
  homes built** rather than applied site by site.

**Premier Chris Minns**: "Essential workers are feeling the impact of the immense cost of housing and
many can't afford to live near their jobs… The former WestConnex dive site in Sydney's inner west has
sat unused for years, and thanks to our plan to identify vacant land, it will now deliver housing for
essential workers close to their jobs."

## Use it for

- **shel.3.4 — the state as developer, not just regulator or funder.** Most Shelter evidence is about
  the law telling someone else what to do. Here the government owns the land, owns the developer
  (**Landcom**), and will own the building. Name it when a question asks about the role of government
  organisations.
- **Surplus public land as a housing policy (shel.3.1)** — a land audit, a padlocked concrete slab
  from a motorway project, and 500 homes. A very concrete answer to "what can government actually
  do?", and worth comparing with the **sale** of public property in
  `mckell-building-contract-for-sale`: the same asset class, opposite policy.
- **"Essential workers" is a new eligibility category (shel.t.values)** — not the lowest income, not
  social housing. Teachers, nurses and police at about **20 per cent** below market. Ask the hard
  question: is subsidising employed professionals the best use of scarce public land, when
  `pawson-2023-social-housing-in-australia` puts NSW unmet need for social housing at about **144,000
  households**? There are defensible answers either way, which is what makes it examinable.
- **The 30 per cent promise, quietly softened** — a **mandatory** site-by-site requirement became a
  figure "spread across the quantum of homes built". That is the sharpest accountability point in the
  record, and it is the kind of detail that distinguishes a precise answer from a general one.
- **Policy moving in the right direction, under pressure (chg.1.3)** — 100 apartments with no
  affordable component in July, about 500 with a social and affordable component by February, after
  public criticism. Use it to show how scrutiny changes outcomes.
- **Read it with the tax record** — `build-to-rent-tax-concessions-2024` is the private BTR pathway
  (50+ dwellings, 15 years, 10% affordable); this is the public one. Two routes to the same asset
  class, one bought with forgone revenue and one with public land.

## Caution

An **exclusive** reporting an **announcement**, not a completed project: as at February 2025 this was
a **padlocked site** with no approval, no construction and **undecided discounts**. Check what has
actually been built, how many dwellings, at what rents and for whom before using it as evidence of
outcome rather than intent — housing announcements routinely shrink. The **$450 million** scheme
figure and the **200 / 300 / 500** dwelling numbers are as announced. The **20 per cent** discount is
the Premier's earlier suggestion, expressly "yet to be decided". "Affordable housing" and "subsidised
for essential workers" are **different things** and the article runs them together; keep them apart,
because only the first has a statutory meaning. The claim that the 30 per cent requirement was
softened is the journalist's account of a change in government position, and the government is not
quoted on it. The source PDF is a **full newspaper page** carrying unrelated items, including a
council rates story. Landcom is a **state-owned corporation**, so "the government is building this"
is right in substance but the legal entity matters if you are writing about accountability.
