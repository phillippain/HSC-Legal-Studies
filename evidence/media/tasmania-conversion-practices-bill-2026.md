The **Conversion Practices Prohibition Bill 2026 (Tas)**, drafted by the Greens (Dr Rosalie Woodruff)
after a 2022 **Tasmania Law Reform Institute** report (18 recommendations), was to be debated in the
35-member House of Assembly from **Wednesday 28 October 2026**. Labor supports it, the Liberals have a
**conscience vote**, and 18 votes are needed; the Legislative Council must then agree. An earlier
government bill (December 2023) was criticised by the Greens as inadequate.

## What the bill would do (as reported)

| | Now | Under the bill |
|---|---|---|
| Ban | No dedicated law | Practices to change, suppress or eradicate sexual orientation or gender identity |
| Consent | — | **Irrelevant**: willing adults still covered |
| Serious harm | No specific offence | **Indictable** offence; aggravated for children or impaired capacity |
| Removal, advertising | Not an offence | Offences to take someone interstate for, or advertise, practices |
| Oversight | — | Anti-Discrimination Commissioner as conversion practices commissioner |
| Victims | — | Draft had **no civil cause of action** (advocates' key gap) |
| Religion, parents, health | — | Prayer, teaching and parental talk excluded unless part of a practice; appropriate health care exempt |

National youth survey (*Writing Themselves In 4*): about 1 in 20 young LGBTQIA+ people subjected to
conversion practices; 3.5x the PTSD rate and almost 4x the suicide-attempt rate.

## The bill as tabled (No 45 of 2026)

The Year 11 summary checked the tabled bill and its fact sheet:

| | Tasmanian bill | Victoria (2021) / NSW (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Definition (s 5) | Any attempt to change, suppress or eradicate sexual orientation or gender identity; **consent no defence** (s 20(2)) | Similar |
| Core offence (s 12) | Intentional practice + negligence as to injury; indictable, **no maximum in the bill** | Vic up to 10 years (serious injury); NSW up to 5 years |
| Removal (s 14) / advertising (s 16) | Indictable if injury; 60 penalty units (300 for a body corporate) | Vic up to 2 years; NSW up to 3 years |
| Positive duty (s 11) | Reasonable steps to eliminate conversion practices | — |
| Civil scheme | Anti-Discrimination Commissioner as Conversion Practices Commissioner (s 22); TASCAT referral with consent can award compensation (s 57) | VEOHRC scheme; NSW: NCAT, damages capped at $100,000 |
| Exceptions (s 5(3)–(4)) | Good-faith health care; prayer, religious teaching, public debate, parent discussions not of themselves conversion practices | NSW: religious teaching and parent discussions not restricted |

Ministers Felix Ellis and Gavin Pearce and MP Michael Ferguson said they would vote no; Premier Rockliff
backs a ban but says the bill goes too far. Commencement: by proclamation or automatically 12 months
after assent. For Year 11 this is a ready-made **chg.6 area of law reform** case study: changing values
(reason), the TLRI, advocates and the Greens (drivers), a private member's bill (process), likely
benefits and limitations (outcomes).
