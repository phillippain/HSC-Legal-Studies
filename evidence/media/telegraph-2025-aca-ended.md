For the first time in **more than two decades**, no child in NSW was living in unaccredited
emergency accommodation. The last child in an **alternative care arrangement** was moved into
accredited accommodation, and the Minister for Families and Communities, **Kate Washington**,
attributed it to a specialist team set up inside DCJ for the purpose and to a recruitment drive that
placed **more than 1,000 children in homes instead of hotels**.

The article reports high-cost emergency accommodation placements down **35%** since November, and
puts the cost of an ACA at **up to $2 million per child annually**.

The report that drove it is quoted back in the child's own words: one child likened the experience
to "a dog being moved from cage to cage."

## Use it for

- **chg.3 (mechanisms of reform)** — the rarest thing in this library: a **completed reform cycle**.
  *Family is Culture* (2019) → Audit Office reports (2024) → the ACYP Special Inquiry (August 2024)
  → the practice ended (April 2025). Most case studies stop at "recommendations were made"; this one
  finishes.
- **Reform without legislation** — nothing was enacted. A minister, a specialist unit and a
  recruitment campaign did it. Excellent for arguing that **non-legislative responses** can be the
  effective ones, and for the limits of treating law reform as the only mechanism.
- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — pair directly with the Special Inquiry record. Quote the 2024
  finding and the 2025 outcome together and you have both halves of an evaluation.
- **Responsiveness as a criterion** — eight months from report to result is fast by the standards of
  this topic; set it against *Family is Culture*, where 12 of 126 recommendations had been
  implemented after five years.
- **What it does not fix** — the children were moved to **accredited** emergency accommodation, not
  to permanent placements, and DCJ estimates it needs **1,000 extra foster carers a year**. The
  problem that produced ACAs is still there.

## Caution

A **government announcement reported as news**: the minister is the only source quoted, there is no
independent verification, and no opposition or sector response appears. "No children in unaccredited
accommodation" is a **point-in-time claim** about one category of placement — check whether it held,
and do not let it stand for the condition of the care system as a whole. The **$2 million** figure
sits oddly beside the Special Inquiry's **$965,000 average**; one is presumably a maximum, so
attribute each to its source rather than treating them as the same number. NSW law bars identifying
children who have been in out-of-home care until they turn 25.
