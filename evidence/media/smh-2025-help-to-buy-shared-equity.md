Two Commonwealth schemes, side by side, with a household that used one of them.

**Matthew Armstrong** and **Mandy Warren** were renting in Strathfield with no family help and
expected to need "another three to five years to get over that **20 per cent deposit** hurdle".
Instead they used the **First Home Guarantee**, which **waives lender's mortgage insurance for buyers
with a 5 per cent deposit**, and bought a townhouse in **Kingswood**. "The scheme was a game
changer." They had wanted a house with a backyard: "we knew we'd have to go to **St Marys, Mt Druitt
or further west**."

**Help to Buy**, expanded in that week's Budget, is the alternative: a **2 per cent deposit**, with
the **government co-purchasing up to 40 per cent** of the property. **10,000 places a year**. Price
caps were lifted — Sydney buyers can look at properties up to **$1.3 million** — and income
thresholds set at **$100,000 for a single** and **$160,000 for a couple**. At the time of the article
the First Home Guarantee's Sydney cap was **$900,000**.

## What the experts said

- **Dr Nicola Powell** (Domain) welcomed the higher caps as "one angle that needed to be targeted
  better to meet the current pricing". But she is **cautious about co-purchasing with the government,
  which will reap a share of the benefits if prices rise**, and notes that overseas evidence suggests
  shared equity schemes mostly help "first home buyers that are **already close to purchasing**".
- **Professor Nicole Gurran** (University of Sydney) thought Help to Buy could extend to **key
  workers** and "targets moderate income earners who currently face years and years of saving". But
  **First Home Guarantee participation fell 33% from 2020–21 to 2023–24** (Housing Australia data),
  and she suspects the thresholds were expanded "due to **insufficient take-up**". On the 10,000
  places: "**it's too small to fundamentally alter the trajectory of home ownership** for those who
  have been shut out."
- A mortgage broker on the then **$900,000** First Home Guarantee cap in Sydney: "In most suburbs, you
  might just get a one-bedroom apartment… People are back to square one." Some buyers moved to
  **Queensland**, where the cap was $700,000 but affordability better.
- **ANZ economist Madeline Dunk**: "They might not want to co-own with the government, but… given how
  challenging affordability is, many will jump at the chance."

## Use it for

- **shel.2.1 — a legal response to the deposit, not to the price.** Both schemes attack the **deposit
  barrier**: one by removing the insurance a small deposit normally requires, the other by having the
  state take an equity share. Neither changes what the house costs. That distinction is the whole
  evaluation.
- **Shared equity is a change in the nature of ownership (shel.1.2)** — the government becomes a
  **co-owner with a share of the capital gain**. Students write about home ownership as binary; here
  the law creates a third thing, and Powell's caution about the state "reaping a share of the
  benefits if prices rise" is the point to argue.
- **The take-up figure is the strongest evidence here (shel.t.effectiveness)** — participation **down
  33%** across three years, and eligibility widened in response. A scheme whose uptake falls as prices
  rise is evidence that the caps, not the deposit, were binding.
- **"Too small to fundamentally alter the trajectory"** — **10,000 places a year** against the number
  of households shut out. Quote it when a question asks whether a response is adequate rather than
  whether it exists.
- **Geography as the real eligibility test (shel.3.3)** — Strathfield to Kingswood, then St Marys and
  Mt Druitt, then Queensland. A price cap is a map of where you are allowed to live.
- **Federal cooperation (inv.6.4)** — Commonwealth schemes delivered through Housing Australia, on
  top of state stamp duty concessions. Pair with `first-home-buyer-concessions-nsw`.
- **The "bank of mum and dad" as an unlegislated institution (shel.t.values)** — the headline's
  premise is that the normal route to a first home is **inherited wealth**. Set it against the
  family home tax break record for the mechanism by which that wealth accumulates untaxed.

## Caution

A **property-section feature built around one successful household**, published in a masthead whose
property pages carry real estate listings — the framing ("game changer") is a buyer's enthusiasm, not
an evaluation. **The figures are from March 2025 and have moved**: the First Home Guarantee was
expanded from **1 October 2025**, removing income caps and lifting the Sydney price cap from $900,000
to **$1.5 million**, which is recorded at `first-home-guarantee-expansion-2025` — so the $900,000 cap
and the $100,000/$160,000 thresholds described here are **superseded**, and the two records should be
read together as a before-and-after. **Help to Buy's commencement, caps and thresholds should be
checked** against Housing Australia before being stated; at the time of writing the scheme was
announced and expanded but its operation was still being set up. Powell and Gurran are cautious, not
opposed, and Gurran's "insufficient take-up" explanation is her **inference**. The 33% fall in
participation is Housing Australia data covering **2020–21 to 2023–24**, a period of rising rates and
prices, so it is not evidence about the scheme's design alone. Nobody who was refused or who struggled
under either scheme is quoted.
