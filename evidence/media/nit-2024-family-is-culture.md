Five years after the Aboriginal-led, government-commissioned **Family Is Culture Review**, Aboriginal
organisations across NSW called for control of the child protection system to be transferred to
**Indigenous-led organisations**. Only **12 of the review's 126 recommendations** had been fully
implemented.

> "The Family Is Culture Review offers us a clear path forward, but we must hold the government
> accountable." — John Leha, CEO, AbSec (the NSW Child, Family and Community Peak Aboriginal
> Corporation)

Aunty Debra Swan, a founding member of **Grandmothers Against Removals** and a former DCJ caseworker,
said what she saw was systemic rather than incidental: "It wasn't bad luck, and it wasn't a bad
caseworker on a bad day. Far too often, across too many offices, our families were not being
supported, and our kids were being taken."

## Use it for

- **Responsiveness with a number attached**: 12 of 126 in five years is the answer to "does the law
  reform process respond to identified failures?" — a review can be commissioned, accepted and then
  not implemented, and implementation is the stage nobody measures.
- **Non-government organisations** (`fam.2.6`) — AbSec and Grandmothers Against Removals as the
  mechanism holding a department accountable where the internal mechanisms did not.
- **Self-determination** as a reform direction: the argument is not for better departmental practice
  but for transferring the decision itself, which connects the Family option to native title and to
  the wider question of Aboriginal control of Aboriginal affairs.
