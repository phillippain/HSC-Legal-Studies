Cost, waiting lists and donor shortages are pushing Australians from regulated IVF to **private sperm
donors** found through Facebook groups and apps (one group has about 23,000 members) and at-home
insemination. One lawyer charged $1,500 for a donor agreement; IVF often costs $5,000–$10,000 a cycle
out of pocket.

## Clinic route v informal route

| | Regulated clinic | Informal / private |
|---|---|---|
| Screening | Infectious disease and genetic screening | Only if the parties arrange it |
| Counselling | Required | Self-arranged |
| Donor limits | **Vic: 10 families; NSW: five women (s 27)** | No enforced limit — one donor reached **15 women, 27 children** (2020–24) |
| Records | Births reported; records kept 50 years (NSW) | NSW lets parties add details **voluntarily** (s 33A) |
| Parentage | Statutory rules, e.g. s 60H (a consenting partner is a parent) | An agreement is **evidence only**; courts decide on best interests |
| Child's information | NSW donor-conceived adults can apply from 18 | Only what was recorded |

***Masson v Parsons* (2019)**: a known donor who intended to co-parent and acted as a parent was the legal
father, whatever the paperwork said. Payment for sperm (valuable consideration) is banned; reasonable
expenses are allowed.

## Reform

A 2024 review recommended a **national donor registry**; in 2025 Health Ministers agreed to an **ALRC
review** of donor laws (formal referral still awaited in April 2026). The 2025 clinic regulation review did
not address informal donation.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of a news feature**; birth and membership figures are self-reported by a Facebook
group. The 'access at 16' age, the IVF cost range and how far the payment ban reaches private donors were not
confirmed. Check current state laws before quoting donor limits.
