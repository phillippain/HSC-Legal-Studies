## The numbers

| Since 1975 | |
|---|---|
| Average capital city home price | **+3,435%** |
| Median full-time wages | **+1,183%** |
| Sydney house prices | **+4,645%** |
| Sydney median home | **$34,000 (1975) → $1,627,625 (2024)** |

Research expert **Peter Drennan** of Money.com.au:

> "The Silent Generation and Baby Boomers bought homes when prices were just **four to five times
> the median wage**, while today's families are paying double to triple that."

> "Even with **two full-time incomes**, buying an average house in Sydney today means facing house
> prices at **9.5 times the median wage**."

The Sydney price-to-income ratio has **nearly quadrupled**, and two incomes are now needed to
service the repayments where one once was.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 (affordability)** — the single most efficient statistic in the stack. One comparison —
  3,435% against 1,183% — establishes the whole problem, and it is far more usable in an answer than
  a median price, because it is a **ratio** and so survives inflation.
- **The generational shift** — four to five times one wage, then nine and a half times two wages.
  That is the change in what "a home" costs measured in work, and it is the fact that makes
  affordability a question about equality rather than about prices.
- **chg.1.1 (recognising changing values)** — when a household needs two full-time incomes to buy,
  the law and policy built around single-income home ownership (first home grants, stamp duty
  concessions, the family home's tax treatment) is working on a model that no longer exists.
- **Pair it with the policy records** — Grattan's recommendations, the expanded First Home Guarantee
  and the 2026 negative gearing changes are all responses to this curve. Use the figure to ask
  whether any of them bends it.
- **Rent follows purchase price** — connects affordability to leasing and to homelessness, so it is a
  good opening statistic for several Shelter dot points, not just this one.

## Caution

**Not a peer-reviewed or official source.** The figures are a **commercial comparison site's
analysis** (Money.com.au) reported by a **real estate industry trade publication**, and neither is
disinterested. Attribute them as "analysis by Money.com.au reported in Real Estate Business" rather
than as ABS or Bureau figures — the underlying data is not specified, and "average home price" and
"median full-time wages" are different kinds of measure. Percentage change since 1975 is **nominal**
unless stated otherwise, which is why the ratio comparison, not the raw 3,435%, is the part worth
quoting. From **November 2024**: prices have moved since. For official data use the ABS or the
Productivity Commission; use this for the shape of the change.
