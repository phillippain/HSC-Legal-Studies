Melbourne cyclist **Bianca Pickett** and an 18-year-old rider describe a man who messaged the younger
woman on **Strava**, then turned up to a women's ride and followed it; earlier complaints to cycling
clubs went nowhere until a new female president banned him. Strava says messaging defaults to
Friends Only and the first and last 200 m of maps are hidden. **No charge or court finding is
reported; the man is unnamed.**

## What the law already covers v what the platform controls

| | The law | The app or club |
|---|---|---|
| Unwanted messages | Vic s 21A: electronic contact can be stalking (max **10 years**) | Messaging defaults; advocates want strangers off unless opted in |
| Tracking | NSW s 8: monitoring or tracking by technology is stalking from **1 Dec 2024**; s 13 max **5 years** | Map privacy zones, heatmap opt-outs |
| Protection | Personal safety intervention order from a magistrate, no police needed; breach is a crime | Block, report, ban from club |
| Online abuse | Criminal Code s 474.17 (5 years); eSafety removal notices | Trust and Safety review (company's own claim) |

**ABS Personal Safety Survey 2021–22**: 20% of women stalked since 15; 8.6% of women stalked by a male
reported electronic tracking; only **25%** said police were contacted, and of those 19% said the stalker
was charged.

## Use it for

- **crime.1.8 / crime.1.9** — technology as a factor in offending, and situational prevention by design
  (privacy defaults) set against legal deterrence.
- **crime.2.2 (reporting crime)** — low reporting rates; victims choosing non-legal routes first.
- **Law reform** — NSW's 2024 tracking amendment and the VLRC *Stalking* report (2022, 45
  recommendations) show the law catching up with the technology.
