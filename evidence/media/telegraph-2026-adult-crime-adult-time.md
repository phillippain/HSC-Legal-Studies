After two men were shot dead in Sydney's organised-crime conflict, the Minns government announced
"adult time" for youth offenders.

**The case at the centre of it.** A 15-year-old arrested under Taskforce Falcon, **charged on 94
separate occasions with no conviction ever recorded**, charged over a shooting in which 28 bullets
were fired into the wrong Merrylands home. He pleaded guilty to a reduced number of charges, none
of them serious children's indictable offences, was sentenced to **24 months** in youth detention
and, with time served, walked free the same day.

**The proposal.** Four offence types elevated to SCIOs — firing at homes, buildings or vehicles;
causing danger with a firearm; using explosives near buildings; damaging property by fire or
explosives with intent to injure (up to 25 years). Plus Interim Serious Crime Prevention Orders and
$15 million for cracking encrypted phones.

Premier Chris Minns: "By elevating these offences from the Children's Court jurisdiction to the
adult courts they'll be subject to adult penalties."

## What the article gets wrong, and why that is the point

- **"Charged on 94 occasions" is not 94 proven offences.** Charges are allegations.
- **"No conviction recorded" is not "no punishment".** s 14 CCPA *prohibits* recording a conviction
  against a child under 16; he received a control order at the **statutory maximum** for a single
  offence (s 33 CCPA, 2 years).
- Loaded language throughout; the strongest claims come from an **unnamed police source**; no
  defence lawyer, youth advocate, criminologist or young person is quoted; no counter-evidence on
  whether adult sentencing reduces youth reoffending.
- Minns quoted **outdated** Taskforce Falcon figures (611 arrests / 1500 charges; the true figures
  were 784 / 2037).

## Use it for

- **Law reform**: conditions giving rise to reform (failure of existing law, changing values, new
  technology), agencies of reform (the media — *this front page* — police, Parliament), and the
  distinction between **announced** and **enacted**: no bill, no timetable.
- **Discretion** produced the outcome at three points: police charge selection, prosecutorial charge
  negotiation, judicial sentencing. The reform reduces only the third.
- Paired with the BOCSAR diversion evidence, it argues that **increasing penalties and reducing
  reoffending are not the same objective**.

## Caution

At the date of the article the changes were an announcement, not an Act. Verify the final form of
any 2026 amendment before quoting it as law.
