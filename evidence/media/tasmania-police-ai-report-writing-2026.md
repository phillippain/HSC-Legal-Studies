In 2025 Tasmania Police tested **Draft One** (made by US company **Axon**), which turns body-worn camera
audio into a draft police narrative that officers review, edit and approve: first on about **150**
historical family violence recordings, then for several months in Launceston. The evaluation, obtained
by the ABC under Right to Information, recommends a phased rollout only after an ethical AI policy.
**No rollout has been approved.**

## The claims and the checks

| Police evaluation | Independent evidence |
|---|---|
| Report written and validated in **42 minutes**, almost twice as fast | A US randomised trial (85 officers, 2024) found **no significant** time saving |
| ~160 weeks and **$434,187** in wages saved a year | An estimate; licence and extra staff costs not netted off |
| Flagged coercive behaviours officers missed | Also missed threats or assaults in a small percentage; one **false firearm threat** |
| Officer must approve every draft | Draft One keeps **no original draft** (EFF, 2025), so AI and officer words can't be separated |
| No legislative change needed | California legislated disclosure and an audit trail (SB 524, from 2026); King County prosecutors refuse AI narratives |

Under *Evidence Act 2001* (Tas) **s 33**, an officer may read a signed statement as evidence — so an
uncorrected AI error could become sworn evidence.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.3 / crime.2.1** — technology entering the first record of an investigation, and police
  accountability for what they sign.
- **crime.3.7** — reliability of police evidence and the accused's ability to test it.
- **Balance and effectiveness** — saved resources for DV callouts against fair-trial risk; critics
  (Digital Rights Watch, Engender Equality) want human judgement and victim-survivor input.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary** of a news report of an internal evaluation the summary could not read;
all time and money figures are unverified police estimates. Tasmanian, not NSW, law. Overseas
findings may not reflect Tasmania's settings.
