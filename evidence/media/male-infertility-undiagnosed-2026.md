Male factors contribute to **up to 50%** of couples' infertility, and about one in six couples experience
infertility. Yet in 2024, **75.1%** of male-factor ART cycles had **no explained cause**, and men were often
tested late — after their partners had begun IVF. One man estimated spending **$90,000** over 13 years (a
personal figure; a first IVF cycle costs about $6,700).

## What the 2025 guidelines change

| | Before (as the article describes) | After the November 2025 guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| Who is assessed | Woman first; man late | Both partners **at the same time** |
| First tests | Semen analysis only if pushed for | History, examination, semen analysis, repeated after about six weeks if abnormal |
| Referral | Cause left unexplained | Abnormal results to a male reproduction specialist |
| Testosterone | — | Not to be prescribed to men trying to conceive |
| Legal force | No national male guideline | A **clinical guideline** — not an Act or regulation |
| Who pays | Treatment burden on women | Unchanged: the NSW rebate excludes male treatment costs |

Clinics sit in a **patchwork**: accreditation by an industry committee (RTAC), registration by states, rebates
by Medicare and some states. Victoria announced stronger IVF protections in June 2026 after the 2025
Monash IVF errors.
