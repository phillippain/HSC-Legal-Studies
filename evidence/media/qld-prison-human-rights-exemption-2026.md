Introduced on **15 September 2026**, clause 4 of the bill inserts **s 5B** into the *Corrective Services Act
2006*, disapplying **s 58** of Queensland's *Human Rights Act 2019* for QCS decisions about prisoners and
supervised dangerous-prisoner offenders. Prisons hold **11,278** people (June 2025, up 54% in a decade) at
about **135%** of built capacity.

## Now v under the bill

| | Human Rights Act now | Under new s 5B |
|---|---|---|
| Duty | QCS must act compatibly and properly consider rights (s 58) | **Removed** for covered decisions |
| Rights on paper | e.g. humane treatment in detention (s 30) | Still exist — but no duty or enforcement |
| Complaint | To QCS, then the Commission after 45 business days (ss 63–65) | No basis for a complaint |
| Court | Rights ground added to judicial review — how *Owen-D'Arcy* (2021) won | Unavailable |
| Override rules | s 43: Parliament may declare a law operates despite incompatibility, in **exceptional circumstances** | Used here; lapses after **5 years** unless renewed (s 45) |

Queensland has overridden its Act at least three times since 2023, mostly in youth justice.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.14 (a charter of rights)** — the strength and weakness of a statutory charter: parliamentary
  sovereignty lets it be switched off; compare constitutional protection and NSW, which has no charter.
- **hr.2.9** — statute giving rights and statute taking them away; the Human Rights Commission says overrides
  are for war or emergency.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of investigative reporting** built partly on anonymous officers; QCS did not
respond to their claims. The bill had not passed and may be amended. The committee report and statement of
compatibility were not retrieved.
