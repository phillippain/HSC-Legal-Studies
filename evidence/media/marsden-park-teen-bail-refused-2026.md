Police shut down a house party in Marsden Park on the night of **30 October 2026**; a 17-year-old was arrested
(with help from members of the public) and charged with **reckless wounding** (s 35(4), max **7 years**) and
possessing a prohibited drug. Police refused bail and he was due before the **Children's Court** the next day.
**The charges are allegations; he cannot be named.**

## Adult v young accused at the bail stage

| | Adult | Under 18 (as here) |
|---|---|---|
| Show cause | Applies to listed serious offences | **Never** applies if under 18 at the time |
| Unacceptable risk test | Four bail concerns | Same test, same concerns |
| Special vulnerability | Aboriginality, cognitive or mental impairment | **Youth itself** is listed |
| Extra test | — | **s 22C** (ages 14–17, repeat car theft or serious break-in on bail) — not triggered here |
| Second application | New information or changed circumstances | Also allowed if the first was made at first appearance |
| Publicity | May generally be named | Naming a child accused is an offence (s 15A) |
| Remand share, March 2026 | 47.3% of adult prisoners unsentenced | **79.6%** of young people in detention on remand |

69% of s 22C first appearances ended in remand (2024–26), and 79% involved Aboriginal young people.
UNCROC art 37(b): detention of a child only as a last resort.
