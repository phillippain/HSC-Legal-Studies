## The supply problem behind every other problem

| | |
|---|---|
| Households with one or more registered carers | about **11,000** |
| Children needing care and protection | nearly **15,000** |
| Of those, placed with a foster family | **one third** |
| Carers who left in the past year | nearly **1,000** |
| Extra carers DCJ estimates it needs | **1,000 every year** |

Carers describe **low payments**, **limited support for children with behavioural issues**, and
**poor communication** from DCJ and from the non-government organisations that place children with
them. They also raise children spending more than two years as wards of the state being moved
through a succession of homes, and a difficult pathway to adoption.

Minister **Kate Washington** said the government "inherited a child protection system spiralling out
of control, where foster carer numbers have plummeted and vulnerable children are paying the price",
and that "the system depends on foster carers but they've been walking away in droves for years".
She has elsewhere conceded the system is **"broken"**.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the cause, where the other records are the consequence. The
  Special Inquiry found children in motels at $965,000 a year; this explains why there was nowhere
  else to put them. **Two-thirds of children in care are not with a foster family.**
- **Resource efficiency as a criterion** — the clearest illustration in the topic. A system that
  will pay nearly a million dollars a year for a hotel placement while reporting that carers quit
  over low payments is not short of money; it is spending it at the wrong end.
- **fam.2.6 (non-government organisations)** — the NGOs here are both the placement agencies carers
  complain about and the advocates for reform. Useful for the complexity of the non-legal response.
- **The minister's own words** — a serving minister calling the system "broken" is a quotable
  admission, and it dates the reform narrative: this is March 2024, five months before the Special
  Inquiry and thirteen before ACAs ended.
- **fam.1.6 (adoption)** — the carers' complaint that the adoption pathway stays difficult connects
  this dot point to adoption and to permanency policy.

## Caution

A **news report built on carers' accounts and a ministerial statement**, not a study: the figures
are attributed to DCJ and to the sector but are not sourced to a named publication, so check them
against the **Report on Government Services** or DCJ's own data before quoting. "Nearly 1,000 left
in the past year" is a gross figure — the article does not give how many joined, so it is not a net
loss. The minister is making a political point about an inherited system. NSW legislation bars
identifying children who have been in out-of-home care until they turn 25, and **foster carers
cannot be identified either**, which is why the carers in this article are anonymous. From **March
2024**: check what has changed, particularly since the government's recruitment drive reported in
April 2025.
