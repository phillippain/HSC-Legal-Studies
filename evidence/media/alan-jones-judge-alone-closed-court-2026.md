Former broadcaster **Alan Jones** pleaded not guilty to **22 charges** (20 indecent assault, 2 sexual
touching) relating to alleged conduct towards young men between 2003 and 2020. His trial began on
**3 August 2026** before a Local Court judge alone, with complainants giving evidence in a **closed
court**. **Every charge is an untested allegation; he is presumed innocent, and no verdict had been
reported in the sources (to 7 September 2026).**

## District Court jury trial v Local Court judge alone

| | District Court, jury | Local Court, judge alone |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides guilt | Jury; judge directs on law | Judge decides fact and law **and gives reasons** |
| Public access | Open court the usual rule | Closed while complainants testify; media by leave |
| Publicity risk | Jurors may be influenced | Judge refused a suppression order: no jury lowers the risk |
| Maximum penalty (as the article says, unchecked) | Up to 10 years | 2 years (Local Court limit) |
| Standard of proof | Beyond reasonable doubt | The same |

BOCSAR (2011–2019 higher courts) found judge-alone trials had about a **12-point higher** acquittal
rate — an association, not proof of cause, and not about the Local Court. The prosecution's choice to
keep the matter in the Local Court is the subject of the article's correction note.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of a procedural explainer** on a live matter. Do not predict or assume an
outcome and **do not name complainants**. Charges were amended before trial. The penalty figures and
section numbers were not checked against the Acts. Check for a verdict before using the case further.
