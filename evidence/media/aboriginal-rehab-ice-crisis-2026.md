An ABC feature on **Bunjilwarra** (Victoria, 12 beds, ages 16–25, run by the Victorian Aboriginal
Health Service with YSAS) and **The Glen** (NSW Central Coast) as methamphetamine use hits records.
Six Bunjilwarra residents are on **bail**; The Glen has **closed applications** with about 100
assessed and 60–70 pending.

## The numbers

**$11.05b** estimated methylamphetamine street value, Aug 2024 – Aug 2025 (77% of a record $14.3b
across four drugs; consumption up **23%**, Australia second per capita of 33 countries) · Indigenous
unintentional overdose death rate **4.4×** non-Indigenous (29.3 vs 6.6 per 100,000, 2024,
preliminary) · Aboriginal adult imprisonment **2,500.2 per 100,000** in 2025 and **worsening** ·
Aboriginal people **36.4%** of the daily prison population.

## The legal pathway (NSW, as the comparison point)

The article never explains how residents come to be on bail at a rehab. In NSW, the *Bail Act 2013*
**s 28** lets a court impose an accommodation requirement enabling admission to residential rehab,
and conduct requirements can oblige a person to live at the facility. **MERIT** (Magistrates Early
Referral Into Treatment) lets a Local Court magistrate approve voluntary diversion for an adult on
bail with a treatable drug problem, make participation a bail condition, take reports at about 6 and
12 weeks, and weigh completion at sentence. MERIT excludes sexual assault charges and District Court
matters.

## Use it for

- **crime.4.7 / crime.2.5 (alternative sentencing, bail)** — the concrete mechanism linking a bail
  decision to a treatment bed, and the reason diversion fails where beds are full. If the bed does
  not exist, remand is the default.
- **Effectiveness for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples** — Closing the Gap Targets 10
  and 11 are both off track, adult imprisonment going backwards. The **ALRC's *Pathways to Justice***
  (2018) made 35 recommendations including diversion and justice reinvestment; the **NSW Ice inquiry**
  (2020) made 109, of which 86 were supported, with harm-reduction recommendations such as
  decriminalising personal use rejected.
- **ls.2.9 / ls.2.10 (customary law and its relevance)** — Elders, yarning circles and bush walks as
  the treatment itself, not an add-on; a case for culturally safe justice responses.
- **The honest counter-argument** — a 2022 NSW study of 2,326 admissions to Aboriginal AOD
  residential rehab found **justice-referred clients were more likely to self-discharge**, which
  raises the question of coerced treatment, and stimulant users more likely to be re-admitted. No
  published jail-outcome study was found.

## Caution

A **class-made summary** of a **human-interest feature**, not an evaluation. The three claims a
student would most want — lower return to jail, cheaper than custody, the waitlist numbers — all come
from interviewees and are **unverified**; the cost claim is only plausible from the custody figures.
Bunjilwarra is in **Victoria**, and the NSW bail and MERIT law here is a comparison only; Victorian
bail law was not checked. Penington's 2024 figures are preliminary and the 4.4× ratio is calculated,
not stated. No government, police or victim voice appears in the article.
