A multi-disciplinary team inside **NSW Fair Trading**, funded at **$8.4 million**, led by a Rental
Taskforce Manager reporting to the **NSW Rental Commissioner, Trina Jones**. It analyses trends in the
rental market and conducts **inspections, audits and blitzes**.

Three priorities:

1. **ending solicited rent bidding**;
2. **implementing the reforms preventing no-grounds evictions**;
3. **improving responses to repairs and maintenance**.

A **Portable Rental Bond Scheme** was flagged for the second half of 2025.

> "While the majority of agents and landlords are doing the right thing, this $8.4m investment targets
> bad actors who make life tougher for renters." — Minister Anoulack Chanthivong

## Six months on (13 September 2025)

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Illegal fees recovered | **over $166,000**, for **more than 2,000 renters** |
| Penalty notices | **more than 200**, totalling just under **$240,000** |
| Properties monitored by the new automated tool | **more than 950,000** |

The compliance tool uses **data-matching** — cross-referencing landlords' own reports, rental
listings, bond lodgements and tenant complaints — to detect **unlawful re-letting** during an
exclusion period. That is enforcement of a rule no individual renter would ever litigate, and it is
the concrete answer to "is a right without enforcement a right?"

## Use it for

- **The enforceability criterion**, which is where most tenancy answers should end up. The 2024 Act
  created the rights; this created the capacity to enforce them, and the two dates — October 2024 and
  February 2025 — show enforcement being retrofitted to a reform.
- **The role of government organisations** (`shel.2.8`) — a regulator acting on its own motion, as
  against NCAT, which acts only when a tenant brings a case. That distinction is worth the paragraph:
  a tenant who fears retaliation will not litigate, and a blitz does not need them to.
- **Proportion**: $8.4 million and one taskforce against **2.3 million renters** and roughly 600,000
  landlords. Use the numbers rather than adjectives when evaluating.
