The AFP analysed **55 radicalised children**, all **boys aged 12 to 17**. **All** were radicalised wholly or
partly **online** (sometimes within days); about **three in five** had received mental health support;
about a third had witnessed or experienced caregiver violence; over a quarter lacked a stable male role
model, while some had secure family bonds. Jihadism was most common, but **mixed or unclear ideologies**
are growing. Separately, 35 of 65 children targeted by counter-terrorism police since 2020 were charged.

## The pathway

Identification (parents, schools, hotline; most referrals still from police, often late) → voluntary
**disengagement programs** (Step Together) → investigation; with the Attorney-General's consent, a
**control order** (Criminal Code Div 104) for children **14 and over**, at most three months at a time →
**criminal responsibility**: none under 10 (s 7.1); 10–14 *doli incapax* must be rebutted → prosecution,
usually in a state children's court, which the **INSLM** (2018) considered ill-suited; it recommended the
s 19AG 75% non-parole rule not apply to under-18s.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of a news report of an unpublished agency analysis**; only the AFP
Commissioner is quoted. The period covered is not stated, and the 55 and 65 figures are different data
sets. A 2025 study found about half of intervention clients were drawn in through a direct relationship,
so "online" is not the whole story.
