## The argument this evidence makes

Prohibiting commercial surrogacy in NSW did not end commercial surrogacy for people in NSW. It moved
it to jurisdictions with weaker protection for the surrogate and no reliable route to parentage or
citizenship for the child. **A prohibition that only reaches one jurisdiction can shift harm rather
than prevent it** — which is a law reform argument, not a description.

Note the accessibility point that runs the other way: at $150,000 to $184,000, this is a right only
the wealthy can exercise at all.

## Use it for

Surrogacy and birth technologies, and the wider theme of **law lagging behind technology**. Pair it
with *Daniels & Rooney*, where a court had to invent a category for a surplus embryo because no
statute supplied one, and with *Wilkerson & Croxford* on known-donor conception.
