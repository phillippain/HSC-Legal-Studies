The **Secretaries Board** (heads of Commonwealth departments) told a parliamentary standards committee
that clips of officials' committee evidence are being posted without context to invite ridicule and
abuse, and asked Parliament to consider preventing that use. Reported fallbacks: post only **full,
unedited** excerpts, no captions, and **comments off**. On **15 October 2026** the Senate directed the
PM&C Secretary to appear at Estimates over the request.

## The implied freedom applied (Beck's argument)

1. **Source** — ss 7 and 24 (representatives directly chosen by the people); a limit on legislative
   power, not a personal right.
2. **Burden?** — clips of committee evidence are political communication; a ban or conditions burden it.
3. **Legitimate purpose?** — protecting officials from criticism is not legitimate (*Monis*: preventing
   offence can fail); *Banerji* accepted an apolitical, trusted public service.
4. **Proportionate?** — suitable, necessary, adequate in balance; *Brown v Tasmania* failed on breadth.
   Defamation and harassment laws already target genuine harm.

Outcome: invalid (as in *Nationwide News* and *Brown*) or valid (as in *Banerji*). Whether a
**parliamentary rule** rather than a statute could even be reviewed is unclear.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.8** — the Constitution's only implied right, explained through a live dispute.
- **hr.3.1 / media** — scrutiny of government through social media against protecting witnesses.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of an opinion piece** by an author who is a Labor Party member (per Mirage
News). The Board's submission was not retrieved and reports of its wording differ. No court has ruled.
