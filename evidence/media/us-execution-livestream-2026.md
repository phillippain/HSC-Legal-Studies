**Nidal Hasan**, the Army psychiatrist who killed 13 people and injured 32 at Fort Hood on
5 November 2009, was sentenced to death by court-martial in 2013. On **7–8 October 2026** Defense
Secretary Hegseth said the execution would be public and the Pentagon confirmed a **livestream** of
the **firing squad** execution scheduled for **3 December 2026** — the first US military execution
since 1961. After backlash in the US and abroad, **President Trump cancelled the livestream on
15 October** (US time), saying a broadcast "might have been a strong deterrent" but many would
rather not watch; victims' families may still attend.

In Australia, the Greens wrote to eSafety; **Commissioner Julie Inman Grant said she had no power
to stop** the video being shown here; the Prime Minister called a livestreamed death "abhorrent" and
said platforms had a moral duty to block it, while admitting there was no easy lever.

## Why the *Online Safety Act* could not reach it

1. Removal powers act **after** material is online; eSafety's guidance does not provide for pre-emptive orders.
2. A **class 1 removal notice** (ss 106, 109) needs the footage to be Refused Classification — untested.
3. **Abhorrent violent conduct** blocking (ss 95, 99) turns on "murder" under *Criminal Code* s 474.32, which requires conduct that is an **offence** — a lawful, court-ordered execution may fall outside it; and professional news reporting is exempt (s 104).
4. Practically: eSafety lost its 2024 bid to make X remove the Wakeley church stabbing video.

Digital Rights Watch argued the footage would likely be class 1, so powers already existed; no eSafety ruling was made.
