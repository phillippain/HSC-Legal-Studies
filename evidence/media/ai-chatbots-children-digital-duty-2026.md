eSafety surveyed **1,950** children aged 10 to 17 (February–March 2026): **78%** had used an AI assistant and **8%**
an AI companion; **54%** of users used them for personal or social reasons; **20%** reported a potentially harmful
interaction; about 32% had shared personal information. eSafety found four companion apps lacked robust age checks
and three did not direct users to crisis support when self-harm was detected. **Character.AI** filed for bankruptcy in
October 2026, raising the question of who gets children's intimate chat data.

## What the law covers v the gaps

| | Current law or the bill | Gap or status |
|---|---|---|
| Harmful content | Codes (from 9 March 2026): AI services assess risk of generating porn, self-harm or violence material | "Locks and blocks" cannot reach harm from repeated interactions |
| Age checks | High-risk AI services need age assurance where feasible | Weak in practice |
| Duty | Bill: a duty to provide a "safe online environment" | Authors want all reasonably foreseeable risks covered |
| Emotional dependence | **Chaney amendment** absorbed on 28 October: stop chatbots fostering children's dependence | Came the day after the article |
| Intimate data | Draft Children's Online Privacy Code, due December 2026 | Outside the duty's definition |
| Redress | No right to sue; Greens want one | California SB 243 lets injured users sue, $1,000 per violation minimum |
