**Pari Nikau**, a New Zealand citizen, was sleeping rough at Sydney's Central Station after being
discharged from hospital without accommodation. Each morning NSW Police directed him to move on;
on some occasions his belongings were taken. As a New Zealander on a Special Category visa he
was outside most of the housing assistance available to citizens and permanent residents. The
Justice and Equity Centre's solicitor spoke for him in the article; Homelessness NSW and other
advocates called for trauma-informed engagement by police rather than daily displacement.

## The legal mechanism

- **Move-on directions** — Part 14 of the *Law Enforcement (Powers and Responsibilities) Act 2002*
  (NSW) lets police direct a person in a public place to leave if their presence is obstructing,
  harassing, causing fear or (for intoxicated persons) disorderly. Disobeying is an offence. The
  *NSW Protocol for Homeless People in Public Places* says homelessness itself is not a reason to
  move someone on.
- **No right to housing** — NSW has no enforceable right to shelter; the *Housing Act 2001* (NSW)
  creates a landlord, not an entitlement. Eligibility for social housing and most specialist services
  turns on residency status.
