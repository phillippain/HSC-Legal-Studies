Community Justice Centres, established under the *Community Justice Centres Act 1983* (NSW),
provided **free mediation** of neighbour and civil disputes for more than forty years. They **closed
on 30 June 2025**. In **2022–23 they resolved 79%** of the neighbourhood disputes they mediated.

Local Government NSW opposed the closure. Its president, Mayor Phyllis Miller OAM: *"CJCs offer an
equitable legal pathway that vulnerable people in our communities might not otherwise afford."* The
association warned that residents would be pushed toward formal legal proceedings they may not be
able to afford, adding to court workloads and weakening community cohesion.

## Use it for

- **Alternative dispute resolution** (`shel.2.7`) — and the striking thing to say about it is that
  ADR's role in NSW is **contracting, not expanding**, at the same time as NCAT's Consumer and
  Commercial Division is receiving 52,608 applications a year. Disputes are being pushed **up** the
  escalation ladder, not resolved earlier on it.
- **A 79% resolution rate is the counter-argument to the closure**, and it is the kind of figure that
  turns an assertion about ADR's effectiveness into an evaluation.
- **Accessibility and resource efficiency in tension** — closing a service saves money in one budget
  and spends it in another, and the cost falls on the people least able to pay for the alternative.
- Neighbour disputes, boarding houses, land lease communities and strata are exactly the settings
  where the parties must keep living alongside each other, which is where mediation earns its place.
