Daniel Billings **pleaded guilty** to murdering Molly Ticehurst in a 2024 domestic violence attack.
Her parents were told he would be sentenced on 1 June 2026, ending two years of process. Days
beforehand they learned sentencing would be pushed back to **at least September**, because for the
first time he had invoked **mental health** to mitigate the sentence.

The defence is relying on a psychiatrist's report finding he was suffering from depression at the
time of the attack, "which gives rise to an abnormal state of mind at the time of the offence" and
was "likely to have affected his perception of the events and also his capacity to exercise proper
control over his actions".

Their local MP, Phil Donato — a former police prosecutor — told the *Herald* it was **"a travesty
for all victims of crime"**.

## Use it for

- **The role of the victim in sentencing**, which is the dot point most often answered with a
  description of a victim impact statement. This shows what the role actually amounts to: a family
  is entitled to be heard at sentencing, and has no say in **when** sentencing happens.
- **Factors affecting a sentencing decision.** Mental health as a mitigating factor, raised late,
  by an offender who has already pleaded guilty. It is a legitimate matter the court must consider,
  and it costs the family months. Both halves of that belong in an answer.
- **Delay as a cost borne by someone.** Court delay is usually argued as inefficiency; here it has a
  name attached.
- **The media and an MP as non-legal mechanisms** — the article itself is the family's avenue.

## Caution

Reported in May 2026 and **not a judgment**: the mental health material is what the defence proposed
to rely on, not a finding. Check what the court ultimately did with it before quoting an outcome. A
guilty plea to murder means the facts are not in dispute; the dispute is about sentence.
