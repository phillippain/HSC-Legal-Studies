The Special Rapporteur's official visit runs **2–13 November 2026**. The NT and Queensland refused planned visits to
**Holtze** and **Cleveland**, where about **97%** of children at Cleveland were Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander at
inspection. It is the second time in under a year the NT has blocked UN experts. First Nations young people are detained at **25.7 per 10,000** (2024–25), against a
Closing the Gap target of a 30% fall by 2031; the Productivity Commission assesses no change.

## What the mandate asks v what happened

| UN terms of reference | November 2026 |
|---|---|
| Access to all places of detention | Holtze and Cleveland visits refused |
| Confidential contact with detainees | No in-centre contact with children |
| Contact with authorities in every branch | NT and Qld refused to engage, the Rapporteur says |
| Freedom of movement | Visit otherwise proceeding |
| Findings reported to the Human Rights Council | Precedent: the WGAD relied on public reports for the NT |

The terms of reference are UN guidelines, not a treaty: visits depend on the state's invitation and cooperation.

## ABC report (4 November 2026)

Queensland gave a brief statement; the NT and the Commonwealth did not respond. Cleveland held **101** children against a
safe capacity of **95** (August 2025); about **90%** at Cleveland identified as Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander (68.2%
across Queensland's centres). First Nations young people are detained at **26 times** the non-Indigenous rate (2023–24).
Queensland's mandatory 12 months for serious offending on bail starts on 30 November. Special Rapporteurs visit under UN
terms of reference, **not OPCAT**; cooperation rests on invitation. Preliminary findings are due in Sydney on **13
November 2026**.

## ABC report (5 November 2026): "It would be a scandal"

Roxanne Fitzgerald's report accuses the NT government of hiding what is happening inside Holtze. The Special Rapporteur, **Dr Albert K. Barume**, is on a country visit to seven locations (2–13 November) under Australia's **2008 standing invitation** to UN special procedures; the refusal of Holtze and Cleveland was announced in a late-October statement.

| UN framework expects | What happened (as reported) |
|---|---|
| Access to all places of detention; contact with every branch of government | NT and Queensland refused both access and meetings, according to his statement |
| Invitation given by the Commonwealth for all of Australia | The federal Attorney-General's office says access is for each state and territory, and the refusal does not reflect the federal government's position |
| Independent preventive monitoring (OPCAT, ratified 2017) | Queensland's inspector not nominated; the NT's designation law was not in effect as at April 2024 |

- **Pattern**: the NT blocked the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention in December 2025; in 2022 the UN torture-prevention subcommittee ended its Australian visit after NSW and Queensland refused access. The NT lowered the minimum age of criminal responsibility to **10** in October 2024.
- **Allegations in the article (unverified)**: Holtze recorded **127** detainee-on-detainee assaults, nine self-harm hospitalisations and two sexual assaults in its first year (from an earlier FOI-based ABC report, not retrieved), and spit hoods have been reintroduced. John Lawrence SC says the NT government has "a lot to hide" — an opinion, not a finding.
- No Queensland government response appears in the ABC, AAP or Amnesty reports. A special rapporteur's access rests on consent and terms of reference, **not a treaty right of entry**; preliminary findings are due **13 November**.

## International standards v what the NT and Queensland did (summary of 9 November 2026)

A fuller class summary of the 5 November ABC report sets the governments' conduct against the standards they have accepted.

| International expectation | NT / Queensland |
|---|---|
| Access to detention guaranteed under the terms of reference | Holtze and Cleveland tours cancelled |
| Meetings with central and local officials | Requested meetings in the Torres Strait, Townsville and Darwin not held |
| Detention of children as a last resort (2017 Rapporteur) | NT reforms and Queensland's 2024 laws remove "last resort" |
| Age 10 below international standards (UN bodies) | NT lowered the age back to 10 (October 2024) |
| Humane treatment (CROC art 37); the NPM opposes spit hoods | NT reintroduced spit hoods for children |
| OPCAT: appoint, legislate and resource NPMs | NT Ombudsman is interim NPM; Queensland Ombudsman inspects youth centres |

- **Access was given before**: the previous Rapporteur visited Cleveland in **2017**, so refusal is a change in practice.
- **Government responses**: NT Corrections says independent oversight already exists; in December 2025 the NT minister cited
  operational capacity, safety and workforce when refusing UN access. Queensland gave no reason but wished the Rapporteur well.
- **Critics**: the HRLC says the Commonwealth should use levers such as funding.

## Another summary (10 November 2026)

A [further class summary of the 5 November ABC report](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1carJR_ffyzU94SSDwVeywpXLRB5DR6Yu/view) counts **three** UN monitoring visits obstructed since 2022 —
the Subcommittee on Prevention of Torture, the Working Group on Arbitrary Detention and now the Special Rapporteur — and
stresses that his powers come from cooperation, not compulsion.

## First Nations media coverage (NITV, 10 November 2026)

Carli Willis's NITV report centres community and Social Justice Commissioner voices: **120** submissions from First Nations people
came in before the visit, and communities called on the Prime Minister to step in. NT Parliament answers record **209** physical
assaults at Holtze in 2025. The final report goes to the Human Rights Council in **September 2027**.
