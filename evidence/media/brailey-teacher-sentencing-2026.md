A former Sydney high school teacher, arrested in August 2024, pleaded guilty in Campbelltown Local Court
(4 March 2026) to **four of eleven** charges; prosecutors withdrew the rest, including aggravated sexual
assault of a person under her authority. On 28 October 2026 she was sentenced to **15 months' imprisonment**,
eligible for parole on **24 April 2027**, and placed on the child protection register for **15 years**.

## Maximums v the sentence

| Offence | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Sexual intercourse with a 17-year-old under **special care** (s 73(2)) | 4 years |
| Sexual touching of a 16-year-old under special care (s 73A) | 4 years |
| Accessing child abuse material; causing it to be transmitted (Cth s 474.22) | 15 years each |
| **Sentence imposed** | **15 months**; parole from 24 April 2027 |

The court weighed a victim impact statement, a psychologist's report on bipolar disorder and family letters;
imprisonment only if no other penalty is appropriate (s 5). The teacher relationship is an element of the
special care offences, so abuse of trust cannot be counted again as aggravating (s 21A).

## Mental illness and the weighing (updated summary, 30 October)

| Pushes the sentence up | Pushes it down |
|---|---|
| Teaching role used to abuse two students (but weight limited where trust is already an element) | **Bipolar disorder**, likely manic episode: may reduce moral culpability and general deterrence (*De La Rosa*; *Muldrock*) |
| Harm: shame, flashbacks, left school before Year 12 | Guilty plea in the Local Court (s 25D discount) |
| She initiated contact with one student | Lost husband, job and reputation — but good character carries little weight for teachers (*Stanton v R*) |

The judge reached the sentence by **instinctive synthesis** (*Markarian*): no single correct sentence. A statutory
parole order applies at the end of the non-parole period for sentences of 3 years or less (s 158). The case also
raises the debate about consistency in sentencing female offenders.
