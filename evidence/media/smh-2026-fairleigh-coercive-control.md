Callum Fairleigh, 34 — the first person in NSW to serve prison time for coercive control.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 Jul 2024 | s 54D *Crimes Act 1900* (NSW) commences |
| Feb 2026 | Convicted of coercive control, stalking and **six AVO breaches**. **Two years' imprisonment** |
| 14 Apr 2026 | Released on parole after 15 months; 10-year ADVO |
| 23 Apr 2026 | 39 calls in 30 minutes; property damage; arrested |
| 11 Sep 2026 | **Stalking charge dropped in a plea deal**; guilty to the Commonwealth carriage service charge. **12-month community correction order** |
| 15 Sep 2026 | Released on parole — four days after sentencing |

Judge Susan McIntyre: "How many opportunities does he want to understand that this type of violence
and control directed at an intimate partner is completely unacceptable and indeed, highly illegal".
He yawned during proceedings and smiled at the ruling.

The complainant: "This shows why so many people don't bother reporting things … It is a flawed
system."

## The jurisdictional point — the strongest insight, and the one most students miss

In September 2026 he was convicted only of a **Commonwealth** offence (*Criminal Code Act 1995*
(Cth) s 474.17) after the NSW stalking charge was dropped. The NSW provisions that push domestic
violence sentencing towards custody — s 4A CSPA, s 14(4) CDPVA — are built around **NSW** offences.
A course of conduct that began as coercive control was answered by a telecommunications offence
sentenced outside the framework the state built for it.

Serves **Crime** (jurisdiction, charge negotiation, post-sentencing) and **Family** (state/federal
division, core in 2024(a)).

## Cautions

Not a reported judgment — cite as "as reported in the *Sydney Morning Herald* on 16 September 2026".
A District Court sentencing decision is not precedent. The car incident and property damage are
reported as **alleged**. One case proves possibility, never typicality. Parole can be revoked, so
write "as at September 2026".
