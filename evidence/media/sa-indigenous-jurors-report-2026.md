The South Australian Law Reform Institute (SALRI) made **34 recommendations** to the SA Attorney-General (law stated at
30 April 2026). About **37%** of Australian prisoners were Indigenous (30 June 2025), but there is **no reliable data** on
Indigenous juror numbers. The Attorney-General says the government will respond "in due course".

## Current law v SALRI

| Stage | Now | SALRI recommends |
|---|---|---|
| Source list | Electoral roll only; Indigenous voters under-enrolled | Supplementary lists, possibly SA First Nations Voice data (Canadian model) |
| Criminal history (s 12) | Any imprisonable conviction in five years disqualifies | A proportionate sliding scale, narrower exclusions |
| Driving | Licence disqualification over six months disqualifies | Remove it as a ground |
| Summons | Formal, legalistic; post unreliable in many communities | Plainer summons explaining jury service |
| Regional courts | District Court circuits only Port Augusta and Mount Gambier | Add Port Pirie, Berri, Port Lincoln (resources permitting) |
| Peremptory challenges | Three per accused | Keep, with de-identified data collection |
| Data | None | Collect data; an Implementation Committee with Indigenous input |

SALRI also recommends **cultural obligations** as a ground for excusal, but rejects automatic exemption. Barriers stack:
each stage — roll, jury district, disqualification, summons, challenge — removes more Indigenous people.
