**Maria and John Tsolakis** bought a townhouse at **Ettalong Beach in 2021**. The listing promised a
"low-maintenance" home fusing "impeccable quality with exquisite modern design". "The first summer was
almost magical."

Then the drips started — bedroom, living room, garage — revealing a **waterproofing failure**:
mouldy stairs, moist wooden beams, and a **balcony railing secured by two screws through a concrete
sheet**.

## The disclosure failure

They did everything a careful purchaser is told to do. They **bought a strata report** and **had a
lawyer read it** before agreeing to buy. The report included the **owners' corporation's minutes**,
which recorded a water issue "**that a builder had agreed to fix**". Their agent reassured them:
"It's all good… All new buildings have little water leaks."

What the minutes did **not** record was that a **building work rectification order** had been made
for the site, and that **other members of the owners' corporation knew** about the saga. The minutes
were the disclosure, and the disclosure was incomplete.

## The system around it

- **At least 4.2 million Australians** live in buildings managed by **owners' corporations** — a
  group of owners who jointly take on the legal and financial responsibility for the building's
  upkeep.
- That system was **first legislated in NSW in 1961**, and strata experts warn Australia's
  **60-year-old strata system** is "**going to implode**" under the pressure of the defects crisis,
  which is producing a "**lawyer's breakfast**" as unit owners spend thousands fighting developers and
  builders in court.
- Owners who find defects are described as battling **secretive owners' corporations**, **obfuscating
  strata management companies**, **the Building Commission removing some orders before works are
  completed**, and **outdated legislation** — "causing stress and millions of dollars in legal fees".
- Context from the series: defects cost Sydney homeowners and taxpayers about **$700 million a year**.
  **Premier Chris Minns** defended the status quo, pointing to the Building Commission's record under
  its inaugural commissioner **David Chandler** and its focus on prosecution; there are inevitably
  "bad operators in the system", he said. The opposition building spokesman called revelations about
  **dodgy training certifications** "shocking".

## Use it for

- **shel.2.4 — strata as the mechanism, and the mechanism failing.** Most defect answers are about
  builders and warranties. This is about the **owners' corporation**: a body corporate of amateurs
  carrying statutory responsibility for a building they did not construct, under a scheme designed in
  **1961** for a very different housing market. "**At least 4.2 million Australians**" is the number
  to quote.
- **The best disclosure example in the library (shel.2.1 / inv.1.2)** — read it immediately after
  `law-society-selling-a-home-checklist` and `contract-for-sale-of-land-2022-residential`. Vendor
  disclosure plus a purchased strata report plus a lawyer's review **still did not reveal a
  rectification order**, because the obligation ran to the **minutes** and the minutes were silent.
  A precise illustration that a disclosure regime is only as good as what must be recorded.
- **Information asymmetry between co-owners (shel.2.5)** — the people who knew were the **other lot
  owners**, and they were under no duty to tell a purchaser. Ask what reform would fix that: a duty
  to disclose rectification orders on the certificate, rather than relying on minutes.
- **Remedy versus cost (dis.2.7)** — "a lawyer's breakfast", "millions of dollars in legal fees", and
  owners litigating against developers. The right exists; whether it is worth exercising is the real
  question, and it links to `pafburn-2024` on the duty of care and to the statutory warranty period.
- **Regulator behaviour (shel.2.8)** — the allegation that the Building Commission **removed orders
  before works were completed** is the most serious enforcement claim here. Pair it with
  `nsw-building-commissioner` and `smh-2025-defects-cost-700m`.
- **Reform in train (chg.1.3)** — the *Strata Schemes Legislation Amendment (Miscellaneous) Bill
  2025* and the Design and Building Practitioners and Residential Apartment Buildings Acts are the
  legal responses; this is the evidence of what they have not yet reached.

## Caution

A **newspaper feature in a campaigning series** ("Shoddy Sydney"), written to build pressure for
reform — the framing is deliberate and the government's position is given only briefly. The
Tsolakises' account is **theirs**: that the minutes omitted the rectification order, that other
owners knew, and what the agent said are **their allegations**, untested by any court or tribunal,
and **no builder, developer, strata manager or agent has been found to have done anything wrong**.
Do not name or blame anyone.

The claims about the **Building Commission removing orders before completion** and about **fraudulent
training certifications** are reported allegations from the series, not findings; the **$700 million**
figure is the series' own estimate and is already recorded at `smh-2025-defects-cost-700m` — this
record is the **strata and disclosure** angle, so use the two together rather than double-counting
the series. "At least 4.2 million Australians" and "60-year-old strata system" are the article's
figures. Published **April 2025**: the *Strata Schemes Legislation Amendment (Miscellaneous) Bill
2025* and any Building Commission changes have moved since, and the Building Commissioner named in
the series is **James Sherrard**, not Chandler — check who holds the office. Strata law is **NSW**
specific.
