On **4 November 2026** WA Aboriginal Affairs Minister **Don Punch** denied direct responsibility for the Pilbara child protection crisis, agreed to visit South Hedland and refused calls to resign. The crisis broke on **14 September 2026** after *The Australian* reported **up to 15 children** living unsupervised in a state-owned South Hedland home despite complaints; the government disputes that they were regularly unsupervised but concedes the investigation was too slow. **The National Commissioner's review has not reported.**

## What the law requires v what critics report

| Children and Community Services Act 2004 (WA) | What elders and critics report |
|---|---|
| Best interests paramount (s 7) | Children reportedly unsupervised despite complaints from residents and police |
| Prompt action; intervention as a last resort | Minister concedes the department was too slow; the town about five officers short |
| Aboriginal participation in decisions (ss 13–14) | Elders say government repeatedly fails to engage them |
| Placement priority: family, then Aboriginal community, then culturally responsive carers (s 12) | Elders want children moved to safer homes |
| Oversight: National Commissioner may inquire (2026 Act s 17) | Review under way; advocates want a WA Aboriginal children's commissioner |

## The pathway for a concern about a child

1. **Report and inquiry** — the CEO may inquire (s 31); certain people must report child sexual abuse (s 124B).
2. **Assessment** — support services, planning meetings or investigation of whether the child is in need of protection (ss 28, 32).
3. **Taking into care** — with a warrant, or without where risk is immediate and substantial (s 37).
4. **Protection order** — Children's Court (ss 45–66).
5. **Placement** — the s 12 priority order, with consultation before placing an Aboriginal child (s 81; confirmed by heading only).

The South Hedland case reportedly stalled at steps 1–2.
