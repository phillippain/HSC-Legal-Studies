A months-long investigation into the NSW child protection system.

| Finding | Figure |
|---|---|
| Children in state care | **15,000** |
| Child protection reports **not assessed** by DCJ | **more than 9,000** |
| Children spending **over five years** in care before exiting | **half** — four times the national average |
| Children moved through **six or more placements** | **200** |
| Extra foster carers needed each year | **600** |
| Annual cost to the state | **$3.1 billion** |
| Caseworker vacancy rate (Q3 FY2023) | **7%**, and about **15%** on the Mid North Coast, New England and northern NSW; ~10% in Sydney's west and south-west |
| Caseworkers on long-term compensation in the last financial year | **50** |

Caseworkers speaking anonymously described staff shortages, growing administrative demands and
thinning budgets, alongside an over-reliance on emergency accommodation.

## Use it for

- **The resource efficiency criterion**, which students usually assert. Here it is measured: 9,000
  unassessed reports is what "under-resourced" means, and $3.1 billion a year is what a failure to
  intervene early costs the same budget.
- **Mandatory reporting without capacity to respond** — the statutory obligation to report generates
  the notifications; nothing guarantees the assessment. Pair with the AIHW figure that notifications
  rose while substantiations fell.
- **The role of the media** (`fam.2.7`) — freedom of information data and anonymous caseworkers
  produced what departmental reporting did not.
