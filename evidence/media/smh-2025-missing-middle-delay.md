The Minns government announced its **"missing middle"** policy in **November 2023** — low- and
mid-rise housing in established suburbs — saying it would create capacity for more than **112,000
homes**, about **30% of its National Housing Accord target of 377,000 new dwellings by mid-2029**.

**Stage one**, allowing dual occupancies and semi-detached dwellings across most of NSW, commenced
in July 2024. **Stage two** — terraces, townhouses and small apartment blocks near transport hubs
and town centres — was **delayed until after the September 2024 local council elections**,
indicated for later in 2024, and still had not arrived by February 2025.

Urban Development Institute chief executive **Stuart Ayres**, on the fourteen-month delay: "The
heavy lifter of housing reform is now looking more like a handbrake on new" homes — industry groups
warning the uncertainty itself was stalling delivery.

## Use it for

- **shel.3.1 / shel.3.4 (affordability, social housing)** — supply is the half of the problem that
  planning law controls, and this is what happens to it in practice. Grattan's recommendation was
  denser development "as of right"; this is the attempt, and its timetable.
- **chg.4.2 (limitations of reform)** — a reform **announced** is not a reform **in force**. The
  Accord number counts dwellings the policy would *enable*, not dwellings built, and even that
  capacity is unavailable while the instrument is unmade. Excellent against any answer that treats
  an announcement as a response.
- **chg.3.2 (executive methods) and the politics of delay** — stage two was held back **until after
  a local council election**, which is the sort of concrete fact that makes an argument about
  political will rather than asserting it.
- **inv.5.2 (division of powers)** — the Commonwealth sets the Accord target, the state writes the
  planning rules, councils resist the density. Three levels, one target, and the friction between
  them is where the homes go missing.
- **Uncertainty as a cost** — the industry's complaint is not that the rules are wrong but that
  nobody knows what they will be. Good for the rule-of-law idea that law must be knowable in advance
  to be useful.

## Caution

**February 2025**, and the whole point is a policy in motion: **check whether stage two has since
commenced, and in what form**, before using any of this in the present tense. The "100,000 homes" in
the headline and the 112,000 in the text are **capacity figures from the government's own
modelling** — they are not homes built, approved or funded, and should never be quoted as delivery.
The sources quoted are **development industry groups**, who want the reforms and have a commercial
interest in them; councils and residents opposing density are not represented here. One article in a
continuing story.
