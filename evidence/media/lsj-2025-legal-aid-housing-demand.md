Legal Aid NSW's 2023–24 annual report recorded a **19% increase** in demand for housing-related legal
help — **almost 4,500 people a year** seeking assistance with housing and tenancy. Natalie Bradshaw,
Senior Solicitor heading its Housing and Homelessness teams: "We've seen an increase in rents in
recent years and a lot of people who were already struggling to find affordable housing have just
felt that pressure in the NSW rental market." Many are first-time users of legal services.

- **Rationing**: "We do have limited resources and that means that we have to restrict eligibility for
  some of our ongoing services, to make sure that we're helping the most vulnerable people."
- **Reform helps, but**: "the end of no grounds terminations will help people in the private rental
  market, just to create a more stable housing environment"; Legal Aid will keep speaking to
  government about "that balance between consumer rights and protections for tenants".
- **No respite**: "Given that so many people are paying such a high percentage of their incomes
  towards housing … we'll still see that demand for our services."

## Caution

A short **news report of an annual-report figure**; the 19% and 4,500 are Legal Aid's own numbers
and were not checked against the annual report. The article predates the reforms' commencement
(19 May 2025) — the effect on demand is a forecast. Law Society Journal is the professional body's
magazine, not an independent evaluator.
