ACT police respond to about **12** domestic and family violence incidents a day. In NSW, **21.1%** of ADVOs in force in
late 2025 were breached at least once (BOCSAR); no ACT breach rate was found. The six-month trial starts on
**Monday 9 November 2026**.

## What the trial adds

| | Current law and practice | SMS trial |
|---|---|---|
| Knowing the order | Respondent present when made, or personally served | Texts only to people already served |
| Reminders | None after service | Reminders of order and bail obligations |
| Support | Depends on individual police contact | Support services and a police contact point |
| Breach | Offence under s 43: up to 500 penalty units, 5 years' jail | Unchanged — texts add to, not replace, enforcement |
| Coverage | Everyone bound by an order | Not everyone will receive messages |

## Earlier trials

NSW court-date texts to **4,388** people with ADVOs (2016–18) cut non-attendance but not breaches significantly;
Queensland reported SMS contact cut repeat offending (2022). DVCS ACT CEO Sue Webeck is sceptical, and a listener feared
texts could provoke perpetrators; police were not concerned.

## Use it for

- **fam.2.4** — whether protection orders work, and the gap between making an order and compliance.
- **Effectiveness** — a cheap, technology-based non-legal measure; the result depends on an evaluation not yet designed in public.

## Caution

A **class evidence summary of a police announcement**; how recipients are chosen and how results will be measured were
not published.
