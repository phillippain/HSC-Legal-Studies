**DB** was two years and nine months old when she was murdered in December 2016 by **Mohammed
Khazma**, her mother's partner of about three months, whom her mother had met on Facebook. She died
of brain and spinal cord injuries. At his 2019 trial the jury heard she had sustained **114
injuries**, including burns from the top of a cigarette lighter, which he called "smileys". Justice
Fullerton was satisfied he derived "some perverse pleasure" from her pain and jailed him for **44
years, non-parole 33**. Her mother pleaded guilty to **manslaughter** for failing to protect her and
was jailed for three years, non-parole 16 months.

The inquest asked a different question: why was DB "not visible to people who could have assisted
her"?

## The chain of failures the Coroner found

| | |
|---|---|
| May 2015 | The family came to DCJ's notice on a **risk of significant harm** report from an anonymous caller, and was referred to **Brighter Futures**, run by The Benevolent Society. The Coroner accepted the referral was **"premature"**, had a **"devastating flow-on effect"**, and was a missed opportunity for a formal departmental response |
| Over three months | **Seven ROSH calls** about DB and her brother, raising abuse and neglect and noting the children "were coming into contact with people who may be dangerous" |
| | A caseworker characterised the reports as **"malicious" or "vexatious"** after the mother said a former friend was making them — **unaware they came from five different women** |
| August 2015 | The seventh call, from a Benevolent Society caseworker, warned the mother would not seek medical help for any injury and that "future concerns may go unnoticed given the family has moved". The Coroner called this report **"chilling"**; it "should have prompted immediate action" |
| | **The file did not follow the family** to the new district, so there was no further risk assessment and the case was closed. "No adequate reason has been given for this major failing… I consider this a very significant failure" |
| Aug 2015 – Dec 2016 | **No recorded contact** between DCJ and the family, and no further Helpline call, until DB died |

The Coroner called the closure, on the information available at the time, **"inappropriate"**.

## Use it for

- **fam.3.4 (care and protection)** — the mandatory reporting system worked: seven reports were
  made. What failed was what the department did with them. That distinction is what separates a good
  answer from a vague one about "under-resourcing".
- **Discretion and assessment** — the single most teachable fact is that a caseworker could dismiss
  reports as malicious **without knowing who had made them**. The s 23 ROSH threshold and the s 30
  discretion to investigate "as it considers necessary" are where this happened.
- **The non-government partner** — the referral to Brighter Futures was itself a failure, because it
  substituted for a departmental response. Good for `fam.2.6` and for whether outsourcing early
  intervention weakens statutory protection.
- **chg.2 (agencies of reform)** — the coroner again as the body that examines what no one else
  does. Pair with the **BLGN and DG** inquest (two infant sisters, five years apart, reports never
  acted on) and the NSW Ombudsman's finding that DCJ closes most screened-in ROSH reports without
  allocating a caseworker. Three bodies, the same finding, a decade apart.
- **Crime cross-over** — a murder conviction at 44 years and a manslaughter conviction for failure
  to protect, from the same facts.

## Caution

The library holds the **SMH report of the findings**, not the findings themselves; the full
**50-page document is public** on the Coroners Court site and should be read before quoting the
Coroner directly. The child is **DB** and her mother cannot be named for legal reasons — keep to
those protections. Coronial findings are made on the balance of probabilities and the
recommendations are **not binding**; the article notes DCJ "has commenced addressing some of the
key recommendations", which is the department's account and not a finding. The events are from
**2015–16**, so the practices criticised may have changed; check against the Ombudsman's 2026 ROSH
report before saying this is how the system works now. The injury detail is distressing and should
be used sparingly.
