Police were called to a bar on **Liverpool Street in Kings Cross** at about **4.30pm on Thursday 6
February 2025** after reports that a man was displaying a flag bearing a swastika. The venue is
**less than 150 metres from the Sydney Jewish Museum**.

A **51-year-old** and a **44-year-old** were arrested nearby and taken to Kings Cross Police Station.
The **flag and several electronic devices were seized**. Both were charged with "**knowingly display
by public act Nazi symbol without excuse**" and were **refused bail**, to appear before the **Downing
Centre Local Court** the following day.

The arrests came as the state government announced a package including a **new criminal offence of
intentionally inciting racial hatred**, **strengthened protections for places of worship**, and an
**aggravated offence for graffiti** on those sites. Premier Chris Minns was to introduce the laws
when Parliament resumed.

## Use it for

- **hr.2.9 — the offence being used, days before it was strengthened.** The charge was laid under the
  **pre-existing NSW Nazi symbol offence** (inserted into the *Crimes Act 1900* in 2022), while the
  government was announcing the 2025 package. That is an unusually neat demonstration that
  **enforcement and reform run in parallel**: the law already existed, was being enforced, and was
  about to be toughened.
- **The elements are visible in the charge wording (crime.1.2)** — "**knowingly** display **by public
  act** Nazi symbol **without excuse**". Four components: a mental element, a public act, a defined
  symbol, and a defence of reasonable excuse. Use it to show how an offence is constructed, and note
  that the **reasonable excuse** carve-out (educational, artistic, historical and religious uses) is
  what keeps the offence compatible with free expression.
- **Place matters** — **150 metres from the Sydney Jewish Museum**. The 2025 NSW package then created
  a specific offence of displaying Nazi symbols **on or near synagogues, Jewish schools or the Sydney
  Jewish Museum**, so this incident is effectively the fact pattern that reform was written for. A
  very tight problem-to-response pairing.
- **Bail refused (crime.2.6)** — for a summary offence with a then-maximum of about one year. Worth
  asking why, under the *Bail Act 2013*, and whether that reflects unacceptable risk or the
  circumstances of the offence. Note the Commonwealth later raised the symbol maxima to **five years**
  with a **12-month mandatory minimum**, which changes the bail calculus entirely.
- **Symbolic harm as legal harm (hr.1.1)** — nobody was physically hurt. The law treats the **display
  itself** as the wrong, which is the clearest available illustration that vilification is a harm the
  law recognises, and the beginning of the free speech argument.
- **Read it with `hate-crimes-reform-2025-cth-and-nsw`** for what passed, and with
  `wertheim-v-haddad-2025` for the **civil** vilification route. Criminal charge, civil proceeding,
  legislative reform — three mechanisms responding to the same issue, which is exactly what an hr.2
  question wants.

## Caution

**An arrest report published the night of the arrest.** Both men were **charged, not convicted**, and
"alleged display" is doing real work — the outcome of the charges is **not recorded here and must be
checked** before the record is used as evidence of anything beyond a charge being laid. Neither man is
named in the article (ages only), and they should not be identified.

The record does **not** give the section number: the NSW Nazi symbol offence was inserted by the
*Crimes Amendment (Prohibition on Display of Nazi Symbols) Act 2022* and sits in Part 11A of the
*Crimes Act 1900* — **check the current provision and penalty** rather than relying on the charge
wording quoted by police, and note the **penalties and the mandatory minimum changed within weeks**
of this incident. The reforms described at the end of the article were, at publication, **announced
and not yet introduced**; see the legislation record for what was actually enacted by 21 February
2025. A two-page news report is thin: use it for the **charge and the setting**, and take the law from
the statute.
