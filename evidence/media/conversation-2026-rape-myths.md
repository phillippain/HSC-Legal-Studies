Jane Gilmore (Monash University), published the day after the NSW reforms were introduced, explaining
what Premier Chris Minns means by curbing "rape myth questions".

## What a rape myth is

False beliefs that minimise, erase or justify sexual violence, organised around a script of "real
rape": a young, conventionally attractive, morally unassailable woman, attacked at night in public
by a stranger who is obviously a monster; she fights back, is visibly injured, reports immediately,
and recalls every detail in order — tearful, but not numb, wild or angry.

Everything outside that script reduces credibility. The myths also carry race, age, disability and
sexuality: women of colour placed outside the "pure victim" requirement, older people and people
with disability presumed to have no sexuality at all, and men, trans and non-binary people, lesbians
and gay men erased from the ideal-victim construction. Alcohol and drugs cut both ways — reducing
responsibility for the man, increasing blame for the woman. Behind all of it sits the belief that
**false allegations are common**.

## Why it matters legally

- **Reporting**: about **8%** of women who were raped or sexually assaulted reported it to police in
  2021–22, down from **19%** in 2012.
- **Trial**: the myths enter through questioning, which is what the NSW pilot is aimed at, alongside
  the reforms on pre-recorded evidence-in-chief and counselling records.
- Most sexual violence is committed by men the victim knows — the "monster myth" is what lets that
  be ignored.
