At the **Education Ministers Meeting (EMM) on 2 November 2026** the Commonwealth, states and territories agreed that technology and AI in schools must be **evidence-based and purposeful** and restricted where they do not support learning and wellbeing. Education Minister Jason Clare had floated a "good digital detox" after **PISA 2025** showed record-low maths and reading scores. **Most decisions are commitments to introduce, trial or consider; states, territories and sectors decide implementation.**

## Current settings v what ministers agreed

| | Current | Agreed / proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Screen limits | No national limits; decided school by school | Ministers to introduce, trial or consider limits; states decide |
| Generative AI | Schools choose which tools students use | Restricted in primary schools and in secondary student assessments |
| Year 5 NAPLAN writing | Completed online | Handwritten from **2028**, subject to ACARA advice on feasibility and cost |
| NSW | Phones banned in public high schools since Term 4 2023 | Device caps by year level (K–2: 15 min a day); recommended 2027, mandatory 2028 |
| Victoria | — | 90-minute daily device limit for Years 3–6 from 2027 |
| Western Australia | No state-wide approach | Six-month K–2 screen pilot in 10 public schools from 2027 ($50,000 each) |
| Disability adjustments | — | NSW and WA exempt adjustments and assistive technology; the national communique is silent |

A **Digital Detox Taskforce** led by an independent expert will advise; devices and AI stay a standing ministers' agenda item until at least the end of 2027. Only the new Australian Teaching and Learning Commission (merging ACARA, AITSL, AERO and ESA) needs Commonwealth legislation.

## The rights timeline

- UDHR art 26 (1948): everyone has the right to education.
- ICESCR art 13: Australia ratified in 1975.
- CRC arts 28–29: ratified 1990. **General Comment No. 25 (2021)** applies children's rights to the digital environment: technology must not undermine in-person education, yet digital literacy must still be taught.
- December 2025: social media minimum age of 16 takes effect.

## Competing perspectives

- **Governments**: the OECD links leisure device use during school with much lower performance; only **23%** of Australian students report no device distraction against an OECD average of **39%**.
- **Researchers and educators**: how screens are used matters more than time; children have rights to protection **and** participation; evidence is mixed; Victorian principals say screen time is a bigger problem at home.
