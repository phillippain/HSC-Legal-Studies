## The numbers

**225** young people in custody in December 2024, **up 54** on the year before — a **32%** rise.
Young Indigenous people in detention up almost **22%**, to **129**, which is well over half the
total. Over the same period BOCSAR's crime statistics showed **youth crime rates steady**, with only
a slight uptick in shoplifting.

BOCSAR's executive director **Jackie Fitzgerald** attributed the rise to more children held on
**remand** after an "increase in the severity of bail decisions":

> "Over the past year the remand rate has increased across a whole range of offences, not just car
> theft and break in, but assault charges too."

## The law behind it

In 2024 the Minns government introduced a **one-year change to the youth bail laws** making it
harder for a young person who allegedly commits a break and enter or car theft **while already on
bail** to get bail again. The **NSW Bar Association** and the **Law Society of NSW** opposed it,
arguing it would jail more vulnerable children. The government then moved to **extend the laws to
2028**, alongside a further **$2 million** for diversionary programs.

Fitzgerald's judgement is the careful bit: the rise was **not entirely** due to the legislative
change, given the spread of offences — but she thought it likely had an influence, because

> "even though it is specific to a certain offence, it can send a signal about the government's
> intention with bail and that can be influential on bail authorities."

Independent MP **Roy Butler** (Barwon), a former police administrator, said he would vote for the
extension but "it's not actually going to change the long-term outcomes."

## Caution

The data is **December 2024** and the article **February 2025**; custody numbers move, so check
BOCSAR's current quarterly release before quoting figures as present-day. The causal claim is
**contested by the person who produced the data** — she says the legislative change is not the whole
explanation — so do not write that the bail laws caused the rise. The "youth crime rates remained
steady" line refers to recorded crime, which is not the same as offending. The extension to 2028 was
still before Parliament when this was written: confirm it passed before relying on it.
