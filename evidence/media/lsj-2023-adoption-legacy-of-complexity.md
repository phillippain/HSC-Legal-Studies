A Law Society Journal survey of NSW adoption law, written for lawyers, which is why it is unusually
precise about **process and timeframes**. Five types of adoption in NSW: local (including known-child),
intercountry, out-of-home care (carer) adoption, special needs, and intrafamily.

## The permanency hierarchy — s 10A of the Care Act

Inserted by the **Child Protection Legislation Amendment Act 2014**, the **Permanent Placement
Principles** rank long-term options in order:

1. **restoration** to the birth parents, to preserve the family relationship;
2. **guardianship** with a relative, kin or other suitable person;
3. allocation of **parental responsibility** under s 79;
4. **adoption** — except for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child;
5. parental responsibility to the **Minister**;
6. **adoption of an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child** — last.

## The numbers

- **208** adoptions finalised across Australia in 2021–22, down from 264; **92%** of children already
  living in Australia; just **16** from overseas. NSW had **131** — more than half the national total.
- **15,895** children in out-of-home care in NSW; **over 46,000** nationally (2021 AIHW data). Of
  45,400 Australian children in care in 2022, **19,400** were Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander.
- Adopt Change's 2017 *Barriers to Adoption* survey: **almost 83%** reported barriers, **over 80%**
  found the process "complex and overwhelming", **56%** met unexplained delays. The process took
  **1–4 years** for almost 60%, **5–9 years** for about a third, and **ten years or more** for just
  over **6%**.
- Median wait for an **intercountry** adoption: **four years** (South Korea two, the Philippines
  seven).
- Cost: local adoption **over $3,000**; intercountry about **$10,000** before travel, immigration and
  translation. No fee for a foster carer adopting a child in their care. A DCJ **Hardship Policy**
  offers fee relief.
- Consent of both parents was **dispensed with or not required in 75%** of carer adoptions in
  2021–22.

## Guardianship, open adoption, and the Indigenous principles

The 2014 changes also created the **guardianship** regime: a Children's Court order placing a child
with an authorised carer until 18, **without adoption**. The article's lawyer sources argue adoption
is "more permanent and robust" — guardianship **expires at 18**, the legal relationship with the
birth parents continues, and a birth parent can apply to **vary or rescind** the order, which
unsettles the placement even when the application fails. Children on guardianship orders are not in
statutory care and so are "effectively under the radar".

All Australian jurisdictions follow **open adoption**: the adoptive parents support the child's
contact with their birth family and heritage, with research linking openness to better self-esteem
and mental health. The *Adoption Act* ss 35 and 39 contain the **Aboriginal and Torres Strait
Islander Child Placement Principles**, require consultation with an approved Indigenous person, and
require the court to be satisfied adoptive parents can help the child build a positive cultural
identity and cope with racism. A child aged **12 or over** with sufficient capacity can consent to
their own adoption alone.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.6 (adoption)** — the one source that gives you the **statutory ranking** rather than just
  "adoption is a legal option". Knowing adoption sits **fourth**, and last for Aboriginal children,
  is the kind of specific detail that lifts a response.
- **fam.3.4 (care and protection) — permanency as the problem.** Over 46,000 children in care against
  208 adoptions a year. Use the gap, not the adjective.
- **Effectiveness, with data** — delay (years to a decade), cost ($3,000 / $10,000), and complexity,
  all sourced. Then the counterweight: assessment delay exists to protect children, which is why
  "faster" is not automatically "better".
- **Guardianship vs adoption (fam.3.2)** — a genuine, examinable comparison. Guardianship is less
  severing and reversible; adoption is permanent past 18. Which better serves the child is a real
  argument with evidence on both sides.
- **Aboriginal self-determination (hr.2.9 / fam.t.values)** — the placement principles, the
  consultation requirement, and the ranking of Indigenous adoption last are a direct legislative
  response to the Stolen Generations. Set against 19,400 Aboriginal children in care, they make the
  point that a legal safeguard is not the same as an outcome.
- **"The government should not be a 'parent'"** — Adopt Change's CEO. A short, pointed quote for the
  non-legal response to care and protection.

## Caution

**Published December 2023**, and the data in it is from **2021–22** AIHW reporting — do not present
any figure here as current. It is written for the **Law Society Journal**, so the voices quoted are
practitioners, DCJ and **advocacy organisations** (Adopt Change, Life Without Barriers) with
positions of their own: the case for more adoption is argued, not neutral, and no critic of
adoption-from-care is quoted. The Adopt Change survey is **self-selected** among people who pursued
adoption, so its barrier figures describe that group, not all families. The s 10A hierarchy is
summarised here; check the Care Act text before quoting the order. Nothing in the article is a
court ruling.
