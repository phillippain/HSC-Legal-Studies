The *Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024* (NSW) ended no-grounds evictions from **19 May
2025**: a landlord must now give one of the prescribed reasons, including **significant repairs or
renovations**. The regulation originally required supporting evidence — quotes from licensed
builders, receipts for materials — alongside the written statement. In late June 2025 the Government
amended the *Residential Tenancies Amendment Regulation 2025* to remove the additional evidence
requirement. A **written statement** is now enough.

## The reaction

- **28 organisations**, led by the Tenants' Union of NSW and including Community Legal Centres NSW
  and the Aboriginal Legal Service, issued a joint statement: without the full evidence requirements
  "there is a real risk that the Government's commitment to end no-ground evictions … will be
  undermined".
- Leo Patterson Ross (TU CEO): "While strong penalties exist on paper, the Government folded so
  quickly on this evidence requirement that it raises concern about their willingness to stand by their
  own much-needed legislation … This further opens the door to abuse." The TU had warned of
  Canada's experience of "renovictions" used to lift rents.
- The change was made "behind closed doors": CLCNSW was not consulted and the TU was briefed
  after the decision.
- **Minister Anoulack Chanthivong**: "a minor change" showing "we will continue to adapt to industry
  and renter's concerns"; "we have not changed the fact landlords need a legitimate reason to end a
  lease". Consultation occurred during drafting of the regulation.
- The **SMH** (2 September 2025) placed it alongside a second concession to landlords and framed
  the Premier as having "acted for landlords, twice". A disallowance motion was moved in the
  Legislative Council in August 2025.

## Use it for

- **shel.2.3 (termination)** — the sharpest current example of how a reform's effectiveness depends
  on its *evidentiary* detail: a prescribed ground is only as strong as what must be proved.
- **shel.2.9 / shel.2.10 / chg.2** — NGOs (a 28-signatory joint statement) and the media (LSJ, SMH)
  as agencies of reform *after* the Act passed — scrutiny of delegated legislation.
- **inv.3.3 / inv.4.4 (delegated legislation)** — the change was made by **regulation**, not by Parliament,
  which is why it could be done quietly and why the Legislative Council's disallowance power
  mattered.
- **Evaluation** — enforceability: penalties "on paper" versus the $8.4 million Rental Taskforce's
  capacity to check whether a stated renovation ever happens.

## Caution

The LSJ article is the primary document here; the SMH item in the library is a **class evidence
table**, not the article, and some of its "evaluation" entries (an interstate case, a 9% rent-rise
figure) were not checked — use the table's reform list, not its commentary, and go to the SMH for
quotes. The outcome of the disallowance motion was not confirmed for this record. The regulation
number and commencement date should be taken from the NSW legislation website before citing
them in an exam answer.
