When a camera catches an offence, the owner can **nominate** the real driver within 28 days by statutory or online
declaration. 7.30 found sellers taking fines and points for cash, and agencies relying largely on **trust** at the transfer
stage. Police allege a Woodridge woman, 27, had **1,150 points** and **1,266 infringements** (about $620,000 in fines)
put on her licence from 2022 to June 2026; she is charged with one count of fraud and presumed innocent.

## Legitimate v fraudulent nomination

| | Legitimate | Black market |
|---|---|---|
| Who is named | The person actually driving | A paid stranger, fake identity or unwitting third party |
| Declaration | Truthful, within 28 days (s 114) | Knowingly false, through the same system |
| Road safety | Points land on the risky driver | Drivers who should be suspended keep driving |
| Legal status | A defence for the owner | False declaration (s 194) or fraud (s 408C), both imprisonable |

## The cases

- **Nicholas Anforth** (Operation Blot, 695 points falsely nominated): three years' jail, Beenleigh District Court, June 2026.
- **Bin Zhang** (20 false declarations over six years): four months wholly suspended plus six months' disqualification;
  appeal dismissed in *Zhang v Commissioner of Police* [2025] QDC 31.
- A District Court scheme (2012–2022): about 1,400 false renominations avoided more than $420,000 in fines.
- NSW ran a 2024 taskforce on about 350 drivers with 20+ points. Neither state's nomination page describes any **cap** on
  nominations to one licence.

## Use it for

- **crime.2.3 / crime.2.1** — detection depends on police inquiry or a victim complaint; 7.30 reports investigations were
  limited by resources.
- **crime.4.2** — general deterrence: a three-year sentence for a seller against a suspended sentence for a buyer.
- **Effectiveness** — a system that works only while people tell the truth.
