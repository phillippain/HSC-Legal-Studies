The HSC companion to the Year 11 summary of the same story, and a different argument: not
*why law reform is hard*, but **which law is supposed to protect you, and why none of it does**.
Three topics meet on one $89 product.

| Where it fits | The law it tests |
|---|---|
| **Human Rights** — contemporary issue | ICCPR art 17 and CROC art 16 are **ratified, not incorporated**; no federal or NSW charter; *ABC v Lenah Game Meats* (2001) left a privacy tort to Parliament, which took 24 years |
| **Crime** | Voyeurism and intimate image offences, *Crimes Act 1900* (NSW) ss 91K–91R, need a **private act**; *Surveillance Devices Act 2007* s 7 covers private conversations, with a lawful-interest exception |
| **Family** | Covert monitoring as coercive control, s 54D (from 1 July 2024); ADVO conditions; *Family Law Act* s 4AB |

## The numbers the summary turns on

- **0** new federal privacy laws passed in response, as at 7 October 2026.
- **2 years** maximum for basic voyeurism under s 91K — and only where a private act is filmed.
- **379** coercive control incidents recorded in NSW in the 12 months to June 2026 (BOCSAR), up
  25.1%; the first prison sentence came in February 2026, and that man was released days after
  reoffending.
- **43,000+** petition signatures; **12+** security flaws found in the HeyCyan app by ABC testing;
  one **OAIC investigation** opened on 7 October into the Shenzhen developer.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 / hr.2.7** — the clearest available illustration that ratification is not protection: the
  right exists in the treaty and nowhere enforceable in domestic law.
- **hr.2.14 (a charter of rights)** — the gap is exactly the argument for one, and the government's
  refusal of an import ban is the argument about competing rights (assistive use by vision-impaired
  people, recording in public, lawful trade).
- **fam.2.4** — the strongest current link between consumer technology and coercive control: a
  course-of-conduct offence needs proof the victim can only give once they know.
- **crime.1.5 / crime.2.2** — offences that exist but miss the conduct: filming in a street, a shop
  or a gym is generally lawful.
- **Evaluation** — the summary's own verdict is worth memorising: *non-legal pressure removed one
  product, not the next one.*

## Caution

A **class-made summary**, not a primary source, and one that reasons across three topics at once —
check that the argument you borrow belongs to the topic you are writing about. Facts are current to
7 October 2026; the OAIC investigation was confirmed in reporting of 6–7 October. The summary itself
says to check before reuse: whether the Greens bill was introduced, how the OAIC investigation ends,
any tranche 2 *Privacy Act* announcement, the OAIC Children's Online Privacy Code (due 10 December
2026), and any revocation of the Fairleigh parole. The BOCSAR figure of 379 is the 12 months to June
2026; the monitoring report record in this collection counts 682 over the offence's first two years.
