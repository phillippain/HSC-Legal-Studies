The **Social Media Minimum Age** obligation (*Online Safety Act 2021* s 63D) commenced on **10 December
2025**: age-restricted platforms must take **reasonable steps** to stop Australians under 16 holding
accounts. By mid-December 4.7 million accounts had been removed or restricted. In **March 2026** eSafety
named compliance concerns and opened investigations into **Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok and
YouTube**. Its **July 2026** evaluation found **42.1%** of children aged 10–15 still held an account
(from 52.4%), half of them never asked their age. The *Strengthening Enforcement* Act, assented to in
September 2026, **doubled the maximum penalty to A$109.2 million** per contravention and added
examination powers. On 20 October Minister **Anika Wells** said she expected the Commissioner to seek
civil penalties in the Federal Court.

## How enforcement works

1. **Obligation** — reasonable steps under s 63D, explained in eSafety guidance.
2. **Monitoring** — information-gathering notices to ten platforms.
3. **Investigation** — five platforms since March 2026; new examination powers from 12 September 2026.
4. **Federal Court** — civil penalty proceedings, which only the Commissioner can bring and only a
   court can decide. A platform that took reasonable steps is not liable even if some children got
   through.

## Use it for

- **hr.3.1 (a contemporary issue)** — protecting children (CROC art 19) against their rights to
  information, expression and participation (arts 12, 13); the AHRC warned in 2024 that a blanket ban
  limits children's rights. A High Court implied-freedom challenge (Digital Freedom Project) is
  pending.
- **chg.1.3 / chg.6.1 / chg.6** — the move from making a technology law to enforcing it, and a reform that
  needed a second Act within a year.
- **Evaluation** — enforceability measured in accounts, surveys and court outcomes, none yet decisive.

## Caution

A **short wire report** of the minister's comments: **no proceedings had been filed** as at 20 October
2026, and the platforms are presumed compliant unless a court finds otherwise. The 4.7 million counts
accounts, not children; the 42.1% is from a survey of 803 children, not a compliance finding. eSafety's
March investigations are independently confirmed in international coverage.
