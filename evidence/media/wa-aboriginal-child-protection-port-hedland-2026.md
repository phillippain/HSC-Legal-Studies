Pilbara elders accuse Aboriginal Affairs Minister Don Punch of passing the buck, with responsibility split across ministers.
After reports of children living unsupervised in South Hedland, ministers announced measures (23 September 2026), a senior
coordinator was appointed (10 October), and on 15 October the Attorney-General requested an **inquest** on fresh evidence
into three children killed by their mother in 2022, with a short review of the *Coroners Act*.

## WA law on paper v outcomes

| | On paper | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Placement (s 12) | Family first, then Aboriginal community carers | 52.6% with kin; **30.8%** with non-Indigenous carers (2024) |
| Prevention (s 32) | CEO may provide services before investigation or removal | **4.4%** of spending on family support (national 15.6%) |
| Partnership | 2021 amendments require consultation with Aboriginal representative organisations | Not proclaimed in force |
| Connection (s 8) | Best interests include cultural identity | Reunification 7% (2022–23); 73.4% on long-term orders |
| Oversight | Inquest mandatory for deaths of children held in care (s 22) | Port Hedland children not in care: inquest refused, then requested years later |
| NSW comparison | Five Placement Principle elements and an "active efforts" duty (from 2023) | WA has no active efforts duty; HRW recommends one |

WA's rate ratio was **17.8×** at 30 June 2024, the highest in Australia (HRW puts it above 20×).
