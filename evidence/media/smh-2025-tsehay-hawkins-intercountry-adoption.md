**Tsehay Hawkins**, 19, the Yellow Wiggle, was born in **Addis Ababa in 2005** and **adopted in 2006**
by Robyn and Reg. She has always known she was adopted. When she turned **18** she travelled to
**Ethiopia** for the first time, looking for her birth parents.

> "I definitely felt a connection once I was there, and just seeing everyone that looked like me and
> had similar mannerisms, it was surreal."

Her parents came to adoption after Robyn, who has **endometriosis**, was told she would not be able
to have children, and after other pathways including **IVF**. "It was a very, very emotional journey,
deciding what we were going to do." They **waited three and a half years** before the call came, and
first met their daughter at a **foster home in Ethiopia**. They adopted a second child, Kendly, from
**Colombia in 2012**. Finding their daughter's birth parents was never something they shied away
from — the article notes that the adoption process impresses that expectation from the beginning.

The family discussed all of it publicly on a podcast with two other Wiggles.

## Use it for

- **fam.1.6 — the adopted person's own perspective**, which the library otherwise lacks. The statutes
  and reports describe adoption from the outside; this is someone who grew up inside it, at the point
  where she becomes an adult and goes looking.
- **"The right to know their origins" made concrete (hr.2.7)** — the principle in **CROC arts 7 and
  8** and the stated rationale for the **integrated birth certificate**. Read the two records
  together: `integrated-birth-certificates-act-2020` is what the law can give an adopted person; a
  flight to Addis Ababa is what it cannot. That contrast is the strongest use of this record.
- **Intercountry adoption as a legal process (inv.6.6)** — **three and a half years**, two
  countries, a foster home abroad, and (from `lsj-2023-adoption-legacy-of-complexity`) a median wait
  of about **four years** and costs around **$10,000** before travel. Use it for the practical
  accessibility of adoption, and note that **only 16 children** were adopted from overseas across
  Australia in 2021–22.
- **Open adoption as a culture, not just a rule (fam.3.2)** — her parents treated the search as
  expected rather than threatening, which is precisely what open adoption is meant to produce. The
  *Adoption Act 2000* requires prospective parents to appreciate the importance of contact with the
  birth family; this is that requirement working.
- **Infertility as the route in (fam.3.3)** — endometriosis, IVF, then adoption. A link between the
  birth technology and adoption dot points, and a reminder that adoption law sits downstream of
  medical options and their limits.
- **Identity and culture (fam.1.1)** — "seeing everyone that looked like me". Connect it to the
  **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Child Placement Principles**, which exist because
  separating a child from their culture does lasting harm, and to
  `australian-2025-adoption-aboriginality`.
- **Two countries, two systems** — Ethiopia and Colombia, in the same family. Both have since
  restricted intercountry adoption, which is worth checking and makes the point that this pathway is
  narrowing.

## Caution

A **lifestyle feature** built around a celebrity and a podcast promotion — warm, consensual and not
legal analysis. It names **no legislation, no process and no authority**, so it is evidence of
**experience**, not of law: cite the *Adoption Act 2000*, the *Hague Convention on Intercountry
Adoption* and the IBC record for anything legal, and use this for the human dimension.

Everyone is **named and spoke publicly**, including the adoptive parents and both adopted children,
so there is no privacy problem — but it is a **family's personal story** and should be discussed with
that in mind, not as a case study to be picked apart. The article does **not say whether she found
her birth parents**, and nothing should be assumed either way. The adoption was in **2006** and the
**Ethiopian intercountry program has since changed substantially** (Ethiopia suspended intercountry
adoption in 2018), so this is **not** a description of a currently available pathway — check the
current list of partner countries with the Attorney-General's Department before saying what is
possible now. Robyn's **endometriosis** and the family's fertility history are personal medical
details; the legal point is the pathway, not the diagnosis. One family's positive experience is not
evidence that intercountry adoption generally works well: `smh-2025-korea-adoption-inquiry` is in the
library for the other side of that story, and should be read alongside it.
