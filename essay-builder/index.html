Choose your level of support

When you start a plan, the builder asks two quick questions and suggests a level. The level shows above the plan, you can change it at any time, and it is recorded on the plan you print or copy.

Step down. Start where you need to, then drop a level each time you plan a response. Aim to plan on your own before the exam.

Your own evidence

You are not limited to the Evidence Finder. In step 3, open My evidence in the evidence list to add a case, an Act, a news story, a report or a statistic from your own research.

Name it and cite it. Give each piece a name, its citation or its source and date, and one line on what it shows.

Stimulus. When a question comes with a stimulus, add what it gives you as Stimulus evidence. It stays with that question.

Kept on this device. Your evidence is offered in every plan, but only in this browser. Save a backup file from My evidence, and load it on another device to carry it across.

The shape of a 15-mark response

About four writing booklet pages, or 600 words. Allow about 30 minutes, including a few minutes to plan.

Introduction. Answer the question in your first sentence with a clear judgement, then name the areas you will cover.

Body. Three or four TEEACL paragraphs. Each one makes a single point, proves it with two or more pieces of evidence, and judges it against a criterion.

Conclusion. Restate your judgement and the strongest reason for it. No new evidence.
