How this site works

1The three ways in

Content walks the syllabus: course → topic → section → dot point. Themes lists each topic’s themes and challenges. Kind filters by the form of the evidence, and by the eight criteria the syllabus gives for evaluating effectiveness. Search cuts across all three at once.

2One course at a time

The switch at the top right — Both, Preliminary, HSC — scopes the whole page: the tree, the coverage grid, the search, the counts and the library. It is remembered, so a Year 11 class and a Year 12 class each open the finder where they left it.

Evidence is not hidden at the course boundary. A Crime case that supports a Preliminary content point still appears under that point while you are in Preliminary, carrying a dashed HSC evidence mark so you can see at a glance that it belongs to the other course. Those cross-course links are a large part of what the finder is for.

3Core and supporting

A piece of evidence is core to a content point when the point is what the evidence is about, and supporting when it is useful there but is really about something else. Choosing a dot point shows its core evidence first. A solid chip is core; a faint one is supporting.

4What the numbers mean

The number beside a row is how many pieces of evidence sit at or below it. A number in orange means nothing is filed there yet — on the coverage grid those cells are dashed. Where the question finder has past questions on the same dot point, the card footer says so.

5The coverage grid

One cell per dot point, darker where you hold more. It is the fastest way to see what a topic is missing before an assessment. Press a cell to jump to that dot point.

6Building an evidence sheet

Tick the box on any card. The bar at the bottom counts what you have chosen and prints it as a sheet — title, citation, one-line significance and the syllabus tags for each.

7Adding evidence

New material is dropped into the inbox folder in the shared Evidence Finder drive. It is filed, tagged against the syllabus and published from there, so the site always reflects the library. Ask the Legal Studies team if you have something to add.
