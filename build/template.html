What is in here. Every question from 2011 to 2025, the life of the 2009 syllabus: 300 Section I objective-response questions with their options and NESA's answer key, 51 Human Rights short answers, the 15 Crime extended responses, and both alternatives of the Family and Shelter option questions — 426 in all.

Two levels of tagging. Section I and Section II Part A are tagged to a single syllabus dot point, because a one- or two-mark question turns on one. The extended responses are tagged to whole syllabus sections, because a 15- or 25-mark answer draws on a section rather than a dot point.

How questions are matched. Each question carries a core focus — the elements it is built on, which you cannot answer without — and supporting elements, expected as evidence or context. Selecting a content point or theme shows both, and “Core focus only” narrows to the questions genuinely about it. This is a reading of the questions, not an official NESA mapping.

Section III sets two alternatives. Candidates answer part (a) or part (b), so the thirty alternatives in each option are thirty separate questions for revision but only fifteen papers.

Ticks are private to this device. They are kept in your own browser and are not sent anywhere. Ticking is also how the worksheet is built — tick the questions you want, then print.

Marking criteria. Band criteria are shown for every short answer and extended response, 2011 to 2025, with NESA's sample answers where they were published and the “answers could include” notes where NESA gave them — a recent practice, and much fuller from 2024. They unfold in stages, so a question can be attempted before the marking is read. Two gaps are NESA's own rather than ours: 2020 published no sample answers at all, and the 2013 guidelines set out their “answers could include” notes in a way that cannot be reproduced faithfully here — use the link to that year's guidelines instead.

Papers and marking guidelines. The examination papers and NESA marking guidelines for 2015 to 2025 sit beside this page, so a year link opens the paper itself and the guidelines are one click away. The 2011 to 2014 papers and guidelines are not held here; those links go to the Board of Studies archive, which still serves them.
