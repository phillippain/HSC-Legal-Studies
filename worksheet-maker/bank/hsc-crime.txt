# HSC Crime (2009 syllabus). Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.

=== mc cr-mc-01
c: crime.1.2
lv: 1
Q: Which term describes the mental element of a crime?
A: Actus reus
B: Mens rea
C: Causation
D: Strict liability
ANS: B
WHY: Mens rea is the guilty mind (intention, recklessness or criminal negligence). Actus reus is the physical act.

=== mc cr-mc-02
c: crime.1.3
lv: 2
Q: Why is speeding classed as a strict liability offence?
A: The prosecution must prove the driver intended to speed
B: It can only be heard in the District Court
C: The prosecution only needs to prove the act, not a guilty mind
D: No penalty can be imposed for it
ANS: C
WHY: Strict liability offences need proof of the actus reus only. An honest and reasonable mistake of fact can still be raised.

=== mc cr-mc-03
c: crime.1.4
lv: 2
LEAD: Sam punches Lee. Lee falls, hits his head and later dies in hospital from the head injury.
Q: Which element must the prosecution prove to link Sam's punch to Lee's death?
A: Causation
B: Motive
C: Recklessness
D: Complicity
ANS: A
WHY: Causation requires an unbroken link between the accused's act and the harm.

=== mc cr-mc-04

B: They are less serious offences heard by a magistrate in the Local Court

=== mc cr-mc-05

A: Principal in the first degree
B: Accessory after the fact
C: Accessory before the fact
D: Principal in the second degree
ANS: D
WHY: A principal in the second degree is present and assists while the crime is committed. Kim, who commits the act, is the principal in the first degree.

=== mc cr-mc-06

A: A parenting program for young families
B: Extra funding for school attendance officers
C: Better street lighting and CCTV at a train station

ANS: C
WHY: Situational prevention makes a crime harder or riskier to commit in a place. The other options are social prevention, which targets the causes of offending.

=== mc cr-mc-14

A: Self-defence
B: Duress
C: Substantial impairment because of mental health impairment or cognitive impairment
D: Necessity
ANS: C

=== order cr-or-01
c: crime.2.4 crime.3.1
TITLE: An indictable offence, from arrest to sentence
LEAD: An adult is accused of a serious indictable offence in NSW and pleads not guilty.
- Police arrest and charge the suspect
- A bail decision is made
- The charge is first mentioned in the Local Court
- The matter is committed to the District Court for trial
- A jury is empanelled
- The prosecution and the defence present their evidence
- The jury gives its verdict
- The judge sentences the offender
NOTE: Every indictable matter starts in the Local Court. Only the higher court can hold the trial.

=== order cr-or-02
c: crime.3.7
TITLE: The order of a criminal trial
- The prosecution makes its opening address
- Prosecution witnesses give evidence in chief
- The defence cross-examines prosecution witnesses
- The defence presents its case
- Both sides give closing addresses
- The judge sums up and directs the jury on the law
- The jury retires to consider its verdict
NOTE: The prosecution goes first because it carries the burden of proof.

=== chain cr-ch-01
c: crime.t.reform crime.2.5
TITLE: How a crisis can change bail law
TRIGGER: A person on bail commits a serious violent offence that is widely reported.
- The media report the case and question the bail decision => Media coverage shapes public opinion about the law.
- The community and victims' groups call for tougher laws => Public pressure reaches members of parliament.
- The government announces a review or a bill => Parliament responds to keep public confidence.
- Parliament amends the Bail Act 2013 (NSW) => Statute law can change quickly.
- More accused people are refused bail => The test is harder to meet.
- The remand population rises => More people who have not been convicted are held in custody.
