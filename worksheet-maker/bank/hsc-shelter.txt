# HSC Shelter (2009 syllabus, Option 5). Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.

=== terms shel-terms-01
c: shel.1.1 shel.1.2 shel.1.4
- Shelter => A place to live that gives protection, privacy and security.
- Adequate housing => Housing that is secure, affordable, habitable, accessible and in a suitable location, as described in international law.
- Social housing => Rental housing provided by the government or community organisations for people on low incomes.
- Boarding house => Premises where residents rent a room, not the whole dwelling, and share facilities.
- Strata title => Ownership of a unit or lot in a building, together with a share in the common property.

=== terms shel-terms-02
c: shel.2.1
- Torrens title => The system in which the government keeps a register of land ownership and guarantees the title shown on it.
- Private treaty => The sale of a property at a price negotiated between the seller and the buyer.
- Auction => A public sale in which the property is sold to the highest bidder, with no cooling-off period.
- Cooling-off period => A short time after contracts are exchanged in which a buyer can withdraw from a private treaty sale.
- Mortgage => A loan secured over land. The lender can sell the property if the borrower defaults.
- Caveat emptor => "Let the buyer beware": the buyer is responsible for checking the property before buying.
- Conveyancing => The legal work of transferring ownership of land from the seller to the buyer.
- Exchange of contracts => The point at which the buyer and the seller swap signed contracts and the sale becomes binding.

=== terms shel-terms-03
c: shel.2.2 shel.2.3 shel.2.6
- Residential tenancy agreement => A lease: a contract in which a landlord lets a tenant live in a property in return for rent.
- Rental bond => Money paid by a tenant at the start of a lease as security, lodged with NSW Fair Trading.
- Condition report => A record of the state of a rented property at the start of a tenancy.
- Termination notice => A written notice from a landlord or a tenant that ends a tenancy on a stated date.
- NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal => The tribunal that hears most tenancy, strata and home building disputes in NSW.
- Security of tenure => A tenant's confidence that they can stay in their home as long as they meet their obligations.

=== terms shel-terms-04
c: shel.3.1 shel.3.3
- Housing affordability => The relationship between housing costs and household income.
- Housing stress => Often defined as a lower-income household spending more than 30 per cent of its income on housing.
- Homelessness => Having no home, or living in housing that is inadequate, insecure or without control over one's own space.
- Crisis accommodation => Short-term emergency housing for people who have nowhere safe to stay.

=== mc shel-mc-01
c: shel.1.2
lv: 2
Q: Which international instrument recognises a right to adequate housing as part of an adequate standard of living?
A: International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights
B: International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights
C: Rome Statute
D: Refugee Convention
ANS: B
WHY: Article 11 of the Covenant covers an adequate standard of living, including housing. Article 25 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights says the same.

=== mc shel-mc-03

Q: A buyer purchases a house at auction in NSW. Which statement is correct?
A: The buyer has a five business day cooling-off period
B: The buyer has no cooling-off period
C: The buyer can withdraw at any time before settlement
D: The seller can accept a higher offer after the auction
ANS: B
WHY: The cooling-off period applies to private treaty sales only. An auction sale is binding when the hammer falls.

B: 0.25 per cent of the purchase price

ANS: B

=== mc shel-mc-05

A: Two weeks' rent
B: Four weeks' rent
C: Eight weeks' rent
D: Any amount the tenant agrees to
ANS: B
WHY: The Residential Tenancies Act 2010 (NSW) caps the bond at four weeks' rent. It must be lodged with NSW Fair Trading.

=== mc shel-mc-06
c: shel.2.2
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is an obligation of a landlord under a residential tenancy agreement?
A: To pay the tenant's electricity bills
B: To keep the premises in a reasonable state of repair
C: To inspect the premises every week
D: To approve the tenant's guests
ANS: B
WHY: The landlord must provide premises that are fit to live in and keep them in reasonable repair. Entry is limited and needs notice.

=== scen shel-sc-01
c: shel.2.2 shel.2.3 shel.2.6
lv: 2
LEAD: Mei rents a flat in Sydney on a 12-month lease. In month four the hot water system breaks. She emails the agent three times over two weeks and nothing happens. She pays a plumber $900 to fix it and asks the landlord to repay her. The landlord refuses and says she will be evicted for "causing trouble".
PART (a) 2: Identify the landlord's obligation that has been breached.
PTS:
- To keep the premises in reasonable repair (1)
- A failed hot water service is an urgent repair (1)
PART (b) 3: Explain whether Mei can recover the $900.
PTS:
- A tenant can arrange urgent repairs if the landlord cannot be contacted or does not act (1)
- The landlord must repay reasonable costs up to the limit set by the regulations (1)
- She should keep her notices and receipts as evidence (1)
PART (c) 3: Explain how the dispute could be resolved and whether the landlord's threat is lawful.
PTS:
- She can apply to the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal for an order (1)
- The Tribunal can refuse to end a tenancy where the landlord's notice is retaliatory (1)
- A landlord now needs a ground set out in the Act to end a tenancy (1)

=== scen shel-sc-02
c: shel.2.1
lv: 2
LEAD: Sam and Alex find a house they love. The agent says another buyer is interested and urges them to sign today. They exchange contracts by private treaty on Monday and pay a deposit. On Wednesday a building inspector finds serious termite damage.
PART (a) 2: Outline what the cooling-off period allows Sam and Alex to do.
PTS:
- Withdraw from the contract within five business days of exchange (1)
- They forfeit 0.25 per cent of the price (1)
PART (b) 3: Explain how the outcome would differ if they had bought the house at auction.
PTS:
- No cooling-off period applies at auction (1)
- They would be bound to complete or lose their deposit (1)
- Inspections must be done before bidding (1)
PART (c) 2: Explain what caveat emptor means for buyers.
PTS:
- The buyer bears the risk of defects they could have found (1)
- Buyers should get building, pest and strata reports before they commit (1)

Q: Evaluate the effectiveness of the law in protecting the rights of tenants.
PTS:
- Residential Tenancies Act 2010 (NSW): bonds, repairs, rent increases, entry
- The 2025 reforms: the end of no-grounds evictions and rules on pets
- The NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal: cheap and fast, but tenants often do not use it
- Boarders and lodgers: weaker protection
- Discrimination and affordability as limits on rights in practice
- Non-legal responses: the Tenants' Union, tenant advice services, the media
- A judgement using criteria such as accessibility, enforceability and responsiveness
PLAN:
- The rights on paper
- Enforcement through the Tribunal
- Reform: security of tenure
- Gaps: marginal renters and affordability
- Judgement

=== order shel-or-01
c: shel.2.1
TITLE: Buying a home by private treaty in NSW
- The buyer arranges loan pre-approval
- The buyer inspects the property and makes an offer
- The seller's agent sends the contract for sale to the buyer's conveyancer
- Contracts are exchanged and the deposit is paid
- The cooling-off period runs
- The lender finalises the mortgage
- Settlement takes place and the balance is paid
- The transfer is registered and the buyer becomes the registered owner
NOTE: The sale becomes binding at exchange, not when an offer is accepted.
