=== er y11-er-01

m: 12

Q: Explain how laws are made in Australia. In your answer, refer to both parliament and the courts.
PTS:
- Statute law: the stages of a bill and royal assent
- Common law: precedent, ratio decidendi and the court hierarchy
- The relationship between the two: statute overrides common law; courts interpret statutes
- The Constitution sets the limits of law-making power
- At least one named Act and one named case
PLAN:
- How parliament makes law
- How courts make law
- How the two work together
- Conclusion

Q: Assess the outcomes of law reform in relation to native title.
PTS:
- Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992) and the rejection of terra nullius
- Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) and later amendments
- Benefits: recognition, successful claims, agreements over land use
- Limitations: the burden of proving continuous connection, extinguishment, delay and cost
- A judgement
PLAN:
- What changed
- Benefits
- Limitations
- Judgement

Q: To what extent does the legal system achieve justice for young people?
PTS:
- Protection in domestic and international law
- Police powers and safeguards for young people
- The Children's Court and diversion under the Young Offenders Act 1997 (NSW)
- Barriers: knowledge, access to legal help, over-representation of some groups, detention
- A judgement on extent, with evidence
PLAN:
- Protections that work
- Diversion
- Barriers
- Judgement

=== er y11-er-05
c: exp.5 exp.6
m: 12
lv: 3
Q: Assess the responsiveness of the legal system to asylum seekers and refugees.
PTS:
- International obligations: the Refugee Convention and non-refoulement
- Migration Act 1958 (Cth): detention and visas
- Review by the Administrative Review Tribunal and the courts, including NZYQ (2023)
- Advocates: the Australian Human Rights Commission and non-government organisations
- Barriers: delay, detention, limited legal help
- A judgement
PLAN:
- Obligations
- Domestic law and review
- Barriers
- Judgement
