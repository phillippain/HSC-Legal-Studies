# Year 11 (2025 syllabus), Focus area 4: Experiences with the law. Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.
# The second group is the class's choice: asylum seekers and refugees.

=== terms y11-exp-terms-01
c: exp.1 exp.2 exp.3
- Evolving capacity => The idea that children gain the ability to make their own decisions as they mature.
- Age of criminal responsibility => The age below which a child cannot be charged with a criminal offence: 10 in NSW.
- Doli incapax => The presumption that a child aged 10 to under 14 does not know that their conduct is seriously wrong.
- Recidivism => Reoffending after being dealt with by the criminal justice system.
- Diversion => Dealing with an offender, especially a young person, outside the formal court process.
- Youth justice conference => A meeting in which a young offender, the victim and others agree on a plan to repair the harm.
- Convention on the Rights of the Child => The 1989 treaty that sets out the rights of everyone under 18.

=== terms y11-exp-terms-02
c: exp.4 exp.5 exp.6
- Asylum seeker => A person who has left their country and applied for protection as a refugee, and whose claim has not yet been decided.
- Refugee => A person outside their country who has a well-founded fear of persecution because of their race, religion, nationality, political opinion or membership of a particular social group.
- Non-refoulement => The duty not to return a person to a place where they face persecution or serious harm.
- Mandatory detention => The legal requirement that a non-citizen in Australia without a valid visa be detained.
- Offshore processing => The assessment of asylum claims in a country other than the one where protection is sought.
- Protection visa => A visa granted to a person in Australia who is found to be owed protection.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-01
c: exp.1
lv: 1
Q: Why does the law give young people special protection?
A: Young people have more money than adults
B: Young people are still developing and are more vulnerable to harm and exploitation
C: Young people cannot break the law
D: The Constitution requires it
ANS: B
WHY: The law recognises limited maturity and experience, while giving more independence as capacity grows.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-02
c: exp.1
lv: 2
Q: What is the minimum age of criminal responsibility in NSW?
A: 8
B: 10
C: 14
D: 16
ANS: B
WHY: A child under 10 cannot be charged. From 10 to under 14, doli incapax applies.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-03
c: exp.1
lv: 2
Q: What does a high rate of recidivism among young people who have been in detention suggest?
A: Detention is working well as a deterrent
B: Detention often fails to address the causes of offending
C: Young people commit more serious crimes than adults
D: The age of criminal responsibility is too high
ANS: B
WHY: If many young people reoffend after detention, rehabilitation has not been achieved. This supports diversion.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-04
c: exp.2
lv: 2
Q: Which international treaty sets out the rights of people under 18?
A: Refugee Convention
B: Convention on the Rights of the Child
C: Rome Statute
D: International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights
ANS: B
WHY: Australia ratified the Convention in 1990. It says the best interests of the child must be a primary consideration.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-05

A: The Local Court
B: The Children's Court
C: The District Court
D: The Coroner's Court
ANS: B
WHY: The Children's Court is a closed, specialist court. Very serious offences can be sent to a higher court.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-07

A: The National Children's Commissioner
B: The Director of Public Prosecutions
C: The Reserve Bank of Australia
D: The State Parole Authority
ANS: A
WHY: The Commissioner sits within the Australian Human Rights Commission. NSW also has an Advocate for Children and Young People.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-08
c: exp.3
lv: 2
Q: Which is a barrier to justice for many young people?
A: They are not allowed to have a lawyer
B: They often do not know their rights or how to get legal help
C: They cannot be witnesses
D: Courts are open only on weekends
ANS: B
WHY: Lack of knowledge, cost, distance and distrust of authority all limit access. Legal Aid NSW runs a Youth Hotline to help.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-09
c: exp.4
lv: 1
Q: What is the difference between an asylum seeker and a refugee?
A: An asylum seeker has been found to be owed protection; a refugee has not
B: An asylum seeker's claim for protection has not yet been decided; a refugee meets the legal definition
C: They are the same thing
D: A refugee always arrives by boat
ANS: B
WHY: Every refugee was first an asylum seeker. How a person arrives does not decide whether they are a refugee.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-10

A: Convention on the Rights of the Child
B: Convention relating to the Status of Refugees (1951)
C: Universal Declaration of Human Rights
D: Rome Statute
ANS: B
WHY: The Convention and its 1967 Protocol bind Australia in international law.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-11

=== mc y11-exp-mc-12
c: exp.5 exp.6
lv: 3
Q: What did the High Court decide in NZYQ v Minister for Immigration (2023)?
A: That offshore processing is unlawful
B: That indefinite immigration detention is unlawful where there is no real prospect of removing the person in the reasonably foreseeable future
C: That all asylum seekers must be given permanent visas
D: That the Refugee Convention is part of the Constitution
ANS: B
WHY: The decision overturned Al-Kateb v Godwin (2004) and led to the release of people held in long-term detention.

=== mc y11-exp-mc-13

ANS: B
WHY: The Tribunal replaced the Administrative Appeals Tribunal in October 2024 and conducts merits review of Commonwealth decisions.

=== scen y11-exp-sc-01
c: exp.2
lv: 2
LEAD: Kai, aged 15, is stopped by police at a train station after a security guard says he saw Kai take a pair of headphones from a shop. Kai admits he took them. It is his first contact with police. The officer wants to take a formal statement from him straight away, on the platform.
PART (a) 2: Outline TWO rights Kai has when police question him.
PTS:
- The right to silence, apart from giving his name and address (1)
- The right to have a responsible adult present and to get legal advice, for example from the Legal Aid Youth Hotline (1)
PART (b) 3: Explain the most likely outcome for Kai and why.
PTS:
- A caution, or possibly a warning, under the Young Offenders Act 1997 (NSW) (1)
- The offence is minor, he admits it and it is his first (1)
- Diversion aims to keep young people out of court (1)

=== scen y11-exp-sc-02
c: exp.4 exp.5
lv: 2
LEAD: Samira fled her country after she was threatened because of her work as a journalist. She arrived in Australia on a student visa and has now applied for a protection visa. A departmental officer refuses her application, saying she could safely live in another part of her country.
PART (a) 2: Explain whether Samira is an asylum seeker or a refugee at this point.
PTS:
- She is an asylum seeker because her claim has not been finally decided (1)
- She would be a refugee if she is found to have a well-founded fear of persecution for a Convention reason, such as political opinion (1)
PART (b) 3: Outline what Samira can do about the refusal.
PTS:
- Apply for merits review by the Administrative Review Tribunal (1)
- Seek help from a refugee legal service or advocacy group (1)
- Judicial review by a court is possible if there was a legal error (1)
