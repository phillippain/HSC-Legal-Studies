# Year 11 (2025 syllabus), Focus area 3: Changing the law. Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.
# Case study 2 is the class's choice: the social media minimum age.

=== terms y11-chg-terms-01
c: chg.1 chg.2 chg.3 chg.4
- Law reform => The process of changing the law so that it better meets the needs and values of society.
- Law reform commission => An independent body that reviews areas of law and recommends changes to the government.
- Royal commission => A major public inquiry, set up by the government, with strong powers to gather evidence.
- Advocacy group => An organisation that campaigns to influence public opinion and the law on an issue.
- Repeal => The removal of a law by parliament.
- Amendment => A change made to an existing law or to a bill.
- Statutory interpretation => The process by which courts work out the meaning of the words in an Act.

=== terms y11-chg-terms-02
c: chg.5
- Terra nullius => "Land belonging to no one": the doctrine, rejected in 1992, that Australia had no owners before British settlement.
- Native title => The recognition in Australian law of the rights and interests of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples in land and waters under their traditional laws and customs.
- Land rights => Rights to land granted to Aboriginal people by legislation, such as the Aboriginal Land Rights Act 1983 (NSW).
- Aboriginal Land Council => A body set up under NSW legislation that can claim certain Crown land and manage it for Aboriginal people.
- Extinguishment => The ending of native title, for example by a grant of freehold land.

=== terms y11-chg-terms-03
c: chg.6
- eSafety Commissioner => Australia's independent regulator for online safety.
- Age assurance => Methods used to check or estimate the age of a person using an online service.
- Age-restricted social media platform => A social media service that Australian law requires to take reasonable steps to stop people under 16 from holding accounts.

=== mc y11-chg-mc-01

Q: Which of the following is a reason for law reform?
A: Laws should never change
B: Changing social values, new technology and the failure of existing law
C: Judges prefer new laws
D: The Constitution requires a new law every year
ANS: B
WHY: Law must keep up with society. When it falls behind, people lose confidence in it.

B: Strike down invalid laws

ANS: C

=== mc y11-chg-mc-05
c: chg.3
lv: 2
Q: How can courts change the law?
A: By holding a referendum
B: By interpreting statutes and creating precedents in the cases that come before them
C: By introducing bills into parliament
D: By giving royal assent
ANS: B
WHY: Judges make law only when a case raises the issue. Parliament can override a decision by passing a statute.

=== mc y11-chg-mc-06
c: chg.4
lv: 2
Q: Which is a limitation of law reform through parliament?
A: Parliament cannot change statute law
B: Reform can be slow and shaped by political interests
C: Parliament can only act when a case is brought
D: Acts of parliament apply to one person only
ANS: B
WHY: Governments may avoid controversial reforms or lack the numbers to pass them. Courts, not parliament, must wait for a case.

=== mc y11-chg-mc-07

Q: What did the High Court decide in Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992)?
A: That terra nullius applied to Australia
B: That native title exists at common law where traditional connection to the land has continued
C: That all freehold land must be returned
D: That the Constitution recognises Aboriginal sovereignty
ANS: B
WHY: The Court rejected terra nullius and recognised the native title of the Meriam people to the Murray Islands.

=== mc y11-chg-mc-09
c: chg.5
lv: 3
Q: How do land rights under the Aboriginal Land Rights Act 1983 (NSW) differ from native title?
A: Land rights are granted by statute and do not need proof of continuous traditional connection
B: Land rights come from the common law
C: Land rights apply only in the Torres Strait
D: There is no difference
ANS: A
WHY: Land councils can claim certain unused Crown land as compensation for dispossession. Native title recognises rights that have survived under traditional law and custom.

=== mc y11-chg-mc-10

=== chain y11-chg-ch-01
c: chg.6 chg.2
TITLE: From concern to law: the social media minimum age
TRIGGER: Parents, researchers and the media raise concerns about the effect of social media on young people's mental health.
- Advocacy groups and media campaigns call for an age limit => Informal methods build public pressure.
- Governments announce support for a minimum age => Political will is needed before parliament acts.
- A bill is introduced into the Commonwealth Parliament => Statute law is the fastest way to regulate a new problem.
- Parliament passes the Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024 (Cth) => Both houses agreed to the bill.
- The eSafety Commissioner prepares to enforce the law => An independent regulator is given the job.
- Platforms must take reasonable steps to keep under-16s from holding accounts => The duty began on 10 December 2025.

=== chain y11-chg-ch-02
c: chg.5
TITLE: From the Murray Islands to the Native Title Act
TRIGGER: In 1982 Eddie Koiki Mabo and other Meriam people begin a legal claim to their traditional lands.
- The case reaches the High Court => Only the High Court could overturn the doctrine of terra nullius.
- The High Court rejects terra nullius in 1992 => The Court found the doctrine was wrong in fact and unjust.
- Native title is recognised at common law => Traditional rights survived where connection continued and had not been extinguished.
- Governments, miners and farmers seek certainty => No one knew which land could be claimed.
- Parliament passes the Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) => Statute set up a process for claims.

=== order y11-chg-or-01
c: chg.2 chg.3
TITLE: How a law reform commission inquiry leads to a new law
- The Attorney-General gives the commission terms of reference
- The commission researches the law and releases a discussion paper
- The public and experts make submissions
- The commission gives its final report with recommendations
- The government decides which recommendations to accept
- A bill is introduced and passed by parliament
