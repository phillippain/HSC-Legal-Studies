# HSC Family (2009 syllabus, Option 3). Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.

=== terms fam-terms-01
c: fam.1.1 fam.1.3 fam.1.4
- Marriage => In Australian law, the union of two people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life.
- De facto relationship => A relationship between two people who live together as a couple on a genuine domestic basis and are not married to each other.
- Blended family => A family formed when a couple live together with children from earlier relationships.
- Kinship => The system of family relationships and obligations that connects Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people to each other, to community and to Country.
- Void marriage => A marriage that has no legal effect, for example because one person was already married or did not give real consent.

=== terms fam-terms-02
c: fam.1.5 fam.1.6 fam.2.2
- Parental responsibility => All the duties, powers, responsibilities and authority that parents have by law in relation to their children.
- Best interests of the child => The paramount consideration a court must apply when it makes a parenting order.
- Adoption => The legal process that permanently transfers parental rights and responsibilities from the birth parents to the adoptive parents.
- Parenting plan => A written, signed and dated agreement between parents about the care of their children. It is not enforceable by a court.
- Parenting order => A court order about who a child lives with, spends time with and communicates with, and who makes major decisions.
- Child support => Money paid by a parent towards the cost of raising a child after separation.

=== terms fam-terms-03
c: fam.2.1 fam.2.3 fam.2.4 fam.2.5
- Divorce => The legal ending of a marriage by a court order.
- Irretrievable breakdown => The only ground for divorce in Australia, shown by 12 months of separation.
- Property settlement => The division of the assets and debts of a couple after separation.
- Binding financial agreement => A written agreement between partners, made with independent legal advice, about how property will be divided if they separate.
- Apprehended domestic violence order => A court order that restricts a person's behaviour to protect someone they have a domestic relationship with.
- Family dispute resolution => A process in which an independent practitioner helps separating families to agree, usually required before applying for a parenting order.
- Coercive control => A pattern of abusive behaviour used to control or dominate an intimate partner.

=== terms fam-terms-04
c: fam.3.3 fam.3.4
- Surrogacy => An arrangement in which a woman agrees to carry a child for another person or couple who will become the child's parents.
- Altruistic surrogacy => Surrogacy in which the birth mother receives no payment beyond her reasonable costs.
- Parentage order => A court order that transfers legal parentage of a child from the birth mother to the intended parents after surrogacy.
- Mandatory reporting => The legal duty of certain workers, such as teachers and doctors, to report a child at risk of significant harm.
- Out-of-home care => Care for a child who cannot live safely at home, such as foster care or kinship care.

=== mc fam-mc-01
c: fam.1.2
lv: 2
Q: Which level of government makes laws about marriage and divorce in Australia?
A: The states, under their residual powers
B: The Commonwealth, under the Constitution
C: Local councils
D: The United Nations
ANS: B
WHY: The Constitution gives the Commonwealth power over marriage and divorce. The states keep areas such as adoption and child protection.

=== mc fam-mc-02
c: fam.1.3
lv: 1
Q: What is the marriageable age in Australia?
A: 16 for everyone
B: 18, or 16 to 17 with a court order in exceptional circumstances
C: 21
D: Any age with parental consent
ANS: B
WHY: The Marriage Act 1961 (Cth) sets 18. A person aged 16 or 17 needs a court order and parental consent to marry someone who is 18 or over.

=== mc fam-mc-03
c: fam.1.3
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is NOT a requirement of a valid marriage in Australia?
A: Both people give real consent
B: A notice of intended marriage is given at least one month before the wedding
C: The ceremony is held in a religious building
D: Two adult witnesses are present
ANS: C
WHY: A marriage can be held anywhere by an authorised celebrant. Consent, notice and witnesses are required.

=== mc fam-mc-04
c: fam.2.1
lv: 1
Q: What must be shown to obtain a divorce in Australia?
A: That one spouse was at fault
B: That the marriage has broken down irretrievably, shown by 12 months of separation
C: That both spouses agree to the divorce
D: That the couple have no children
ANS: B
WHY: The Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) introduced no-fault divorce. Neither fault nor agreement is needed.

=== mc fam-mc-05
c: fam.2.2
lv: 2
Q: What is the paramount consideration when a court makes a parenting order?
A: The wishes of the parents
B: The best interests of the child
C: The income of each parent
D: An equal split of time
ANS: B
WHY: The Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) makes the child's best interests paramount. There is no rule that time must be equal.

=== mc fam-mc-06
c: fam.3.2
lv: 3
Q: What did the Family Law Amendment Act 2023 (Cth) change about parenting orders?
A: It removed the best interests test
B: It removed the presumption of equal shared parental responsibility
C: It ended family dispute resolution
D: It moved parenting cases to the Local Court
ANS: B
WHY: From May 2024 the presumption introduced in 2006 no longer applies, and the list of best interests factors was simplified with a stronger focus on safety.

=== mc fam-mc-07
c: fam.2.3
lv: 2
Q: How does a court divide property after a marriage ends?
A: Each person keeps what is in their name
B: Property is always divided equally
C: The court makes an order that is just and equitable after weighing contributions and future needs
D: The person who earned more receives everything
ANS: C
WHY: The court counts financial and non-financial contributions, including homemaking and parenting, and each person's future needs.

Q: Explain how the law recognises de facto relationships.
PTS:
- A de facto couple live together on a genuine domestic basis (1)
- Courts look at factors such as how long they have lived together and shared finances (1)
- Since 2009 the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) has covered property and maintenance for most de facto couples (1)
- Children are treated the same whether or not their parents are married (1)

=== sa fam-sa-02

m: 5
lv: 2
Q: Explain why the law encourages families to resolve disputes without going to court.
PTS:
- Family dispute resolution is usually required before applying for a parenting order (1)
- It is cheaper and faster than a hearing (1)
- Agreements parents make themselves are more likely to last (1)
- It reduces conflict that harms children (1)
- A limit: it is not suitable where there is family violence or an imbalance of power (1)

=== scen fam-sc-01
c: fam.2.1 fam.2.2
lv: 2
LEAD: Priya and Tom married eight years ago and have a daughter, Mia, aged 6. They separated ten months ago but still live in the same house because neither can afford to move out. Tom wants a divorce now. Priya wants Mia to live mainly with her.
PART (a) 3: Explain whether Tom can apply for a divorce now.
PTS:
- The ground is irretrievable breakdown, shown by 12 months of separation (1)
- Only ten months have passed, so he must wait (1)
- Separation under one roof can count, but it must be proved (1)
PART (b) 4: Explain how a decision about Mia's living arrangements would be made if Priya and Tom cannot agree.
PTS:
- They must usually try family dispute resolution first (1)
- A court can then make a parenting order (1)
- Mia's best interests are the paramount consideration (1)
- The court considers her safety, her views and her need for a relationship with both parents where that is safe (1)

=== scen fam-sc-02
c: fam.2.4
lv: 2
LEAD: Over two years, Jordan's partner has checked Jordan's phone every day, controlled all the money, and stopped Jordan from seeing friends. Last week the partner threatened to hurt Jordan if Jordan left. Jordan lives in NSW and is frightened.
PART (a) 2: Identify TWO legal protections available to Jordan.
PTS:
- An apprehended domestic violence order (1)
- Criminal charges, such as for intimidation or coercive control (1)
PART (b) 4: Assess how effective these protections are likely to be.
PTS:
- An order sets clear conditions and police can apply for it (1)
- A breach is a criminal offence (1)
- Limits: orders are sometimes breached and victims may fear reporting (1)
- A judgement, with reference to support services or recent reform (1)

=== scen fam-sc-03
c: fam.2.3
lv: 3
LEAD: Elena and Chris were married for 15 years. Chris worked full time and paid the mortgage. Elena left paid work to care for their three children and now earns much less than Chris. The house is in Chris's name only. They have separated and cannot agree on how to divide their property.
PART (a) 2: Outline TWO types of contribution a court would consider.
PTS:
- Financial contributions, such as income and mortgage payments (1)
- Non-financial contributions, such as homemaking and caring for children (1)
PART (b) 4: Explain why Elena could receive a share of the house even though it is in Chris's name.
PTS:
- The court looks at all the property of both people, whoever holds title (1)
- Her contributions as homemaker and parent are recognised (1)
- Future needs matter: her lower earning capacity and care of the children (1)
- The order must be just and equitable in all the circumstances (1)

=== order fam-or-01
c: fam.2.1 fam.2.2
TITLE: Resolving a parenting dispute after separation
- The parents separate
- The parents try to agree between themselves, perhaps in a parenting plan
- The parents attend family dispute resolution
- A certificate is issued if no agreement is reached
- One parent applies to the court for a parenting order
- The court considers the best interests of the child
- The court makes a parenting order
NOTE: Court is the last step. Cases involving family violence or urgency can skip dispute resolution.
