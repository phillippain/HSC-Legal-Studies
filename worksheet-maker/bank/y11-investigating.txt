# Year 11 (2025 syllabus), Focus area 1: Investigating the law. Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.

=== terms y11-inv-terms-01
c: inv.1
- Law => A set of rules made by the state that binds everyone in the community and is enforced by the courts.
- Rule of law => The principle that everyone, including the government, is subject to the law and equal before it.
- Justice => The fair and impartial treatment of all people under the law.
- Procedural fairness => The right to a fair hearing by an unbiased decision-maker.
- Public law => Law that governs the relationship between individuals and the state, such as criminal and constitutional law.
- Private law => Law that governs relationships between individuals, such as contract and tort law.
- Anarchy => The absence of law and government.

=== terms y11-inv-terms-02
c: inv.3
- Common law => Law made by judges through their decisions in cases.
- Statute law => Law made by parliament.
- Precedent => A court decision that guides or binds later courts deciding similar cases.
- Ratio decidendi => The legal reason for a court's decision, which forms the binding precedent.
- Obiter dicta => Remarks made by a judge in passing that are not binding but may be persuasive.
- Stare decisis => The principle that courts stand by what has already been decided.
- Bill => A proposed law that has not yet been passed by parliament.
- Royal assent => The formal approval of a bill by the Governor-General or a Governor, which makes it an Act.
- Delegated legislation => Rules made by a body that parliament has given the power to make them, such as regulations and by-laws.

=== terms y11-inv-terms-03
c: inv.4 inv.5
- Jurisdiction => The power of a court to hear a particular kind of matter.
- Original jurisdiction => The power of a court to hear a case for the first time.
- Appellate jurisdiction => The power of a court to hear appeals from lower courts.
- Adversarial system => A system of trial in which two opposing sides present their cases to an impartial judge or jury.
- Division of powers => The way the Constitution shares law-making power between the Commonwealth and the states.
- Separation of powers => The division of government into the legislature, the executive and the judiciary so that no one body holds all power.
- Exclusive powers => Law-making powers that only the Commonwealth Parliament can use.
- Concurrent powers => Law-making powers shared by the Commonwealth and the states.
- Residual powers => Law-making powers left with the states because the Constitution does not give them to the Commonwealth.
- Referendum => A national vote on a proposal to change the Constitution.

=== terms y11-inv-terms-04
c: inv.2 inv.6
- Customary Lore => The laws, customs and practices of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples, passed on by word of mouth, story, song and ceremony.
- Elder => A respected senior member of an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander community who holds and passes on knowledge and Lore.
- Country => The lands, waters and skies to which Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples are connected.
- Customary international law => Rules that bind states because states follow them consistently out of a sense of legal duty.
- Nation-state => A political unit with a defined territory, a permanent population, a government and the capacity to deal with other states.

=== mc y11-inv-mc-01
c: inv.1
lv: 1
Q: What is the main difference between a law and a rule?
A: Laws are written down; rules are not
B: Laws apply to everyone in society and are enforced by the state
C: Rules carry heavier penalties than laws
D: Laws are made by schools and clubs
ANS: B
WHY: Rules apply to members of a group, such as a school. Laws bind the whole community and are enforced by police and courts.

=== mc y11-inv-mc-02
c: inv.1
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is a characteristic of an effective law?
A: It is kept secret until it is enforced
B: It is known, clear and able to be enforced
C: It applies only to some people
D: It never changes
ANS: B
WHY: People can only obey laws they can find out about and understand, and a law that cannot be enforced will be ignored.

=== mc y11-inv-mc-03

A: Criminal law
B: Constitutional law
C: Private (civil) law
D: International law
ANS: C
WHY: A dispute between individuals over a contract is private law. The state is not a party.

=== mc y11-inv-mc-14

B: A written agreement between states that binds the states that ratify it
C: A decision of the International Court of Justice

ANS: B
WHY: Treaties bind only the states that agree to them. In Australia a treaty also needs legislation before it applies in domestic law.

WHY: Australia has no national bill of rights. Rights come from several sources, each with limits.

=== sa y11-inv-sa-01
c: inv.3
m: 4
lv: 2
Q: Explain how the doctrine of precedent promotes fairness and consistency.
PTS:
- Courts follow earlier decisions of higher courts in the same hierarchy (1)
- Like cases are decided alike (1)
- People and lawyers can predict how the law will apply (1)
- Judges can still distinguish a case on its facts, which allows the law to develop (1)

=== sa y11-inv-sa-02
c: inv.5
m: 4
lv: 2
Q: Explain why the separation of powers is important.
PTS:
- Names the three arms and what each does (1)
- No single body makes, carries out and judges the law (1)
- Independent courts can check the other arms (1)
- This protects individuals from the abuse of power (1)

=== scen y11-inv-sc-01

LEAD: The NSW Parliament passes a law that says shops may refuse to accept cash. A year later the Commonwealth Parliament passes a valid law, under its currency power, that says all shops must accept cash for purchases under $500. A shop owner in Sydney does not know which law to follow.

=== order y11-inv-or-01
c: inv.3
TITLE: How a bill becomes an Act of the Commonwealth Parliament
- The bill is drafted
- First reading: the bill is introduced
- Second reading: the minister explains the bill and it is debated
- Consideration in detail: the bill is examined clause by clause
- Third reading: the house votes on the bill
- The bill goes through the same stages in the other house
- The Governor-General gives royal assent
NOTE: A bill must pass both houses in the same form before royal assent.
