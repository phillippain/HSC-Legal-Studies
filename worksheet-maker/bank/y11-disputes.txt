# Year 11 (2025 syllabus), Focus area 2: Disputes under the law. Written 28 November 2026 by Claude. Not yet reviewed.

=== terms y11-dis-terms-01
c: dis.1
- Prosecution => The party that brings a criminal case against an accused person on behalf of the state.
- Accused => A person charged with a criminal offence.
- Presumption of innocence => The principle that an accused person is treated as innocent until proved guilty.
- Beyond reasonable doubt => The standard of proof in a criminal case.
- Summary offence => A less serious offence heard by a magistrate in the Local Court.
- Indictable offence => A serious offence usually tried by a judge and jury.
- Sanction => A penalty imposed by a court on a person found guilty of an offence.

=== terms y11-dis-terms-02
c: dis.2
- Plaintiff => The person who starts a civil case.
- Defendant => In a civil case, the person who is being sued.
- Balance of probabilities => The standard of proof in a civil case: more likely than not.
- Tort => A civil wrong, other than a breach of contract, for which the law provides a remedy.
- Negligence => A failure to take reasonable care that causes harm to a person to whom a duty of care is owed.
- Duty of care => A legal obligation to avoid acts or omissions that could reasonably be foreseen to harm others.
- Contract => A legally binding agreement between two or more parties.
- Damages => Money awarded by a court to compensate a plaintiff for loss.
- Injunction => A court order that requires a person to do, or to stop doing, something.

=== terms y11-dis-terms-03
c: dis.3
- Administrative law => The area of law that deals with the decisions and actions of government bodies and officials.
- Judicial review => A court's review of whether a government decision was made lawfully.
- Merits review => A fresh look at a government decision by a tribunal, which can change the decision.
- Tribunal => A body that resolves certain disputes with less cost and formality than a court.
- Ombudsman => An independent official who investigates complaints about government agencies.
- Alternative dispute resolution => Ways of settling a dispute without a court hearing, such as negotiation, mediation and conciliation.
- Mediation => A process in which a neutral third person helps the parties reach their own agreement.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-01
c: dis.1
lv: 1
Q: Who brings a criminal case to court?
A: The victim
B: The state, through the police or the Director of Public Prosecutions
C: The plaintiff
D: The jury
ANS: B
WHY: A crime is treated as a wrong against the whole community, so the state prosecutes.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-02
c: dis.1
lv: 2
Q: What must the prosecution prove for most criminal offences?
A: Motive and opportunity
B: Actus reus and mens rea
C: Breach and loss
D: Offer and acceptance
ANS: B
WHY: The physical act and the guilty mind must both be proved. Strict liability offences are the exception.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-03
c: dis.1
lv: 2
LEAD: A driver is fined for travelling 15 km/h over the speed limit. She says she did not notice the sign.
Q: Why is she still liable?
A: Speeding is an indictable offence
B: Speeding is a strict liability offence, so intention need not be proved
C: She has the burden of proving her innocence beyond reasonable doubt
D: Driving offences are civil matters
ANS: B
WHY: For strict liability offences the prosecution proves the act only.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-04
c: dis.2
lv: 1
Q: What is the standard of proof in a civil case?
A: Beyond reasonable doubt
B: On the balance of probabilities
C: Beyond any doubt
D: Reasonable suspicion
ANS: B
WHY: The plaintiff must show their version is more likely than not.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-05
c: dis.2
lv: 2
Q: Which case established the modern law of negligence and the "neighbour principle"?
A: Mabo v Queensland (No 2)
B: Donoghue v Stevenson
C: Dietrich v The Queen
D: RP v The Queen
ANS: B
WHY: In 1932 the House of Lords held that a manufacturer owed a duty of care to the person who consumed its product.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-06
c: dis.2
lv: 2
Q: What must a plaintiff prove to succeed in a negligence claim?
A: Intention, act and motive
B: A duty of care, a breach of that duty, and damage caused by the breach
C: Offer, acceptance and consideration
D: Guilt beyond reasonable doubt
ANS: B
WHY: These are the elements of negligence. Offer, acceptance and consideration are elements of a contract.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-07
c: dis.2
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is needed for a valid contract?
A: A written document signed in front of a witness
B: Offer, acceptance, consideration and an intention to create legal relations
C: Approval by a court
D: Payment in full in advance
ANS: B
WHY: Many contracts are verbal. The elements, not the form, make an agreement binding.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-08
c: dis.2
lv: 1
Q: What is the most common remedy in a civil case?
A: Imprisonment
B: A fine paid to the state
C: Damages paid to the plaintiff
D: A community correction order
ANS: C
WHY: Civil remedies aim to compensate the person who was wronged. Fines and imprisonment are criminal sanctions.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-09
c: dis.3
lv: 2
Q: A person wants an independent body to take a fresh look at a Commonwealth visa decision and, if it is wrong, change it. Which process is this?
A: Judicial review
B: Merits review
C: A criminal appeal
D: A referendum
ANS: B
WHY: Merits review, by a body such as the Administrative Review Tribunal, reconsiders the facts, law and policy. Judicial review only asks whether the decision was lawful.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-10
c: dis.3
lv: 2
Q: Which body investigates complaints about the conduct of NSW government agencies?
A: The NSW Ombudsman
B: The Director of Public Prosecutions
C: The Coroner's Court
D: The Law Society of NSW
ANS: A
WHY: The Ombudsman is independent, free to use and can recommend changes, but cannot usually force an agency to act.

=== mc y11-dis-mc-11
c: dis.3
lv: 2
Q: Which is an advantage of using a tribunal rather than a court?
A: Tribunals can impose prison sentences
B: Tribunals are usually cheaper, quicker and less formal
C: Tribunal decisions can never be appealed
D: Tribunals always use juries
ANS: B
WHY: Tribunals such as the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal are designed to be accessible to people without lawyers.

Q: Distinguish between criminal law and civil law.
PTS:
- Purpose: criminal law punishes and protects the community; civil law compensates (1)

=== scen y11-dis-sc-01
c: dis.2
lv: 2
LEAD: Ben buys a takeaway salad from a cafe. Halfway through eating it he finds a piece of broken glass and cuts his mouth. He needs stitches and misses three days of work. The cafe owner says a jar broke in the kitchen that morning and the staff "thought they had cleaned it all up".
PART (a) 3: Explain whether the cafe owed Ben a duty of care and whether it was breached.
PTS:
- A business that sells food owes a duty of care to its customers (1)
- Harm from glass in food is reasonably foreseeable (1)
- Serving food after a jar broke nearby, without proper checks, is a failure to take reasonable care (1)
PART (b) 2: Identify the remedy Ben would seek and what it would cover.
PTS:
- Damages (1)
- Medical costs, lost wages, and pain and suffering (1)
PART (c) 2: Explain why Ben's case is a civil matter and not a criminal one.
PTS:
- It is a dispute between private parties about compensation (1)
- Ben, as plaintiff, must prove his case on the balance of probabilities (1)

=== scen y11-dis-sc-02
c: dis.3
lv: 2
LEAD: Lina's application for a disability support payment is refused by a Commonwealth agency. The letter gives no clear reasons. She believes the officer ignored her doctor's report.
PART (a) 2: Outline the first steps Lina should take.
PTS:
- Ask the agency for reasons and for an internal review (1)
- Keep to the time limits for review (1)
PART (b) 3: Explain how a tribunal could help if the internal review fails.
PTS:
- The Administrative Review Tribunal can conduct a merits review (1)
- It looks at the evidence afresh, including the doctor's report (1)
- It can affirm, vary or set aside the decision (1)

=== order y11-dis-or-01
c: dis.2
TITLE: Steps in a civil dispute
- The dispute arises
- The parties try to negotiate
- A letter of demand is sent
- The parties attend mediation
- The plaintiff files a statement of claim
- The court hears the case
- The court gives judgment and orders a remedy
NOTE: Most civil disputes settle before a hearing.
