Key terms

Shelter
Protection from the elements and a place to live. At its widest, adequate housing.
Adequate housing
Housing that meets the seven elements in CESCR General Comment No 4: security of tenure, services, affordability, habitability, accessibility, location and cultural adequacy.
Tenure
The legal basis on which a person occupies a home, such as owning, renting or boarding.
Security of tenure
Legal protection against being forced out of your home without a good reason and a fair process.
Habitability
Being fit to live in: safe, dry, with enough space and protection from cold, damp and heat.
Asset
Something owned that holds or grows in value. A house is both a home and an asset.
Homelessness (ABS)
Having no suitable alternative and living in a dwelling that is inadequate, has no secure tenure, or gives no control over space. It includes severe overcrowding.

The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966). It protects economic, social and cultural rights such as work, health, education and an adequate standard of living.

What does it mean to say Australia has a dualist legal system?

Hint: international law into domestic law
A treaty Australia ratifies has no effect in Australian courts until Parliament passes an Act to put it into domestic law.

Why are economic and social rights harder to enforce than civil and political rights?

Hint: progressive realisation
States only promise to achieve them progressively, within available resources, so they read as goals. They are rarely written into domestic law that a court can apply.

1.1What does 'shelter' mean?

What it isShelter is protection from the elements and a place to live. At its widest it means adequate housing: a home a person can afford, is safe in, and can stay in.
The lawNo NSW or Commonwealth Act defines 'shelter'. The fullest definition is international: ICESCR art 11(1) and CESCR General Comment No 4 (1991), which lists seven elements of adequacy.
An exampleThe ABS counts a person in a severely crowded dwelling as homeless even though they have a roof. 122,494 people were counted as homeless on Census night 2021.
Why it mattersThe definition sets the test. If shelter is only a roof, the law looks effective. If it is adequate housing, then affordability and security of tenure count too, and the law looks much weaker.

Adequate housing: the seven elements

Security of tenurelegal protection from forced eviction
Serviceswater, energy, sanitation
Affordabilityhousing costs leave enough for other needs
Habitabilitysafe, dry, enough space
Accessibilityusable by people with disability and other groups in need
Locationnear work, school, health care
Cultural adequacyrespects cultural identity
Source: CESCR General Comment No 4 (1991), para 8

Use it: These seven elements are ready-made criteria for judging any shelter law.

2. Write a one-sentence definition of shelter that you could use to open an extended response.

Shelter is more than a physical structure: it is adequate housing, meaning a home that is secure, affordable and habitable, as set out in ICESCR art 11 and General Comment No 4.

3. Why does a wide definition make the law look less effective?

A wide definition brings affordability, location and security of tenure into the test. Many people with a roof fail those standards, so more of the law's gaps are counted.

1.2A home or an asset?

What it isIn Australia a house does two jobs. It is a place to live and the main way households build wealth. The two jobs pull apart: rising prices reward owners and lock out people seeking shelter.
The lawThe law protects the asset precisely: indefeasible title (Real Property Act 1900 (NSW) s 42) and the tax-free family home. Protection of housing as shelter is newer and thinner (Residential Tenancies Act 2010 (NSW), strengthened in 2024).
An exampleOutright owners' disposable income is 34% above renters'. Count the untaxed income a home gives its owner and the gap is 86% (Siminski and Wilkins, 2025).
Why it mattersIt shows what the law has valued. Values are shifting: the 2026 Budget limited negative gearing for established homes bought after budget night, from 1 July 2027.
CASE STUDY

The 'home owners' welfare state'

What the tax system does for people who already own
86%gap between outright owners' and renters' disposable income once housing income is counted
  • Research by Professors Peter Siminski and Roger Wilkins, using HILDA data, reported by ABC News on 13 August 2025
  • Standard income figures ignore what a home gives its owner: living rent-free and untaxed capital gains
  • The family home is exempt from capital gains tax (about $50 billion a year in revenue forgone) and from the Age Pension assets test
  • Counting housing income moves Australia from 16th to 10th most unequal in the OECD (the authors' own calculation)
Law: Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Cth), main residence exemption
How this shows shelter as an asset
The research shows a legal system that rewards people who already own a home. Tax-free housing draws money into housing and lifts prices, which makes it harder for younger people and renters to secure shelter at all.
Your turn: How does the tax treatment of the family home affect people who are still seeking shelter?
Because gains on the family home are untaxed, housing is an attractive place to hold wealth. That pushes prices up, so people without a home need a larger deposit and loan, while renters receive none of the benefit.

2. 'Australian law treats housing as an asset first and as shelter second.' Do you agree? Answer in two sentences with one piece of evidence.

Largely, yes. Owners have had indefeasible title since 1900 and a tax-free home, while renters only gained protection from no-grounds eviction in 2025; the 86% income gap shows who the settings favour. The 2024 tenancy reforms and 2026 Budget suggest the balance is starting to shift.
