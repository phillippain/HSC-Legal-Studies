How a judge reaches a sentence

Sentencing follows a guilty plea or verdict. The judge alone decides. A jury never sentences.

  1. Find the facts. Agreed facts, criminal record, reports and any victim impact statement.
  2. Fix the limits. The maximum penalty set by Parliament, any standard non-parole period, and guideline judgments.
  3. Assess the seriousness of the offence itself.
  4. Weigh the purposes of sentencing in s 3A.
  5. Weigh the factors in s 21A: aggravating, mitigating and those personal to the offender.
  6. Apply discounts for a guilty plea or for assisting authorities.
  7. Choose the penalty. Prison only if no other penalty is appropriate (s 5).

All sections are from the Crimes (Sentencing Procedure) Act 1999 (NSW).
