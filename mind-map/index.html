How to use the map

Read a dot point

Click any box. Boxes joined to it stay bright; the rest fade. On a computer the links are drawn as lines. The panel at the bottom explains why each link exists, shows how often the dot point has been examined, and lists evidence, case summaries and the past questions for it.

The four kinds of link

    The four views

    • Topic groups dot points the way the syllabus does.
    • Theme regroups them under the themes and challenges that run across topics (for example Compliance & non-compliance or Law reform), so you can see one theme through Crime, Human Rights, Family and Shelter at once.
    • Past questions shades each HSC dot point by how often it has been a core focus of a question since 2011, and flags any that have not been core for six years or more. Year 11 content is not examined in the HSC, so it stays grey.
    • Exam skill colours each dot point by the highest demand a written question on it has reached (D describe, E explain, A analyse, V evaluate), so you know how far to take your answer.

    Pathways, Flow viewer, Print

    Pathways are guided walks through one idea. Flow viewer opens for the dot point you have selected and shows what leads into it and out of it, two steps each way. Print prints the whole map on A3 landscape, with the selected dot point's details underneath.

    Keep track

    Tick I have revised this in the bottom panel. Ticks are stored in this browser only, so they will not follow you to another device.

    About the links

    The links are a teaching judgement, not part of the syllabus. They are written to help you make connections, so if one seems wrong or one is missing, tell your teacher.
